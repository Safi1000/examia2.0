The 0264 and 7081 specimen papers are Cambridge's official samples of the new Business exams from 2027. Paper 1 has four questions of 20 marks each, split 2, 4, 6 and 8, in 1 hour 30 minutes. Paper 2 has four questions on one case study, each an 8 mark part (a) and a 12 mark part (b), also in 1 hour 30 minutes.

I describe every question in my own words. I do not reproduce Cambridge's questions, case study or mark scheme answers, so keep the papers open as you read.

## Where can I find the 0264 and 7081 specimen papers?

Download them from the Cambridge syllabus page for your code. Look for the specimen papers marked for examination from 2027. Each code has five documents:

| Document | What it contains |
|---|---|
| Paper 1 specimen paper | Four questions, each built on a short description of a business |
| Paper 1 specimen mark scheme | Model points for every part, with marking rules |
| Paper 2 specimen paper | Four questions on the case study |
| Paper 2 specimen insert | The case study itself, with three appendices |
| Paper 2 specimen mark scheme | Model points, level tables and example recommendations |

The syllabus tells teachers to use these updated specimen materials alongside it, because they show the new command words and question styles. If you want the full story of what changed and why, read my [guide to the 0264 and 7081 changes for parents](/blog/igcse-business-0264-o-level-7081-changes-2027-parents). This article is about the papers themselves.

For the three 7081 files I have, yes. I compared their text with the 0264 versions line by line. The only differences are the qualification name and the code in the headers. Every question in Paper 2 is identical, and so is every line of both mark schemes.

So everything below is checked against the 0264 papers. O Level students can follow it with the 7081 files, keeping that one caution in mind.

## What does the 2027 Paper 1 specimen look like?

| Feature | 2027 specimen Paper 1 |
|---|---|
| Name | Short Answer and Data Response |
| Questions | Four, all compulsory |
| Parts per question | (a) 2, (b) 4, (c) 6, (d) 8 marks |
| Total | 80 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Weighting (from the syllabus) | 50% of the qualification |
| Calculator | Allowed |

The syllabus lists this as a change: each Paper 1 question now has four parts instead of five, and the marks are split differently. Divide the time by the marks and you get about 22 minutes per question, or just over a minute per mark. That split is my own arithmetic, not a Cambridge rule.

### Which topics does Paper 1 test?

Each question opens with a few lines about one business, then asks four parts on different topics. I matched every part to a section of the 2027 syllabus. This is my own mapping, because Cambridge does not label the questions.

| Question | Business | Syllabus sections tested |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A gold mining company that exports most of its output | 1.1, 6.2, 6.3, 4.2 |
| 2 | A planned children's nursery run by one owner | 3.3, 5.2, 3.3, 5.1 |
| 3 | A chocolate manufacturer, a private limited company | 4.1, 1.5, 4.5, 1.3 |
| 4 | A fashion retailer that is a multinational company | 6.2, 3.1, 2.3, 2.1 |

All six syllabus topics appear. No question stays inside one topic, so revising chapter by chapter is not enough. You need to switch topics from one part to the next.

### Question 1: the gold miner

- **(a) 2 marks, Define.** Give the meaning of a term from topic 1. The mark scheme gives 2 for a clear definition and 1 for a partial one, and gives nothing for examples.
- **(b) 4 marks, Outline.** Give two ways a rise in the value of the country's currency could affect the miner's exports. Each way earns 1 mark for the point and 1 for linking it to this business.
- **(c) 6 marks, Explain.** Explain two ways the miner's activities could harm the environment. Each way earns 1 for the point, 1 for application and 1 for developing the effect.
- **(d) 8 marks, Justify.** Decide whether the advantages of bringing new technology into the mines outweigh the disadvantages.

**What is new for 2027:** part (d) sits in 4.2, Technology and production of goods and services, one of the two new topics.

### Question 2: the nursery

- **(a) 2 marks, Define.** Give the meaning of one of the pricing methods in the syllabus.
- **(b)(i) 2 marks, Calculate.** Find two missing figures in a short cash flow forecast extract. One of them is negative, and the mark scheme expects it shown that way.
- **(b)(ii) 2 marks, Identify.** Name one cash inflow and one cash outflow that any business might have. Only your first answer for each is marked.
- **(c) 6 marks, Explain.** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of the owner advertising with leaflets.
- **(d) 8 marks, Explain and justify.** Explain two factors the owner should weigh when choosing a source of finance, then decide which matters most.

**Watch the wording in (d).** It asks for factors, not sources. The mark scheme says naming sources of finance earns nothing here. It also rejects existing borrowing as a factor, because the business has not started yet.

### Question 3: the chocolate maker

- **(a) 2 marks, Calculate.** Work out labour productivity from a small table. The question tells you to show your working, and the mark scheme gives 1 mark for the correct method if the answer is wrong.
- **(b) 4 marks, Outline.** Give two reasons why business objectives matter for this company.
- **(c) 6 marks, Explain.** Explain two reasons why high quality could matter to the company.
- **(d) 8 marks, Justify.** Decide whether the advantages of horizontal integration outweigh the disadvantages.

**A trap in (a):** the table gives both actual output and maximum possible output. The mark scheme gives no credit for using the maximum. It also limits you to 1 mark if you write the answer as a sum of money, because labour productivity is measured in units per employee.

### Question 4: the fashion retailer

- **(a) 2 marks, Identify.** Name two disadvantages of a multinational company for the country where it is located.
- **(b) 4 marks, Identify.** Name four reasons some markets have become more competitive.
- **(c) 6 marks, Explain.** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of the retailer using email with its suppliers.
- **(d) 8 marks, Explain and justify.** Explain two methods of training that would improve customer service, then decide which is best for this retailer.

**What is new for 2027:** part (c) tests digital communication, which the syllabus has added to topic 2. In (a) and (b), only the first two and first four answers are marked, so a longer list gains nothing. In (d), the mark scheme rejects answers that clearly refer to production, because this business is a retailer.

### How is a Paper 1 part (d) marked?

Every 8 mark part (d) on the specimen uses the same split: up to 2 marks each for knowledge, application, analysis and a justified decision. It is point marking, not levels. The specimen uses two formats:

| Format | Questions | What you write |
|---|---|---|
| Are the advantages greater than the disadvantages? | 1(d), 3(d) | One advantage and one disadvantage, each developed, then a decision |
| Explain two, then say which is most important or best | 2(d), 4(d) | Two factors or methods, each developed, then a choice |

The decision is worth a quarter of the marks, and it has a rule. The Paper 1 mark scheme says "the justification should not repeat earlier points". A sentence like "the advantages are greater because it saves costs", when you already wrote about cost saving, adds nothing.

The example answers in the mark scheme show what does count: a reason that weighs one side against the other, often tied to a fact from the stem. For a full method on using those facts, see my guide to [application marks in 0264 and 7081](/blog/igcse-o-level-business-application-marks).

<Tip title="Make the decision a new sentence with a new reason">
Start your decision with "Overall" and give one reason you have not used yet. Comparing size works well: why one effect is bigger, lasts longer or matters more to this particular business than the other.
</Tip>

This is an easy place to lose marks without noticing. Two good points followed by a conclusion that only repeats them can cost 2 marks on every part (d), which is 8 marks across the paper.

## What does the 2027 Paper 2 specimen look like?

| Feature | 2027 specimen Paper 2 |
|---|---|
| Name | Case Study |
| Questions | Four, all compulsory, all on one case |
| Parts per question | (a) 8 marks, (b) 12 marks |
| Total | 80 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Weighting (from the syllabus) | 50% of the qualification |
| Case study | Printed in a separate insert |

The case is a chain of dessert cafes, run as a private limited company, that plans to open one more cafe. The insert covers how the expansion might be financed, the staff the new cafe needs, two ideas for motivating employees, weekly cost figures and the firm's packaging. It ends with three appendices: an advertisement, two years of financial data, and a newspaper article about tax and interest rate changes.

### What does each Paper 2 question ask?

| Part | Task in my words | Marks | Syllabus |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1(a) | Explain two advantages and two disadvantages of the firm's legal form | 8 | 1.4 |
| 1(b) | Consider three market research methods for choosing new products; recommend one | 12 | 3.2 |
| 2(a) | Explain two benefits of giving employees employment contracts | 8 | 2.1 |
| 2(b) | Consider two motivation methods; recommend one | 12 | 2.4 |
| 3(a)(i) and (ii) | Calculate two costs per dessert, showing working | 2 + 2 | 4.4 |
| 3(a)(iii) | State four ways a business could use cost data | 4 | 4.4 |
| 3(b) | Decide whether the firm's financial position improved, using calculations | 12 | 5.4, 5.5 |
| 4(a) | Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of environmentally friendly packaging | 8 | 4.3 |
| 4(b) | Consider three changes in the economy; judge which affects the firm most | 12 | 6.1 |

Question 3 breaks the pattern: its part (a) is three small parts adding to 8 marks.

### How are the 8 mark (a) parts marked?

The syllabus says every Paper 2 part (a) is now an applied question. The specimen shows two ways of marking them.

- **Question 1(a)** gives 1 mark for each of four points, plus 1 more for each point explained in the context of the case. That is 2 marks per point.
- **Questions 2(a) and 4(a)** give 1 mark for each of two points, plus up to 3 more for explaining each one. At least one of those 3 must use the case.

So in 2(a) and 4(a), each point needs a chain: the point, why it happens, what it leads to, with a fact from the case inside. A list of four benefits with one line each would earn far less than two benefits followed through.

### How are the 12 mark (b) parts marked?

Every 12 mark part uses a levels table. In my own words:

| Level | Marks | What the answer needs |
|---|---:|---|
| 3 | 9 to 12 | Very strong knowledge in context, at least two options discussed in detail, and a well justified recommendation |
| 2 | 5 to 8 | Sound knowledge in context, at least one option discussed in detail, a decision with some justification |
| 1 | 1 to 4 | Limited explanation and a simple judgement |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing creditable |

Question 3(b) has no options to compare. Its table asks for a detailed discussion of the firm's financial position instead.

Three details in the guidance decide where in a level you land:

- **Outlining every option only reaches the top of Level 1.** Three options in one line each is worth 4 marks at most.
- **The top of Level 3 needs all the options and why you rejected the others.** In 1(b) and 4(b) that means three; in 2(b), with two methods, both.
- **3(b) needs numbers.** It asks you to justify your answer with suitable calculations, and the mark scheme puts simple statements about rising or falling figures at Level 1. The higher levels come from calculating ratios and explaining what they show.

The ratio formulas you need are printed in the 2027 syllabus formula list. For 3(b), work out the 2025 current ratio and acid test ratio, compare them with the 2024 figures given, then judge. You can check your answers with my [ratio calculator](/tools/ratio-calculator).

The mark scheme gives more than one acceptable recommendation for each 12 mark part. So no single choice is the right one. What matters is the quality of the reasons. My guide to [justify and evaluate questions](/blog/igcse-business-0450-justify-evaluate-questions) shows a paragraph plan for this question type.

<Tip title="Name the options you reject">
End every 12 mark answer with a paragraph that chooses one option and then names each rejected option with one reason it loses for this business. The top marks in Level 3 depend on it.
</Tip>

## How does the specimen test the 2027 changes?

| Change in the 2027 syllabus | Where it appears in the specimen |
|---|---|
| Application weighting increased, now 30% | Application marks in most parts of both papers |
| Paper 1: four parts instead of five | Every question is (a) to (d), 2, 4, 6 and 8 marks |
| Paper 2: every part (a) is applied | 1(a), 2(a) and 4(a) all need the case |
| New 4.2 Technology and production | Paper 1, 1(d) |
| New 4.3 Sustainable production | Paper 2, 4(a) |
| Digital communication added to topic 2 | Paper 1, 4(c) |
| New formula list | Labour productivity (P1 3a), costs per unit (P2 3a), liquidity ratios (P2 3b) |

The syllabus sets approximate weightings for each paper. Paper 1 is 40% knowledge, 30% application, 20% analysis and 10% evaluation. Paper 2 is 30%, 30%, 25% and 15%. You can see that in the specimen: Paper 1 carries lots of short knowledge marks, while Paper 2 gives 48 of its 80 marks to the four 12 mark parts.

A specimen is one sample, not a list of what will come up. Many sections do not appear at all: for example location decisions, pressure groups, ethical issues and break even calculations. ## Where do students lose marks on the new papers?

Nobody has sat these papers yet, so there are no examiner reports for 0264 or 7081. Every row below comes from the guidance in the specimen mark schemes.

| Mistake | What the mark scheme does |
|---|---|
| Giving an example instead of a definition | No marks for examples in a Define question |
| Using maximum output for labour productivity | No credit for the wrong output figure |
| Writing labour productivity in dollars | 1 mark at most |
| Listing more answers than asked | Only the first ones given are marked |
| Naming sources of finance when asked for factors | No credit |
| The advantage of one method restated as the drawback of another | Mirror points are credited once |
| A decision that repeats earlier points | No evaluation marks for repetition |
| Outlining every option in a 12 mark part | Top of Level 1, so 4 marks at most |

## How should I use the specimen in revision?

There is only one specimen of each paper. Keep it for a full mock once you have covered all six topics.

1. **Practise by topic on old papers first.** Use the [0450 past papers by topic](/past-papers/igcse-business-studies-0450/topics). The skills are the same, but old Paper 1 questions have five parts, not four.
2. **Sit Paper 1 in 90 minutes.** Give each question about 22 minutes, and leave at least 9 minutes for every part (d). These timings are my own suggestion, not a Cambridge rule.
3. **Sit Paper 2 in 90 minutes** with the insert, ideally on another day. My suggestion is about 8 minutes on each part (a) and 13 to 14 minutes on each 12 mark part, which leaves a few minutes to read the insert.
4. **Mark both papers strictly** with the mark schemes. For Paper 1, tag each mark you earned as knowledge, application, analysis or evaluation. For each 12 mark part, decide honestly which level you reached.
5. **Find your pattern.** If most lost marks are application, use a different fact from the case in every point. If they are evaluation, rewrite your decisions using the tip above.
6. **Rewrite your weakest 12 mark answer** and mark it again.

The [0450 grade thresholds](/grade-thresholds/igcse-business-0450) show how many marks each grade needed on the old papers. No thresholds exist for 0264 or 7081 yet, so treat them as background only.

## Can you answer three questions in the new style?

These are my own questions, with my own figures.

**1. Calculate (2 marks).** A bakery employs 25 production workers. It makes 15,000 loaves a week and could make up to 18,000. Calculate labour productivity. Show your working.

*Answer:* output divided by number of employees: 15,000 ÷ 25 = **600 loaves per employee per week**. Use the actual output, not the maximum, and give the answer in units, not dollars.

**2. Calculate and interpret (Paper 2 style).** A shop has inventory of $4m, trade receivables of $3m and cash of $1m. Its trade payables are $2m and its bank overdraft is $3m. Calculate the current ratio and the acid test ratio, and comment.

*Answer:* current assets are $4m + $3m + $1m = $8m. Current liabilities are $2m + $3m = $5m. Current ratio = 8 ÷ 5 = **1.6**. Acid test ratio = (8 minus 4) ÷ 5 = **0.8**. The shop can cover its current liabilities only if it sells inventory, because without inventory it has 80 cents of liquid assets for every dollar it owes soon. Half of its current assets are inventory, and the overdraft is its largest current liability.

**3. Justify (8 marks, Paper 1 style).** A family restaurant with 12 staff is thinking of replacing paper order pads with tablets. Are the advantages of this change greater than the disadvantages? Justify your answer.

*Answer outline:*
- **Advantage:** orders go straight to the kitchen (knowledge), so fewer orders are lost or misread across 12 staff (application), which means fewer wasted meals and lower costs (analysis).
- **Disadvantage:** tablets cost money to buy and set up (knowledge), a large outflow for a small family business (application), which may mean borrowing and paying interest (analysis).
- **Decision:** the advantages are greater, because the tablets are a single cost while the saving on wasted meals repeats every week, as long as staff are trained to use them (evaluation).

The decision earns its marks by comparing a one off cost with a saving that repeats. It does not just repeat the two points above it.

## FAQ

### Where can I find the 0264 specimen paper 2027?

On the Cambridge syllabus page for IGCSE Business 0264, under specimen papers for examination from 2027. There are five files: both question papers, both mark schemes and the Paper 2 insert.

### Is the 7081 specimen paper the same as the 0264 one?

The 7081 Paper 2 question paper and both 7081 mark schemes are identical to the 0264 versions apart from the code and qualification name. I have not seen the 7081 Paper 1 question paper or insert, so check those two yourself.

### How many questions are in 0264 Paper 1 from 2027?

Four compulsory questions of 20 marks each, 80 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes. Each has four parts worth 2, 4, 6 and 8 marks.

### How is the Paper 2 12 mark question marked?

With three levels. Level 3 (9 to 12 marks) needs at least two options discussed in detail and a well justified recommendation. Outlining every option briefly reaches 4 marks at most.

### Is there a 12 mark question on Paper 1?

Not in the specimen. The longest Paper 1 part is the 8 mark part (d), which is point marked: 2 marks each for knowledge, application, analysis and a justified decision.

### Can I still use 0450 and 7115 past papers?

Yes, for practice by topic, since the skills tested are the same. Remember that old Paper 1 questions have five parts, the old papers were not written for the new topics 4.2 and 4.3, and the new papers give more weight to application.
