AS Business 9609 Paper 1 is 40 marks in 1 hour 15 minutes. Section A has four compulsory short answer questions worth 5 marks each. Section B is one essay from a choice of two, in two parts worth 8 and 12 marks. Each question type rewards a fixed mix of skills, so the fastest way to raise your mark is to know exactly what each one wants and write nothing else.

## What is the format of 9609 Paper 1?

This is the format in the 9609 syllabus for 2026 to 2028. It has been the same in every Paper 1 I checked from March 2023 onwards, including the 2023 specimen.

| Question | Command word in recent papers | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1(a), 2(a), 3(a) | Define | 2 each |
| 1(b), 2(b), 3(b) | Explain one | 3 each |
| 4 | Analyse one | 5 |
| 5(a) or 6(a) | Analyse two | 8 |
| 5(b) or 6(b) | Evaluate | 12 |

Paper 1 is 40% of the AS Level and 20% of the full A Level. Questions come from the AS content, topics 1.1 to 5.5.

The 2022 papers looked different. In March, June and November 2022, the 5 mark question came third, and Section B had three questions, one of them a single 20 mark essay. If you practise on 2022 papers, the skills carry over, but the numbering does not.

### Which skills does Paper 1 test?

The syllabus gives the weightings for Paper 1: knowledge and understanding 35%, application 30%, analysis 20% and evaluation 15%. In my words:

| Skill | What it means in an answer |
|---|---|
| Knowledge (AO1) | You know the term or the point and state it correctly |
| Application (AO2) | You use it in a real business situation, not in general |
| Analysis (AO3) | You follow a chain: this happens, so this follows, which leads to that |
| Evaluation (AO4) | You weigh the arguments and reach a judgement for this business |

## How do I answer the define questions?

Each define question is worth 2 marks. The schemes give 2 for a clear understanding and 1 for a partial one. So a definition with only half the idea earns 1 at most.

The November 2023 Paper 11 and Paper 12 reports said a clear definition needs one or possibly two sentences, and should be succinct.

**Do not repeat the words of the term.** The March 2025 Paper 12 report said a few definitions of psychographic segmentation were tautologous: they just said it was segmentation based on psychographic features. That explains nothing.

**Do not give an example instead of a meaning.** The same report said a significant minority found indirect costs hard to define: many could give an example, such as rent, but not the meaning of the term.

Model answer, my own question:

> Define the term batch production. [2]
>
> Batch production is making a set quantity of one identical product at a time, with the whole group moving through each stage of production together, before the machines are set up for a different product.

Two ideas are there: a group of identical items, and a switch to a different product after each batch. Half of that would be "making products in groups", which I would expect to earn 1 mark at most.

## How do I answer the explain questions?

Each explain one question is worth 3 marks: 1 for knowledge and 2 for application or explanation. In the June 2025 Paper 12 scheme, the 2 marks went to a developed explanation of one point, and 1 to a limited one.

The March 2025 Paper 12 report described the difference. Strong answers gave one point with two linked pieces of explanation. Weaker answers gave only one piece. The June 2023 Paper 13 report put the length at two or three developed sentences.

**Give one point only.** The question says "one", and only one can be credited. The March 2025 report said weaker answers on budgets gave two or three benefits instead of one explained benefit.

Model answer, my own question:

> Explain one disadvantage to a business of using penetration pricing. [3]
>
> Penetration pricing means setting a low price to win market share quickly, so profit per unit is low at first. A new juice brand selling at below its rivals' prices may need many months of high sales before it covers its launch costs. If customers come to expect the low price, raising it later may drive them back to rivals, so the brand could stay at a low margin for longer than planned.

The first sentence shows knowledge: the point is low profit per unit. The next two develop it in a business situation, in two linked steps.

## How do I answer Question 4?

Question 4 asks you to analyse one thing for 5 marks. In the June 2025 Paper 12 scheme, the split was 1 mark for knowledge, 2 for application and 2 for analysis. That scheme also said no marks at all could be given if a relevant reason was not identified.

Analysis is a chain of cause and effect. The June 2023 Paper 13 report advised candidates to practise "three stage reasoning", using words such as because, therefore and as a result. The November 2023 reports defined analysis as applying a concept, explaining why it matters to the business, then following its causes, impacts and consequences.

Model answer, my own question:

> Analyse one way a business may benefit from batch production. [5]
>
> One benefit is flexibility: the business can make several different products on the same machines. A bakery can bake a batch of white loaves in the morning and a batch of seeded rolls in the afternoon. Because it offers more choice, it can serve more customers, such as cafes that want rolls and shops that want loaves. As a result its sales are spread across several products, so if demand for one falls, the others keep revenue coming in, and the bakery is less likely to face a cash flow problem.

Here the knowledge is flexibility. The bakery and its products are the application. The chain from more choice, to more customers, to spread sales, to lower risk is the analysis.

**Answer the question asked, about the right people.** Several reports said marks were lost because candidates analysed the wrong thing. The June 2023 Paper 11 report said a significant number wrote about the business's responsibilities to employees when the question asked about employees' responsibilities to the business. The March 2025 and March 2026 Paper 12 reports said some analysed effects on the business when the question asked about employees.

The March 2023 report added that Question 4 asks for one way, not several, and that it does not need evaluation.

## How is the essay marked?

You choose Question 5 or Question 6. Both have two parts.

### Part (a): analyse two, for 8 marks

The November 2023 Paper 11 grid gave 2 marks for knowledge, 2 for application and 4 for analysis. The June 2025 Paper 12 scheme set it out per point: for each of the two points, 1 for knowledge, 1 for application and 2 for analysis.

So each point is worth 4 marks, and only two count. The March 2023 report said that often only one factor was identified and analysed, which caps you at half the marks. The March 2026 report said the same for some answers.

Part (a) does not need evaluation. Stay on the side the question asks for: the November 2023 Paper 11 report said that when a question asked for advantages, analysis of disadvantages could not be credited.

### Part (b): evaluate, for 12 marks

This is where most marks are won or lost.

| Skill | Marks | What earns the top marks, in my words |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | 2 | Developed understanding of the key terms |
| Application | 2 | Developed use of the business in the question |
| Analysis | 2 | Developed chains of cause and consequence |
| Evaluation | 6 | A developed judgement, made for this business |

That split is the same in the November 2023 Paper 11 grid and the June 2025 Paper 12 scheme. Half of the 12 marks are for evaluation.

The June 2025 Paper 12 scheme graded evaluation in steps. In my summary: the bottom mark is for an attempt to balance arguments; a supported judgement without context can reach 4; and the top marks, 5 and 6, need evaluation in the context of the business in the question. The March 2025 report said the same in its key messages: the judgement must be in the context of the question before 5 or 6 can be awarded.

**Context means detail, not the name.** The June 2023 Paper 13 report said that repeating the words of the business type, such as "airline", does not count as application. Answers needed the words of that business: cabin crew, tickets and flights for an airline; tyres, chains and components for a bicycle maker. The June 2025 Paper 12 scheme for a hospital essay has a note on this. Its wording is not fully clear, but as I read it, the application marks needed explicit reference to hospital activity, such as operations, treatment or surgery.

**A summary is not a judgement.** The November 2023 Paper 11 and Paper 12 reports said many final judgements just summarised earlier points. The March 2026 report said a single line of judgement is not enough, and that a substantial conclusion in context was needed.

<Tip title="Write the conclusion first in your head">
Before you start part (b), decide your answer to the question and the one feature of this business that decides it. Then every paragraph builds towards that judgement, and you will not run out of time before you reach it.
</Tip>

In my teaching, this is where good students lose marks: their analysis is sound, then the last paragraph says "in conclusion, it depends" and stops. The examiner reports from March 2023, June 2023, November 2023 and March 2026 all say evaluation is where candidates fall short.

## What does a top evaluation look like?

Here is my own question and the evaluation section I would write. The analysis paragraphs before it are not shown.

> Evaluate whether penetration pricing is the best way for a new gym to attract customers. [12]

> On balance, penetration pricing is not the best way for this gym to attract customers, unless it opens in an area full of rival gyms. A low monthly fee will fill the gym quickly in the first months, but gym members often join in January and leave by spring, so members won on price alone may cancel as soon as the offer ends. The gym's costs, such as rent, trainers' wages and machine maintenance, are mostly fixed, so it needs members who stay for a year, not three months. Free trial sessions with a personal trainer, or group classes that build friendships, may keep members longer at a full price. Penetration pricing is most useful if the gym's main rival is a cheaper chain nearby, because then price is what local customers compare first. So the best method depends most on the local competition, and for a gym in an area without a cheap rival, building loyalty through classes and trainers would attract more paying members over a year than a low price.

Why this would score well, in my judgement:

- It answers the question in the first sentence and again in the last.
- It uses gym details throughout: monthly fees, January joiners, trainers, machines, group classes.
- It weighs penetration pricing against an alternative and says when each wins.
- It names the deciding factor, local competition, instead of only writing "it depends".

## How long should I spend on each question?

These timings are my advice, not a Cambridge rule. They add up to 75 minutes.

| Part | Marks | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Read the paper and choose your essay | | 3 |
| Questions 1 to 3 (8 minutes each) | 15 | 24 |
| Question 4 | 5 | 9 |
| Essay part (a) | 8 | 14 |
| Essay part (b) | 12 | 22 |
| Final check | | 3 |
| Total | 40 | 75 |

Part (b) gets the most time because it carries the most marks and the evaluation comes last. The November 2023 Paper 11 report said some candidates did not complete the final questions because of time management, for example by spending too much time on Question 5(a). It also said rewriting the question before answering wastes time.

## What do examiners say students get wrong?

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Tautologous definitions that repeat the term (a few, March 2025 P12) | Explain the meaning in other words |
| Giving an example instead of a definition (a significant minority, March 2025 P12) | Define first; add an example only if time allows |
| Two or three points where the question asks for one (weaker answers, March 2025 P12) | One point, explained in two linked steps |
| Analysing for the wrong group, such as the business instead of employees (June 2023 P11; March 2025 and March 2026 P12) | Underline who the question is about |
| Only one point in an "analyse two" question (often, March 2023 P12) | Two points, each with its own chain |
| Context that only repeats the business type (June 2023 P13) | Use the products, staff and customers of that business |
| A final judgement that only summarises (many, November 2023 P11 and P12) | Weigh the points and make a decision for this business |
| Running out of time on part (b) (some, November 2023 P11) | Follow a time plan and start part (b) with at least 22 minutes left |

## Practice questions with answers

Each question is my own. Try it on paper before reading the answer.

### Practice question 1

> Define the term market share. [2]

**Answer.** The sales of one business as a percentage of the total sales of all businesses in the same market over a period.

### Practice question 2

> Explain one advantage to a business of using job production. [3]

**Answer.** Job production makes one unique product to each customer's order, so the business can charge a high price. A wedding dress maker can design each dress to the bride's wishes, and because no other bride will have the same dress, customers will pay more for it than for a dress off the rail. This gives a higher profit margin on each item.

### Practice question 3

> Analyse two benefits to a small online clothing retailer of using social media for promotion. [8]

**Answer outline.**

- Low cost: posting is cheap compared with print or television advertising. The retailer can show new dresses to thousands of followers for little money, so more of its small budget is left for buying stock, which helps it keep popular sizes in stock and avoid lost sales.
- Targeting: the retailer can pay to show its posts only to people of a chosen age and location. Its adverts for school uniform coats reach parents in its delivery area, so a higher share of viewers buy, and the cost of each sale falls.

Each point has knowledge, application to clothing, and a chain to a business result. My marks here are my own judgement for practice, not a Cambridge scheme.

## FAQ

### How many questions are in 9609 Paper 1?

Six questions are printed, and you answer five: the four compulsory Section A questions and one of the two Section B essays. The paper is worth 40 marks.

### How long is 9609 Paper 1?

1 hour 15 minutes, in the 2026 to 2028 syllabus.

### Do I need to evaluate in Question 4 or part (a)?

No. The March 2023 report said Questions 4, 5(a) and 6(a) do not need evaluation. Evaluation is needed only in part (b) of the essay.

### How many marks are for evaluation in 9609 Paper 1?

In recent schemes, 6 of the 12 marks in essay part (b) are for evaluation. That is the only place in the paper where evaluation is credited.

### Does 9609 Paper 1 have a case study?

No. Paper 1 has short answer questions and one essay. Paper 2 is based on texts with data, and Paper 3 on a case study. Section B questions often name a type of business, such as a hospital or an airline, and your answer must use it.

### What is the best way to start an evaluation?

Answer the question directly in your first sentence of evaluation, then give the reason that decides it for this business, and the condition under which your answer would change.
