Absorption costing charges every production cost to the product: direct materials, direct labour and a share of the factory overheads. You share the overheads in three steps. Allocate and apportion them to departments, reapportion service departments into production departments, then divide by machine hours or labour hours to get an overhead absorption rate. In AS Accounting 9706 this is syllabus section 2.2.2.

## What does the 9706 syllabus ask you to know?

Absorption costing sits in section 2.2, traditional costing methods, in the 9706 syllabus for 2026 to 2028. Section 2.2.1 asks you to prepare costing statements using unit, job and batch costing. Section 2.2.2 lists eight points. Here is what each one means in an exam, in my words.

| Syllabus point | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Cost centre and cost unit | Define both and explain the difference |
| Allocate and apportion overheads | Share overheads between production and service departments |
| Overhead absorption rates | Choose a basis and calculate a rate for each production department |
| Under and over absorption | Calculate it, say which it is, and give the causes |
| Costing and profit statements | Price a job or batch, and prepare a profit statement |
| Uses and limitations | Explain why a business uses it and where it falls short |
| Usefulness for decisions | Judge whether the cost data helps managers decide |
| Factors that are not financial | Bring in quality, staff, customers and reliability of forecasts |

On our past paper pages, 155 questions from 9706 Papers 1 and 2 of 2022 to 2026 carry the 2.2 tag, as main or second tag. That tag covers marginal costing as well, so not all of them are absorption costing. You can see them on the [9706 traditional costing methods topic page](/past-papers/a-level-accounting-9706/topics/2-2-traditional-costing-methods).

## Where does absorption costing appear in the exam?

In the 2026 to 2028 syllabus, Paper 2 lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and has 90 marks. Question 4 is the cost and management accounting question and carries 30 marks. In the Paper 2 mark schemes I checked from 2022 to 2026, Question 4 asked for an apportionment table or an overhead absorption rate in at least one variant of most sessions. It often runs straight into marginal costing in the later parts. The 2022 Paper 2 papers lasted 1 hour 30 minutes, not 1 hour 45, though they also had 90 marks, so their Question 4 sits in a shorter paper.

Paper 1 is 30 multiple choice questions in 1 hour. Absorption costing turns up there as single questions on causes of under absorption, the right basis of apportionment, or the effect on inventory and profit.

## What are the key terms?

Learn these first. Paper 2 often opens Question 4 with short definitions worth 1 or 2 marks each.

| Term | Meaning, in my words |
|---|---|
| Overheads | Indirect costs: factory costs you cannot trace to one product, such as factory rent or a supervisor's salary |
| Cost centre | A department, location or activity that collects costs, such as the machining department or the canteen |
| Cost unit | One unit of output that the costs are charged to, such as one chair, one batch or one job |
| Allocation | Charging a whole overhead to one cost centre, because it clearly belongs there |
| Apportionment | Sharing an overhead that several cost centres use, on a fair basis such as floor area |
| Reapportionment | Moving the total cost of a service department into the production departments |
| Overhead absorption rate | Budgeted overheads of a production department divided by its budgeted machine hours or labour hours |

The difference between allocation and apportionment is worth getting exactly right. In the June 2022 Paper 22 scheme, each definition was worth 2 marks: one for the idea of charging overheads to a cost centre, one for the condition (directly identified for allocation, shared on a suitable basis for apportionment). The June 2022 report said almost all candidates tried these, but many answers were too vague to earn marks. The June 2023 report for Paper 22 said the same about the cost centre definition.

In the November 2023 Paper 21 scheme, the difference between a cost centre and a cost unit was a 2 mark "explain one difference" question. You need both sides: a cost centre is where costs are collected, whereas a cost unit is the unit of output that is charged.

## How do you allocate and apportion overheads?

Start from the budgeted overheads for the year. Some overheads belong clearly to one department, such as the wages of the canteen staff. Allocate those in full. Others are shared, such as rent for the whole factory. Apportion those on a basis that matches how the cost arises.

These are the bases I teach. Use the basis the question gives you if it gives one.

| Overhead | Usual basis |
|---|---|
| Rent, rates, heating, building insurance | Floor area |
| Insurance of machinery, depreciation of machinery | Value (cost) of machinery |
| Canteen costs, supervision, personnel department | Number of employees |
| Power for machines | Machine hours, or units of power used |
| Maintenance department | Maintenance hours worked in each department |
| Stores department | Number or value of stores requisitions |

The basis matters in Paper 1 too. In November 2022 Paper 12 Question 24, candidates had to apportion machinery insurance. The report said 48 per cent chose the number of machines, and only 22 per cent chose the correct answer, which was based on value. Insurance follows value, because a dearer machine costs more to insure.

**Read who shares each cost.** The March 2022 Paper 22 report said many good answers were spoiled by sharing the production department supervisor's wages over the service departments as well. If the cost belongs only to production departments, apportion it only to them.

## How do you reapportion service department costs?

Service departments, such as maintenance, stores or the canteen, do not make products. Their costs must still reach the products, so you move them into the production departments. The November 2022 Paper 23 scheme gave the reason: if service department costs are left out, they will not be recovered and the selling price will be wrong.

When one service department also serves another, reapportion the one that serves the other first. Its cost is shared over the production departments and the other service department. Then reapportion the second service department over the production departments only. This is how the March 2022 Paper 22, June 2023 Paper 21 and June 2025 Paper 21 schemes set out the table.

## Worked example: Zarin Furniture

Here is my own example, with every number checked.

> Zarin Furniture makes wooden tables. It has two production departments, Machining and Assembly, and two service departments, Maintenance and Canteen. Budgeted figures for the year are below.

| Allocated overheads | $ |
|---|---|
| Machining | 52,000 |
| Assembly | 31,000 |
| Maintenance | 9,400 |
| Canteen | 6,600 |

Shared overheads for the year are rent $24,000, machinery insurance $6,000 and production supervisors' salaries $21,000. The supervisors work only in the two production departments.

| Department | Floor area (m²) | Machinery value ($) | Employees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machining | 2,000 | 250,000 | 30 |
| Assembly | 1,500 | 40,000 | 40 |
| Maintenance | 300 | 10,000 | 10 |
| Canteen | 200 | 0 | 5 |

The canteen serves the staff of the other three departments. Maintenance works 600 hours for Machining and 150 hours for Assembly.

### Step 1: allocate and apportion

- Rent by floor area: $24,000 ÷ 4,000 m² = $6 per m². Machining $12,000, Assembly $9,000, Maintenance $1,800, Canteen $1,200.
- Insurance by machinery value: $6,000 ÷ $300,000 = 2 cents per dollar. Machining $5,000, Assembly $800, Maintenance $200, Canteen nil.
- Supervisors by production employees only: $21,000 ÷ 70 = $300 each. Machining $9,000, Assembly $12,000.

| Department | Total after step 1 ($) |
|---|---|
| Machining | 52,000 + 12,000 + 5,000 + 9,000 = 78,000 |
| Assembly | 31,000 + 9,000 + 800 + 12,000 = 52,800 |
| Maintenance | 9,400 + 1,800 + 200 = 11,400 |
| Canteen | 6,600 + 1,200 = 7,800 |

Check: the four totals add to $150,000, which equals the allocated $99,000 plus the shared $51,000. Always do this check. The March 2025 Paper 22 report said a common mistake was leaving out the overheads already allocated, and errors in addition were common too.

### Step 2: reapportion the canteen first

The canteen serves Maintenance, so it goes first. Share $7,800 over the 80 employees outside the canteen: $97.50 each.

- Machining 30 × $97.50 = $2,925
- Assembly 40 × $97.50 = $3,900
- Maintenance 10 × $97.50 = $975

Maintenance now totals $11,400 + $975 = $12,375.

### Step 3: reapportion maintenance

Share $12,375 over 750 maintenance hours: $16.50 an hour.

- Machining 600 × $16.50 = $9,900
- Assembly 150 × $16.50 = $2,475

| Production department | Total overheads ($) |
|---|---|
| Machining | 78,000 + 2,925 + 9,900 = 90,825 |
| Assembly | 52,800 + 3,900 + 2,475 = 59,175 |

Check: $90,825 + $59,175 = $150,000. Every dollar of overhead is now in a production department.

## How do you calculate an overhead absorption rate?

Divide each production department's budgeted overheads by its budgeted hours. Pick the basis that matches how the department works.

| Department | Budgeted machine hours | Budgeted labour hours |
|---|---|---|
| Machining | 13,500 | 4,000 |
| Assembly | 1,500 | 12,000 |

Machining runs mainly on machines, so use machine hours. Assembly runs mainly on people, so use labour hours.

- Machining: $90,825 ÷ 13,500 = $6.7277..., so **$6.73 per machine hour**
- Assembly: $59,175 ÷ 12,000 = $4.93125, so **$4.93 per labour hour**

Write the unit every time. In the June 2022 Paper 22, March 2025 Paper 22 and June 2025 Paper 21 schemes, each rate earned one mark for the figure and a separate mark for "per machine hour" or "per labour hour". The June 2023 and November 2023 reports both said candidates often still did not state the basis. The March 2025 report added that some candidates gave both bases, which earned no marks.

Decimal places cost marks too. The March 2022 and November 2022 reports said some candidates did not give the rate to two decimal places as instructed. The June 2023 and November 2023 reports said many did not, and the March 2025 report said a few. Read the question line before you write the figure.

<Tip title="Two marks hide in one rate">
Write the rate as "$6.73 per machine hour", never "$6.73". In several schemes the words "per machine hour" carry their own mark, and the reports keep saying candidates leave them out.
</Tip>

## How do you calculate under or over absorption?

The rate is set before the year starts, from budgets. During the year, overheads are charged to products at that rate on the **actual** hours worked. At the end of the year, compare what was absorbed with what was really spent.

> Overheads absorbed = actual hours × overhead absorption rate
>
> Under absorbed: actual overheads are more than overheads absorbed
>
> Over absorbed: actual overheads are less than overheads absorbed

For Zarin Furniture, the actual results for the year were:

| Department | Actual hours | Actual overheads ($) |
|---|---|---|
| Machining | 13,200 machine hours | 93,400 |
| Assembly | 12,400 labour hours | 58,300 |

- Machining absorbed 13,200 × $6.73 = $88,836. Actual $93,400 is higher, so overheads were **under absorbed by $4,564**.
- Assembly absorbed 12,400 × $4.93 = $61,132. Actual $58,300 is lower, so overheads were **over absorbed by $2,832**.

Always say which it is. In the June 2023 Paper 22 and November 2022 Paper 23 schemes, the words "under absorbed" or "over absorbed" earned a mark of their own. The June 2022 report said some candidates were unsure which figure to compare with which. The June 2023 and November 2023 reports said some confused over absorption with under absorption.

### What causes under or over absorption?

There are two causes, and they work in both directions.

| Under absorption | Over absorption |
|---|---|
| Actual overheads were higher than budgeted | Actual overheads were lower than budgeted |
| Actual activity (hours or output) was lower than budgeted | Actual activity was higher than budgeted |

These two pairs are what the March 2022, June 2023 and June 2025 schemes credit for under absorption, and the November 2023 Paper 21 and June 2026 Paper 22 schemes for over absorption. Each was a 2 mark "state two" question.

Zarin shows both causes at work. Machining worked 300 fewer machine hours than budgeted, and spent $2,575 more than its budget of $90,825. Both push towards under absorption.

The November 2022 report said answers on causes were "often very vague and frequently mixed up". In November 2023 Paper 13 Question 26, some candidates chose an option that paired activity below budget with over absorption. It causes under absorption: fewer hours means fewer overheads absorbed.

## How do you price a job using absorption costing?

Once you have the rates, you can cost a job, a batch or one unit. Add direct costs, then absorbed overheads, then profit.

> Zarin receives an order for 20 tables. Each table needs $38 of materials, 2 direct labour hours in Machining at $12 an hour, 3 direct labour hours in Assembly at $10 an hour, and 1.5 machine hours in Machining. Zarin wants a profit margin of 20%.

| Cost of the order | $ |
|---|---|
| Direct materials 20 × $38 | 760.00 |
| Direct labour, Machining 20 × 2 × $12 | 480.00 |
| Direct labour, Assembly 20 × 3 × $10 | 600.00 |
| Overheads, Machining 20 × 1.5 × $6.73 | 201.90 |
| Overheads, Assembly 20 × 3 × $4.93 | 295.80 |
| Total cost | 2,337.70 |
| Profit (20% margin) | 584.43 |
| Selling price | 2,922.13 |

Machining overheads use the machine hours, not the labour hours, because that department's rate is per machine hour.

**Margin is not mark-up.** A margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. A mark-up is profit as a percentage of cost. A 20% margin means cost is 80% of the price, so price = $2,337.70 ÷ 0.8 = $2,922.13 (rounded from $2,922.125). That is the same as a 25% mark-up on cost. A 20% mark-up would give only $2,805.24, which is wrong here.

The November 2022 Paper 23 report said the most common error on the selling price task was, once again, a mix up between mark-up and margin. In that scheme, a 25% margin became a profit of 25/75 of cost. The June 2023 Paper 21 report said some candidates struggled to turn a 25 per cent margin into a selling price. The scheme added one third of cost.

The same November 2022 report said some candidates did not know how to bring the absorbed overheads into the selling price. This is the point where my own students most often get stuck: they cost the materials and labour correctly, then forget that the rates they just calculated must be used again.

## How does an absorption costing profit statement work?

In a profit statement, each unit made carries its full production cost, fixed overheads included. Closing inventory is valued at that full cost. Any under absorption is then deducted, and any over absorption added back, because the products were charged the budgeted amount, not the actual amount.

My own example: a business makes 5,000 units in a month and sells 4,000 at $25. Variable production cost is $10 a unit. Budgeted fixed production overheads are $30,000 for 5,000 units, so the rate is $6 a unit and full cost is $16. Actual fixed overheads were $31,500.

| Profit statement for the month | $ |
|---|---|
| Revenue 4,000 × $25 | 100,000 |
| Production cost 5,000 × $16 | 80,000 |
| Less closing inventory 1,000 × $16 | (16,000) |
| Cost of sales | 64,000 |
| Gross profit | 36,000 |
| Under absorbed overheads ($31,500 minus $30,000) | (1,500) |
| Profit | 34,500 |

The November 2023 Paper 21 report said this kind of statement was not well answered. Candidates needed to calculate a closing inventory and to record the under absorption. That scheme deducted the under absorption after the gross figure, as above.

Closing inventory is higher under absorption costing than under marginal costing, because it carries fixed overheads. In March 2025 Paper 12 Question 27, a business in its first year of trading had valued inventory using marginal costing, and its finance director wanted to use absorption costing instead. The report said many candidates saw that inventory would rise but missed that profit rises too: higher closing inventory lowers cost of sales. The full comparison and the reconciliation statement are in my [marginal costing and break-even guide for 9706](/blog/as-level-accounting-9706-marginal-costing-break-even).

## What are the uses and limitations of absorption costing?

These are my summary, in my own words, of points in the June 2022 Paper 22, June 2023 Paper 23, November 2023 Paper 21 and June 2024 Paper 22 schemes. The November 2023 scheme lists its points on absorption costing without sorting them, so placing those in a column is my judgement.

| Uses | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Sets a selling price that covers all production costs | Bases of apportionment can be arbitrary |
| Values inventory in a way accepted under IAS 2 for financial statements | Not useful for short term decisions, such as a special order |
| Follows the matching concept: fixed production costs go with the units they helped make | Not suited to judging managers' performance |
| No need to split costs into fixed and variable | |
| Useful for long term decisions | |

There is one honest disagreement in the sources. The June 2022 report said under or over absorption "is not a limitation" of absorption costing, when candidates gave it as one. But the November 2023 Paper 21 scheme, in an advice question, credited the point that under and over absorption of overheads can arise with absorption costing, and the March 2023 Paper 22 and June 2024 Paper 21 schemes credited avoiding it as an advantage of marginal costing. My advice: when asked for a limitation, give the arbitrary basis or the short term decision point first. Use under or over absorption only in a "compare" or "advise" question, and explain it.

### Should a business use one rate for the whole factory?

The November 2022 Paper 23 question asked candidates to advise on one factory wide rate instead of separate department rates. The scheme credited both sides. One rate is easier, because it needs less analysis. But it is less accurate, because some jobs use more machine hours and others more labour hours. That leads to overheads being over or under absorbed on each job, which can set a price too high (lower demand) or too low (costs not covered). The report said many candidates earned two marks for reasons in favour of one rate, but answers against were generally very weak. Always give both sides, then decide.

## What do examiners say students get wrong?

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Vague definitions of allocation and apportionment (many candidates, June 2022 P22) | Say who gets the cost and why: directly identified, or shared on a basis |
| Supervisor's wages shared over service departments too (many good answers spoiled, March 2022 P22) | Apportion a cost only to the departments that use it |
| Overheads already allocated left out of the table (a common mistake, March 2025 P22) | Start every column with the allocated figure, then check the grand total |
| No "per machine hour" or "per labour hour" (often, June 2023 and November 2023 P22) | Write the basis with every rate |
| Rate not given to two decimal places (March 2022 P22, November 2022 P23, June 2023 P21) | Follow the instruction in the question |
| Mixing up over and under absorption (some, June 2023 and November 2023 P22) | Absorbed bigger than actual is over; actual bigger than absorbed is under |
| Margin treated as mark-up when pricing an order (most common error, November 2022 P23) | Margin is on price, mark-up is on cost |
| Under or over absorption given as a limitation (many, June 2022 P22) | Use the arbitrary basis or the short term decision point |

## Practice questions with answers

Each question is my own. Try it on paper before reading the answer.

### Practice question 1

> A factory has rent of $36,000 a year. Department P has 600 m² of floor space, Department Q has 400 m² and the Stores have 200 m².
>
> (a) Apportion the rent. [2]
>
> (b) Suggest a suitable basis for apportioning the canteen costs and the insurance of machinery. [2]

**Answer**

(a) $36,000 ÷ 1,200 m² = $30 per m². P $18,000, Q $12,000, Stores $6,000.

(b) Canteen: number of employees. Insurance of machinery: value of machinery.

### Practice question 2

> Department K budgeted overheads of $84,000 and 10,500 direct labour hours. It worked 9,800 direct labour hours and spent $82,000 on overheads.
>
> (a) Calculate the overhead absorption rate. [2]
>
> (b) Calculate the under or over absorption and say which it is. [3]
>
> (c) Explain why it happened, using the figures. [2]

**Answer**

(a) $84,000 ÷ 10,500 = $8.00 per direct labour hour.

(b) Absorbed 9,800 × $8.00 = $78,400. Actual $82,000. Under absorbed by $3,600.

(c) Hours were 700 below budget, so 700 × $8 = $5,600 less overhead was absorbed. Spending was $2,000 below budget, which cut the gap. $5,600 minus $2,000 = $3,600 under absorbed.

### Practice question 3

> A job costs $4,500 in total, including absorbed overheads.
>
> (a) Calculate the selling price for a profit margin of 40%. [2]
>
> (b) Calculate the selling price for a mark-up of 40%. [1]

**Answer**

(a) Cost is 60% of price, so price = $4,500 ÷ 0.6 = $7,500. Profit is $3,000, which is 40% of $7,500.

(b) $4,500 × 1.4 = $6,300.

These marks are my own judgement for practice, not a Cambridge scheme.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between allocation and apportionment?

Allocation charges a whole overhead to one cost centre because it clearly belongs there. Apportionment shares an overhead between several cost centres on a fair basis, such as floor area or number of employees.

### How do I choose between machine hours and labour hours?

Look at the budgeted hours for each production department. If machines do most of the work, use machine hours. If people do most of the work, use labour hours.

### What causes under absorption of overheads?

Actual overheads higher than budgeted, or actual activity lower than budgeted. Either one means the products were charged less overhead than was really spent.

### Is under absorption added or subtracted in a profit statement?

Under absorption is deducted, because too little overhead was charged to products. Over absorption is added back.

### Why does absorption costing give a higher profit when inventory rises?

Closing inventory carries part of the fixed overheads, so less of them reaches cost of sales this period. When inventory falls, the opposite happens.

### Do I need to know activity based costing for AS?

Not in section 2.2. Activity based costing is in the A Level part of the 9706 syllabus, not the AS content.
