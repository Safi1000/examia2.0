"How many marks for an A\*?" has a different answer for each of my three A Level subjects, and the answers are further apart than most students expect. In June 2026, an A\* in A Level Economics 9708 needed as little as 66% of the marks on one set of papers. In A Level Business 9609, it needed at least 76.5%. Here are the real numbers from Cambridge's grade threshold tables, how the A\* line is worked out, and the target I would set for 2027.

## The short answer

These are the A\* thresholds for the four most recent sessions, across all the variants Cambridge published for the full A Level taken in one session.

| Syllabus | Maximum mark | June 2026 A\* | Lowest A\* in the last four sessions | Highest A\* in the last four sessions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Accounting 9706 | 250 | 189 to 192 | 182 (72.8%) | 197 (78.8%) |
| Economics 9708 | 180 | 119 to 128 | 115 (63.9%) | 129 (71.7%) |
| Business 9609 | 200 | 153 to 157 | 149 (74.5%) | 164 (82.0%) |

The four sessions are June 2024, June 2025, November 2025 and June 2026. In round numbers: an A\* has needed roughly 73% to 79% in Accounting, 64% to 72% in Economics and 75% to 82% in Business.

There is no A\* at AS Level. The top AS grade is a.

## How Cambridge sets the A\* line

Cambridge sets the A and B thresholds for each paper after marking, based on how hard the papers turned out to be. It then adds the papers together to get the A and B lines for the whole A Level. Cambridge says grade A\* "does not exist at the level of an individual component", so it is calculated last, from the A and B lines.

You can check the rule yourself in every table: the A\* line sits as far above A as A sits above B.

| June 2026, variant 1 | B | A | Gap | A\* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Accounting 9706 | 136 | 164 | 28 | 192 |
| Economics 9708 | 94 | 109 | 15 | 124 |
| Business 9609 | 135 | 146 | 11 | 157 |

This explains why Business needs a higher percentage. Its A and B lines are close together, so the A\* is only 11 marks above the A. In Accounting the gap is 28 marks. A student who is comfortably at A in Accounting can still be a long way from A\*. If you want the background on why thresholds move at all, read my [guide to Cambridge grade thresholds](/blog/cambridge-grade-thresholds-explained).

## How the papers add up

In all three subjects, the AS papers make up half of the A Level, but the papers are not all worth the same.

| Syllabus | AS papers | A Level papers | How they are added |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting 9706 | Paper 1 (30 marks), Paper 2 (90 marks) | Paper 3 (75 marks), Paper 4 (50 marks) | Papers 1 and 2 are scaled to 125 in total, then Papers 3 and 4 are added: 250 |
| Economics 9708 | Paper 1 (30), Paper 2 (60) | Paper 3 (30), Paper 4 (60) | Raw marks added: 180 |
| Business 9609 | Paper 1 (40), Paper 2 (60) | Paper 3 (60), Paper 4 (40) | Raw marks added: 200 |

In Accounting, each Paper 1 mark is worth 1.25 weighted marks and each Paper 2 mark about 0.97. In Economics and Business, a mark is a mark on every paper.

## What an A looked like, paper by paper

Cambridge does not give an A\* for single papers, but it does publish the A line for each one. These are the June 2026 figures for variant 1:

| Paper | Accounting 9706 | Economics 9708 | Business 9609 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 20 out of 30 | 25 out of 30 | 34 out of 40 |
| Paper 2 | 62 out of 90 | 24 out of 60 | 44 out of 60 |
| Paper 3 | 44 out of 75 | 24 out of 30 | 41 out of 60 |
| Paper 4 | 35 out of 50 | 36 out of 60 | 27 out of 40 |

Look at Economics. An A on the multiple choice papers needed 80% or more, but an A on Paper 2, the data response and essay paper, needed 24 out of 60: 40%. The written paper is marked hard, so the line is low. That does not make it easy. It means every extra 12 mark essay answer that moves up a level is worth a lot. I cover how to do that in [how to score in 9708 Paper 2 and 9609 Paper 3](/blog/9708-paper-2-and-9609-paper-3-how-to-score).

In Business, the opposite is true. The A line on Paper 1 was 34 out of 40, so there is very little room for lost marks on the short answer paper.

## Staged or all at once: the line moves

If you sit AS first and carry it forward, Cambridge treats your combination as a different option, with its own thresholds. In June 2026:

| Syllabus | A\* when all papers were sat in June 2026 | A\* for staged combinations, June 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting 9706 | 189 to 192 | 187 to 194 |
| Economics 9708 | 119 to 128 | 117 to 125 |
| Business 9609 | 153 to 157 | 153 to 163 |

So a staged Business student could have needed up to 163 out of 200 (81.5%). If you took AS in an earlier session, do not plan around the linear line.

## Worked example: Business 9609, June 2026, variant 1

Ayesha scores 32 out of 40 on Paper 1, 45 out of 60 on Paper 2, 44 out of 60 on Paper 3 and 30 out of 40 on Paper 4. Her total is **151 out of 200**.

The thresholds were A 146 and A\* 157, so she gets an **A**, six marks short of A\*. Her Paper 1 is two marks below the paper's A line. Her Paper 3 is three above it, but the 12 mark evaluation questions there are where six more marks are most realistic: one evaluation level higher on all three of them would be enough.

## The target I would set for 2027

Thresholds for June 2027 will not be known until after the exams. The safe plan is to aim at the highest A\* line of recent sessions, not the average:

- **Accounting 9706:** about 197 out of 250 (79%)
- **Economics 9708:** about 129 out of 180 (72%)
- **Business 9609:** about 164 out of 200 (82%)

If your mocks clear those numbers, the difficulty of your particular paper stops mattering. If they do not, you can see exactly how far you are from each grade.
