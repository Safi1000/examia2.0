Accounting concepts are the basic rules that decide what goes into the books and at what value. The IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029 list ten: business entity, consistency, duality, going concern, historic cost, matching (accruals), materiality, money measurement, prudence and realisation. They sit in section 7.1, and exam questions ask you to name, explain or apply them.

## Which accounting concepts are in the 0452 and 7707 syllabus?

Section 7.1 lists exactly these ten. The wording is the same in both syllabuses. Learn these and no others: if a textbook adds more, they are not on the 2027 list.

| Concept | What it means (my words) | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| Business entity | The business is treated as separate from its owner. Only business transactions are recorded. | The owner's private car is not a business asset |
| Consistency | Once a method is chosen, it is used from one year to the next | Depreciation stays reducing balance every year |
| Duality | Every transaction has two equal aspects, a debit and a credit | A loan raises bank (debit) and loan (credit) |
| Going concern | The business is expected to continue for the foreseeable future | Non-current assets are shown at cost less depreciation, not at what they would sell for |
| Historic cost | Assets and expenses are recorded at their actual cost when bought | A machine stays at its purchase price even if its market value rises |
| Matching (accruals) | Income and expenses are matched to the year they belong to, not the year cash moves | Rent owing at the year end is still an expense of that year |
| Materiality | Items too small to affect the accounts can be treated in the simplest way | A $6 stapler is an expense, not a non-current asset |
| Money measurement | Only items that can be measured in money are recorded | A skilled, loyal workforce is not in the accounts |
| Prudence | Profits and assets are not overstated; losses and liabilities are not understated | An allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Realisation | Revenue is recognised when goods or services pass to the customer, not when cash arrives | A credit sale is revenue on delivery |

### Concept or principle: which word do I use?

The 2027 syllabus and specimen papers say **accounting concept**. Most older papers say **accounting principle**. I searched the 98 question papers for 0452 and 7707 from March 2022 to June 2026 (0452: 62, 7707: 36). The phrase "accounting principle" is in 61 of them (0452: 39, 7707: 22). "Accounting concept" is in only two, the June 2026 Paper 22 of each syllabus. They are the same ideas, so practise on the old papers without worry, but expect "concept" in your 2027 exam.

On our past paper pages, 91 questions from the 0452 and 7707 papers of 2022 to 2026 carry the 7.1 tag, as main or second tag (0452: 57, 7707: 34). Almost all of these are Paper 1 multiple choice questions (0452: 54 of the 57, 7707: 33 of the 34). The count leaves out most of the 1 and 2 mark concept parts that sit inside bigger Paper 2 questions, so the real number is higher.

## What does each concept mean in practice?

### Business entity

The owner and the business are two separate people in the books. When the owner takes goods or cash for private use, that is **drawings**, not an expense. The 2027 specimen Paper 2 question 2(e) credited business entity for correcting goods taken by the owner for his own use. The March 2026 Paper 22 scheme gave it for not recording the owner's private vehicle in the business books.

### Consistency

Use the same methods every year: the same depreciation method, the same inventory valuation. The June 2022 Paper 21 report stressed that consistency means from one financial year to the next, not just within one year. It makes this year's profit comparable with last year's. The report also said it does not make the accounts comparable with other businesses.

### Duality

Every transaction affects two accounts, with equal debit and credit. This is the basis of double entry and of the accounting equation. The March 2026 Paper 22 report said limited answers only wrote that everything is entered twice, with no more explanation. Say what the two entries are: one debit and one credit of the same amount.

### Going concern

The accounts assume the business will keep trading for the foreseeable future, with no plan to close or shrink a lot. So assets are shown at cost less depreciation, not at what they would fetch in a closing down sale. The November 2022 Paper 22 report said one mark was gained for "the business will continue" for the foreseeable future, but the second, about having no plan to close the business or cut it back a lot, was rarely awarded.

### Historic cost

Record assets and expenses at what they actually cost. The June 2025 Paper 21 report said only a few candidates could explain that assets and expenses are recorded at their actual cost; many wrote about inventory valuation instead.

### Matching

Also called accruals. Income and expenses belong to the year they relate to. This is why you adjust for accruals, prepayments and depreciation. For example, if annual rent is $12,000 and $1,000 is still owing at the year end, the income statement still shows $12,000. The November 2023 7707 Paper 23 report said matching is applied to rent because only the current year's rent goes to the income statement.

### Materiality

Small items need not follow the full rules. A cheap tool is written off as an expense rather than kept as a non-current asset. The June 2022 Paper 22 scheme credited ideas such as these: the cost of recording a small asset can be greater than the benefit, and what is material for a small business may not be for a large one. The report said the first of these was rarely explored.

### Money measurement

Only things with a money value go in the accounts. Staff skill, a good location or customer loyalty may matter a great deal, but they are left out. The November 2023 7707 Paper 22 report gave the efficiency of staff as an example.

### Prudence

Do not be over optimistic. Do not overstate profit or assets; do not understate losses or liabilities. It explains the allowance for irrecoverable debts and why inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value. The June 2023 Paper 22 report said the first mark came from saying profits or assets are not overstated, with a second for linking it to the item in the question.

### Realisation

Revenue counts when the goods or services pass to the customer, not when the money arrives. The March 2025 Paper 12 report said some candidates thought a sale was not recognised until a cheque was received.

## How do I apply the concepts to a business?

Here is my own example. For each item, name the concept and give the reason in one line, which is the skill most questions test.

> Zoya runs a printing shop. Her financial year ends on 30 June 2027.

| Item | Concept | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Zoya paid her son's school fees of $900 from the business bank account and recorded them as drawings | Business entity | A private payment is not a business expense |
| A stapler costing $8 was recorded as an expense | Materiality | Too small to record as a non-current asset |
| A printer bought for $4,500 is still shown at cost less depreciation, though a dealer would now pay $5,200 for it | Historic cost | Assets stay at what they cost |
| A customer collected printed banners worth $650 on 28 June 2027 and paid on 10 July 2027 | Realisation | The sale belongs to the year to 30 June, when the goods passed to the customer |
| July 2027 rent of $1,000 was paid on 25 June 2027 | Matching | It is a prepayment, an expense of the next year |
| Zoya uses reducing balance depreciation every year | Consistency | Profits can be compared year to year |
| A customer owing $1,200 is in difficulty, so an allowance is made against the debt | Prudence | Assets and profit are not overstated |
| Zoya's two staff are highly skilled, but this is not in the accounts | Money measurement | Skill has no reliable money value |
| A $10,000 bank loan was debited to bank and credited to the loan account | Duality | Two equal aspects |
| The printer is not shown at what it would fetch in a sale | Going concern | The shop is expected to continue trading |

Two items here could fit more than one concept. The printer is both historic cost (recorded at cost) and going concern (not valued at sale price). In an exam, read the exact situation: a value kept at purchase price points to historic cost; a business that is expected to carry on trading points to going concern.

### Is reducing an allowance prudence or matching?

Past papers link irrecoverable debts to prudence. The March 2022 Paper 22 scheme gave **prudence** for writing off irrecoverable debts, and the June 2023 Paper 21 report called keeping a provision for doubtful debts an application of prudence. The 2027 specimen Paper 2 scheme, though, printed **matching** for an allowance for irrecoverable debts that had not been updated from $180 to $135. The situations differ: writing off or keeping an allowance in the past papers, updating its amount in the specimen. My reading, not a Cambridge statement: writing off a debt or creating an allowance stops assets being overstated, so prudence fits; bringing the allowance down to the right level is about the correct amount for this year, so matching fits. Name the concept that matches what the business is doing in the question.

<Tip title="Name, then reason">
When asked to state a concept, give its name. When asked to explain or apply it, add one line linking it to the exact item in the question. A definition on its own rarely earns the second mark.
</Tip>

## How are accounting concepts tested?

Concepts come up in both papers. Paper 1 is 40 multiple choice questions in 1 hour 30 minutes for both 0452 and 7707 from 2027. Paper 2 is five structured questions, 100 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes.

| Question type | Marks | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Which concept is being applied? (multiple choice) | 1 | 7707 November 2023 P12, 0452 March 2025 P12, 2027 specimen Paper 1 |
| Name the concept for a situation | 1 each | March 2022 P22, March 2025 P22, March 2026 P22, 2027 specimen Paper 2 |
| Explain a concept | 2 | November 2022 P22 (going concern), March 2026 P22 (how duality applies to a control account) |
| Meaning, importance or use of a concept | 2 | June 2022 P21 (consistency), June 2022 P22 (materiality) |
| State how a concept is applied to an item | 1 | November 2023 7707 P23 (prudence to inventory, matching to rent), June 2025 P21 (historic cost) |

In the 2 mark explain questions in these schemes, one mark is usually for the meaning and one for a second point: a further detail, the reason it matters, or the effect. For consistency in June 2022 Paper 21, the scheme gave one mark for using methods from one period to the next and one for accurate comparison or undistorted profit. For going concern in November 2022 Paper 22, the second mark was for no intention to close or shrink the business, or for non-current assets at book value.

### How do I answer "explain why the concept is important"?

Do not just define it. The November 2022 Paper 23 report said many candidates gave a definition of matching when the question asked why it was important. Importance means the result: the correct income and expenses for the year, a correct profit, and reliable comparisons and decisions.

The same trap catches "state how the concept is applied". In the November 2023 7707 Paper 23 report, a common response to how prudence applies to inventory was to define prudence. The answer wanted was that inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value.

In my teaching, this is the most common way students lose easy concept marks: they know every definition, but they answer the question they expected, not the one printed.

## What mistakes do examiners report?

| Mistake (session and paper) | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Confusing realisation with historic cost, and prudence with money measurement (many, March 2026 P22) | Learn each concept with its own example, as in the first table |
| Giving historic cost for items left out of the statement of financial position (common, March 2025 P22) | Left out because small: materiality (that scheme also accepted prudence). Left out because no money value: money measurement |
| Defining a concept when asked why it is important (many, November 2022 P23) | Give the result: correct profit, fair comparison |
| Defining a concept when asked how it is applied (common, November 2023 7707 P23) | Say what is done to the item |
| Duality as "everything is entered twice" (limited answers, March 2026 P22) | One debit and one credit of equal value |
| Going concern with only the first half (second mark rarely awarded, November 2022 P22) | Add that there is no plan to close or shrink the business |
| Treating a sale as revenue only when the cheque arrives (some, March 2025 P12) | Revenue on delivery: realisation |
| Consistency taken to mean within one year (June 2022 P21) | From one year to the next |

## Practice questions with answers

All three are my own.

### Practice question 1

> Name the accounting concept applied in each case. [3]
>
> (a) A trader pays $45 for a waste bin and records it as general expenses.
>
> (b) A trader buys a delivery van on credit. The motor vehicles account is debited and the supplier's account is credited with the same amount.
>
> (c) Goods sent to a customer on credit on 29 December are included in sales for the year ended 31 December.

**Answer**

(a) Materiality. (b) Duality. (c) Realisation.

### Practice question 2

> Bilal sells phones. At his year end he holds 12 phones of an old model. They cost $90 each and can now be sold for only $70 each. He values them at $70 each. Explain how this follows the prudence concept. [2]

**Answer**

The phones are valued at net realisable value because it is lower than cost: 12 × $70 = $840, not 12 × $90 = $1,080 **[1]**. This means inventory and profit are not overstated by $240 **[1]**.

### Practice question 3

> Imran has depreciated his office computers at 30% a year by the reducing balance method for three years. Profit has fallen, so he plans to use a lower rate this year only. Name the concept this would break and explain why it matters to Imran. [2]

**Answer**

Consistency: the depreciation method and rate should stay the same from one year to the next **[1]**. A lower rate used only to lift profit means this year cannot be compared fairly with the three years before **[1]**.

How I would mark these is my own judgement, modelled on the two part pattern in the schemes named above.

## FAQ

### How many accounting concepts are in the 0452 and 7707 syllabus?

Ten: business entity, consistency, duality, going concern, historic cost, matching (accruals), materiality, money measurement, prudence and realisation.

### Is an accounting concept the same as an accounting principle?

For this exam, yes. Most past papers from 2022 to 2026 say principle; the 2027 syllabus and specimen papers say concept.

### What is the difference between prudence and matching?

Prudence stops profit and assets being overstated. Matching puts income and expenses in the year they belong to. An accrued expense is matching. Past papers link an allowance for irrecoverable debts to prudence, but the 2027 specimen gave matching for bringing one up to date.

### Which concept explains drawings?

Business entity. The owner's private spending is kept apart from the business, so it is drawings, not an expense.

### Why is a cheap item treated as an expense?

Materiality. Recording a small item as a non-current asset costs more effort than it is worth, and it would not change anyone's view of the accounts.
