Depreciation questions at AS Level look routine until the disposal arrives. Then a vehicle is traded in halfway through the year, the policy says "month by month", and the marks start to slip. This article covers everything the 9706 syllabus asks for on depreciation, then works through two exam-style questions line by line.

## What 9706 expects

Syllabus section 1.3.2 (Changing asset values) asks you to:

- calculate depreciation using the **straight-line** and **reducing balance** methods, and choose the most appropriate one
- measure non-current assets by the **cost model** or the **revaluation model**
- prepare ledger accounts and journal entries for acquisition, revaluation, depreciation and **disposal, including part exchange**
- calculate the **profit or loss on disposal**
- show the effect of depreciation in the **statement of profit or loss** and the **statement of financial position**

Use the international names. 9706 papers say "statement of profit or loss", and the instructions on the question paper ask for international terms and formats. The value of an asset after depreciation is its **carrying amount**, the term used in IAS 16 (older books say "net book value").

Cambridge's teacher guidance on IAS 16 defines depreciation as "the systematic allocation of the depreciable amount" over the asset's useful life. The depreciable amount is cost less residual value. Depreciation spreads the cost of an asset over the years that benefit from it, so it is matched against the revenue it helps to earn. It is not a fund of cash, and it does not show what the asset could be sold for.

## The two methods

**Straight line**: the same charge every year.

Annual depreciation = (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life

A van costs $26 000, will be used for 4 years and then sold for $2 000. Annual depreciation = ($26 000 − $2 000) ÷ 4 = $6 000. Questions often give a percentage of cost instead ("20% per annum on cost"), which works the same way.

**Reducing balance**: a fixed percentage of the **carrying amount** at the start of each year. The charge is highest in year 1 and falls every year after.

| Use straight line when … | Use reducing balance when … |
|---|---|
| the asset gives equal benefit each year (buildings, fixtures) | the asset gives most benefit when new (vehicles, computers) |
| simplicity matters | repair costs rise as the asset ages, so a falling depreciation charge evens out the total cost |

Land is not depreciated, because it has an unlimited useful life. Buildings are.

## Part-year depreciation: read the policy

Every question tells you the policy. There are two common ones:

1. **Month by month (time apportioned)**: charge for the months the asset was owned in the year of purchase and the year of disposal.
2. **Full year in the year of purchase, none in the year of disposal**.

9706/21 (May/June 2024) Question 1 used the first policy: depreciation "is provided on a month-by-month basis". A motor vehicle costing $16 000, depreciated at 20% straight line, was sold on 1 July. The mark scheme charged six months, $1 600, giving a carrying amount of $14 400 against proceeds of $11 700: a loss on disposal of $2 700.

## The three ledger accounts

| Account | What it holds | Normal balance |
|---|---|---|
| Non-current asset (at cost) | The cost of assets still owned | Debit |
| Provision for depreciation | Depreciation built up on those assets | Credit |
| Disposal | One asset's cost, its depreciation and its proceeds | Clears to the statement of profit or loss |

The journal entries you need:

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Annual charge | Statement of profit or loss (depreciation) | Provision for depreciation |
| Asset sold: remove cost | Disposal | Non-current asset |
| Asset sold: remove depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| Cash proceeds | Bank | Disposal |
| Part-exchange allowance | Non-current asset (new asset) | Disposal |
| Profit on disposal | Disposal | Statement of profit or loss |
| Loss on disposal | Statement of profit or loss | Disposal |

In a **part exchange**, the old asset is handed to the supplier as part payment for the new one. The allowance is the old asset's "proceeds". It goes to the credit of the disposal account, and to the debit of the asset account as part of the new asset's cost.

## The revaluation model

Under the cost model, an asset is carried at cost less accumulated depreciation. Under the revaluation model, it is carried at a revalued amount (its fair value) less any later depreciation.

When a limited company revalues an asset upwards:

1. Remove the accumulated depreciation: debit provision for depreciation, credit the asset account.
2. Increase the asset account to the new value: debit the asset account, credit **revaluation reserve**.

The revaluation reserve is a capital reserve in equity. The gain is not profit, because nothing has been sold. Depreciation then continues on the revalued amount over the remaining useful life.

A fall in value is taken first from any revaluation reserve on the same asset. Any amount above that is an expense. 9706/21 (May/June 2024) Question 3 tested this: property previously revalued at $450 000 was revalued at $390 000. The mark scheme took $45 000 of the $60 000 fall from the revaluation reserve (its full balance) and the other $15 000 from retained earnings.

## Worked question 1: straight line, part exchange, ledger accounts

**Question.** Kamran runs a delivery business with a year end of 31 December. On 1 January 2025 his motor vehicles were:

| Vehicle | Bought | Cost $ | Provision for depreciation at 1 January 2025 $ |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | 1 January 2022 | 30 000 | 18 000 |
| B | 1 January 2024 | 50 000 | 10 000 |
| | | 80 000 | 28 000 |

On 1 April 2025, vehicle A was part exchanged for vehicle C, costing $45 000. The supplier gave a part-exchange allowance of $9 000, and Kamran paid the balance by bank.

Vehicles are depreciated at 20% per annum on cost, month by month. Prepare the motor vehicles account, the provision for depreciation account and the disposal account for 2025, and the statement of financial position extract at 31 December 2025.

**Step 1: the depreciation for 2025.**

| Vehicle | Working | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| A | $30 000 × 20% × 3/12 | 1 500 |
| B | $50 000 × 20% | 10 000 |
| C | $45 000 × 20% × 9/12 | 6 750 |
| Total charge for 2025 | | 18 250 |

**Step 2: vehicle A at disposal.** Provision $18 000 + $1 500 = $19 500. Carrying amount $30 000 − $19 500 = $10 500. Allowance $9 000, so a **loss of $1 500**.

**Motor vehicles account**

| Dr | $ | Cr | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan Balance b/d | 80 000 | 1 Apr Disposal | 30 000 |
| 1 Apr Disposal (part-exchange allowance) | 9 000 | 31 Dec Balance c/d | 95 000 |
| 1 Apr Bank | 36 000 | | |
| | 125 000 | | 125 000 |
| 2026 1 Jan Balance b/d | 95 000 | | |

**Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles account**

| Dr | $ | Cr | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Apr Disposal | 19 500 | 1 Jan Balance b/d | 28 000 |
| 31 Dec Balance c/d | 26 750 | 1 Apr Statement of profit or loss (vehicle A) | 1 500 |
| | | 31 Dec Statement of profit or loss (vehicles B and C) | 16 750 |
| | 46 250 | | 46 250 |
| | | 2026 1 Jan Balance b/d | 26 750 |

**Disposal account**

| Dr | $ | Cr | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Apr Motor vehicles | 30 000 | 1 Apr Provision for depreciation | 19 500 |
| | | 1 Apr Motor vehicles (part-exchange allowance) | 9 000 |
| | | 31 Dec Statement of profit or loss (loss on disposal) | 1 500 |
| | 30 000 | | 30 000 |

**Statement of financial position (extract) at 31 December 2025**

| | Cost $ | Accumulated depreciation $ | Carrying amount $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Motor vehicles | 95 000 | 26 750 | 68 250 |

Check the closing figures against the vehicles still owned. Cost: B $50 000 + C $45 000 = $95 000. Depreciation: B $20 000 (two years) + C $6 750 = $26 750. Both agree.

**If the policy changed.** With a full year in the year of purchase and none in the year of disposal, vehicle A gets no charge for 2025. Its carrying amount is $30 000 − $18 000 = $12 000, so the loss is $3 000. The charge for the year becomes B $10 000 + C $9 000 = $19 000. Same transactions, different answers: that is why you read the policy first.

<Tip>
Always do the depreciation on the disposed asset up to the date of disposal **before** you open the disposal account. A common error is transferring the provision balance at the start of the year and forgetting the months since.
</Tip>

## Worked question 2: reducing balance and revaluation

**Question.** Orchid Limited has a year end of 31 December.

- Machinery bought on 1 January 2023 for $64 000 is depreciated at 25% per annum, reducing balance.
- A building bought on 1 January 2021 for $400 000 is depreciated at 2% per annum, straight line (a 50-year life, no residual value).
- On 31 December 2025, after the year's depreciation, the directors revalued the building at $450 000.

(a) Calculate the depreciation on machinery for each year to 31 December 2025.  
(b) Prepare the journal entries for the revaluation.  
(c) Calculate the depreciation on the building for 2026.  
(d) Explain the treatment if, on 31 December 2026, the building is valued at $340 000.

**(a) Machinery, reducing balance**

| Year | Carrying amount at start $ | Depreciation at 25% $ | Carrying amount at end $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 64 000 | 16 000 | 48 000 |
| 2024 | 48 000 | 12 000 | 36 000 |
| 2025 | 36 000 | 9 000 | 27 000 |

Accumulated depreciation at 31 December 2025 is $37 000, and $64 000 − $37 000 = $27 000.

**(b) Revaluation.** Building depreciation is $400 000 × 2% = $8 000 a year. After five years (2021 to 2025), accumulated depreciation is $40 000 and the carrying amount is $360 000. The surplus is $450 000 − $360 000 = $90 000.

| Journal | Debit $ | Credit $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Provision for depreciation of building | 40 000 | |
| Building | | 40 000 |
| *Removing accumulated depreciation on revaluation* | | |
| Building | 90 000 | |
| Revaluation reserve | | 90 000 |
| *Surplus on revaluation of building* | | |

The building account now shows $400 000 − $40 000 + $90 000 = $450 000. In the statement of financial position, the building appears at $450 000 with no accumulated depreciation, and equity includes a revaluation reserve of $90 000.

**(c) Depreciation for 2026.** Five of the fifty years have gone, leaving 45. Depreciation = $450 000 ÷ 45 = **$10 000** a year. It rises from $8 000 because it is now based on the higher value.

**(d) A fall in value.** At 31 December 2026, the carrying amount is $450 000 − $10 000 = $440 000. A valuation of $340 000 is a fall of $100 000. The first $90 000 is taken from the revaluation reserve on this building, which reduces it to zero. The remaining **$10 000** is an expense in the statement of profit or loss.

## Mistakes that cost marks

- **Reducing balance on cost.** The percentage applies to the carrying amount at the start of each year, never to cost after year 1.
- **Ignoring the policy.** Month by month and full-year policies give different charges and a different profit or loss on disposal.
- **Proceeds in the wrong place.** A part-exchange allowance is credited to disposal and debited to the asset account. It never goes through the bank.
- **Revaluation gain as profit.** It goes to the revaluation reserve, not the statement of profit or loss.
- **Old depreciation after revaluation.** The charge must be recalculated on the revalued amount.
