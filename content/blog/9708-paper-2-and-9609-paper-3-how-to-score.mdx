These two papers look different. Economics Paper 2 is a data response and two essays. Business Paper 3 is a case study. But they lose marks in the same way. Students know the content, write a lot, and still land in the middle levels, because the marks at the top are for analysis and judgement, not for knowledge. Here is how each paper is marked, what the examiners said, and what to do differently.

## The two papers at a glance

| | Economics 9708 Paper 2 | Business 9609 Paper 3 |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | AS Level | A Level (second year) |
| Time | 2 hours | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Marks | 60 | 60 |
| What you answer | Three questions: Section A, the data response (compulsory), then one essay from Section B and one from Section C | All questions, based on a case study in a separate insert |
| Question marks (June 2025) | Data response 20, each essay 20 (part (a) 8, part (b) 12) | 8, 8, then 2, 2 and 12, then 2, 2 and 12, then 12 |

## What an A needed in June 2026

This is the part most students never look at. Cambridge publishes the A line for each paper, and these two are very different.

| Paper | Variant 1 | Variant 2 | Variant 3 | Variant 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Economics 9708 Paper 2 | 24 out of 60 | 25 out of 60 | 26 out of 60 | 24 out of 60 |
| Business 9609 Paper 3 | 41 out of 60 | 39 out of 60 | 39 out of 60 | none |

An A on Economics Paper 2 needed about 40% to 43%. On Business Paper 3 it needed 65% to 68%. Economics Paper 2 is marked hard: a student who writes a decent but one sided essay can come away with very few marks. In Business Paper 3, the examiner reports say many candidates score well on the calculations and the 8 mark questions, while marks are lower on the 12 mark questions. That is where the grade is decided. Full tables are on the threshold pages for [Economics 9708](/grade-thresholds/a-level-economics-9708) and [Business 9609](/grade-thresholds/a-level-business-9609).

## Economics 9708 Paper 2: where the marks are

### Section A: the data response

You may use a calculator on this paper. The first parts are short: identify, compare, calculate. Then come the 4 and 6 mark questions, and these behave like small essays. In the June 2025 Paper 22 mark scheme, the 6 mark questions gave up to 3 marks for one side of the argument and up to 3 for the other, with a maximum of 4 for analysis, and one mark reserved for a justified conclusion. The 4 mark question also kept a mark back for a valid conclusion.

So a 6 mark answer needs both sides and a one line judgement that answers the question. Without the conclusion, the best you can do is 5.

### Sections B and C: the essays

Each essay has two parts.

| Part | Marks | How they are split |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | 8 | Up to 3 for knowledge and understanding, up to 3 for analysis, up to 2 for evaluation |
| (b) | 12 | 8 for knowledge and analysis together, marked in three levels, plus 4 for evaluation, marked in two levels |

For part (b), the top level for knowledge and analysis (6 to 8 marks) needs analysis that is "developed and detailed", with diagrams that are accurate and fully explained. The top level for evaluation (3 to 4 marks) needs a justified conclusion that addresses the specific question.

The June 2023 examiner report made one point very clearly: a part (b) answer that only looks at one side of the question is unlikely to get past the middle of Level 2 for analysis, and is unlikely to get any evaluation marks at all.

### What the examiners said in March 2026

The March 2026 examiner report for Paper 22 listed what separated the strong answers:

- **Full definitions of syllabus terms, with the formula where there is one.** Some candidates used the price elasticity formula for a question on income elasticity.
- **Diagrams that are accurate, labelled and linked to the writing.** A diagram that sits on its own, with no explanation, does little.
- **Use the data, do not copy it.** Repeating the extract earned nothing.
- **Explain the reasoning behind a judgement.** Unsupported assertions were a common weakness.
- **Read the whole question.** Many essay parts ask for two things, such as the benefits and the drawbacks. Answers that covered only one missed half the task.

The June 2023 report also noted that some candidates spent too long on the 2 mark questions. Keep them short.

### A time plan for 2 hours

| Section | Marks | Time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Section A, data response | 20 | 40 minutes |
| Section B, one essay | 20 | 40 minutes |
| Section C, one essay | 20 | 40 minutes |

Two minutes a mark. Inside each essay, give part (a) about 15 minutes and part (b) about 25, including a short plan and a final paragraph that answers the question.

## Business 9609 Paper 3: where the marks are

### Analyse questions (8 marks)

In recent papers, Questions 1 and 2 ask you to analyse two advantages, two ways or two impacts. In the June 2025 Paper 31 mark scheme, the 8 marks were split: 2 for knowledge, 2 for application, 4 for analysis. The examiner report for June 2023 reminded candidates that these questions need no evaluation. Use the time for two developed chains of reasoning, each tied to the business in the case.

### Calculations (2 marks each)

The examiner reports keep repeating the same two points. Use the formula in the syllabus, and state the unit, such as days or a percentage. The March 2026 report noted candidates losing marks for using a different formula and for leaving units out.

### Evaluate questions (12 marks)

Each 12 mark question gave 2 marks for knowledge, 2 for application, 2 for analysis and **6 for evaluation**. The June 2023 report said these questions allocate half their marks to evaluation, and that many candidates stopped short of a judgement even after sound analysis.

The evaluation marks come in three levels:

| Level | Marks | What it needs |
|---|---:|---|
| 3 | 5 to 6 | A developed judgement made in the business context, balancing key arguments in the context |
| 2 | 3 to 4 | A developed judgement, balancing some key arguments |
| 1 | 1 to 2 | A judgement with limited support, or an attempt at balance |

The difference between Level 2 and Level 3 is the business. A judgement that would fit any company stays at Level 2.

### What the examiners said

From the March 2026 and June 2023 reports for Paper 3:

- **Repeating the case is not application.** Interpret the data and use it to support your point.
- **Fewer points, developed further.** Answers that tried to cover many factors ended up with shallow analysis and weak evaluation. Two or three key points, followed through, did better.
- **Your conclusion should add something new.** A conclusion that only repeats earlier points, then names an option, was a common weakness. Weigh short term against long term, or say which factor matters most for this business and why.
- **Answer the exact question.** In March 2026, one question asked how delegation could encourage intrapreneurship. Most answers discussed delegation in general and never made the link.

### A time plan for 1 hour 45 minutes

The June 2023 report advises reading all the questions first, then the case with those questions in mind. Allow about 10 minutes for that. It leaves 95 minutes for 60 marks, a little over a minute and a half per mark:

| Question type | Marks | Time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Each 8 mark analyse question | 8 | 12 minutes |
| Each 2 mark calculation | 2 | 3 minutes |
| Each 12 mark evaluate question | 12 | 19 minutes |

## One habit that works on both papers

For every extended answer, write your final judgement first, in one sentence, before you start the paragraphs. Then make each paragraph earn that judgement. It stops you drifting into a list, and it means that if time runs out, the conclusion is already on the page.

<Tip>
After a timed Business Paper 3, mark your own 12 mark answers against the level tables, not the list of possible points. Ask one question: did my judgement use a fact from the case that a different business would not have? If not, the evaluation cannot reach Level 3, however good the analysis was. In Economics, ask whether your conclusion answers the exact question set, not a general version of it.
</Tip>
