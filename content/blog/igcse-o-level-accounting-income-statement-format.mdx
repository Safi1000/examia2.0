The income statement for IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 has two parts. The trading section takes revenue, less sales returns, less cost of sales, to give gross profit. Then you add other income, deduct expenses, and arrive at profit for the year. From 2027 Cambridge calls it the statement of profit or loss. The layout does not change.

This page is the full guide to that one statement. You get the layout, what every line means and how each year-end adjustment changes it. Then comes a complete worked example from a trial balance, how examiners mark it, and the mistakes their reports keep listing. For the other layouts (statement of financial position, partnership, manufacturing and club accounts), see my [IGCSE and O Level accounting formats guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-formats).

## What is the income statement format for O Level and IGCSE?

Here is the full layout for a trading sole trader. X is a figure, (X) is a figure being deducted. Items go in the inner columns and subtotals in the outer column.

**[Business name]: Statement of profit or loss for the year ended [date]**

| | $ | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | | | X |
| Less sales returns | | | (X) |
| | | | X |
| Less cost of sales | | | |
| Opening inventory | | X | |
| Purchases | X | | |
| Less purchases returns | (X) | | |
| Less goods taken by owner | (X) | | |
| Carriage inwards | X | X | |
| | | X | |
| Less closing inventory | | (X) | (X) |
| **Gross profit** | | | **X** |
| Add other income (listed) | | X | X |
| | | | X |
| Less expenses (listed) | | X | (X) |
| **Profit for the year** | | | **X** |

Three rules sit behind this layout:

- **The heading has three parts**: the business name, the name of the statement, and "for the year ended" with the date. It covers a period, so it is never "at" a date.
- **Order matters.** Cost of sales is built in a fixed order: opening inventory, net purchases, then closing inventory.
- **Labels matter.** Write "Cost of sales", "Gross profit" and "Profit for the year" in full. The March 2026 examiner report calls labels such as "GP" unacceptable.

## What names does Cambridge use from 2027?

Both the 0452 and 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029 use the newer names. The subject content now says statement of profit or loss where older papers said income statement. Past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports up to 2026 still say income statement, so you will see both.

| Name in older papers and textbooks | Name in the 2027 syllabus |
|---|---|
| Income statement, trading and profit and loss account | Statement of profit or loss |
| Sales | Revenue |
| Returns inwards | Sales returns |
| Returns outwards | Purchases returns |
| Cost of goods sold | Cost of sales |
| Net profit | Profit for the year |
| Stock | Inventory |
| Bad debts | Irrecoverable debts |
| Provision for doubtful debts | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Provision for depreciation | Accumulated depreciation |
| Accruals and prepayments (year-end balances) | Other payables and other receivables |

The left column mixes two sources. Some names still appear in Cambridge mark schemes and examiner reports from 2022 to 2026: income statement, returns inwards, cost of goods sold, net profit and both provisions. The others are textbook names.

The 2027 syllabus itself still mixes some names. Topic 4.2 asks for a provision for depreciation account, while topic 5.1 talks about accumulated depreciation. Mark schemes mix them too: the March 2025 one headed the section "Cost of goods sold". My advice is simple. Use the newest names, and recognise the old ones when a question uses them.

## What goes in the trading section?

The trading section works out gross profit: the profit made on buying and selling goods, before any running costs.

| Line | What it means | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Total sales of goods, cash and credit | Start here |
| Sales returns | Goods customers sent back | Deduct from revenue |
| Opening inventory | Unsold goods at the start of the year | First line of cost of sales |
| Purchases | Goods bought for resale | Add |
| Purchases returns | Goods you sent back to suppliers | Deduct from purchases |
| Carriage inwards | Delivery cost of goods coming in | Add to purchases |
| Closing inventory | Unsold goods at the end of the year | Deduct to give cost of sales |
| Cost of sales | The cost of the goods actually sold | Deduct from net revenue |
| Gross profit | Net revenue less cost of sales | Subtotal |

A few points to get right every time:

- **Opening inventory** is in the trial balance. **Closing inventory** is not. It is given as a note below the trial balance, and it appears twice: in cost of sales and as a current asset.
- **Carriage inwards** belongs with purchases, inside cost of sales. It is part of the cost of getting the goods to your shop.
- **Inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value** (the expected selling price less any costs to sell it). If closing inventory is overvalued, cost of sales is too low, so gross profit and profit for the year are both overstated.
- **Trade discount** never appears in the income statement. It is taken off the invoice before anything is recorded.

### What if the business is a service business?

The 2027 syllabus covers trading, service and manufacturing sole traders. A service business (a tutor, a repair shop) sells no goods, so there is no trading section, no cost of sales and no gross profit. Start with fees or revenue, add other income, deduct expenses, and you reach profit for the year.

## What comes after gross profit?

### What counts as other income?

Other income is money earned outside normal trading: discount received, rent received, commission received, interest received, a decrease in the allowance for irrecoverable debts, or irrecoverable debts recovered. **Add it to gross profit** before deducting expenses. It is never added to revenue and never deducted from expenses.

### Which expenses are deducted?

Expenses are the running costs of the year: wages, rent and rates, insurance, electricity, carriage outwards, discount allowed, irrecoverable debts, any increase in the allowance, and depreciation. List each one, total them, and deduct the total.

### How do you reach profit for the year?

Gross profit plus other income, less expenses, gives **profit for the year**. If expenses are larger, write "Loss for the year" and show the figure in brackets. This figure then goes to the capital section of the statement of financial position.

### What never goes in the income statement?

Capital items and owner's items do not belong here: drawings, capital, the cost of non-current assets, accumulated depreciation balances, bank and cash, trade receivables and trade payables. The March 2026 examiner report lists drawings, asset cost and the opening provision for depreciation as irrelevant items candidates included. The trial balance shows them, but they go to the statement of financial position.

## How do adjustments change the income statement?

Adjustments are the notes below the trial balance. Each one changes a figure before you write it in. The 2027 syllabus lists depreciation, accruals and prepayments of expenses and income, irrecoverable debts and the allowance, and drawings of goods or of private expenses paid by the business.

| Adjustment | Effect on the income statement |
|---|---|
| Expense accrued (owing) | Add to the expense |
| Expense prepaid (paid in advance) | Deduct from the expense |
| Income accrued (still to receive) | Add to the income |
| Income prepaid (received in advance) | Deduct from the income |
| Depreciation for the year | New expense |
| Irrecoverable debt written off | Add to irrecoverable debts expense |
| Increase in allowance | Expense |
| Decrease in allowance | Other income |
| Goods taken by owner | Deduct from purchases |
| Owner's private expense paid by business | Remove from the expense (it is drawings) |

### How do accruals and prepayments change a figure?

These apply the matching (accruals) concept: the statement shows the cost of the year, not the cash paid in the year. If rent paid was $6 000 but $500 of it covers next year, the rent expense is $5 500. If wages of $800 are still owing at the year end, add $800 to the wages paid.

Watch for opening balances too. If $300 of wages was owing at the start of the year, part of this year's payments cleared last year's cost. So deduct the opening accrual and add the closing one.

### How is depreciation shown?

Depreciation spreads the cost of a non-current asset over its useful life. The syllabus names three methods: straight-line, reducing balance and revaluation.

- **Straight-line**: a percentage of **cost** each year.
- **Reducing balance**: a percentage of **net book value** (cost less accumulated depreciation to date).

Only the charge for the year goes in the income statement. The November 2023 O Level report found many candidates working out the charge as a percentage of the provision for depreciation. That is wrong under either method.

### How do irrecoverable debts and the allowance work?

An irrecoverable debt is money a customer will never pay. Write it off as an expense, and reduce trade receivables by the same amount **before** you work out the new allowance.

The allowance for irrecoverable debts is an estimate of debts that may not be paid. Only the **change** in the allowance goes in the income statement. An increase is an expense. A decrease is other income. The June 2023 reports for both 0452 and 7707 list the classic error: moving the whole year-end balance to the income statement, not just the increase.

### What if the owner takes goods or pays private bills?

When the owner takes goods for their own use, those goods were not sold. Deduct their cost from purchases, and add the same amount to drawings. If the business paid the owner's private bill, take it out of the expense and treat it as drawings. Drawings themselves never appear in the income statement.

### Where do carriage outwards and discounts go?

- **Carriage outwards** (delivery to customers) is a selling cost, so it is an **expense**. Do not confuse it with carriage inwards.
- **Discount allowed** (cash discount you give customers for paying early) is an **expense**.
- **Discount received** (cash discount suppliers give you) is **other income**, added to gross profit.

<Tip title="Tick as you go">
Put a small tick next to each trial balance figure and each note once you have used it. Before you total, check that every note has a tick. A missed note usually costs both the adjusted figure and the profit figure.
</Tip>

### The notes at 31 December 2026

1. Inventory was valued at $16 500.
2. Wages of $1 400 were owing.
3. Insurance of $600 was paid in advance.
4. The tenant still owed $500 of rent for December.
5. Electricity of $250 was owing.
6. Sana took goods costing $700 for her own use. No entry has been made.
7. General expenses include $350 for Sana's private phone bill.
8. Depreciation: fixtures and fittings at 10% a year on cost (straight-line), motor vehicle at 25% a year on net book value (reducing balance).
9. A debt of $500 is to be written off. The allowance is to be 4% of the remaining trade receivables.

### Workings

| Item | Working | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Net purchases | 112 000 less 2 500 less 700 + 1 800 | 110 600 |
| Wages | 31 000 + 1 400 | 32 400 |
| Insurance | 2 400 less 600 | 1 800 |
| Rent received | 4 500 + 500 | 5 000 |
| Electricity | 3 100 + 250 | 3 350 |
| General expenses | 2 750 less 350 | 2 400 |
| Depreciation: fixtures | 30 000 × 10% | 3 000 |
| Depreciation: vehicle | (24 000 less 6 000) × 25% | 4 500 |
| Irrecoverable debts | 650 + 500 | 1 150 |
| New allowance | (18 000 less 500) × 4% | 700 |
| Increase in allowance | 700 less 500 | 200 |

Notice two things. The vehicle uses net book value ($18 000), not cost. And the debt is written off before the 4% is applied.

## How is the income statement marked in Paper 2?

Paper 2 for both 0452 and 7707 is 1 hour 45 minutes, with five compulsory questions of 20 marks each. It is worth 70% of the qualification. The income statement is usually one part of a question. In March 2025 it was followed by a statement of financial position extract and an advice part.

That part carries a large share of the 20 marks. In the March 2025 0452 Paper 22 mark scheme it carried 9 marks. The November 2022 0452 report mentions 12 marks for the income statement in Question 1.

Looking at that March 2025 mark scheme, the marks sat in a clear pattern:

- one mark for revenue less sales returns
- one mark for the cost of sales figure
- one mark for each figure that needed an adjustment (accruals, prepayments, depreciation)
- "own figure" marks for gross profit and for profit for the year

Figures copied straight from the trial balance with no adjustment did not earn a mark of their own. The marks go to the thinking.

**Own figure marks** are worth understanding. If your cost of sales is wrong but you subtract it correctly, you can still earn the gross profit mark. The examiner follows your figures through. So never leave a total out because an earlier figure might be wrong. Finish the statement.

You can see how many marks past grades needed on the [0452 grade thresholds page](/grade-thresholds/igcse-accounting-0452).

## Where do candidates lose marks?

These come from the Paper 2 sections of Cambridge examiner reports for 0452 and 7707, from 2022 to 2026. The same errors keep coming back.

| Error | Reported in |
|---|---|
| Sales returns added to revenue, or left out | 0452 and 7707 June 2022, 0452 March 2025 |
| Cost of sales items in the wrong order | 0452 and 7707 June 2022, June 2023 |
| Carriage inwards left out, placed after closing inventory, or deducted | 0452 November 2022 |
| Goods taken by the owner treated wrongly | 0452 June 2022 |
| Discount received left out or in the wrong place | 0452 June 2022, June 2025 |
| Rent received added to revenue or deducted from expenses | 0452 March 2025 |
| Accruals and prepayments adjusted wrongly | 0452 June 2022, March 2025 |
| Depreciation worked out wrongly | 0452 March 2025, March 2026, 7707 November 2023 |
| The whole allowance balance used instead of the change | 0452 June 2023, 7707 June 2023 |
| Labels missing or abbreviated | 0452 March 2022, March 2025, March 2026 |
| Extra items included, such as drawings or non-current assets | 0452 June 2022, March 2026 |

The good news is in the same reports. The March 2025 report says the income statement was generally well attempted, with many high marks. The March 2026 report describes the strongest answers: a proper format, the three key labels in place, and other income added to gross profit before expenses came off. That is the layout on this page.

<Tip title="Write the labels first">
Before any figures, write the three labels down the page: Cost of sales, Gross profit, Profit for the year. Then fill in around them. Missing labels appear in report after report, and they are the easiest marks to protect.
</Tip>

## How do partnership and limited company statements differ?

The syllabus says the same 5.1 adjustments apply to partnerships and limited companies. The statement of profit or loss itself looks almost the same. The differences come after profit for the year.

| Business | Statement of profit or loss | What follows it |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | As above | Profit added to capital |
| Partnership | As above, plus interest on partners' loans as an expense | Appropriation account shares out the profit |
| Limited company | As above, plus interest on loans such as debentures as an expense | Statement of changes in equity shows dividends and reserves |

Two traps: interest on capital, partners' salaries and interest on drawings go in the appropriation account, not the statement of profit or loss. And a company's dividends go in the statement of changes in equity. The November 2023 O Level report lists dividends among the extra items candidates wrongly put in an income statement. My [partnership accounts guide](/blog/igcse-accounting-partnership-accounts) covers the appropriation account in full.

### Question 2: adjusting the figures

Calculate the amount for each item in the income statement for the year ended 31 March 2027, and say where it goes.

1. Rent paid in the year was $7 800. Of this, $600 was for April 2027.
2. Wages paid were $24 500. Wages owing were $300 at the start of the year and $450 at the end.
3. Commission received was $2 100. A further $250 was due but not yet received.
4. Trade receivables are $12 000. The allowance at the start of the year was $480. It is to be kept at 3% of trade receivables.
5. Equipment cost $20 000. Accumulated depreciation is $7 200. Depreciation is 20% a year by the reducing balance method.

**Answer**

| Item | Working | Amount and place |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Rent | 7 800 less 600 | $7 200, expense |
| 2 Wages | 24 500 less 300 + 450 | $24 650, expense |
| 3 Commission received | 2 100 + 250 | $2 350, other income |
| 4 Allowance | 12 000 × 3% = 360; 480 less 360 | $120 decrease, other income |
| 5 Depreciation | (20 000 less 7 200) × 20% | $2 560, expense |

In item 4 the allowance falls, so the $120 is added to gross profit. In item 5, reducing balance uses net book value ($12 800), not cost.

### Question 3: correcting a draft profit

Laila's draft profit for the year is $15 000. Then these errors are found:

1. Closing inventory was included at $6 000. Its correct value is $5 200.
2. Carriage outwards of $400 was included in cost of sales.
3. Goods costing $500 taken by Laila were not recorded.
4. Insurance of $300 paid in advance was not adjusted.
5. Depreciation of $1 200 was not charged.

Calculate the corrected profit for the year.

**Answer**

| Item | Effect on profit | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Draft profit for the year | | 15 000 |
| 1 Inventory overvalued | Cost of sales too low | (800) |
| 2 Carriage outwards misplaced | Moves from cost of sales to expenses | 0 |
| 3 Goods taken by owner | Purchases too high | 500 |
| 4 Insurance prepaid | Expense too high | 300 |
| 5 Depreciation omitted | Expense missing | (1 200) |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | | **13 800** |

## FAQ

### What is the format of an income statement in O Level Accounting?

A heading in three parts, then revenue less sales returns, less cost of sales, to give gross profit. Add other income, deduct expenses, and you reach profit for the year. The format is the same for IGCSE 0452.

### Is the income statement the same as the statement of profit or loss?

Yes. The 2027 syllabuses for 0452 and 7707 use "statement of profit or loss". Older papers and mark schemes say "income statement". The layout is the same.

### Where does carriage inwards go in the income statement?

Inside cost of sales, added to purchases after purchases returns. Carriage outwards is different: it is an expense below gross profit.

### Is discount received income or an expense?

Discount received is other income, added to gross profit. Discount allowed is an expense. Trade discount does not appear in the income statement at all.

### Does closing inventory go in the income statement?

Yes. It is deducted in cost of sales. It is also shown as a current asset in the statement of financial position.

### How do I treat the allowance for irrecoverable debts?

Only the change goes in the income statement. An increase is an expense and a decrease is other income. The full closing balance is deducted from trade receivables in the statement of financial position.

### Do drawings go in the income statement?

No. Drawings are taken out of capital in the statement of financial position. Goods taken by the owner are deducted from purchases, and private bills are removed from expenses.
