Break even output is the number of units a business must sell for its total revenue to equal its total costs. The formula is fixed costs divided by contribution per unit, where contribution is selling price minus variable cost per unit. In IGCSE Business 0264 and O Level Business 7081 this is syllabus section 4.4.3, together with the break even chart, the margin of safety and the limitations of the method.

## What is break even analysis in 0264 and 7081?

At break even, a business makes no profit and no loss. Every unit sold below that point adds to a loss. Every unit sold above it adds to profit. Break even analysis uses costs, price and sales to find that point and to test decisions before the business makes them.

You need three cost words first. **Fixed costs** stay the same whatever the output, such as rent. **Variable costs** change with output, such as materials. **Total cost** is fixed costs plus total variable costs.

The 0264 and 7081 syllabuses use the same wording for 4.4.3 and the same formula list. I teach from the new syllabus, then point you to the old 0450 and 7115 papers for practice.

## What does the 2027 syllabus ask you to do?

Section 4.4.3 has seven points. Here is what each one means in an exam, in my words.

| Syllabus point | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Concept of break even | Explain what break even means for a business |
| Complete or amend a simple chart | Add a missing line, or show how a line moves after a change |
| Interpret a chart | Read break even output, profit, loss and margin of safety from it |
| Calculate break even output | Use the formula with given figures |
| Margin of safety | Define it, calculate it and say what it means |

Read the chart point carefully. The syllabus asks you to complete or amend a simple chart. It does not ask you to draw a full chart from a blank page. My reading is that you will be given a chart and asked to finish it, change it or read it.

## What is the break even formula?

The 0264 and 7081 syllabuses print a list of formulas to support teaching. These are the break even ones:

| Formula | Answer in |
|---|---|
| Contribution per unit = selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit | $ |
| Break even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit | units |
| Margin of safety = actual number of sales minus break even number of sales | units |

The syllabus does not say this list is printed on the exam paper, so learn it.

**Contribution** is what each unit sold adds towards paying the fixed costs. Once the fixed costs are covered, each further unit's contribution is profit.

**Break even output is a number of units, never dollars.** The June 2022 Paper 12 mark scheme gave a maximum of 1 mark out of 2 when a candidate put a $ sign on break even output, since the answer is a quantity and not money. The March 2026 Paper 12 scheme did the same for a margin of safety written as $3000.

### How should I define break even output and margin of safety?

The two mark schemes I checked give 2 marks for a full definition and 1 for a partial one, so the wording matters.

| Term | Full mark version | Only worth 1 mark |
|---|---|---|
| Break even output | The output a business must make and sell so that total revenue equals total cost | "No profit and no loss", or "revenue equals cost" without the word total |
| Margin of safety | How far current sales (or output) are above break even output | "Units above break even", with no mention of sales or current output |

These follow the March 2024 Paper 12 and November 2025 Paper 11 mark schemes. The March 2024 report said the best definitions stated that total costs and revenue are equal, while the weakest just repeated the words break even.

## How do I work through a full break even question?

Here is my own example, with every number checked.

> Sitara Crafts makes embroidered cushion covers in a small workshop. Each cover sells for $24. Fabric, thread and the embroiderer's piece rate cost $12 per cover. Rent, salaries and insurance come to $5,400 a month. Sitara sells 600 covers a month. The workshop can make at most 800 covers a month.

**Step 1: contribution per unit.**
$24 minus $12 = $12 per cover.

**Step 2: break even output.**
$5,400 ÷ $12 = 450 covers a month.

**Step 3: check it.** At 450 covers, total revenue is 450 × $24 = $10,800. Total cost is $5,400 + (450 × $12) = $5,400 + $5,400 = $10,800. Revenue equals cost, so 450 is right. The check is quick and catches slips.

**Step 4: margin of safety.**
600 minus 450 = 150 covers. Sales could fall by 150 covers a month before Sitara makes a loss.

Use current sales, 600, not the maximum of 800. 800 minus 450 = 350 is wrong. The June 2022 Paper 11 report said many candidates made exactly this mistake.

**Step 5: profit at current sales.**
Total revenue = 600 × $24 = $14,400. Total cost = $5,400 + (600 × $12) = $12,600. Profit = $14,400 minus $12,600 = $1,800.

Quicker: the 150 covers above break even each earn $12 of contribution, and 150 × $12 = $1,800.

### What should my working look like?

Write the formula in words, then the numbers, then the answer with its unit:

> Break even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit = $5,400 ÷ ($24 minus $12) = 450 covers

The five old calculation questions in the marking table further down were each worth 2 marks: 2 for the right answer, 1 for the right method with a wrong answer. The June 2022, March 2024 and March 2026 schemes gave 2 marks for a correct answer with no working. The June 2022 report for Paper 12 still advised showing the formula, because the method mark can save you when the answer is wrong. My advice is to always show it.

<Tip title="Units, not dollars">
Break even output and margin of safety are always numbers of products: covers, rolls, loads. Write the product name after the number and never a $ sign. Contribution, break even revenue and profit are the answers in dollars.
</Tip>

## How do I read a break even chart?

A break even chart has output in units along the bottom and costs and revenue in dollars up the side. It has three straight lines.

| Line | Shape | Where it starts |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed costs | Flat | At the fixed cost figure on the vertical axis |
| Total costs | Rises | At the same point as fixed costs |
| Total revenue | Rises more steeply | At zero |

For Sitara Crafts, the figures behind the chart are:

| Covers a month | Fixed costs ($) | Total costs ($) | Total revenue ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 5,400 | 5,400 | 0 |
| 200 | 5,400 | 7,800 | 4,800 |
| 400 | 5,400 | 10,200 | 9,600 |
| 600 | 5,400 | 12,600 | 14,400 |
| 800 | 5,400 | 15,000 | 19,200 |

Picture it. The fixed cost line runs flat at $5,400. Total costs starts at $5,400 and climbs to $15,000 at 800 covers. Total revenue starts at zero and climbs faster, to $19,200. The two rising lines cross at 450 covers and $10,800. That crossing is the **break even point**.

Now read the chart:

- **Break even output**: go straight down from the crossing to the output axis. 450 covers.
- **Break even revenue**: go across from the crossing to the money axis. $10,800. The June 2023 Paper 12 question asked for this value too.
- **Loss**: to the left of 450, total costs are above total revenue. The gap between the lines is the loss.
- **Profit**: to the right of 450, total revenue is above total costs. At 600 covers the gap is $14,400 minus $12,600 = $1,800.
- **Margin of safety**: the distance along the output axis from break even output to current output, 450 to 600.

In the March 2022 Paper 12 question, the margin of safety was one of the labels to identify on a chart. The report said many candidates struggled with it, and profit was a common wrong answer. Profit is a vertical gap measured in dollars. Margin of safety is a horizontal distance measured in units.

### How do I complete or amend a break even chart?

Every line on the chart is straight, so two points fix each one. To complete a missing line, work out its value at zero output and at the highest output shown, mark both points and join them with a ruler.

- Total revenue: zero at the start, price × highest output at the end. For Sitara, $24 × 800 = $19,200.
- Total costs: fixed costs at the start, fixed costs + (variable cost × highest output) at the end. For Sitara, $5,400 + $9,600 = $15,000.

Label every line you draw. Old papers gave marks for naming parts of a chart. The November 2024 Paper 13 question asked for the lines labelled X and Y. In November 2025 Paper 11, X was the break even point and Y was total revenue.

## What happens to break even when price or costs change?

The syllabus names three changes: price, fixed costs and variable cost per unit. Each one moves the chart in a different way. Here they are for Sitara Crafts, one change at a time, with sales staying at 600 covers.

| Change | Break even output | Margin of safety | Profit at 600 |
|---|---|---|---|
| None (contribution $12) | 450 | 150 | $1,800 |
| Price rises to $27 (contribution $15) | 360 | 240 | $3,600 |
| Rent rises by $1,200, so fixed costs are $6,600 (contribution $12) | 550 | 50 | $600 |
| Variable cost rises to $15 (contribution $9) | 600 | 0 | $0 |

With variable cost at $15, Sitara would only just break even at its current sales.

What each change does to the chart:

- **Higher price**: the total revenue line starts at zero but rises more steeply. It crosses total costs sooner, so break even output falls and the margin of safety grows.
- **Higher fixed costs**: the fixed cost line and the total cost line both move up by the same amount and stay parallel. Break even output rises.
- **Higher variable cost per unit**: the total cost line still starts at the fixed cost figure but rises more steeply. Break even output rises. Fixed costs do not move.

A fall in any of these works the other way round. To lower break even output, a business can raise its price, cut fixed costs or cut variable cost per unit. Those are the three ways listed in the November 2023 Paper 11 mark scheme.

Two traps sit here. The November 2023 report for that paper said the weakest answers suggested lowering the price, which raises break even output. And the June 2023 Paper 12 report said some candidates wrote about the effect of higher prices on sales. On the chart itself, sales are not part of the change, because the chart assumes everything made is sold. Keep the demand argument for the evaluation, not for "what happens to the chart".

In my teaching, this is the step that needs the most practice: the formula is easy, but showing a change on the chart while keeping the other lines still is not.

## What are the benefits and limitations of break even analysis?

The syllabus asks for limitations by name, and old Paper 2 questions paired them with benefits. The June 2022 Paper 21 report said many candidates did not know break even charts well, and that many missed that the chart is "a predictive rather than an analytical tool". In other words, it is a forecast for planning, not a record of what happened.

The points below are my summary, in my own words, of those listed in the June 2022 Paper 21, November 2023 Paper 11 and June 2024 Paper 23 mark schemes, and in the November 2023 Paper 22 report.

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Tells the owner how many sales are needed to cover all costs | Assumes every unit made is sold, so no unsold inventory |
| Shows profit or loss at each level of output | Assumes one product at one price, but most businesses sell several |
| Shows the margin of safety, so the owner knows how far sales can fall | Assumes the price stays the same for every unit, with no discounts |
| Lets the owner try a decision on paper first, such as a price rise | Assumes costs and revenue rise in straight lines |
| | Some costs are hard to label as fixed or variable |
| | Fixed costs can change when the business grows or shrinks |
| | It is a forecast, so costs nobody planned for are left out |

In a Paper 2 answer, tie each point to the case. "Assumes one product" is only a strong limitation if the business in the case sells several products. The June 2022 Paper 21 report applied it that way to a bakery whose owner planned to make cakes as well as bread.

## What do examiners say students get wrong?

These come from the 0450 examiner reports. Where the June or November paper was shared with 7115, the 7115 report for that session gives the same comment.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Writing break even output as a dollar value (some candidates, June 2022 P12; a common mistake, June 2023 P12) | Give units and the product name |
| Using maximum output for margin of safety (many candidates, June 2022 P11) | Current sales minus break even output |
| Dividing instead of subtracting for margin of safety (many candidates, March 2026 P12) | It is a subtraction, and the answer is in units |
| Working out revenue or contribution when asked for margin of safety (a common mistake, March 2024 P12) | Read what the question asks for before using the table |
| Defining break even by repeating "break even" (the weakest answers, March 2024 P12) | Total revenue equals total cost |
| Calling the margin of safety profit on a chart (a common wrong answer, March 2022 P12) | Margin of safety is along the output axis, in units |
| Lowering the price to lower break even output (the weakest answers, November 2023 P11) | Raise the price, or cut fixed or variable costs |
| Writing about the effect of a higher price on sales in a question about the chart (some candidates, June 2023 P12) | The chart assumes all output is sold |

## Practice questions with answers

Each question is my own. Try it on paper before reading the answer.

### Practice question 1

> Bilal runs a food stall selling paratha rolls. Each roll sells for $2.50 and costs $1.10 in ingredients and packaging. His fixed costs are $1,000 a week. He sells 1,000 rolls a week.
>
> (a) How many rolls must Bilal sell each week to break even? Show your working. [2]
>
> (b) By how many rolls could weekly sales fall before Bilal makes a loss? [2]

**Answer**

(a) Contribution = $2.50 minus $1.10 = $1.40. Break even output = $1,000 ÷ $1.40 = 714.29, so 715 rolls.

You cannot sell part of a roll, and 714 rolls would leave a tiny loss, so I round up. That is my advice, not a mark scheme rule. Show 714.29 in your working too, so the examiner sees both.

(b) This is the margin of safety. 1,000 minus 715 = 285 rolls.

### Practice question 2

> Shine Laundry washes and irons school uniforms, charging per load. On its break even chart, the fixed cost line is flat at $2,400 a month. The total cost and total revenue lines cross at 200 loads and $5,000. Shine currently washes 320 loads a month.
>
> (a) Use the chart to calculate the price per load and the variable cost per load. [4]
>
> (b) The landlord raises Shine's rent by $360 a month. Outline two effects on Shine's break even chart. [4]

**Answer**

(a) Price = $5,000 ÷ 200 = $25 per load. At break even, total costs are also $5,000. Total variable costs = $5,000 minus $2,400 = $2,600, so variable cost = $2,600 ÷ 200 = $13 per load.

Check: contribution is $25 minus $13 = $12, and $2,400 ÷ $12 = 200 loads. It matches the chart.

(b)

- The fixed cost and total cost lines move up by the same amount **[k]**, from $2,400 to $2,760 at zero output **[app]**.
- Break even output rises **[k]**, from 200 to $2,760 ÷ $12 = 230 loads, so the margin of safety falls from 120 to 90 loads **[app]**.

The total revenue line does not move, because rent is a fixed cost.

### Practice question 3

> Zain prints and sells phone covers. Each cover sells for $8 and costs $3 to make. His fixed costs are $2,700 a month and he sells 700 covers a month. He wants to lower his break even output and has two options:
>
> Option A: move to a cheaper unit, which cuts fixed costs to $2,250 a month.
>
> Option B: use a cheaper supplier, which cuts the cost of each cover to $2.60.
>
> Which option is better for Zain? Support your choice with break even calculations. [6]

**Answer**

Now: contribution $5, break even output $2,700 ÷ $5 = 540 covers, margin of safety 160.

| | Option A | Option B |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution per cover | $5.00 | $5.40 |
| Fixed costs | $2,250 | $2,700 |
| Break even output | 450 covers | 500 covers |
| Margin of safety at 700 | 250 covers | 200 covers |
| Profit at 700 | $1,250 | $1,080 |

Option A cuts break even output by 90 covers, against 40 for Option B, and gives $170 more profit a month. Option B keeps Zain in the same unit, but cheaper materials could lower the quality of the covers and lead to complaints.

Zain should choose Option A, because it lowers break even output the most and gives the highest profit. This depends on sales staying at 700. If the cheaper unit is in a quieter area and sales fall below 450, Zain would make a loss.

How I would mark it, as my own judgement and not a Cambridge scheme: 1 mark for each correct break even output, 2 for comparing the options using the figures and the case, and 2 for a decision that says why the other option was rejected.

## FAQ

### What is the break even formula for IGCSE and O Level Business?

Break even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit. Contribution per unit is selling price minus variable cost per unit. The answer is a number of units.

### How do you calculate the margin of safety?

Subtract break even output from current sales. If a business sells 600 units and breaks even at 450, its margin of safety is 150 units.

### Do I have to draw a break even chart in 0264 or 7081?

The syllabus asks you to complete or amend a simple chart and to interpret one. That means adding or moving lines on a chart you are given, and reading values from it.

### Why is break even output not in dollars?

It counts products, not money. The June 2022 Paper 12 mark scheme allowed only 1 of 2 marks when a candidate added a $ sign to break even output. The March 2026 Paper 12 scheme did the same for a margin of safety. Break even revenue is the dollar value at the same point.

### What happens to break even if fixed costs rise?

The fixed cost and total cost lines move up, total revenue stays where it is, and break even output rises. The margin of safety falls.
