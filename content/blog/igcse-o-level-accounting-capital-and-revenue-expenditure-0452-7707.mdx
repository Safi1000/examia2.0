Capital expenditure is spending that adds to non-current assets: buying them, getting them ready to use, or making them bigger or better. Revenue expenditure is money spent on the day to day running of the business. Capital expenditure goes in the statement of financial position. Revenue expenditure is an expense that reduces profit. Mixing them up makes both profit and the value of non-current assets wrong.

## What does the 0452 and 7707 syllabus ask you to know?

Capital and revenue expenditure and receipts is topic 4.1 in both the IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. You must be able to:

- tell capital expenditure from revenue expenditure, and account for each
- tell capital receipts from revenue receipts, and account for each
- identify and calculate the effect on **profit** of treating an item the wrong way
- identify and calculate the effect on **asset values** of treating an item the wrong way

It is tested on both papers. Paper 1 is 40 multiple choice questions, and the 2027 specimen Paper 1 includes a question that turns on whether a trial balance reveals capital expenditure treated as revenue expenditure. On Paper 2, recent questions have asked you to tick a classification table, explain the difference in words, or adjust a figure inside an income statement. You can tick off each point on the [0452 syllabus checklist](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452).

## What is the difference between capital and revenue expenditure?

The November 2025 0452 Paper 23 mark scheme gave one mark for each of four short definitions. Here they are in my own words:

| Term | Meaning | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Capital expenditure | Spending that buys a non-current asset, or makes one bigger or better | Non-current assets in the statement of financial position |
| Revenue expenditure | The everyday costs of keeping the business going | An expense in the statement of profit or loss |
| Capital receipt | Money coming in from anything other than the usual trading | Statement of financial position (capital, liabilities or disposal of an asset) |
| Revenue receipt | Income earned from the usual trading, such as sales | Income in the statement of profit or loss |

The 2027 specimen papers call the profit statement the statement of profit or loss. Past papers up to 2026 call it the income statement. Both mean the same thing here.

A simple test I teach: **will this spending give the business a benefit for more than one year, by adding to an asset?** If yes, it is capital. If it keeps an existing asset working, or is used up in running the business, it is revenue. The November 2022 0452 Paper 21 mark scheme gave credit for a time based difference of this kind as well as for the non-current asset idea.

### Which costs of buying a non-current asset are capital?

Every cost needed to get the asset to the business and ready to use is capital. That includes:

- the purchase price
- delivery or carriage on the asset
- installation, and testing before use
- legal fees on buying property
- improvements and extensions that add to what the asset can do

These are revenue, even though they relate to a non-current asset:

- insurance
- repairs and maintenance that keep the asset in its present condition
- fuel, running costs and annual licences
- depreciation itself, which is an expense

The June 2022 0452 report (Paper 22) said that where candidates had gaps, they were about installation and delivery, which form part of the capital cost. A June 2022 Paper 11 multiple choice question (0452 and 7707, Question 14) rested on the same idea, and its report listed the machine, delivery and installation as capital, and insurance and maintenance as revenue.

### What about receipts?

| Capital receipts | Revenue receipts |
|---|---|
| Capital introduced by the owner | Sales of goods |
| A loan received | Commission received |
| Proceeds from selling a non-current asset | Rent received |
| Money from an issue of shares | Interest received on a bank deposit |

A capital receipt never goes in the statement of profit or loss as income. Only a profit or loss on disposal does, not the full proceeds.

## Worked example 1: classifying the costs of a new machine

Tariq runs a print shop. These are his payments in May. The figures are my own.

| Payment | $ | Capital or revenue? |
|---|---:|---|
| New printing machine | 18 500 | Capital |
| Carriage on the new machine | 350 | Capital |
| Engineer's fee to install the new machine | 650 | Capital |
| Scanning unit added to his old printer, so it can now scan | 1 100 | Capital |
| One year's maintenance contract for the new machine | 900 | Revenue |
| Ink and paper | 420 | Revenue |
| Repair to a roller on the old printer | 280 | Revenue |

**Capital expenditure** = 18 500 + 350 + 650 + 1 100 = **$20 600**.

**Revenue expenditure** = 900 + 420 + 280 = **$1 600**.

The new machine is recorded at $19 500 (18 500 + 350 + 650). The scanning unit improves the old printer, because it can now do something it could not do before, so its $1 100 is added to that printer's cost. The roller repair only puts the old printer back to how it was, so it is revenue.

The maintenance contract is the trap. It is about the new machine, but it pays for one year of upkeep, so it is an expense.

### How are these entries made?

| Item | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| New machine, carriage, installation ($19 500) | Machinery account | Bank (or the supplier, if on credit) |
| Scanning unit ($1 100) | Machinery account | Bank |
| Maintenance, ink and paper, repair | Maintenance, stationery and repairs expense accounts | Bank |

The June 2022 0452 report (Paper 21) described a journal entry for equipment bought on credit. The equipment account should have been debited, not purchases, and the supplier credited. Purchases is only for goods bought to resell.

## How do errors change profit and assets?

The syllabus asks for the effect on profit and on asset values. Learn the four directions:

| Error | Effect on profit | Effect on non-current assets |
|---|---|---|
| Capital expenditure treated as revenue expenditure | Understated (expenses too high) | Understated |
| Revenue expenditure treated as capital expenditure | Overstated (expenses too low) | Overstated |
| Capital receipt treated as revenue receipt | Overstated | None for a loan or new capital (a liability or capital is understated instead) |
| Revenue receipt treated as capital receipt | Understated | None (a liability or capital is overstated instead) |

The March 2026 0452 report (Paper 12, Question 15) explained the first row: expenses overstated, profit understated and non-current assets understated. It also said two of the wrong options were common choices.

These errors are **errors of principle**: the amount is right, but it is in the wrong type of account. Debits still equal credits, so a trial balance does not reveal them. The 2027 specimen Paper 1 tests exactly this. My [correction of errors guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-correction-of-errors-suspense-account) covers errors of principle with the other types.

### Do not forget depreciation

When capital expenditure is moved out of an expense account, the asset's cost goes up, and so does depreciation. The November 2022 0452 report (Paper 22, Question 1) described exactly this. Capital spending had been left inside repairs. Candidates who did not add it back to machinery before calculating depreciation got the wrong charge, and many made this mistake.

## Worked example 2: correcting a draft profit

Tariq's draft profit for the year is **$38 900**. He depreciates machinery at 20% a year on cost, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase. These errors are found. The figures are my own.

1. The scanning unit of $1 100 was debited to the repairs account.
2. The maintenance contract of $900 was debited to the machinery account.
3. A loan of $5 000 from Tariq's brother was credited to the sales account.

| Adjustment | Effect on profit $ | Running total $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Draft profit | | 38 900 |
| 1. Remove scanning unit from repairs | + 1 100 | 40 000 |
| 1. Add depreciation on it (20% × 1 100) | − 220 | 39 780 |
| 2. Charge maintenance as an expense | − 900 | 38 880 |
| 2. Remove depreciation charged on it (20% × 900) | + 180 | 39 060 |
| 3. Remove loan from sales | − 5 000 | 34 060 |
| **Corrected profit** | | **34 060** |

Check the logic of each step:

- **Error 1** made expenses too high, so profit goes **up** when it is corrected. The machine now carries $220 more depreciation, so profit comes down by that much.
- **Error 2** made expenses too low, so profit goes **down**. The $900 had been depreciated as if it were machinery, so the $180 of depreciation is taken back out.
- **Error 3** treated a capital receipt as income, so profit goes **down** by the full $5 000. The loan becomes a liability.

**Effect on the statement of financial position.** Machinery at cost rises by $200 (plus 1 100, minus 900). Accumulated depreciation rises by $40 (plus 220, minus 180). So the net book value of machinery rises by **$160**. Non-current liabilities rise by **$5 000** if the loan is repayable after more than a year.

<Tip title="Write the direction before the number">
For every error, write "expenses too high" or "income too high" first. Then the correction is just the opposite. This one line stops most direction mistakes.
</Tip>

## How is capital and revenue expenditure marked on Paper 2?

From 2027, Paper 2 is five compulsory questions for 100 marks in 1 hour 45 minutes. In the schemes I read, this topic was one part of a longer question. These are the forms I found in the 0452 schemes from 2022 to 2026:

| Question form | Example session | How the marks were given |
|---|---|---|
| Tick each item as capital or revenue | June 2022 Paper 22; June 2023 Paper 21; June 2024 Paper 21 | Often one mark for a pair of items, so one wrong tick loses the mark for its partner too |
| Classify items into four columns, including receipts | November 2025 Paper 23 | One mark per item |
| Explain the difference and why it matters | November 2022 Paper 21 (3 marks) | 1 mark for the difference, up to 2 for the effects of getting it wrong |
| State the meaning of a term | March 2024 Paper 22 (revenue receipts, 1 mark) | One precise sentence |
| Correct a figure inside an income statement | November 2022 Paper 22 | A repairs figure with capital spending removed, then depreciation on the new total |

In the November 2022 Paper 21 scheme, the "why it matters" marks went to naming a specific effect: profit understated or overstated, and non-current assets understated or overstated. Paper 23 carried the same question, and the report on Paper 23 said that answers limited to "profits will be incorrect" were not enough. Say which way profit and assets are wrong.

### Materiality: when a small asset is treated as an expense

The June 2022 0452 Paper 22 scheme credited the idea of materiality for a related question. Recording a very cheap item, such as a stapler, as a non-current asset costs more effort than it is worth, so a business may treat it as revenue expenditure. The same amount can matter to a small business and not to a large one. Materiality is one of the accounting concepts in topic 7.1, explained in my [accounting theory questions guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-theory-questions).

## Where do students lose marks on capital and revenue expenditure?

These come from 0452 and 7707 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026. The middle column is my summary.

| Mistake | What to do instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Treating delivery or installation as revenue | They are part of the cost of the asset | 0452 June 2022 Paper 22, Question 4(b)(i); 0452 March 2026 Paper 22, Question 2(d) (some items, especially delivery cost and insurance) |
| Treating insurance or petrol as capital | Running costs are revenue, even for a new vehicle | 0452 June 2025 Papers 21 and 23, Question 2(c) (a large number) |
| Saying capital expenditure is money spent from the owner's capital | It is about what the money buys, not where it came from | 0452 November 2022 Paper 23, Question 1 (a significant number) |
| Calling any expense on a non-current asset capital | Repairs and insurance on an asset are revenue | 0452 November 2022 Paper 23, Question 1 (some) |
| Saying only "profit will be wrong" | Say whether profit and assets are overstated or understated | 0452 November 2022 Paper 23, Question 1 |
| Getting the direction of the effect wrong | Write "expenses too high" or "too low" first | 0452 March 2026 Paper 12, Question 15 |
| Not recalculating depreciation after moving capital out of repairs | Add it to the asset, then depreciate the new total | 0452 November 2022 Paper 22, Question 1 (many) |
| Treating a large purchase as an expense when giving advice | A new non-current asset raises depreciation, not this year's expenses by its full cost | 0452 March 2026 Paper 22, Question 2(f) |

## Can you answer these practice questions?

All three questions are my own. Try each before reading the answer.

**1.** Nadia runs a café. Classify each item as capital expenditure, revenue expenditure, capital receipt or revenue receipt. [5]

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| A new coffee machine | Capital expenditure |
| Cleaning the coffee machine each month | Revenue expenditure |
| Proceeds from selling an old fridge | Capital receipt |
| Rent received for letting a room upstairs | Revenue receipt |
| Building an outdoor seating area | Capital expenditure |

**2.** Nadia's draft profit was $21 400. A payment of $3 000 for new kitchen shelving was debited to the repairs account. Shelving is depreciated at 10% on cost, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase. Calculate the corrected profit. [3]

**Answer.** Repairs were too high, so add back $3 000: 21 400 + 3 000 = 24 400. Depreciation on the shelving = 10% × 3 000 = $300. Corrected profit = 24 400 − 300 = **$24 100**.

**3.** Explain the effect on the statement of financial position of the error in question 2. [2]

**Answer.** Non-current assets were understated. Once corrected, shelving at cost rises by $3 000 and accumulated depreciation by $300, so non-current assets rise by **$2 700** at net book value.

## FAQ

### Is carriage on a new machine capital or revenue expenditure?

Capital. It is a cost of getting the asset to the business, so it is added to the cost of the machine. Carriage on goods for resale is different: that is carriage inwards, an expense.

### Are repairs capital or revenue expenditure?

Repairs that keep an asset in its present condition are revenue. Spending that improves or extends the asset, so it can do more than before, is capital.

### Is a loan a capital receipt?

Yes. A loan is not income from trading. It is recorded as a liability, and only the interest on it is an expense.

### Does a capital and revenue error affect the trial balance?

No. It is an error of principle. The amount is entered on the correct side of the wrong type of account, so the trial balance still agrees.

### What happens to profit if capital expenditure is treated as revenue expenditure?

Expenses are too high, so profit for the year is understated, and non-current assets are understated too.

### Is topic 4.1 the same in 0452 and 7707?

Yes. The 2027 to 2029 syllabuses list the same six points for both.
