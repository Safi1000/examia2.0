Build every 9708 essay around how its marks are split. The 8 mark part (a) gives up to 3 marks for knowledge, 3 for analysis and 2 for evaluation, with the last mark kept for a justified conclusion. The 12 mark part (b) gives 8 marks for knowledge and analysis and 4 for evaluation. A one sided answer gets no evaluation marks.

## How are the 9708 Paper 2 essays marked?

Paper 2 is the AS Level data response and essays paper. The paper facts below are from the 9708 syllabus for 2026 to 2028. The 8 and 12 mark split is from the question papers.

| Paper 2 fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Time and marks | 2 hours, 60 marks |
| Weight | 67% of the AS Level |
| Section A | One compulsory data response question, 20 marks |
| Section B | One essay from a choice of two, mainly microeconomics, 20 marks |
| Section C | One essay from a choice of two, mainly macroeconomics, 20 marks |
| Essay parts | (a) 8 marks and (b) 12 marks |

Evaluation carries more weight on this paper than anywhere else in AS Economics. The syllabus gives evaluation 30% of Paper 2, against 13% of Paper 1. Knowledge and understanding is 33% and analysis is 37%.

The two essay parts are marked in different ways. Part (a) is point based: each correct point earns a mark, up to a limit for each skill. Part (b) is levels based: the examiner reads the whole answer and places it in a level.

| Part | Knowledge and analysis | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| (a) 8 marks | Up to 3 for knowledge and understanding, up to 3 for analysis | Up to 2. The second mark is reserved for a justified conclusion |
| (b) 12 marks | 8 marks in three levels: Level 3 is 6 to 8, Level 2 is 3 to 5, Level 1 is 1 to 2 | 4 marks in two levels: Level 2 is 3 to 4, Level 1 is 1 to 2 |

The same split appears in every Paper 22 mark scheme I checked, from June 2023 to June 2026. If you want the data response questions and a full time plan for the paper, read my [guide to scoring in 9708 Paper 2 and 9609 Paper 3](/blog/9708-paper-2-and-9609-paper-3-how-to-score). This article goes deeper into the two essay parts only.

## What does a part (a) question actually ask?

Recent part (a) questions nearly always have two tasks joined by "and". The first asks you to explain something. The second asks you to "consider" whether, or how far, something is true.

The June 2026 mark scheme shows how the marks follow the two tasks. In the question on real and nominal GDP, the 3 knowledge marks were for explaining the two measures. The 3 analysis marks and the 2 evaluation marks were for the second task, on whether growth needs more resources.

This tells you how to plan. The "explain" task is worth 3 marks. The "consider" task is worth 5. Many students do the opposite: a long explanation, then two rushed lines on the second task.

## How do I structure an 8 mark answer?

Use three blocks, in this order.

1. **Knowledge (3 marks).** Define every key term in the question precisely. If the question asks for a diagram, draw it fully labelled and say what it shows. In June 2026, a correctly labelled diagram earned one of the knowledge marks on its own.
2. **Analysis (3 marks).** Answer the "consider" task with reasons. Explain one way the statement is true, then one way it is not, each as a chain: cause, then effect, then why it matters.
3. **Evaluation (2 marks).** Say what the answer depends on, then give a one or two sentence conclusion that uses the exact words of the question.

Three paragraphs is enough. Each paragraph has one job, so the examiner can find each mark.

### A marked 8 mark sample

This is my own question and my own answer, in the style of recent papers. The figures are invented for the example. The tags show where I would give each mark: K for knowledge, An for analysis, Ev for evaluation.

**Question:** With the help of a diagram, explain how a subsidy to producers affects the market for a product and consider the extent to which consumers gain the full value of the subsidy. [8]

**Answer**

A subsidy is a payment from the government to producers for each unit they make. It lowers their costs of production **[K]**. On a demand and supply diagram, with price on the vertical axis and quantity on the horizontal axis, the supply curve shifts down and to the right, from S1 to S2. The vertical distance between S1 and S2 is the subsidy per unit **[K]**. The equilibrium moves from P1 Q1 to P2 Q2: price falls and the quantity traded rises **[K]**.

Suppose the subsidy is $2 per unit and the price falls from $10 to $8.50. Consumers gain $1.50 per unit. Producers now receive $8.50 from the buyer plus $2 from the government, which is $10.50, so they gain $0.50 per unit **[An]**. The split depends on price elasticity. When demand is price inelastic, consumers do not cut back much as price changes, so producers pass most of the cost saving on and consumers gain most of the subsidy **[An]**. When demand is price elastic, a small fall in price raises sales a lot, so producers keep more of the subsidy as a higher price per unit received **[An]**.

Consumers gain the full $2 only in an extreme case, such as perfectly inelastic demand or perfectly elastic supply **[Ev]**. In most markets both curves slope normally, so consumers gain only part of the subsidy. They gain most when demand is more inelastic than supply, as it often is for basic foods **[Ev]**.

**How I would mark it**

| Skill | Marks | Where they came from |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | 3 out of 3 | Definition, labelled shift of supply, new equilibrium |
| Analysis | 3 out of 3 | Worked split of the subsidy, inelastic case, elastic case |
| Evaluation | 2 out of 2 | The extreme case, then a justified conclusion on "full value" |
| Total | 8 out of 8 | |

Check the sums: $1.50 to consumers plus $0.50 to producers is the full $2 subsidy. In the exam the diagram must be drawn and labelled, not only described. The second knowledge mark depends on it.

Notice what a typical answer does instead. It draws the diagram, explains it well, then writes "consumers benefit from lower prices". That earns the 3 knowledge marks, perhaps 1 for analysis, and nothing for evaluation. That is 4 out of 8 from a student who knew the topic.

<Tip title="Find the second task first">
Before you write, underline the words after "and consider". If your plan does not show at least two lines of reasons and a conclusion on that task, you have planned for 3 marks.
</Tip>

## How do I structure a 12 mark answer?

The 12 mark part starts with "Assess". The syllabus defines assess as making an informed judgement. So the whole answer is built to reach one.

Use this five paragraph plan.

1. **Define the key terms** in the question in one or two sentences. Do not write a general introduction.
2. **The case for** the statement: one or two developed chains of reasoning, with a diagram where it helps.
3. **The case against**: one or two developed chains, as detailed as the case for.
4. **Weigh them**: what does the answer depend on? Which argument matters more, and when?
5. **Conclude**: answer the exact question, with a reason.

Paragraphs 2 and 3 earn the Level 3 knowledge and analysis marks (6 to 8). Paragraphs 4 and 5 earn the Level 2 evaluation marks (3 to 4).

Balance is not optional. The June 2026 mark scheme states that a one sided response cannot earn any evaluation marks, and the March 2025 examiner report expects its analysis to stop around the middle of Level 2. That caps a one sided answer at about 4 out of 12.

### What does Level 3 analysis look like?

The Level 3 descriptor asks for analysis that is "developed and detailed", with diagrams that are accurate and fully explained. In practice, developed means each point runs as a chain of at least three links: what changes, what that causes, and what that means for the question.

| Undeveloped (Level 1 or 2) | Developed (Level 3) |
|---|---|
| Higher interest rates reduce inflation. | Higher interest rates raise the cost of borrowing, so households spend less on goods bought on credit and firms delay investment. Consumption and investment are parts of aggregate demand, so AD falls, which reduces the pressure on prices. |

### A marked 12 mark sample

Again, the question and answer are my own. Part (b) is marked by levels, so here the tags only show which skill each part of the answer displays.

**Question:** Assess whether raising interest rates is always an effective way for a central bank to bring down inflation. [12]

**Answer**

Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level. An increase in interest rates is contractionary monetary policy, because it raises the cost of borrowing and the reward for saving **[K]**.

Higher interest rates can reduce demand pull inflation, which is caused by aggregate demand (AD) growing faster than the economy can produce **[K]**. Borrowing becomes more expensive, so households cut spending on items bought on credit, such as cars and houses. Saving becomes more attractive, so consumption falls further **[An]**. Firms face higher costs of finance, so some investment projects no longer look profitable and are delayed **[An]**. On an AD/AS diagram, with the price level on the vertical axis and real output on the horizontal axis, AD shifts left from AD1 to AD2. The price level falls from P1 to P2 compared with where it would have been, and real output falls from Y1 to Y2 **[An]**.

However, higher interest rates are less effective against cost push inflation, which is caused by rising costs such as imported fuel **[K]**. Here the aggregate supply curve has shifted left. Raising interest rates shifts AD left as well, so output falls twice over and unemployment rises, while the cause of the inflation, the higher fuel cost, is untouched **[An]**. Higher interest rates can even add to firms' costs where they rely on borrowed money **[An]**.

The effect also depends on how much people borrow and on time lags **[Ev]**. Where most loans are at fixed rates, or households borrow little, a rise in interest rates changes spending only slowly. Monetary policy may take many months to work, so a rise aimed at today's inflation may hit an economy that is already slowing **[Ev]**.

Overall, raising interest rates is not always effective. It is likely to bring down inflation when the cause is excess demand, because it acts directly on consumption and investment. It is much less effective when the cause is rising costs, where it reduces inflation mainly by causing lower output and higher unemployment. How effective it is depends first on the cause of the inflation, and second on how sensitive spending is to borrowing costs **[Ev]**.

**How I would mark it**

| Table | Level and marks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge and analysis (out of 8) | Level 3, 7 marks | Accurate terms, developed chains on both sides, AD/AS analysis. Drawn and labelled in the exam, the diagram could lift it to 8 |
| Evaluation (out of 4) | Level 2, 4 marks | Judgement based on the cause of inflation, supported by borrowing and time lag points, answering "always" |
| Total | 11 out of 12 | |

The conclusion does not repeat the paragraphs. It ranks them: the cause of inflation matters most, sensitivity to borrowing comes second. That ranking is what turns a summary into a judgement.

## What kind of evaluation earns marks?

The syllabus describes evaluation as recognising assumptions and limits, weighing the strengths and weaknesses of arguments, and reaching reasoned judgements. The level descriptors for part (b) separate a vague or general conclusion (Level 1, 1 to 2 marks) from a justified one that addresses the specific question (Level 2, 3 to 4 marks).

These are the "it depends on" points that work across most AS topics. Each one appears in recent 9708 mark schemes or examiner reports.

| It depends on | Use it when |
|---|---|
| Price or income elasticity | Any tax, subsidy, price control or change in income. The June 2026 mark scheme rewarded linking the change in price to YED |
| The cause of the problem | Inflation, unemployment, market failure. Demand side and supply side causes need different policies |
| Short run or long run | Price changes, government spending, growth. Effects often differ over time |
| The size of the change | A small tax or a price control close to equilibrium may change very little |
| The type of economy | A government in a lower income economy may not be able to afford the same policies |
| Side effects and opportunity cost | Any government policy: what else could the money have done? |

Two habits turn these into marks. First, attach the "depends on" point to your own analysis, not to the topic in general. Second, finish with a sentence that answers the question's exact words: "to a large extent", "only when", "not always, because".

<Tip title="Write the judgement first">
Before your first paragraph, write your one sentence answer to the question in your plan. Then make every paragraph lead towards it. If time runs out, the judgement is already decided, and you can write it in two lines.
</Tip>

## Where do students lose marks on 9708 essays?

These come from the 9708 Paper 2 examiner reports for June 2023, November 2023, March 2025 and March 2026, and the June 2026 mark scheme.

| Mistake | What the examiners reported | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| One sided part (b) | Analysis held around the middle of Level 2, and no evaluation marks (March 2025) | Give both sides equal depth |
| Summary instead of judgement | Evaluation needs a judgement, not a repeat of earlier points (November 2023) | Rank the arguments and say why |
| No evaluation in part (a) | Relatively few candidates attempted the "consider" task with any evaluation (June 2023) | Give the second task and the conclusion more than half of your part (a) time |
| Judging the wrong thing | Some evaluated the most significant impact when asked for the most immediate (June 2023) | Copy the question's key word into your conclusion |
| Wrong or unlabelled diagram | Movement along a curve drawn instead of a shift (November 2023); AD/AS axes labelled price and quantity (June 2023) | Label axes, curves and equilibrium every time |
| Diagram not explained | Diagrams should be linked to the explanation, not left on their own (March 2026) | Refer to the diagram by its labels in your writing |
| Blended definitions | Public good terms run together instead of each defined separately (March 2025) | Define each term in its own sentence |
| Half the question answered | Answers that covered only the disadvantages when benefits were also asked (March 2026) | Underline every task in the question |

One more point from the March 2025 report: a correct diagram, formula or concept with no explanation is credited as knowledge only. Analysis marks need the explanation that follows it.

## How long should I spend on each part?

Paper 2 gives you 2 hours for 60 marks, so about 2 minutes per mark. Each essay is worth 20 marks, which is about 40 minutes. I tell my students to give part (a) about 15 minutes and part (b) about 25, including a short plan for each. That split is my teaching advice, not a Cambridge rule. The March 2025 report did note that candidates still tend to spend too long on the 2 mark data response questions.

## Practice questions

Write your plan first, then the answer. Check your plan against the answers below before you write in full.

**1.** Explain what is meant by a minimum price and consider the extent to which a minimum price set in a market for an agricultural product benefits producers. [8]

**2.** Assess the extent to which an increase in government spending will reduce unemployment in an economy. [12]

**3.** A student ends a 12 mark answer on a tax on sugary drinks like this: "In conclusion, the tax has advantages and disadvantages, so it may or may not reduce consumption." Rewrite the conclusion so it could reach Level 2 for evaluation.

### Answers

**1.** Knowledge: a minimum price is a legal price floor set above the equilibrium price; diagram with the floor above equilibrium, showing quantity supplied greater than quantity demanded (excess supply). Analysis: producers who sell gain a higher price per unit; but quantity demanded falls, so some output goes unsold, and the gain depends on price elasticity of demand. Evaluation: producers benefit overall only if the government buys the surplus or demand is inelastic; a justified conclusion on "the extent to which".

**2.** Case for: higher government spending raises AD, firms need more workers, cyclical (demand deficient) unemployment falls, shown by AD shifting right on an AD/AS diagram. Case against: it does little for structural or frictional unemployment; if the economy is near full capacity, it mainly raises the price level; it may need higher taxes or borrowing later. Judgement: effective against demand deficient unemployment when there is spare capacity, much less so against structural unemployment, where training or other supply side policy fits better.

**3.** A Level 2 version: "The tax is likely to reduce consumption only a little in the short run, because demand for sugary drinks is price inelastic for regular buyers. It works better in the long run, as habits change and cheaper substitutes such as water are available. So the tax reduces consumption to a small extent, unless it is large or combined with information about health." It names what the result depends on, weighs short run against long run, and answers "the extent".

## FAQ

### Is there a fixed structure for 9708 essays?

Cambridge does not set one. The mark scheme does fix how marks are split, though: 3, 3 and 2 for part (a), and 8 plus 4 for part (b). A structure that gives each skill its own paragraph makes those marks easy to find.

### How many points do I need in a 12 mark answer?

There is no set number. Two developed points on each side, with a judgement, is usually enough for Level 3. Four shallow points on one side will not get past Level 2.

### Do I need a diagram in every essay?

When the question says "with the help of a diagram", yes, and the diagram earns marks. When it does not, the syllabus encourages clearly labelled diagrams where they help. An unexplained diagram adds little.

### Can I earn evaluation marks without a conclusion?

In part (a), you can earn 1 evaluation mark, but the second is reserved for a justified conclusion. In part (b), the top evaluation level needs a justified conclusion that answers the specific question.

### Should I evaluate in every paragraph or only at the end?

Either works if the judgement is justified. Short "this depends on" comments after each point help, but you still need a final conclusion that weighs them and answers the exact question.

### Do I need real world examples?

The Level 3 descriptor asks for explanations supported by examples where appropriate. A short, relevant example helps. A long story about one country does not replace analysis.
