Recruitment is how a business finds people to apply for a job: job description, person specification, then a job advertisement. Selection is how it chooses one of them: CVs, application forms, references, tests and interviews. Training then builds the employee's skills through induction, on-the-job or off-the-job training. In IGCSE Business 0264 and O Level Business 7081 this is syllabus section 2.1.

## What does the 0264 and 7081 syllabus include?

Section 2.1, human resource management, has three parts. The wording is the same in both syllabuses. Here is what each part asks of you, in my words.

| Syllabus part | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| 2.1.1 Recruiting and selecting employees | Know the stages of recruitment, the selection methods, the pros and cons of internal and external recruitment, and recommend who to employ |
| 2.1.2 Employment contracts and legal controls | Know what a contract contains, why it helps both sides, and the effect of laws on dismissal, discrimination, health and safety and minimum wage |
| 2.1.3 Training | Explain why training matters, and the pros and cons of induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training |

This article covers 2.1.1 and 2.1.3 in full, with a short section on contracts.

The syllabus gives examples of job advertisements that include employment agencies and online recruitment. Its examples of selection methods are CVs (also called resumés), application forms, references, testing or assessment centres, and interviews.

Section 2.1 is often tested. On our past paper pages, 64 questions from the old 0450 and 7115 papers of 2022 to 2026 carry the 2.1 tag, as main or second tag (0450: 40, 7115: 24). I teach from the new syllabus and point you to the old papers for practice.

## What are the stages of recruitment?

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Identify the vacancy | Someone leaves, or the business grows |
| Job description | A document setting out the job title, duties and responsibilities |
| Person specification | A document setting out the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities the right person needs |
| Job advertisement | The vacancy is advertised inside the business, or outside it in newspapers, online or through an employment agency |
| Applications and shortlisting | Applicants send CVs or application forms; the best are shortlisted |
| Selection | Interviews, tests and references; the job is offered |

**A job description is about the job. A person specification is about the person.** The June 2023 Paper 11 report said some candidates confused the two, and that the weakest answers simply turned the words round, such as "a description of the job", which earned nothing.

The March 2026 Paper 12 question asked for four stages, 1 mark each. The report listed four traps there:

- Treating **training** as a stage of recruitment. It is not; it comes after the person is employed. The report called this a common misconception.
- Describing an activity instead of naming the stage, such as "listing the tasks they will need to do" instead of job description.
- Listing things to weigh up when choosing who to employ, instead of stages.
- Giving the same stage twice. The report gave job specification and person specification as an example of one stage written twice.

## What is the difference between recruitment and selection?

Recruitment attracts applicants. Selection chooses between them. Mix them up and you lose the marks: the March 2024 Paper 12 report said the most common mistake was writing about selection methods when the question asked about recruitment. The November 2023 Paper 13 report said some candidates discussed interviewing face to face or online when the question was about a recruitment website.

### What selection methods can a business use?

| Method | Advantage | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| CV or resumé | Quick summary of qualifications and experience | Written by the applicant, so it may flatter them |
| Application form | Every applicant answers the same questions, so they are easy to compare | Takes applicants time; some good ones may not apply |
| References | A past employer or teacher confirms what the applicant says | Referees are chosen by the applicant |
| Tests or assessment centre | Shows real skill, such as typing speed or teamwork | Costs time and money to run |
| Interview | Lets the business ask follow up questions and judge communication | Takes managers' time; some people interview well but work badly |

In the June 2022 Paper 13 question on interviews, the report said the best answers pointed out that interviews allow in depth questions, or that they take a lot of time when a large business has many applicants. Vague words like "time" or "cost" needed more explanation to earn the mark.

## Should a business recruit internally or externally?

**Internal recruitment** fills the job with someone already working for the business, often by promotion. **External recruitment** brings in someone from outside.

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Advantages | Already knows the business, so needs no induction; the manager already knows their work; cheaper to advertise; a promotion can motivate other staff | Wider choice of applicants; brings new skills and ideas |
| Disadvantages | Leaves another vacancy to fill; smaller choice; colleagues not chosen may be jealous; no new ideas | Costs more to advertise and select; needs induction; the business knows less about the person |

Two points from the reports are worth learning:

1. **Internal recruits can still need training.** The June 2022 Paper 11 report said many candidates wrote that internal recruits need no training, missing that a promotion usually needs some. They may skip induction but still need training for the new role.
2. **Internal recruitment creates another vacancy.** The November 2023 Paper 22 report said only a minority of stronger candidates saw this.

Read which one the question asks about. The June 2025 Paper 11 report said some candidates explained why external recruitment was better than internal when that was not the question. The June 2025 Paper 12 report said some wrote the disadvantages of external recruitment instead of the advantages of internal.

## How do I recommend who to employ?

The syllabus asks you to "recommend and justify who to employ in a given situation". On the old Paper 2 this was a 12 mark question with three applicants: June 2022 Paper 22 (a marketing manager) and March 2026 Paper 22 (a café manager).

Here is my own example, with a full answer plan.

> FixFast repairs mobile phones in eight shops across Karachi. It needs a supervisor for a new shop. Customers often complain about long waits. There are three applicants.

| | Hina | Omar | Sana |
|---|---|---|---|
| Now | FixFast technician for 4 years | Supervisor at a rival repair chain for 3 years | New business graduate |
| Repair skills | Excellent | Good | None |
| Managed people | Never | Team of 5 | Led a university society |
| Expected pay | $600 a month | $850 a month | $550 a month |
| Can start | In 2 weeks, once replaced | In 2 months (notice period) | Now |

**Step 1: discuss each applicant, with the case.**

- **Hina** knows FixFast's repair system, so she needs no induction and can give new technicians on-the-job training. Her pay is $250 a month less than Omar's. But she has never managed people, so she would need management training. Her move also leaves a technician vacancy to fill.
- **Omar** has run a team for 3 years, which matters in a shop with long waits. He needs little training. But he costs $850 a month, the most of the three, and cannot start for 2 months, so the new shop opens without a supervisor.
- **Sana** is the cheapest at $550 and can start now. But with no repair skills and no work experience, she would need the most training before she could handle angry customers or check repairs.

**Step 2: decide, and say why the others lose.**

> I recommend Hina. Long waits are the main problem, and she can fix phones herself and train new staff in FixFast's methods from day one. Management training will cost something, but she is still $250 a month cheaper than Omar, and she is ready in 2 weeks, not 2 months. A promotion may also motivate other technicians. Sana is cheaper but cannot repair phones, so waiting times could get worse. If FixFast cannot fill Hina's technician post quickly, Omar becomes the better choice.

In the March 2026 Paper 22 scheme, Level 3 (9 to 12 marks) needs a detailed discussion of two or more applicants and a well justified recommendation. Within that level, the top marks go to answers that discuss at least two applicants in detail, in context, and say why the other applicants were rejected. The June 2022 Paper 22 report said that, because analysis of the applicants earlier in the answer was missing, only the strongest candidates reached the highest band for the conclusion.

In my teaching, this is the question where students most often run out of steam: they describe each applicant from the table, then pick one in a single line. The marks are in the comparison.

## Why is training important?

Training gives employees the skills to do the job well. For the business that can mean higher productivity, fewer mistakes, better quality and customer service, and staff who feel valued and stay. For employees it can mean better skills, more confidence and a chance of promotion.

It also has costs: the fees or trainer's time, output lost while staff train, and the risk that trained staff leave for a rival or expect higher pay. The June 2022 Paper 22 report listed several of these effects as rewarded. It also warned against repeating "higher productivity" as the explanation for several different effects, and against writing about effects on employees when the question asked about the business.

## What are the three types of training?

| Type | What it is | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Induction | Introduces a new employee to the business: the workplace, the people, rules and procedures | New staff settle quickly and make fewer mistakes | New staff are paid but not yet working fully; takes managers' time |
| On-the-job | Training at the place of work, often by watching and working with an experienced employee | Cheaper; trained in the business's own methods; still producing or selling while learning | Bad habits can be passed on; slows down the experienced worker |
| Off-the-job | Training away from the workplace, such as at a college or by a specialist trainer | Expert, up to date skills; can lead to qualifications; full focus on learning | Costs more; no work done while away; skilled staff may leave for a rival |

### How should I define on-the-job and off-the-job training?

Both definitions were worth 2 marks in the old Paper 1 schemes, with 1 for a partial answer.

- **On-the-job training**: learning the job at the workplace, guided by an experienced member of staff while doing the work. The November 2022 Paper 11 scheme gave 1 mark for a partial answer such as "training while you are working". Its report said candidates who wrote that it was training for new workers got no marks, because that describes induction.
- **Off-the-job training**: training given away from the workplace, for example at a college or by an outside expert trainer. The June 2022 Paper 12 report said the second mark was often lost because candidates did not say who gave the training or where. Some wrongly took it to mean training outside working hours.

<Tip title="Name who and where">
Off-the-job training is training away from the workplace (where) by specialist trainers or at a college (who). Put both in your definition and both marks are within reach.
</Tip>

## How are recruitment and training questions marked?

The 0264 and 7081 papers are both 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks: Paper 1 is short answer and data response, and Paper 2 is a case study. These patterns come from the mark schemes named.

| Question type | Marks | Example sessions |
|---|---|---|
| Define a term (job description, on-the-job, off-the-job) | 2 | June 2022 P12, November 2022 P11, June 2023 P11 |
| Identify four stages of recruitment | 4 | March 2026 P12 |
| Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of a training method | 6 | March 2026 P12: knowledge, application and analysis for each |
| Explain two training methods and justify the best | 8 | 0264 and 7081 specimen Paper 1 question 4(d) |
| Explain two benefits of employment contracts | 8 | 0264 and 7081 specimen Paper 2 question 2(a) |
| Recommend which applicant to employ | 12 | June 2022 P22, March 2026 P22: levels |

For the 8 mark specimen question on training, the scheme gives up to 2 marks each for knowledge, application, analysis and a justified decision. It also says mirror arguments are credited only once, and that answers about production are not credited because the business is a retailer.

Application means using the case. In the March 2026 Paper 12 question on on-the-job training, the report said application often came from references to the public sector, healthcare and people aged 70 and above. If you are unsure what earns these marks, read my guide to [application marks in 0264 and 7081](/blog/igcse-o-level-business-application-marks).

### What is a mirror answer?

A mirror answer turns one point round and uses it again: "off-the-job training teaches new skills, on-the-job training does not". The November 2022 Paper 21 and March 2023 Paper 22 reports both said these earn no extra credit. The November 2023 Paper 12 report said the same point is only awarded once. Give each method its own reason.

## What about employment contracts?

The syllabus lists the main contents of an employment contract: duties and responsibilities, working hours, holiday entitlement and sick pay. Both sides benefit. The employee knows their rights, and the employer has a clear record of the duties each employee agreed to. The specimen Paper 2 question 2(a) asked for two benefits of contracts, explained and applied to the case.

## What mistakes do examiners report?

These come from the 0450 reports. Where a paper was shared with 7115, the 7115 report for that session gives the same comment.

| Mistake (session and paper) | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Confusing on-the-job or off-the-job training with induction (a common mistake, June 2022 P12 and November 2022 P13; lost marks, November 2022 P11; some, March 2026 P12) | Induction is for new staff; the other two are about where training happens |
| Treating training as a stage of recruitment (a common misconception, March 2026 P12) | Recruitment ends when the job is filled |
| Writing about selection when asked about recruitment (the most common mistake, March 2024 P12) | Recruitment attracts; selection chooses |
| Confusing job description with person specification (some, June 2023 P11) | Job description: the job. Person specification: the person |
| Saying internal recruits need no training (many, June 2022 P11) | A promotion usually needs training for the new role |
| Mirror answers (November 2022 P21, March 2023 P22, November 2023 P12) | A different reason for each method |
| General benefits of training instead of the method asked about (November 2023 P12, March 2026 P12) | Tie each point to induction, on-the-job or off-the-job |
| Words like "easy" or "quick" without saying why (November 2023 P13) | Explain how or why it is quick |

## Practice questions with answers

All three are my own.

### Practice question 1

> Identify two selection methods, other than an interview, and give one limitation of each. [4]

**Answer**

- References **[1]**: the applicant chooses the referees, so they may only pick people who will praise them **[1]**.
- An aptitude test **[1]**: it costs time and money to set and mark, and only shows some of the skills the job needs **[1]**.

### Practice question 2

> GreenLeaf is a supermarket with 60 checkout staff. Next month it starts a new payment system. Explain one reason why on-the-job training could suit GreenLeaf and one reason why off-the-job training could suit GreenLeaf. [6]

**Answer**

On-the-job: staff learn on the real tills while they serve **[k]**, so all 60 checkout staff keep working **[app]**, so GreenLeaf keeps serving customers and does not lose sales during training **[an]**.

Off-the-job: a specialist from the system's supplier can teach it **[k]**, so staff learn the new payment system correctly before it goes live **[app]**, which means fewer payment errors and shorter queues in the first weeks **[an]**.

How I would mark it is my own judgement, following the knowledge, application and analysis pattern in the March 2026 Paper 12 scheme.

### Practice question 3

> A private school must replace its head of science. Give one reason to appoint one of its own science teachers and one reason to appoint a teacher from another school. [4]

**Answer**

Own teacher: the school already knows how well she teaches and works with others **[1]**, so there is less risk of a poor appointment, and her promotion may motivate other teachers **[1]**.

Teacher from another school: the school can choose from a wider pool **[1]**, so it may find someone with new ideas or experience of running a department, which its current teachers lack **[1]**.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between recruitment and selection?

Recruitment is attracting people to apply, through the job description, person specification and advertisement. Selection is choosing between them, using CVs, application forms, references, tests and interviews.

### What are the three types of training in 0264 and 7081?

Induction training, on-the-job training and off-the-job training. The syllabus asks for the advantages and disadvantages of each.

### Is induction the same as on-the-job training?

No. Induction introduces new employees to the business. On-the-job training teaches the job at the workplace and can be for any employee. Several examiner reports name this mix up.

### What is the difference between a job description and a person specification?

A job description sets out the duties and responsibilities of the job. A person specification sets out the skills, qualifications and qualities of the person needed.

### Is internal recruitment always cheaper?

It usually costs less to advertise and needs no induction, but the person may still need training for the new role, and their old job must then be filled.
