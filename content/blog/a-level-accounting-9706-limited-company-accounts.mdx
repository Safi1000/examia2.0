Limited company accounts in 9706 are three linked statements: profit or loss, changes in equity and financial position. Profit for the year goes into retained earnings, and the closing equity figures go into the statement of financial position. If yours does not balance, the size of the difference usually points to one missing or doubled item.

## What does the 9706 syllabus ask for on limited companies?

At AS Level, [syllabus section 1.5.4](/syllabus/a-level-accounting-9706#s-1.5) asks you to:

- explain the features and accounting treatment of ordinary shares, bonus issues, rights issues, debentures, dividends and reserves
- give the advantages and disadvantages of bonus issues, rights issues, share issues and debentures, to the company and to the shareholders
- tell capital reserves (share premium, revaluation reserve) apart from revenue reserves (retained earnings, general reserve)
- prepare ledger accounts for an issue of ordinary shares, a rights issue and a bonus issue, at par or at a premium
- prepare a statement of profit or loss, a statement of financial position and a statement of changes in equity, for a trading or a service business

Two notes in the syllabus matter. Questions will not be set on preference shares. And the revaluation reserve is not to be used for a bonus issue.

At A Level, section 3.1.5 adds the statement of cash flows and the schedule of non-current assets, prepared in line with the international standards listed in 3.2.1 (IAS 1, IAS 10, IAS 16, IAS 37 and others).

| Paper | Length | Marks | Where company accounts appear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 1 hour | 30 | Multiple choice on reserves, bonus issues, dividends |
| Paper 2 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | Questions 1 to 3 are financial accounting (30, 15 and 15 marks) |
| Paper 3 (A Level) | 1 hour 30 minutes | 75 | Three questions of 25 marks; AS content is assumed |

## What is the layout of a company statement of profit or loss?

A company pays interest on its debentures and tax on its profit, so the bottom of the statement has two lines a sole trader never has. This is the layout recent 9706 mark schemes use.

| Line | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Sales less sales returns |
| Cost of sales | Opening inventory + purchases (adjusted) − closing inventory |
| **Gross profit** | |
| Other income | Rent received, discounts received, profit on disposal |
| Distribution costs | Selling and delivery expenses, their share of depreciation |
| Administrative expenses | Office expenses, their share of depreciation |
| **Profit from operations** | |
| Finance costs | Debenture interest and loan interest for the full year |
| **Profit before tax** | |
| Tax | The tax charge for the year |
| **Profit for the year** | Goes to retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity |

Dividends never appear here. They are a payment to the owners, not an expense, so they go in the statement of changes in equity. The June 2022 examiner report noted candidates who added the dividend paid to the profit for the year.

Finance costs are the full year's interest, whatever has been paid. Any interest still unpaid at the year end becomes a current liability.

## What is a statement of changes in equity?

The statement of changes in equity shows how each part of equity moved during the year. Each reserve has its own column, with a total column on the right. Each row is one event.

| Row label | Columns it changes |
|---|---|
| Balances at (opening date) | Every column |
| Issue of shares or rights issue | Share capital and share premium |
| Bonus issue | Share capital up, the reserve used down |
| Revaluation of property | Revaluation reserve |
| Profit for the year | Retained earnings |
| Dividends paid | Retained earnings (in brackets) |
| Transfer to general reserve | General reserve up, retained earnings down |
| Balances at (closing date) | Every column |

The row labels earn marks. Write "Rights issue" or "Dividends paid", not a date. The only dates go on the first and last rows.

The final row must check two ways. Add each column down, then add the closing row across. Both routes must give the same total. The June 2022 examiner report noted answers where the two did not agree.

## What is the layout of a company statement of financial position?

This is the order 9706 mark schemes follow. The statement totals are labelled, and the labels carry marks.

| Section | Items |
|---|---|
| **Assets** | |
| Non-current assets | Property, plant and equipment at carrying amount; intangible assets |
| Current assets | Inventory; trade and other receivables; cash and cash equivalents |
| **Total assets** | |
| **Equity and liabilities** | |
| Equity | Share capital; share premium; revaluation reserve; general reserve; retained earnings |
| **Total equity** | |
| Non-current liabilities | Debentures and loans repayable after more than 12 months |
| Current liabilities | Trade and other payables; tax payable; bank overdraft; debentures due within 12 months |
| **Total liabilities** | |
| **Total equity and liabilities** | Must equal total assets |

"Trade and other receivables" includes prepayments. "Trade and other payables" includes accruals, such as debenture interest owing. Each equity figure is the closing figure from your statement of changes in equity, copied across exactly.

## Worked example: Harbour Ltd

The company and figures are my own. Work through it on paper before reading the answers.

**Harbour Ltd: trial balance at 31 March 2026**

| Account | Debit $ | Credit $ |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | | 640,000 |
| Purchases | 380,000 | |
| Inventory at 1 April 2025 | 42,000 | |
| Distribution costs | 58,000 | |
| Administrative expenses | 71,000 | |
| Debenture interest paid | 3,000 | |
| Property, plant and equipment at cost | 600,000 | |
| Accumulated depreciation at 1 April 2025 | | 150,000 |
| Trade receivables | 37,000 | |
| Trade payables | | 29,000 |
| Bank | 31,000 | |
| Ordinary shares of $1 each | | 250,000 |
| Share premium | | 60,000 |
| General reserve | | 20,000 |
| Retained earnings at 1 April 2025 | | 38,000 |
| 10% debentures (2032) | | 60,000 |
| Dividends paid | 25,000 | |
| **Totals** | **1,247,000** | **1,247,000** |

Additional information:

1. Inventory at 31 March 2026 was $48,000.
2. Administrative expenses include insurance of $3,000 paid in advance.
3. Distribution costs of $2,000 were owing at 31 March 2026.
4. Depreciation is 10% a year on cost, split equally between distribution costs and administrative expenses.
5. The debentures were in issue all year. Only half the year's interest has been paid.
6. Tax for the year is $18,000.
7. On 1 October 2025 the company made a 1 for 4 rights issue at $1.60 per share. It is already in the trial balance.
8. The dividend paid was an interim dividend of $0.10 per share, paid after the rights issue.
9. The directors transfer $10,000 to the general reserve.
10. After the year end the directors proposed a final dividend of $0.12 per share.

### Workings

- **Cost of sales:** 42,000 + 380,000 − 48,000 = $374,000.
- **Depreciation:** 10% × 600,000 = $60,000, so $30,000 to each expense heading.
- **Distribution costs:** 58,000 + 2,000 owing + 30,000 = $90,000.
- **Administrative expenses:** 71,000 − 3,000 prepaid + 30,000 = $98,000.
- **Finance costs:** 10% × 60,000 = $6,000. Paid $3,000, so $3,000 owing.
- **Rights issue:** shares before the issue were 250,000 × 4 ÷ 5 = 200,000. New shares 200,000 ÷ 4 = 50,000. Share capital rises by $50,000 and share premium by 50,000 × $0.60 = $30,000. Cash raised $80,000.
- **Opening share capital and share premium:** 250,000 − 50,000 = $200,000, and 60,000 − 30,000 = $30,000.
- **Interim dividend check:** 250,000 shares × $0.10 = $25,000, which agrees with the trial balance.

The proposed final dividend (250,000 × $0.12 = $30,000) is not recorded. It was proposed after the year end, so under IAS 10 it is not a liability at 31 March 2026. It is disclosed in a note.

Where the figures come from:

- Property, plant and equipment: 600,000 − (150,000 + 60,000) = $390,000.
- Trade and other receivables: 37,000 + 3,000 prepaid = $40,000.
- Trade and other payables: 29,000 + 2,000 distribution costs owing + 3,000 debenture interest owing = $34,000.
- Total liabilities: 60,000 + 52,000 = $112,000.

Every adjustment touched two places. The prepayment cut an expense and added a receivable. The accruals added to expenses and to payables. The depreciation added to expenses and to accumulated depreciation. That is why the statement balances.

## Why does my statement of financial position not balance?

Most students make the first entry for an adjustment, in the statement of profit or loss, and forget the second, in the statement of financial position. So when the totals disagree, work out the difference and match it to one adjustment.

| Your difference | Likely cause | In Harbour Ltd |
|---|---|---|
| Equal to a prepayment | Expense reduced, but prepayment left out of receivables | Assets $3,000 short |
| Equal to an accrual | Expense increased, but amount owing left out of payables | Liabilities $2,000 or $3,000 short |
| Equal to the year's depreciation | Expense charged, but accumulated depreciation not updated | Assets $60,000 too high |
| Equal to the proposed dividend | Final dividend deducted from retained earnings, or shown as a liability, but not both | Out by $30,000 |
| Equal to profit for the year | Profit shown as a separate equity line as well as inside retained earnings | Equity $54,000 too high |
| Equal to the tax charge | Tax deducted in profit, but tax payable left out | Liabilities $18,000 short |
| Twice an item | Item put on the wrong side | For example, a $3,000 prepayment shown as a payable makes it $6,000 out |

Doing both with the proposed dividend makes the totals agree, but the statement is still wrong, so check for it even when you balance. One more check catches most of the rest: every item in the trial balance must be used once, and only once.

<Tip title="Use the difference">
Write the difference in the margin before you change anything. A random search wastes ten minutes. Matching the difference to one adjustment usually takes one.
</Tip>

## How do share issues work?

Every share has a nominal value (also called par value), the face value printed on it, such as $1 or $0.50. Share capital always records the nominal value. Anything paid above it goes to share premium.

| Issue | Journal entry | Effect on net assets |
|---|---|---|
| New shares at a premium | Debit bank. Credit share capital (nominal). Credit share premium (excess) | Up by the cash received |
| Rights issue | Same as a new issue, offered to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings | Up by the cash received |
| Bonus issue | Debit a reserve. Credit share capital | No change |

### Rights issue

A rights issue sells new shares to existing shareholders, usually below the market price. In Harbour Ltd, "1 for 4" meant one new share for every four already held. Always calculate the number of shares from the holding before the issue, not after.

For the company, a rights issue raises permanent capital and, unlike debentures, carries no interest; the June 2023 and November 2022 examiner reports credited those points. The March 2026 report, on a rights issue compared with a new issue of shares, also credited answers saying ownership control is kept and the issue is more likely to be fully taken up.

### Bonus issue

A bonus issue gives existing shareholders free shares by turning reserves into share capital. No cash moves, so net assets stay the same. The June 2022 examiner report noted many candidates did not understand this.

Suppose a company with 500,000 shares of $1 makes a 1 for 5 bonus issue, so 100,000 new shares worth $100,000. It has share premium of $60,000 and retained earnings of $150,000. When the question says the reserves are kept in their most flexible form, as recent papers do, use the share premium of $60,000 first, then $40,000 of retained earnings. Never use the revaluation reserve: the syllabus rules it out.

A company may prefer a bonus issue to a cash dividend when it needs to keep its cash. It is not a source of cash, and in a statement of cash flows the share capital it creates is not a cash inflow. The November 2022 and March 2025 examiner reports noted each of these errors.

## What is the difference between capital and revenue reserves?

| | Capital reserves | Revenue reserves |
|---|---|---|
| Examples in 9706 | Share premium, revaluation reserve | Retained earnings, general reserve |
| Where they come from | Share issues above nominal value, revaluing assets | Profits from trading kept in the business |
| Can they pay a cash dividend? | No | Yes |

A general reserve is profit that the directors have set aside, often for future needs or to keep dividends steady. It is still a revenue reserve, so it can be used to pay dividends. It is not a pile of cash. The March 2026 examiner report put it plainly: "Many thought that a general reserve represents cash".

## Where do students lose marks on company accounts?

| Mistake | Fix | Reported in |
|---|---|---|
| Profit for the year shown as a separate line in equity | Include it only inside retained earnings | June 2022 |
| Share premium left out of a share issue, or the issue valued at nominal only | Multiply new shares by the full issue price, then split | June 2022, November 2022 |
| Dates used as row labels in the statement of changes in equity | Use narratives (Rights issue, Dividends paid); dates on the first and last rows only | November 2023, March 2026 |
| Proposed dividend deducted from retained earnings | Leave it out of the statements; it is a note | March 2023 |
| Debenture interest owing left out of current liabilities | Add the unpaid interest to trade and other payables | November 2022 |
| Debentures due within 12 months left under non-current liabilities | Move them to current liabilities | June 2022 |
| Bonus issue charged to the revaluation reserve | Use share premium, then retained earnings | November 2022 |
| A revaluation loss larger than the revaluation reserve taken in full from that reserve | The reserve cannot go negative; take it down to nil, then deduct the rest from retained earnings on the same row, as the March 2026 mark scheme did | March 2026 |

The June 2022 report also noted answers with no subtotal for current assets. Show each subtotal, even when the question does not ask for it.

## Practice questions

**1.** Delta Ltd has 400,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each and share premium of $20,000. It makes a 1 for 4 rights issue at $0.80 per share, fully taken up. Calculate the increase in share capital, the new share premium balance and the cash raised.

**2.** Fenwick Ltd has 600,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each, share premium of $45,000, a revaluation reserve of $80,000 and retained earnings of $120,000. It makes a 1 for 4 bonus issue, keeping as much of its revenue reserves as possible. Show the share capital and reserve balances after the issue and state the effect on net assets.

**3.** A student's statement of financial position shows total assets of $412,000 and total equity and liabilities of $422,000. The student showed a proposed final dividend of $8,000 as a current liability, and left out rent prepaid of $2,000 when the rent expense had already been reduced. Correct both totals.

### Answers

**1.** New shares: 400,000 ÷ 4 = 100,000. Share capital rises by 100,000 × $0.50 = $50,000. Share premium rises by 100,000 × $0.30 = $30,000, so the balance is $50,000. Cash raised: 100,000 × $0.80 = $80,000.

**2.** New shares: 600,000 ÷ 4 = 150,000, with a nominal value of 150,000 × $0.50 = $75,000. Use all the share premium ($45,000), then $30,000 of retained earnings. Share capital was 600,000 × $0.50 = $300,000, so after the issue it is $375,000. Share premium is nil, the revaluation reserve stays at $80,000 (not used) and retained earnings are $90,000. Net assets do not change.

**3.** Remove the proposed dividend: total equity and liabilities 422,000 − 8,000 = $414,000. Add the prepayment to trade and other receivables: total assets 412,000 + 2,000 = $414,000. The statement now balances. The original difference of $10,000 was the two errors added together.

## FAQ

### Is a proposed dividend a liability in 9706?

No. A final dividend proposed after the year end is not a liability at the year end under IAS 10. It is disclosed in a note, and it appears in the statement of changes in equity of the year in which it is paid.

### Where does share premium go?

In the equity section of the statement of financial position, below share capital. It is a capital reserve, so it cannot be used to pay a cash dividend.

### Does a bonus issue change net assets?

No. Reserves fall and share capital rises by the same amount, so total equity and net assets stay the same. No cash is received.

### Is the statement of changes in equity AS or A Level?

Both. It is in AS section 1.5.4 and again in A Level section 3.1.5, which adds the statement of cash flows and the schedule of non-current assets.

### Can a general reserve be used to pay dividends?

Yes. A general reserve is a revenue reserve made from retained profits, so it is available for dividends, just as retained earnings are.

### Are preference shares tested in 9706?

No. The 2026 to 2028 syllabus states that questions will not be set on preference shares.
