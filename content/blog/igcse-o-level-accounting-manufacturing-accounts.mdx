A manufacturing account works out the cost of the goods a business made in its factory during the year. You start with the raw materials used, add direct labour and direct expenses to get prime cost, add factory overheads, then adjust for work in progress. The answer is the cost of production. It goes into the trading section where a trader would show purchases.

## How often do manufacturing accounts come up?

Often. On my count of O Level 7707 papers from 2022 to 2025, 35 questions were on manufacturing accounts, and the topic appeared in all 8 exam series. That puts it among the [most repeated 7707 topics](/past-papers/o-level-accounting-7707/most-repeated-topics).

It also has a fixed shape. Once you know where each cost belongs, most of the marks are in the layout and the labels, not in hard arithmetic.

## What does the 2027 to 2029 syllabus say?

Manufacturing accounts are topic 5.4 in both the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-5.4) and the [O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus](/syllabus/o-level-accounting-7707#s-5.4). The wording is the same in both. You need to understand:

1. Direct and indirect costs.
2. Direct material, direct labour, prime cost and factory overheads.
3. How to make adjustments for work in progress.
4. How to calculate factory cost of production.
5. How to prepare manufacturing accounts, statements of profit or loss and statements of financial position.
6. How to make the same year end adjustments as for sole traders in topic 5.1: accruals, prepayments, depreciation and the rest.

Point 6 matters. A manufacturing question is also an adjustments question. Expect owing wages, prepaid insurance and depreciation inside the factory figures.

## What are direct and indirect costs?

A **direct cost** can be traced straight to the goods being made. The wood in a chair and the wages of the person who builds it are direct costs. More chairs means more of these costs.

An **indirect cost** is a factory cost that cannot be traced to one product. The supervisor's wages and the factory rent are the same whether you make 900 chairs or 1 000. Indirect factory costs are called **factory overheads**.

Some costs are not factory costs at all. Office and selling costs never go in the manufacturing account. They go in the statement of profit or loss after gross profit.

| Cost | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Raw materials used | Direct material (prime cost) |
| Carriage inwards on raw materials | Direct material (prime cost) |
| Wages of machine operators (direct wages) | Direct labour (prime cost) |
| Royalties paid for each unit made | Direct expense (prime cost) |
| Wages of factory supervisor or manager | Factory overhead |
| Factory power, factory heat and light | Factory overhead |
| Factory rent, rates and insurance | Factory overhead |
| Depreciation of factory machinery | Factory overhead |
| Repairs to factory machinery | Factory overhead |
| Office wages, office rent, office depreciation | Statement of profit or loss |
| Wages of delivery van driver, carriage outwards | Statement of profit or loss |

The June 2025 0452 report made the first point on a multiple choice question: every direct cost of making the goods belongs in prime cost. The November 2023 7707 report dealt with a delivery driver. Those wages are not a manufacturing cost, so they go in the statement of profit or loss.

The November 2022 0452 report covered carriage on raw materials. If you wrongly put it in overheads, prime cost is understated. Cost of production stays the same, because the cost is still added, just in the wrong section.

## What is the manufacturing account layout?

Here is the full layout. X is a figure, (X) is a figure being deducted. Items go in the inner column and subtotals in the outer column.

**[Business name]: Manufacturing account for the year ended [date]**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opening inventory of raw materials | | X |
| Purchases of raw materials | X | |
| Carriage inwards on raw materials | X | X |
| | | X |
| Less closing inventory of raw materials | | (X) |
| **Cost of material consumed** | | X |
| Direct wages | | X |
| Direct expenses (for example, royalties) | | X |
| **Prime cost** | | X |
| Factory overheads | | |
| Wages of factory supervisor | X | |
| Factory power | X | |
| Factory rent and rates | X | |
| Depreciation of factory machinery | X | X |
| | | X |
| Add opening work in progress | | X |
| | | X |
| Less closing work in progress | | (X) |
| **Cost of production** | | X |

If purchases returns of raw materials are given, deduct them from purchases. Cambridge mark schemes use the label "Cost of material consumed". "Cost of materials used" and "raw materials consumed" mean the same.

### How does the work in progress adjustment work?

Work in progress means half made goods. At the start of the year some goods were already partly made. Finishing them this year adds to this year's production, so **add opening work in progress**. At the end, some goods are not yet finished. They are not part of this year's finished output, so **deduct closing work in progress**.

The adjustment comes after prime cost and overheads are totalled. It is never inside the overheads list.

Two quick checks from the multiple choice papers:

- If opening work in progress is higher than closing, the net effect adds to cost of production. The June 2022 reports for both 0452 and 7707 worked through exactly this.
- If closing work in progress is higher than opening, cost of production falls, and so does cost of sales. The June 2025 0452 report said some candidates thought one or both would increase.

## How do you apportion expenses between factory and office?

A trial balance often shows one figure for rent, rates, insurance or electricity. The question tells you how to split it between the factory and the office. That split is called **apportionment**.

1. **Adjust first.** Add any amount owing, deduct any amount prepaid. This gives the full cost for the year.
2. **Then split.** Use the ratio or percentage in the question.
3. **Factory share** goes in factory overheads. **Office share** goes in the statement of profit or loss.

For example, insurance paid is $5 000 and $400 is prepaid. The factory uses 75%. The year's cost is 5 000 less 400 = 4 600. Factory share: 4 600 × 75% = $3 450. Office share: $1 150.

The order matters. Both November 2023 7707 mark schemes (Papers 22 and 23) took the prepayment off rates and insurance before splitting the figure. The report on Paper 23 listed a wrong factory rates and insurance figure among the common errors.

If the split is by floor area, turn it into a fraction. A factory of 1 500 square metres and an office of 500 is 1 500 out of 2 000, so three quarters goes to the factory.

## How does the manufacturing account link to the statement of profit or loss?

The cost of production moves to the trading section. It **replaces purchases**. Everything else in the trading section works as it does for a trader, but the inventory there is **finished goods**.

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | | X |
| Less cost of sales | | |
| Opening inventory of finished goods | X | |
| Cost of production | X | |
| Purchases of finished goods | X | |
| | X | |
| Less closing inventory of finished goods | (X) | (X) |
| **Gross profit** | | **X** |

Purchases of finished goods only appear when the business also buys ready made goods to sell. The June 2023 reports for 0452 and 7707 said many candidates left out either these purchases or the cost of production. They also listed cost of sales items in the wrong order.

After gross profit, the statement continues as normal. Office and selling expenses come off, including the office share of anything apportioned. My [income statement format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format) covers that part line by line.

### Where does each inventory go?

A manufacturer has three inventories. Each appears twice: opening and closing in the accounts, and closing again in the statement of financial position.

| Inventory | Account | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials | Manufacturing account, cost of material consumed | Current asset |
| Work in progress | Manufacturing account, after overheads | Current asset |
| Finished goods | Trading section, cost of sales | Current asset |

The November 2023 7707 report said many candidates showed only finished goods in current assets. List all three separately. My [statement of financial position guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-statement-of-financial-position-format) shows the rest of that statement.

<Tip title="Three inventories, three places">
Before you start, write RM, WIP and FG next to the inventory figures in the question. Raw materials go at the top of the manufacturing account, work in progress at the bottom, finished goods in the trading section.
</Tip>

| Direct wages | 64 800 | |

| Office wages | 21 500 | |

| Office equipment at cost | 30 000 | |

**Notes at 31 December 2026**

1. Inventories: raw materials $9 150, work in progress $7 400, finished goods $16 900.
2. Direct wages of $1 200 were owing.
3. Factory power of $450 was owing.
4. Insurance of $600 was prepaid.
5. Rent and rates are shared between factory and office by floor area: factory 1 500 square metres, office 500 square metres.
6. Insurance is shared 70% factory, 30% office.
7. Depreciation: factory machinery at 15% a year by the reducing balance method, office equipment at 10% a year on cost.

Prepare the manufacturing account and the trading section of the statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2026.

### Step 1: sort and work out each figure

| Item | Working | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Direct wages | 64 800 + 1 200 | 66 000 |
| Factory power | 12 300 + 450 | 12 750 |
| Rent and rates: factory | 18 000 × 1 500 ÷ 2 000 | 13 500 |
| Rent and rates: office | 18 000 less 13 500 | 4 500 |
| Insurance for the year | 4 800 less 600 | 4 200 |
| Insurance: factory | 4 200 × 70% | 2 940 |
| Insurance: office | 4 200 less 2 940 | 1 260 |
| Depreciation: factory machinery | (120 000 less 40 000) × 15% | 12 000 |
| Depreciation: office equipment | 30 000 × 10% | 3 000 |

Revenue, purchases of finished goods, office wages, carriage outwards and both office shares stay out of the manufacturing account. So do the cost and accumulated depreciation of the assets. Only the depreciation for the year goes in.

## How is a manufacturing account marked?

I checked the Paper 2 mark schemes for 0452 March 2022 (Paper 22), 0452 June 2025 (Paper 21) and 7707 November 2023 (Paper 22). The manufacturing account was worth 9, 10 and 11 marks. The pattern was almost the same each time:

- One mark for the **cost of material consumed**.
- One mark for **direct wages**.
- One own figure mark for **prime cost**.
- Usually one mark for **each factory overhead figure**, most of them needing an accrual, an apportionment or a depreciation working. In March 2022 two overheads shared one mark. In November 2023 a prepaid figure that was then split earned two.
- One own figure mark for the **total of prime cost and overheads**.
- One mark for **both work in progress figures** together.
- One own figure mark for **cost of production**.

An own figure mark rewards a correct total even when an earlier figure was wrong. So always finish every subtotal.

The trading section that followed in March 2022 was worth 4 marks. There was one mark for both finished goods inventories, one for bringing in the cost of production, one for cost of sales and one for gross profit.

### What else do the questions ask?

The same question usually has more parts. In the papers I checked, these followed the manufacturing account:

- The trading section or gross profit, using your cost of production.
- The current assets section, with all three inventories (7707 November 2023).
- Advice on whether to make the goods or buy them ready made (0452 March 2022, June 2025 and March 2026). Give points for and against, linked to the figures, then a clear recommendation.
- An accounting principle in a manufacturer's statements, such as historic cost (0452 June 2025).

In Paper 1, the multiple choice questions test what belongs in prime cost and the effect of work in progress or finished goods errors.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the 0452 and 7707 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026. The June 2023 comments are the same in the 0452 and 7707 reports.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Labels for cost of material consumed, prime cost or cost of production missing (0452 March 2022, June 2023, June 2025, 7707 November 2023) | Write all three labels in full, with no abbreviations |
| Overheads such as the supervisor's wages in prime cost (0452 March 2022, June 2023, June 2025, 7707 November 2023) | Prime cost holds direct costs only |
| Office or selling costs in the manufacturing account, such as office wages, the delivery driver or revenue (0452 March 2022, June 2023, June 2025, 7707 November 2023) | Factory costs only. Office costs go after gross profit |
| Work in progress inside the overheads list (0452 March 2022, June 2025, 7707 November 2023) | Adjust it after totalling prime cost and overheads |
| Overheads deducted from prime cost (0452 March 2022, June 2023) | Overheads are added |
| Asset cost or accumulated depreciation listed as an overhead (0452 March 2022, June 2023) | Only the depreciation charge for the year |
| Vague item labels, such as "wages" for direct wages or depreciation with no asset named (0452 June 2025, 7707 November 2023) | Name the item fully: "Depreciation of factory machinery" |
| Trading section missing the cost of production or purchases of finished goods, or including raw materials (0452 March 2022, June 2023, 7707 November 2023) | Finished goods inventories, cost of production and finished goods purchases only |

On labels, the November 2023 7707 report warned against "Inappropriate abbreviations such as C of P (cost of production)". The same report said a large number of candidates started with revenue, as if preparing a statement of profit or loss. The manufacturing account has no revenue.

<Tip title="Write the three labels first">
Before any figures, write down the page: Cost of material consumed, Prime cost, Cost of production. Then fill in around them. Missing labels appear in almost every report on this topic.
</Tip>

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper first. All figures are my own.

### Practice question 1

Say where each cost goes: prime cost, factory overheads, or the statement of profit or loss.

1. Wages of machine operators.
2. Depreciation of delivery vans.
3. Carriage inwards on raw materials.
4. Wages of the factory manager.
5. Royalties paid on each unit made.
6. Repairs to factory machinery.

**Answer**

| Item | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| 1 Machine operators' wages | Prime cost (direct wages) |
| 2 Delivery van depreciation | Statement of profit or loss |
| 3 Carriage inwards on raw materials | Prime cost (cost of material consumed) |
| 4 Factory manager's wages | Factory overheads |
| 5 Royalties | Prime cost (direct expense) |
| 6 Factory machinery repairs | Factory overheads |

### Practice question 2

Prime cost is $84 000 and factory overheads are $31 500. Work in progress was $5 600 at the start of the year and $4 900 at the end. Finished goods inventory was $9 000 at the start and $10 400 at the end. Calculate the cost of production and the cost of sales.

**Answer**

Cost of production: 84 000 + 31 500 = 115 500. Add 5 600 = 121 100. Less 4 900 = $116 200.

Cost of sales: 9 000 + 116 200 = 125 200. Less 10 400 = $114 800.

### Practice question 3

State the effect of each error on prime cost, cost of production and gross profit.

1. Carriage inwards on raw materials of $800 was shown as a factory overhead.
2. Closing work in progress was overstated by $500.
3. Closing inventory of finished goods was overstated by $300.

**Answer**

| Error | Prime cost | Cost of production | Gross profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understated by $800 | No effect | No effect |
| 2 | No effect | Understated by $500 | Overstated by $500 |
| 3 | No effect | No effect | Overstated by $300 |

In error 2, the cost of production is too low, so cost of sales is too low and gross profit too high. Error 3 changes cost of sales only. The March 2026 0452 report made the same distinction.

## FAQ

### What is prime cost?

Prime cost is the total of the direct costs of production: cost of material consumed, direct wages and any direct expenses. It is the first subtotal that includes labour.

### Is carriage inwards part of prime cost?

Yes, when it is carriage on raw materials. It is part of the cost of material consumed. Carriage outwards is a selling cost and goes in the statement of profit or loss.

### Where does work in progress go in a manufacturing account?

After prime cost and factory overheads are totalled. Add the opening work in progress and deduct the closing work in progress to reach cost of production.

### What is the difference between cost of production and cost of sales?

Cost of production is the factory cost of goods finished in the year. Cost of sales adjusts it for finished goods inventory, and any finished goods bought, to give the cost of the goods actually sold.

### Is depreciation of factory machinery a direct or indirect cost?

Indirect. It is a factory overhead, because it cannot be traced to one unit. Only the charge for the year goes in, never the cost or accumulated depreciation.

### Does a manufacturing account include revenue?

No. Revenue belongs in the trading section of the statement of profit or loss. The manufacturing account ends at cost of production.
