Each Section B question in 0455 and 2281 Paper 2 has four parts: a 2 mark define, a 4 mark explain, a 6 mark analyse and an 8 mark discuss. Each one is marked differently. Define needs a precise meaning, explain needs a point plus a reason, analyse needs a chain of links, and discuss needs two developed sides. Below is a marked sample for each.

## How is Paper 2 marked?

From 2027, IGCSE 0455 and O Level 2281 Paper 2 is 2 hours and 80 marks. Section A is one compulsory data question worth 20 marks. In Section B you answer three questions from a choice of four, each worth 20 marks. Each Section B question has the same four parts:

| Part | Marks | Usual command word | How it is marked |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | 2 | Define, Identify | Point marking |
| (b) | 4 | Explain | Point marking |
| (c) | 6 | Analyse | Point marking |
| (d) | 8 | Discuss | Levels (Table A) |

That is the structure of the 2027 specimen Paper 2, and the same pattern ran through recent papers such as 2281/21 (May/June 2024). For exams in 2026 the paper is still 2 hours 15 minutes and 90 marks; the syllabus says the 2027 cut in marks comes from Section A.

**Point marking** means each correct, relevant point earns one mark, up to the total. **Levels marking** means the examiner reads the whole answer, decides which level description fits, then picks a mark inside that level. You can check the full paper changes in my [2027 specimen paper guide](/blog/2281-0455-specimen-paper-2027).

## What do define, explain, analyse and discuss mean?

Here is what each command word in the 0455 and 2281 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029 asks for, in my words:

| Command word | What it asks you to do |
|---|---|
| Define | Give the exact meaning of a term |
| Explain | Give reasons, and show why or how one thing affects another |
| Analyse | Break an issue into parts and show how each part links to the next |
| Discuss | Cover an issue in depth, with your points in a clear order |

"Evaluate" is not on the command word list. Evaluation is a skill (assessment objective AO3), and Paper 2 tests it through Discuss questions. The syllabus says part (d) answers need discussions that "consider two sides of economic arguments". When your teacher says "evaluate", they mean: weigh both sides, then say what the answer depends on.

## How do you get both marks on a 2 mark define question?

A define question has two marks because a good definition has two parts. In the 2027 specimen mark scheme, "international trade" earns one mark for the exchange of goods and one for saying it happens between countries. Sometimes the second mark is for an example instead, when the question asks for one.

**Structure:** one sentence that holds every key part of the definition.

**Sample question (mine):** Define "deflation". [2]

> Deflation is a sustained fall in the general price level in an economy.

| What the answer says | Mark |
|---|---|
| "sustained fall" | 1 |
| "in the general price level" | 1 |
| **Total** | **2** |

A student who writes "when prices go down" gets at most one mark. It misses that the fall continues over time and that it is the general price level, not one product.

## How do you get 4 marks on an explain question?

"Explain two" questions are marked one plus one, twice. The 2027 specimen mark scheme gives "1 mark for identification and 1 mark for explanation (× 2)". So each point needs a reason attached to it.

**Structure:** two short paragraphs. Each states a point, then says why or how, using "because" or "so".

**Sample question (mine):** Explain two reasons why the demand for air travel in a country may increase. [4]

> Incomes may rise. Air travel is a normal good, so households with more disposable income can afford more flights.
>
> The price of a substitute, such as long distance train travel, may rise. Flying then becomes relatively cheaper, so some travellers switch to air travel.

| What the answer says | Mark |
|---|---|
| Point 1: incomes rise | 1 |
| Why: normal good, households can afford more flights | 1 |
| Point 2: price of a substitute rises | 1 |
| Why: flying becomes relatively cheaper, travellers switch | 1 |
| **Total** | **4** |

Two points, each explained once. A third point earns nothing extra, and the specimen guidance says examiners consider only the first three. Put your best two first.

## How do you write a 6 mark analyse answer?

Analyse questions reward a chain of reasoning. Each valid link in the chain can earn a mark. In the 2027 specimen, "Analyse two" questions give up to 2 marks for naming the two items and the rest for analysis. "Analyse how" questions can give marks for showing what the key terms mean, then the rest for the chain.

The cap for not linking is real. In 2281/21 (May/June 2024), one 6 mark Analyse question gave "a maximum of three marks for only identifying the reasons". Naming without linking caps you at half.

**Structure:** cause, then effect, then effect, then final outcome. Use "this leads to", "as a result", "which means".

**Sample question (mine):** Analyse how a fall in the rate of interest may increase employment. [6]

> A lower rate of interest reduces the cost of borrowing. Households take out more loans, so consumer expenditure rises. Firms borrow more to buy machinery, so investment rises. Saving also becomes less rewarding, so households spend more of their income. With higher consumer expenditure and investment, total (aggregate) demand rises. Firms need to produce more output, so they hire more workers and employment increases.

| Link in the chain | Mark |
|---|---|
| Lower cost of borrowing | 1 |
| More loans, consumer expenditure rises | 1 |
| Firms borrow more, investment rises | 1 |
| Saving less rewarding, households spend more | 1 |
| Total (aggregate) demand rises | 1 |
| More output needed, firms hire more workers | 1 |
| **Total** | **6** |

Notice each sentence moves the chain one step forward. Saying "total demand rises" twice would earn it once: the specimen guidance tells examiners not to reward the same point twice.

<Tip title="Diagrams in analyse answers">
Draw a diagram only when it helps the chain, such as a shift in demand or supply. Label both axes, every curve and both equilibrium points, then refer to it in your writing. An unlabelled diagram adds very little.
</Tip>

This is where most students get stuck: they know the content, but their chain stops after two links. Practising the chain out loud, one link per sentence, is the fastest fix I know.

## How do you reach Level 3 on an 8 mark discuss question?

Discuss questions in Section B are marked in levels, using Table A in the 2027 specimen mark scheme:

| Level | Marks | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 6 to 8 | Both sides argued with reasons. One side can be deeper, but each must be developed. |
| 2 | 3 to 5 | Reasoned analysis that is thin in places or covers one side only. |
| 1 | 1 to 2 | Correct definitions or simple statements with no development. |

Only Level 3 asks for both sides to be developed. So a one sided answer, however good, cannot score more than 5.

**Structure:**

1. **Why it might:** one or two points, each a short chain.
2. **Why it might not:** one or two different points, each a short chain.
3. **Judgement:** one or two sentences on what the answer depends on.

**Sample question (mine):** Discuss whether or not a government should make bus travel free in its cities. [8]

> **Why it should.** With no fare to pay, more people travel by bus instead of by car. This reduces traffic congestion and pollution, which are external costs paid by third parties. Free travel also helps low income workers reach jobs further from home, which can reduce unemployment.
>
> **Why it should not.** The policy has an opportunity cost. The government must pay the bus firms, so that money cannot be spent on schools or hospitals, or taxes may need to rise. If drivers value the comfort of a car, few will switch even at a zero fare, so congestion barely falls. Buses may also become overcrowded, which lowers the quality of the service.
>
> **Judgement.** It depends on how many drivers would really switch. Where buses are a close substitute for cars and congestion is serious, free travel is more likely to be worth its cost.

| Where the level is earned | Level reached |
|---|---|
| Uses economics terms correctly: external costs, opportunity cost, substitute | Level 1 |
| "Why it should" is a developed chain: no fare, more bus journeys, fewer cars, lower external costs | Level 2 |
| "Why it should not" is developed with separate reasons, not the first side turned round | Level 3 |
| Judgement names what the answer depends on, adding something new | Top of Level 3: 8 |

The "why it should not" paragraph brings new reasons: the cost, the comfort of a car, overcrowding. Writing only "congestion might not fall" would just reverse the first side, and the 2024 mark scheme told examiners not to reward that.

**Discuss in Section A is marked differently.** The 2027 specimen Section A has a 6 mark Discuss question that is point marked, with up to 4 marks for each side. A one sided answer there stops at 4. The June 2025 examiner report for 0455 noted exactly this: candidates who looked at only one side were held to 4 marks on a Section A Discuss question.

## Where do students lose marks?

| Mistake | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Listing in an Explain question | Two points with no reasons score 2 out of 4 at most | 2027 specimen mark scheme |
| Giving more points than asked | Only the first points are considered, so a weak first point is not rescued by a strong fifth | 2027 specimen mark scheme; June 2025 report |
| Mirror statements, such as "pollution rises" then "pollution does not fall" | The repeat earns nothing | 2027 specimen marking principles |
| Dropping in a key term without using it | No credit for the term alone | 2027 specimen marking principles |
| Ignoring the source material in Section A | The syllabus says you must use the data, and the best answers were built on it | 2027 syllabus; June 2025 report, 0455 |
| One sided Discuss answers | Capped at 5 in Section B, at 4 in Section A | 2027 specimen; June 2025 report |
| A conclusion that only repeats earlier points | No extra credit | June 2025 report, 0455 |
| Answering out of order or mislabelling parts | Risk of skipping a question, and labels that confuse | June 2025 report, 0455 |

The June 2025 report also found that many students spent too long on descriptive answers and ran short of time for the 8 mark parts. Plan your time so each (d) gets the most minutes in its question.

## Practice questions with answers

Try each one before you read the answer.

**1. Explain two reasons why a firm's costs of production may rise. [4]**

Answer: Wages may rise (1) because workers have more bargaining power, or the minimum wage goes up, so the firm pays more for each hour of labour (1). The price of raw materials may rise (1) because of a poor harvest or a fall in the exchange rate, so each unit costs more to make (1).

**2. Analyse how a rise in a country's exports may reduce unemployment. [6]**

Answer: Higher exports mean more demand for the country's goods (1). Exporting firms raise output to meet it (1). They need more workers, so they hire people who were unemployed (1). These workers now earn incomes and spend more (1), which raises demand for other domestic firms (1). Those firms also take on workers, so unemployment falls further (1).

**3. Discuss whether or not a country should reduce tariffs on imported food. [8]**

Answer plan: Why it should: food prices fall, raising the real income of poor households who spend a large share of income on food; more competition pushes domestic farmers to become more efficient. Why it should not: domestic farmers may lose sales and farm workers may become unemployed; the country becomes more dependent on other countries for food; the government loses tariff revenue. Judgement: it depends on how many people work in farming and on whether the government can help displaced workers find new jobs. Both sides developed with a judgement reaches Level 3.

## FAQ

### How long should an 8 mark discuss answer be?

Long enough to develop two separate arguments and give a judgement. In practice that is usually three short paragraphs. The level depends on development, not length.

### Do I need a conclusion in an 8 mark answer?

Not always. The June 2025 report for 0455 said an excellent answer is possible without one. If you write a conclusion, make it a judgement on what the answer depends on, not a repeat of your points.

### Can I get full marks on a 6 mark analyse question with one reason?

For an "Analyse how" question, yes, if your single chain has enough valid links. For an "Analyse two" question, you need both items named and analysed.

### Do I need diagrams in Paper 2?

Only when the question asks for one or when it clearly helps your chain. A diagram question is marked on the drawing: labelled axes, labelled curves, the correct shift and both equilibrium points.

### Is "evaluate" a command word in 0455 and 2281?

No. Evaluation is assessed through Discuss questions, where you weigh two sides and reach a judgement.

### Does the 8 mark structure change in 2027?

The four part structure of Section B stays the same. The paper becomes 2 hours and 80 marks, with Section A reduced to 20 marks.
