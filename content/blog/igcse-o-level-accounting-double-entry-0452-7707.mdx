Double entry bookkeeping records every transaction twice: once on the debit side of one ledger account and once, for the same amount, on the credit side of another. Debit an account when an asset, an expense or drawings increase. Credit it when a liability, capital or income increase. For IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 this is syllabus section 2.1, and in my experience it sits under most Paper 2 questions.

## What does the syllabus ask you to know about double entry?

Section 2.1 has the same wording in the 0452 and 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029. Here is what each point means in an exam, in my words.

| Syllabus point | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| The double entry system | Explain duality and give the debit and credit for any transaction |
| Prepare ledger accounts and post transactions | Write entries with date, details and amount on the right side |
| Balance accounts and transfer to financial statements | Find the balance, carry it down, or close the account at the year end |
| Interpret ledger accounts | Say what an entry or a balance means, such as who owes whom |
| Three-column running balance format | Read and prepare an account with a balance after each line |
| Division of the ledger | Know which accounts sit in the sales, purchases and nominal (general) ledgers |
| Ledger accounts kept digitally | Explain how software keeps the same records |

The syllabus adds a note: you do not need to explain or use folio columns. You can leave them out.

On our past paper pages, 111 questions from the 0452 and 7707 papers of 2022 to 2026 carry the 2.1 tag, as main or second tag (0452: 70, 7707: 41). The examiner reports go further. The March 2022, March 2023 and March 2025 reports for 0452 Paper 2 all say that double entry forms the basis of all the day to day records of a business. You will use it in cash books, control accounts, depreciation, corrections of errors and clubs.

## What are the debit and credit rules?

Every account has two sides. The left side is the debit side. The right side is the credit side. What goes where depends on the type of account.

| Type of account | Increase goes on the | Decrease goes on the |
|---|---|---|
| Asset (bank, cash, inventory, equipment, trade receivables) | Debit | Credit |
| Expense (rent, wages, purchases) | Debit | Credit |
| Drawings | Debit | Credit |
| Liability (loans, trade payables) | Credit | Debit |
| Capital | Credit | Debit |
| Income (sales, commission received, discount received) | Credit | Debit |

Returns reverse the original entry: sales returns are a debit, purchases returns a credit.

The way I teach it: debit is for things the business owns or uses up, and for drawings. Credit is for what the business owes, what the owner put in, and what the business earns. Learn the six rows above and you can post any transaction.

### Why does it always balance?

Each transaction has two sides of equal value. When Farah pays rent by bank, the business gets the use of the premises (rent expense up) and gives away money (bank down). Debit rent, credit bank. Both entries use the same amount, so total debits always equal total credits. That is why a trial balance can be drawn up from the ledger at all.

This also keeps the accounting equation true: assets equal capital plus liabilities. Every debit to an asset is matched by a credit somewhere, so neither side of the equation moves alone.

Write "every transaction is recorded twice, once on the debit side of one account and once on the credit side of another, for the same amount". The March 2026 0452 Paper 22 report said weak answers only said everything is entered twice, with no more explanation.
</Tip>

## How do I find the debit and credit for any transaction?

Use three questions every time.

1. Which two accounts change? Name them exactly: "bank", "rent", "Zoya" (a customer), not "money" or "payment".
2. What type is each account: asset, expense, drawings, liability, capital or income?
3. Is each one increasing or decreasing? Then read the side from the table.

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays $8,000 into the business bank account | Bank | Capital |
| Goods for resale bought on credit from Danish Traders | Purchases | Danish Traders |
| Goods sold on credit to Zoya | Zoya | Sales |
| Rent paid by bank | Rent | Bank |
| Zoya returns goods | Sales returns | Zoya |
| Goods returned to Danish Traders | Danish Traders | Purchases returns |
| Owner takes money from the bank for personal use | Drawings | Bank |
| Cash paid into the bank | Bank | Cash |
| Equipment bought by bank | Equipment | Bank |
| Discount received from a supplier for paying early | Supplier's account | Discount received |
| Discount allowed to a customer for paying early | Discount allowed | Customer's account |

Two traps sit in this table. **Goods for resale go to purchases, not to an inventory account.** And **trade discount is never entered in a ledger account.** It comes off the price before the invoice is recorded. The June 2022 Paper 12 report for both 0452 and 7707 made this point. Only cash discount, given for paying early, has a ledger entry.

## Worked example: Farah Crafts in March

Here is my own example, with every number checked.

> Farah starts a craft supplies business. Her transactions for March 2026 are:
>
> - Mar 1: paid $8,000 of her own money into a business bank account
> - Mar 3: bought goods on credit from Danish Traders, $1,500
> - Mar 6: sold goods on credit to Zoya, $900
> - Mar 9: paid rent by bank, $600
> - Mar 12: Zoya returned goods, $100
> - Mar 15: cash sales of $1,200, paid straight into the bank
> - Mar 20: paid Danish Traders $1,450 by bank in full settlement of $1,500
> - Mar 25: bought a computer for the business by bank, $700
> - Mar 28: took $300 from the bank for personal use
> - Mar 30: Zoya paid $500 by bank transfer

On Mar 20, Farah owed $1,500 but paid $1,450. The $50 difference is discount received. Danish Traders' account is debited twice: $1,450 bank and $50 discount received.

### Step 1: post the entries

Each entry shows the date, the name of the other account, and the amount.

**Bank account**

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Mar 1 Capital 8,000 | Mar 9 Rent 600 |
| Mar 15 Sales 1,200 | Mar 20 Danish Traders 1,450 |
| Mar 30 Zoya 500 | Mar 25 Office equipment 700 |
| | Mar 28 Drawings 300 |
| | Mar 31 Balance c/d 6,650 |
| Total 9,700 | Total 9,700 |
| Apr 1 Balance b/d 6,650 | |

**Zoya account** (sales ledger)

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Mar 6 Sales 900 | Mar 12 Sales returns 100 |
| | Mar 30 Bank 500 |
| | Mar 31 Balance c/d 300 |
| Total 900 | Total 900 |
| Apr 1 Balance b/d 300 | |

**Danish Traders account** (purchases ledger)

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Mar 20 Bank 1,450 | Mar 3 Purchases 1,500 |
| Mar 20 Discount received 50 | |
| Total 1,500 | Total 1,500 |

Danish Traders' account has no balance. Both sides total $1,500, so Farah owes nothing.

The other accounts have one entry each: Capital (credit 8,000), Purchases (debit 1,500), Sales (credit 900 and 1,200), Rent (debit 600), Sales returns (debit 100), Office equipment (debit 700), Drawings (debit 300) and Discount received (credit 50).

Look at the details column. In the bank account, the Mar 9 payment says "Rent", the name of the other account. It does not say "paid rent" or "bank transfer". The June 2022 0452 and 7707 Paper 22 reports said that a method of payment, such as electronic transfer, is not an account name. The November 2022 0452 Paper 22 report said descriptions of actions, such as cash paid to credit suppliers, lost marks.

### Step 2: balance each account

1. Add both sides.
2. Write the bigger total on both sides, level with each other.
3. On the smaller side, enter the difference as "Balance c/d" (carried down), dated the last day of the period.
4. Under the totals, on the opposite side, enter the same figure as "Balance b/d" (brought down), dated the first day of the next period.

In the bank account, debits total $9,700 and credits before the balance total $3,050. The difference is $6,650. It is carried down on the credit side and brought down on the debit side on Apr 1. A debit balance on bank means the business has money in the bank: an asset.

**Write the words in full.** The March 2023 and June 2025 0452 reports said "bbd" is not acceptable for "Balance b/d". The March 2022 report gave the same warning about "b/d" written alone for "Balance".

### Step 3: check with a trial balance

| Account | Debit ($) | Credit ($) |
|---|---|---|
| Bank | 6,650 | |
| Capital | | 8,000 |
| Purchases | 1,500 | |
| Sales | | 2,100 |
| Zoya | 300 | |
| Rent | 600 | |
| Sales returns | 100 | |
| Office equipment | 700 | |
| Drawings | 300 | |
| Discount received | | 50 |
| Totals | 10,150 | 10,150 |

The totals agree, so the arithmetic of the double entry is right. A trial balance cannot catch every error, such as an entry in the wrong account of the same type. My [trial balance guide](/blog/igcse-accounting-trial-balance) lists those errors.

## How do I read a balance on a ledger account?

The syllabus asks you to interpret ledger accounts, and Paper 1 sometimes shows one and asks what it means.

| Balance | On this kind of account | It means |
|---|---|---|
| Debit | Customer (sales ledger) | The customer owes the business: an asset |
| Credit | Supplier (purchases ledger) | The business owes the supplier: a liability |
| Credit | Customer | The customer has overpaid or returned goods after paying, so the business owes them |
| Debit | Supplier | The business has overpaid the supplier |
| Debit | Bank | Money in the bank |
| Credit | Bank | An overdraft: the business owes the bank |

The unusual ones are where marks go. In November 2023 7707 Paper 12 Question 4, a customer's account had a credit balance. The report said a significant proportion of candidates could not recognise that it was a liability. In the 2027 specimen Paper 2 for 0452, Question 1(c) asked why a supplier's account had a debit balance. The scheme credited reasons such as an overpayment.

## How do I close an account at the end of the year?

Income and expense accounts do not carry a balance into the next year. At the year end, their total is transferred to the statement of profit or loss. The transfer closes the account.

For Farah, if March were the last month of her year, the rent account would read:

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Mar 9 Bank 600 | Mar 31 Statement of profit or loss 600 |

Check the name you write. Papers up to 2026 used "income statement". The 2027 specimen Paper 1 for 0452 and 7707 shows the year end transfer in a rent account as "statement of profit or loss". I would use that name from 2027.

Read the instruction before you close an account. In March 2026 0452 Paper 22, the question asked candidates to balance a purchases account. The report said some candidates tried to transfer the difference to the income statement instead of following the instruction.

Assets, liabilities and capital are balanced and carried down, because they still exist next year.

## What is the three-column running balance format?

The 2027 syllabus names it. Instead of two sides, the account has a debit column, a credit column and a balance column. The balance is updated after every line, the way a bank statement works.

Farah's bank account in running balance format:

| Date and details | Debit ($) | Credit ($) | Balance ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 1 Capital | 8,000 | | 8,000 Dr |
| Mar 9 Rent | | 600 | 7,400 Dr |
| Mar 15 Sales | 1,200 | | 8,600 Dr |
| Mar 20 Danish Traders | | 1,450 | 7,150 Dr |
| Mar 25 Office equipment | | 700 | 6,450 Dr |
| Mar 28 Drawings | | 300 | 6,150 Dr |
| Mar 30 Zoya | 500 | | 6,650 Dr |

The final balance, $6,650 Dr, matches the balance on the two sided account. Write "Dr" or "Cr" after each balance so the reader knows which way it runs. The 2027 specimen Paper 1 has a multiple choice question on the features of this format. In its key, the correct features were that it is often used in software and on bank statements, and that it shows the balance after each transaction.

## How is the ledger divided?

Businesses with many accounts split them into three ledgers.

| Ledger | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger | One account for each credit customer |
| Purchases ledger | One account for each credit supplier |
| Nominal (general) ledger | Everything else: sales, purchases, expenses, income, assets, capital, loans, drawings |

The cash book is a book of prime entry and the ledger account for cash and bank at the same time. My [guide to books of prime entry](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-books-of-prime-entry-0452-7707) covers the cash book, the journals and the petty cash book, and how their totals reach the ledger.

When ledgers are kept digitally, the software posts both sides of each entry and updates balances at once. The rules of debit and credit do not change.

## What are the advantages of double entry?

This comes up as a short written question, worth 1 mark in November 2023 7707 Paper 22 and 2 marks in Paper 23. In those two schemes, the credited points included these, which I have put in my own words:

- full records of assets, liabilities, income and expenses
- financial statements are easier to prepare
- the profit or loss is more likely to be accurate
- better information for decisions and control
- fraud is less likely
- results can be compared with earlier years and other businesses
- information is ready when a bank or lender asks for it

Be careful with fraud and errors. The November 2023 7707 Paper 23 report said many candidates claimed double entry completely removes fraud and errors. The Paper 22 report said answers claiming that double entry prevents errors or prevents fraud were not rewarded. Double entry makes them less likely and easier to find. It does not stop them.

## How are ledger account questions marked?

Look at how marks attach to entries in real schemes.

| Question | Marks | What earned them |
|---|---|---|
| June 2023 0452 Paper 21 Q1(b): a supplier's account | 4 | One mark each for three entries, and one for the balance brought down (own figure) |
| March 2026 0452 Paper 22 Q1(b): purchases account | 3 | One mark each for two entries, and one for the balance brought down (own figure) |
| March 2026 0452 Paper 22 Q1(c): discount received account | 2 | The month's total, and the balance brought down |
| 2027 specimen 0452 Paper 2 Q1(d): two suppliers' accounts | 7 | Marks for single entries, and one for the balance brought down on 1 December |

My advice is to treat every entry as needing the right date, details, amount and side. The March 2023 0452 report said attention must be paid to the date and the details, not just the amount. "Own figure" means that if your balance is wrong only because of an earlier slip, you can still earn the mark for bringing it down correctly.

My advice: always balance and bring down unless the question says to close the account. The June 2025 0452 Paper 22 report said all ledger accounts should have a balance brought down where one applies or where the question asks for it.

## What do examiners say students get wrong?

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Details that describe the action or the payment method, such as "electronic transfer" or "cash paid to credit suppliers" (June 2022 P22; November 2022 0452 P22) | Write the name of the other account: bank, purchases, Zoya |
| Abbreviations such as "bbd" or "b/d" alone (March 2022, March 2023, June 2025 0452) | Write "Balance b/d" and "Balance c/d" in full |
| Trade discount entered in a supplier's account (some, June 2023 0452 P21; a common error, June 2025 0452 P21) | Record the invoice after trade discount; only cash discount is posted |
| Entries reversed (often, November 2022 0452 P22; a few whole accounts, June 2023 0452 P21) | Use the six row table every time |
| The difference transferred to the income statement when the question said balance (some, March 2026 0452 P22) | Follow the instruction: carry down and bring down |
| Duality defined as "entered twice" only (March 2026 0452 P22) | Say debit in one account, credit in another, same amount |
| A credit balance on a customer's account not read as a liability (a significant proportion, November 2023 7707 P12) | Credit balance on a customer means the business owes them |
| Saying double entry stops fraud and errors (many, November 2023 7707 P23) | It reduces them and makes them easier to find |

### Practice question 1

> State the account to be debited and the account to be credited for each transaction.
>
> (a) The owner pays an extra $2,000 of capital into the business bank account.
>
> (b) Goods costing $150 are returned to Malik, a credit supplier.
>
> (c) Wages of $400 are paid in cash.
>
> (d) Commission of $250 is received by bank transfer. [8]

**Answer**

(a) Debit bank, credit capital.

(b) Debit Malik, credit purchases returns.

(c) Debit wages, credit cash.

(d) Debit bank, credit commission received.

### Practice question 2

> Kamran is a credit customer. On 1 April, he owed $640. During April: Apr 8 credit sales to Kamran $1,200; Apr 12 Kamran returned goods $90; Apr 20 Kamran paid $600 by bank and was allowed $40 discount to settle the opening balance.
>
> (a) Prepare Kamran's account for April. Balance the account and bring down the balance on 1 May. [5]
>
> (b) State what the balance on 1 May represents. [1]

**Answer**

(a)

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Apr 1 Balance b/d 640 | Apr 12 Sales returns 90 |
| Apr 8 Sales 1,200 | Apr 20 Bank 600 |
| | Apr 20 Discount allowed 40 |
| | Apr 30 Balance c/d 1,110 |
| Total 1,840 | Total 1,840 |
| May 1 Balance b/d 1,110 | |

(b) A debit balance of $1,110: Kamran owes the business this amount, so it is an asset (a trade receivable).

## FAQ

### What is the golden rule of double entry?

Every transaction has a debit entry and a credit entry of the same amount in two different accounts. Total debits always equal total credits.

### Is purchases a debit or a credit?

Purchases is an expense, so it is a debit. The credit goes to bank, cash or the supplier's account, depending on how the goods were paid for.

### Why is capital a credit?

Capital is what the business owes the owner. It is treated like a liability of the business, so an increase in capital is a credit.

### What is the difference between balance c/d and balance b/d?

Balance c/d (carried down) is entered on the smaller side to make both totals equal at the end of the period. Balance b/d (brought down) is the same figure entered on the other side at the start of the next period.

### Do I need folio columns in 0452 or 7707?

No. The syllabus says candidates do not need to explain or use folio columns.

### Is double entry the same in IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707?

Yes. Section 2.1 has the same content in both syllabuses for 2027 to 2029, and both have five compulsory 20 mark questions on Paper 2.
