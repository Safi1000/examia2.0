One early mistake in IGCSE 0452 or O Level 7707 Paper 2 usually costs you only the mark for that step. The mark schemes use the own figure rule: if you carry your own wrong figure into a later part and use a correct, complete method, the later marks are still given. You lose more when you hide your workings, add items that do not belong, or use the wrong formula.

## What is the own figure rule in IGCSE and O Level Accounting?

The own figure rule (shown as **OF** in the mark scheme) covers figures you worked out yourself earlier in a question. If one of those figures is wrong, and you then use it correctly in a later step, the examiner marks the later step on your figure, not on the correct one.

The rule is printed in the marking principles at the front of most Paper 2 mark schemes. The June 2025 Paper 21 scheme says that where an answer uses a candidate's own incorrect figure, "full marks will be given if a correct and complete method is used". The same section says the scheme will add guidance where needed and will note any exceptions.

Two other principles sit next to it in the same section:

- A correct answer to a calculation gets full credit even with no working, unless the question asks for working.
- If you use a sound method the scheme does not show, you still earn the marks for each matching stage you reach.

And in the general principles that open every scheme, marks are awarded positively. Marks are not taken away for errors or for things you leave out. So a mistake is never subtracted from your total. It means you do not gain the marks it affects: often one step, sometimes more, as the scheme notes below show.

## Do all 0452 and 7707 Paper 2 mark schemes use it?

I opened all 49 Paper 2 mark schemes from 2022 to 2026 that this site holds: 31 for 0452 (March, June and November series) and 18 for 7707 (June and November).

| What I checked | Result |
|---|---|
| Own figure rule printed in the marking principles | 44 of 49 schemes |
| Answers marked OF somewhere in the scheme | All 49 schemes |
| Principle that marks are not taken away for errors | All 49 schemes |

The five schemes without the printed rule are 0452 March 2025 Paper 22, June 2025 Paper 22, June 2026 Paper 22, and 7707 June 2025 Paper 22 and June 2026 Paper 22. They do not print the section on calculation questions at all, yet they still mark answers OF. The November 2025 0452 Paper 22 scheme goes further and adds a note under one question that the rule applies there too.

Several recent schemes (March 2024, and every scheme from November 2024 to June 2026 on this site) also print a list of the annotations examiners write on scripts. OF is on that list, standing for own figure, next to a separate annotation for an extraneous figure, meaning an item that should not be in the statement.

The syllabus shows how much rides on Paper 2. Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, five compulsory questions of 20 marks each, and 70% of the qualification in the 2027 to 2029 [0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452) and the 7707 syllabus. The papers from 2022 also had 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks on the cover (I checked 0452 March 2022 Paper 22 and 7707 June 2022 Paper 21). Analysis, which the syllabus describes as selecting, calculating and interpreting data, is 25% of Paper 2. Many Paper 2 parts build on an answer from an earlier part.

## How do I read OF in a Paper 2 mark scheme?

When you mark your own past paper, you need to read the scheme the way an examiner does. These are the patterns I found, with a session where you can see each one.

| What the scheme shows | What it means | Where to see it |
|---|---|---|
| (1) after a figure | One mark for that figure, which must be correct | Every scheme |
| (1)OF after a figure | One mark, given on your own figure if your method is right | Every scheme |
| (1) both, or a star | One mark that needs two figures correct | 0452 June 2024 Paper 22 |
| (1) row, or (1)OF row | One mark for a whole row of a statement of changes in equity | 0452 June 2025 Paper 21 |
| (1) whole formula, then (1)OF | One mark for the ratio set up with figures, one for your answer | 0452 June 2024 Paper 21 |
| Max (2) | Several points listed, but only 2 marks available | Every scheme |

### How does one OF mark lead to the next?

The June 2025 0452 Paper 21 scheme shows a chain. A depreciation total in one part was marked OF. That total was then carried into a disposal account in the next part, where the depreciation entry and the final transfer to the income statement were both marked OF again. A student with a slip in the first part could still score every later mark, as long as the account was built correctly.

Ratios work the same way. In the liquid (acid test) ratio, the scheme gave a mark for setting up the formula and an OF mark for the answer in 0452 June 2022 Paper 22, November 2023 Paper 22, June 2024 Paper 21, March 2025 Paper 22 and June 2026 Paper 21. The split was not identical: the June 2022 scheme gave separate marks for the top and bottom of the ratio, while the later ones gave one mark for the whole formula. In March 2026 Paper 22, even the totals inside the formula were marked OF, because they came from the student's own statement of financial position in an earlier part.

## When does the own figure rule not save you?

The general rule promises OF marks only for a correct and complete method. The schemes then add question by question notes, and the June 2026 schemes print the most. Here is what they say, in my words. These notes belong to those questions only; another question can be marked differently.

| What the student did | What the June 2026 scheme said |
|---|---|
| Included an item that does not belong in an account or calculation | No OF balance, total or answer mark (several questions, 0452 Paper 21) |
| Started a bank reconciliation from the wrong figure | Marks for the items, but no OF answer mark (0452 Paper 21) |
| Treated one reconciliation item the wrong way | The answer could still get the OF mark (0452 Paper 21) |
| Left out one asset in a calculation of a club's accumulated fund | The answer could still get the OF mark (0452 Paper 21) |
| Left out purchases or closing inventory in the cost of raw materials | No OF mark for that figure (0452 Paper 22) |
| Turned a ratio formula upside down | No marks for that ratio (0452 Paper 21) |
| Gave a ratio answer with the wrong symbol, such as % for "times" | No OF answer mark (0452 Paper 21) |

The 7707 June 2026 Paper 21 and Paper 22 schemes carry the same notes as the 0452 papers with the same numbers.

Look at the pattern. A wrong figure is usually forgiven. A wrong method is not. Leaving one item out was forgiven in one question and not in another, so do not count on it. Adding an item that does not belong was the most common reason for losing an OF mark in these notes.

The examiner reports show the same thing from the other side. The June 2023 report on 0452 Paper 21 (the same passage is in the 7707 June 2023 report) described suspense accounts that held the two errors that really affected the trial balance, plus items that did not belong there. Those answers lost the OF mark for the difference on the trial balance.

<Tip title="A wrong figure is fine, a wrong method is not">
The own figure rule forgives a number you carried from an earlier part. It does not forgive an extra item or a flipped formula in the part you are doing now, and a missing item can cost the OF mark too.
</Tip>

The hard part is that you cannot see your own mistakes in the exam. That is why the habits later in this article matter more than any single topic.

## What does one mistake really cost? A worked example

Here is a short question I have written, with a marking plan I have made in the style of the Paper 2 schemes. **The plan is my own judgement, not Cambridge's marking**, but each mark sits on a named step, the way the real schemes do.

**Sana runs a shoe shop.** For the year ended 31 December 2026: revenue $67,200, inventory at 1 January 2026 $7,380, purchases $42,180, returns outwards $930, inventory at 31 December 2026 $8,270. Expenses: wages $9,820, rent $3,520, electricity $1,270, depreciation $950. Sana also took drawings of $2,400.

- (a) Calculate the cost of sales. [3]
- (b) Calculate the profit for the year. [3]
- (c) Calculate the gross profit margin and the profit margin, each to two decimal places. [4]

### The correct answer

**(a)** Net purchases = 42,180 − 930 = $41,250

Cost of sales = 7,380 + 41,250 − 8,270 = **$40,360**

**(b)** Gross profit = 67,200 − 40,360 = $26,840

Total expenses = 9,820 + 3,520 + 1,270 + 950 = $15,560

Profit for the year = 26,840 − 15,560 = **$11,280**

Drawings are not an expense. They are taken from capital, so they stay out.

**(c)** Gross profit margin = 26,840 ÷ 67,200 × 100 = **39.94%**

Profit margin = 11,280 ÷ 67,200 × 100 = **16.79%**

### My marking plan (10 marks)

| Part | Mark given for | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Net purchases $41,250 | 1 |
| (a) | Both inventories in the right place | 1 |
| (a) | Cost of sales $40,360 | 1 OF |
| (b) | Gross profit $26,840 | 1 OF |
| (b) | Total expenses $15,560 | 1 |
| (b) | Profit for the year $11,280 | 1 OF |
| (c) | Gross profit margin formula with figures | 1 |
| (c) | Gross profit margin 39.94% | 1 OF |
| (c) | Profit margin formula with figures | 1 |
| (c) | Profit margin 16.79% | 1 OF |

### Four students, four kinds of mistake

**Student A adds returns outwards instead of taking them away, and shows every step.** Cost of sales becomes 7,380 + 42,180 + 930 − 8,270 = $42,220. Gross profit is 67,200 − 42,220 = $24,980, profit is 24,980 − 15,560 = $9,420, gross profit margin 37.17% and profit margin 14.02%. Every later figure is wrong, but every later method is right.

**Student B makes the same slip but writes only "Cost of sales $42,220"** in part (a), with no working. Parts (b) and (c) are set out in full, exactly like Student A.

**Student C gets part (a) right but adds the drawings to the expenses.** Expenses become $17,960, profit becomes 26,840 − 17,960 = $8,880 and profit margin 8,880 ÷ 67,200 × 100 = 13.21%.

**Student D gets every figure right but works the gross profit margin upside down**, as revenue divided by gross profit.

| Student | The mistake | Marks out of 10 | What it cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Returns added, workings shown | 9 | 1 mark: net purchases |
| B | Same slip, no working in (a) | 7 | 3 marks: the whole of part (a) |
| C | Drawings counted as an expense | 8 | 2 marks: expenses total, and no OF for profit |
| D | Gross profit margin formula inverted | 8 | 2 marks: both gross profit margin marks |

How I reached each score:

- **Student A** loses the net purchases mark in (a) and nothing else. The inventories are placed correctly, and the answer $42,220 follows correctly from the student's own figures, so it earns OF. Parts (b) and (c) all earn OF marks.
- **Student B** gets nothing in (a), because the examiner has no step to tick and the final figure is wrong. The OF marks in (b) and (c) are still earned, because $42,220 is visible and used correctly. The same slip cost three times as much as it did for Student A.
- **Student C** loses the expenses total, and then the profit figure gets no OF mark because an extra item sits inside it. That follows the pattern of the June 2026 notes. In (c), the profit margin is a correct method on the student's own profit, so both marks are earned. The extra item cost marks only where it sat.
- **Student D** loses both gross profit margin marks, following the June 2026 Paper 21 note that an inverted formula earns nothing for that ratio.

So "what one mistake costs" depends less on the mistake and more on what the examiner can see. A carried figure with clear working cost Student A one mark out of ten.

## Where do students lose own figure marks?

These come from the examiner reports, with the session, paper and question each comment was about.

| Session and paper | What the report said |
|---|---|
| 0452 June 2022 Paper 22, Q5(c) (also 7707 June 2022 Paper 22) | Show what each liquid ratio total is made of so the OF answer can be credited; extra items such as non-current assets and reserves were too common |
| 0452 June 2022 Paper 22, general comments | Combining figures into one net amount could cost all 5 marks on Q3(c) |
| 0452 June 2023 Paper 21, Q3(b) (also 7707 June 2023 Paper 21) | Items that did not belong in the suspense account cost the OF mark for the trial balance difference |
| 0452 March 2023 Paper 22, Q4(a) | Common ratio errors: no workings shown, and extra items inside the calculations |
| 7707 November 2023 Paper 22, Q4(c) | One net figure for the profit adjustments, with no workings, left examiners nothing to credit |
| 7707 November 2023 Paper 23, Q4(b) | Some candidates put their own difference in the suspense account, although the question gave the trial balance difference |
| 0452 March 2025 Paper 22, Q4(a) | With no workings, examiners could not easily tell which figures the ratios used |
| 0452 March 2026 Paper 22, Q5(c) | Weaker liquid ratio answers included non-current assets, missed the two decimal places or left out the ":1" |

The reports also show the rule working in students' favour:

- In 0452 June 2023 Paper 22, Q3(a), subscriptions accounts often left out the irrecoverable debts, and the wrong balance still earned an OF answer mark.
- In 7707 November 2023 Paper 22, Q5(a), candidates who mixed up opening and closing inventory got the gross margin wrong, yet the profit margin could still earn credit on their own figure.
- In 0452 November 2022 Paper 23, Q4, the capital employed for the return on capital employed was the candidate's own total from the statement of changes in equity, plus the debentures.
- In 0452 June 2025 Paper 22, Q2(b), the report said the partner's current account scores well because it is built on own figure answers from part (a). Full marks were often seen.

That last report also shows the rule going unused. In Q5(c) of the same paper, answers did not carry their own sales figure from part (b) into the calculation, and the report rated the part poorly answered overall. The OF route was there, and answers did not take it.

## How should I set out workings so OF marks can be given?

The March 2022 report on 0452 Paper 22 made the point that when you show your calculations, part of the marks can still be given for a wrong final answer. This is how I teach my students to make that happen.

1. **Write every figure you add or take away.** Never combine three numbers in your head and write one. A visible step can earn a mark; a hidden one cannot.
2. **Label each line.** "Net purchases 41,250" tells the examiner which step you did. A bare number may not.
3. **Copy your own figure forward, even if you doubt it.** If part (b) needs your answer to part (a), use it. Do not leave a gap because you think (a) is wrong.
4. **Use figures the question gives you.** If the question states a figure, such as a trial balance difference, use it instead of your own version.
5. **Before you total, check each item belongs.** Drawings, non-current assets in a liquid ratio and capital balances in a current account are all extra items that examiner reports have named.
6. **Write the formula with figures, then the answer.** In the ratio schemes above, the set up line earns its own mark.
7. **Follow the form the question asks for.** Two decimal places, a % sign, ":1" or "days".
8. **If you change an answer, cross out the old version clearly.** Then the examiner knows which figure to carry.

<Tip title="Never leave a later part blank">
If your answer to part (a) looks wrong, still carry it into part (b), label it, and use the right method. A blank part earns nothing; a correct method on your own figure can still earn the OF marks.
</Tip>

## Practice questions

**1.** In part (a) of a question you found total current assets of $18,640. The correct figure was $19,180. Inventory is $5,320 and current liabilities are $12,470. Calculate the liquid ratio to two decimal places that would earn the OF answer mark.

**2.** Use Sana's question above. A student leaves depreciation out of the expenses but does everything else correctly. Under my marking plan, what is the profit margin, and how many marks out of 10 does the student score?

**3.** For each habit, say whether it protects OF marks or puts them at risk:

- (i) writing "Cost of sales 42,220" with no working
- (ii) using your own wrong answer from part (a) in part (b)
- (iii) including a non-current asset in the liquid ratio

### Answers

**1.** (18,640 − 5,320) : 12,470 = 13,320 : 12,470 = **1.07:1**. The correct answer would have been 13,860 : 12,470 = 1.11:1, but 1.07:1 earns the OF mark because the method is right.

**2.** Expenses = 9,820 + 3,520 + 1,270 = $14,610. Profit = 26,840 − 14,610 = $12,230. Profit margin = 12,230 ÷ 67,200 × 100 = **18.20%**. Under my plan the student loses only the expenses total and scores **9 out of 10**: leaving one item out is not an extra item, so the profit and the margin keep their OF marks. A real scheme can decide this differently, as the June 2026 notes show.

**3.** (i) At risk: if the figure is wrong, there is no step to mark. (ii) Protects: this is exactly what the rule rewards. (iii) At risk: an extraneous item can cost the OF answer mark.

## FAQ

### What does OF mean in an accounting mark scheme?

OF stands for own figure. A mark labelled OF is given on your own figure from earlier working, as long as your method for that step is correct and complete.

### How many marks will I lose for one mistake in IGCSE Accounting Paper 2?

Often just the mark for that step, if your workings are clear and the later methods are right. You can lose more if the mistake is hidden in a single unexplained figure, if you added an item that does not belong, or if you used a wrong formula.

### Does the own figure rule apply to every question?

The rule is printed in the marking principles of 44 of the 49 Paper 2 schemes from 2022 to 2026 on this site, and all 49 mark some answers OF. But the schemes add notes and exceptions for single questions, so check each scheme when you mark your own work.

### Do I get marks for a correct answer with no working?

Yes. The marking principles in 44 of the 49 schemes say a correct answer gets full credit without working, unless the question asks for working. A wrong answer with no working gives the examiner nothing to credit.

### Do examiners take marks away for mistakes?

No. Every scheme says marks are awarded positively and are not deducted for errors or omissions. You simply do not gain the marks your mistake affects.

### Is the own figure rule the same for O Level 7707 and IGCSE 0452?

Yes, in the schemes I checked. The 7707 schemes print the same rule (except June 2025 and June 2026 Paper 22, matching the June gaps in 0452), and the 7707 June 2026 papers carry the same question notes as the 0452 papers with the same numbers.
