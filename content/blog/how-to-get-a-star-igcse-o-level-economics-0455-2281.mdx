An A\* in IGCSE Economics 0455 or O Level Economics 2281 needed between 112 and 126 out of 150 in the June and November sessions from June 2024 to June 2026, which is about 75% to 84%. Aim for the top of that range on both papers. The marks that separate an A from an A\* are mostly lost in Paper 2: undeveloped points, ignored source material and one sided discuss answers.

In every session above, the 2281 line matches the 0455 line for the same variant. Your variant is the second digit of your paper number: Papers 12 and 22 are variant 2. The A\* line moved by as much as 11 marks between variants in the same session. In June 2025, variant 1 needed 114 and variant 2 needed 125. Cambridge sets each line after marking, based on how hard that set of papers turned out to be. You cannot choose your variant, so plan for the highest line, not the lowest. You can check every grade, not just A\*, on the [0455 grade thresholds page](/grade-thresholds/igcse-economics-0455) and the [2281 grade thresholds page](/grade-thresholds/o-level-economics-2281).

### Why are the Economics lines so high?

The A\* line is a weighted total, and the tables say grade A\* does not exist for a single paper. Cambridge sets the A\* line for the whole syllabus. On Paper 2 alone, the grade A line in November 2025 was 58 to 60 out of 90, around 65%. Yet the overall A\* line that session was 122 to 126 out of 150, over 80%. So an A\* needs a strong Paper 1 as well as a strong Paper 2. My [guide to how grade thresholds work](/blog/cambridge-grade-thresholds-explained) explains why there is no fixed percentage for any grade.

## How do Paper 1 and Paper 2 combine?

Every session in the tables above used the old papers. These are the facts from the threshold tables and the syllabus changes page:

| Paper (up to 2026) | Time | Raw marks | Counts as |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Paper 1: Multiple Choice | 45 minutes | 30 | 45 of 150 (30%) |
| Paper 2: Structured Questions | 2 hours 15 minutes | 90 | 105 of 150 (70%) |

To reach a total out of 150, your Paper 1 mark is multiplied by 1.5 and your Paper 2 mark by about 1.17, then the two are added. That is my working from the 150 maximum, and it reproduces the published lines. So in the old format, **one Paper 1 mark was worth 1% of your total**, and one Paper 2 mark was worth about 0.78%.

### Worked example: is this student on track for an A\*?

A student scores 24 out of 30 on Paper 1 and 68 out of 90 on Paper 2. These are my own figures.

**Step 1.** Paper 1 weighted: 24 × 1.5 = 36.

**Step 2.** Paper 2 weighted: 68 × 105 ÷ 90 = 79.3.

**Step 3.** Total: 36 + 79.3 = **115.3 out of 150**.

That clears the A\* line only in sessions where it was 115 or lower: June 2025 variants 1 and 3, June 2026 variant 2 and June 2024 variant 1. Against the highest line in the tables, 126, the student is about 11 weighted marks short. Paper 1 alone cannot close that gap, because only 6 marks are left there. It would take 10 more marks on Paper 2, or a mix of both.

By my working, 26 out of 30 on Paper 1 (39) and 75 out of 90 on Paper 2 (87.5) gave 126.5, which cleared every A\* line in the tables. That is about 87% on Paper 1 and 83% on Paper 2.

### What changes from 2027?

The 2027 to 2029 syllabuses keep two compulsory papers and the same 30% and 70% weightings, but the papers change.

| Paper (from 2027) | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Paper 1: Multiple Choice | 1 hour | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2: Structured Questions | 2 hours | 80 | 70% |

The changes page says Paper 1 goes from 30 to 40 questions, and Paper 2 goes from 90 to 80 marks with Question 1 in Section A worth less. The weighting of AO3, evaluation, has gone down, and knowledge and analysis have gone up. The syllabus gives the new split: on Paper 2, knowledge is 40%, analysis 45% and evaluation 15%.

No threshold tables exist yet for the new papers, so nobody can tell you the 2027 A\* line. One thing you can work out now. If the 30% and 70% weightings hold, one Paper 1 mark is worth 0.75% of the total (30 ÷ 40) and one Paper 2 mark about 0.88% (70 ÷ 80). That is my arithmetic, not a Cambridge figure. In the old format a Paper 1 mark was worth more. From 2027 a Paper 2 mark is worth a little more, so Paper 2 matters even more than before.

## Where is an A\* won on Paper 1?

Paper 1 is 40 multiple choice questions from 2027, on the whole syllabus, and calculators are allowed. The syllabus says questions may need calculations and diagram analysis. On the old papers, the examiner reports show how much the average candidate dropped. The June 2023 0455 report says 17,146 candidates sat Paper 12 and the mean mark was 20.8 out of 30, about 69%.

An A\* candidate needs to be well above that. The June 2023 0455 reports for Papers 11 and 12 gave the same two pieces of advice: know every topic, especially numerical and diagram work, and read each item carefully before choosing. Paper 1 assesses only knowledge and analysis, so there is no evaluation to hide behind. Each wrong answer is a topic you did not know well enough or a question you misread.

How I teach it:

1. Cover every syllabus topic. Paper 1 is the paper where a skipped topic costs you most directly.
2. Practise calculations until they are automatic: percentages, percentage changes, elasticity and index numbers.
3. Mark every wrong answer with a reason: did not know it, misread it, or fell for a near miss option.
4. Time yourself at 1.5 minutes a question, which is 60 minutes for 40 questions.

My [Paper 1 MCQ guide](/blog/o-level-igcse-economics-paper-1-mcq-tips) has a method for each question type and the common traps.

## Where is an A\* won on Paper 2?

From 2027, Paper 2 is 80 marks in 2 hours. Section A is one compulsory question worth 20 marks, with parts (a) to (f), based on source material about a real economic situation. In Section B you answer three questions from four. Each is worth 20 marks and has four parts. In the specimen paper those parts are worth 2, 4, 6 and 8 marks.

| Part (specimen) | Command word | What an A\* answer needs |
|---|---|---|
| (a) 2 marks | Define, identify | A precise answer, nothing more |
| (b) 4 marks | Explain | Two points, each identified and then explained |
| (c) 6 marks | Analyse | Chains of reasoning, each link explained |
| (d) 8 marks | Discuss | Level 3: both sides considered and developed |

The (d) parts are marked with the levels in Table A of the specimen mark scheme: Level 1 is 1 to 2 marks, Level 2 is 3 to 5, and Level 3 is 6 to 8. Level 2 allows an answer that lacks depth or is one sided. **Level 3 needs both sides considered and developed**, though one side may have more depth. Three (d) parts are 24 marks, 30% of Paper 2. An A\* candidate cannot sit at Level 2 on them.

### Time plan for the 2027 Paper 2

My advice: 120 minutes for 80 marks is 1.5 minutes a mark. Section A and each Section B question are worth 20 marks, so each gets 30 minutes.

| Part of the paper | Marks | Time (my plan) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Section A, Question 1 | 20 | 30 minutes |
| Section B, first question | 20 | 30 minutes |
| Section B, second question | 20 | 30 minutes |
| Section B, third question | 20 | 30 minutes |

Inside each Section B question, give the (d) part about 12 minutes, because it carries 8 of the 20 marks. Use the first two minutes of Section B to choose your three questions by reading the (d) parts first. The June 2022 2281 report (Paper 21) gave that same advice: look carefully at the (d) parts, as they carry the most marks.

### What does a Level 2 and a Level 3 discuss answer look like?

**Question (mine):** Discuss whether or not firms gain when the value of their country's currency falls. [8]

A **Level 2** answer explains one side well:

> A fall in the exchange rate makes exports cheaper in foreign currency. Foreign buyers demand more of the country's exports, so exporting firms sell more, their revenue rises and they may employ more workers. Imported goods also become dearer, so domestic consumers switch to home produced goods, which helps firms that compete with imports.

That is clear analysis, but it is one sided, so it cannot pass 5 marks.

A **Level 3** answer adds a developed second side. I also end with a judgement, which Table A does not demand but which shows evaluation:

> However, firms that buy imported raw materials or machinery now pay more for them. Their costs of production rise, so their profits may fall, or they may raise their prices and lose sales. The higher price of imports can also cause cost push inflation, which may lead workers to demand higher wages. Whether firms benefit overall depends on how much they export compared with how much they import, and on how price elastic the demand for their exports is. A firm that exports most of its output and uses local inputs is likely to gain. A firm that relies on imported inputs and sells at home is likely to lose.

The second side uses new reasons. It does not just reverse the first side. That matters: the March 2026 0455 report (Paper 22) told candidates to avoid reversed reasoning, and said stating the opposite of an earlier explanation should not be the approach.

<Tip title="Two means two">
When a question says two, it is printed in bold for a reason. The June 2025 0455 report (Paper 21) said this was often overlooked, and that in one question numerous candidates gave four aims when two were asked for. Write two points and explain each one fully.
</Tip>

## Where do strong students lose the marks that cost an A\*?

These come from the 0455 and 2281 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026. The middle column is my summary in my own words.

| Mistake | What to do instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Identifying a point but not developing it | After each point, say what it leads to and why | 0455 June 2023 Paper 21 (most candidates on 5(b)); 0455 June 2025 Paper 21 (few (b) answers had enough development) |
| Not using the source material in Section A | Quote the data, then interpret it | 0455 June 2023 Paper 21 (many, on 1(f) and 1(g)); 0455 June 2025 Paper 23 (a significant number); 0455 March 2026 Paper 22 |
| Answering all four Section B questions | Answer three and spend the time on depth | 0455 November 2022 Paper 22 and 2281 November 2022 Paper 22 (an increase in the proportion doing this) |
| Too long on 2 and 4 mark parts, so the (d) part is rushed | Keep (a) and (b) answers short and precise | 0455 November 2022 Paper 21 (a considerable number) |
| Discuss answers in general terms, without economic concepts | Build each side from economic analysis | 0455 June 2023 Paper 22 (a number of candidates, in that and earlier sessions); 0455 November 2022 Paper 22 (a number just stated points) |
| A conclusion that only repeats earlier points | Make the judgement add something: what the outcome depends on | 0455 June 2025 Papers 21 and 23 |
| Writing productivity when you mean production | Productivity is output per worker or per hour | 0455 June 2023 Paper 22; 2281 November 2022 Paper 22 (some) |
| Confusing a budget deficit with a current account deficit | Budget: government revenue and spending. Current account: trade in goods and services, and income flows | 0455 June 2023 Paper 22; 2281 November 2022 Paper 22; 0455 June 2025 Paper 22 |

Two of those are knowledge slips, not technique. They still cost A\* candidates marks, because they turn a correct chain of reasoning into a wrong one.

## How should you use past papers to reach an A\*?

The old papers are still the best practice you have. The changes page says the new assessment objectives test most of the same knowledge and skills. It also lists the content that was removed or significantly reduced, including fixed exchange rates, principles of taxation and deflation, so skip old questions on those.

How I would use them, as my advice:

1. **Learn the topic first**, then answer that topic's questions from the [0455 past papers by topic](/past-papers/igcse-economics-0455/topics).
2. **Mark with the mark scheme**, not by feel. For every lost mark, write which skill was missing: precision, development or the second side.
3. **Rewrite one (d) answer a week** until it meets the Level 3 description.
4. **Sit full timed papers** in the last two months, using the 2027 specimen papers for the new timings.
5. **Track your weighted total** after each full paper. The [grade predictor](/tools/grade-predictor) does the arithmetic for you.

## Can you answer these practice questions?

All three are my own. Use the old format weightings from this article.

**1.** A student scores 27 out of 30 on Paper 1 and 70 out of 90 on Paper 2 in June 2025, variant 2. Did they reach an A\*?

**Answer.** Paper 1: 27 × 1.5 = 40.5. Paper 2: 70 × 105 ÷ 90 = 81.7. Total = 122.2. The June 2025 variant 2 line was 125, so this is an **A, not an A\***. It is 2.8 weighted marks short.

**2.** In question 1, how many more Paper 2 marks would the student have needed?

**Answer.** Each Paper 2 mark is worth 105 ÷ 90, about 1.17 weighted marks. 2.8 ÷ 1.17 = 2.4, so **3 more Paper 2 marks** (73 out of 90) would have done it: 40.5 + 85.2 = 125.7.

**3.** Under the 2027 format, which is worth more of your final grade: one Paper 1 question or one Paper 2 mark? Assume the syllabus weightings of 30% and 70% are applied.

**Answer.** One Paper 1 question is 30% ÷ 40 = 0.75%. One Paper 2 mark is 70% ÷ 80 = 0.875%. **One Paper 2 mark is worth slightly more.**

## FAQ

### What percentage is an A\* in IGCSE Economics?

There is no fixed percentage. In the June and November sessions from June 2024 to June 2026, the 0455 A\* line ran from 112 to 126 out of 150, about 75% to 84%. In March 2026 it was 130.

### Is an A\* harder in O Level 2281 than in IGCSE 0455?

Not on the lines in this article. In every session I compared, the 2281 A\* line matched the 0455 line for the same variant.

### What will the A\* line be in 2027?

Nobody knows yet. The papers change to 40 and 80 marks, and Cambridge will publish new threshold tables after the first session.

### Can I get an A\* with a weak Paper 1?

It is hard. By my working, 75 out of 90 on Paper 2 with 26 out of 30 on Paper 1 cleared every line in the tables. A Paper 1 score in the low 20s leaves you needing a very high Paper 2.

### Do I need a conclusion in my 8 mark answers?

Not always. The June 2025 0455 report (Paper 23) said an excellent answer is possible without one, and that conclusions repeating earlier points add nothing. A judgement that explains what the outcome depends on is worth writing.

### Which sessions are in the threshold tables here?

June 2024, November 2024, June 2025, November 2025 and June 2026, taken from Cambridge's grade threshold tables for 0455 and 2281. The March 2025 and March 2026 lines for 0455 are given in the text.
