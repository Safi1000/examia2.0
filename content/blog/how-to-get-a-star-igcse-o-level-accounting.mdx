An A\* in IGCSE Accounting 0452 or O Level Accounting 7707 needed between 93 and 111 out of 143 in the last four sessions, which is roughly 65% to 78%. Aim for the top of that range: by my working, about 30 out of 35 on Paper 1 and 75 out of 100 on Paper 2 cleared every A\* line. Most lost marks come from Paper 2 layout slips and general discuss answers.

The two syllabuses share the same papers, and their lines match variant for variant. Your variant is the second digit of your paper number: Papers 12 and 22 are variant 2.

The gap between variants is large. In June 2026, variant 1 needed 100 for an A\* and variant 2 needed 111. That is not unfair. Cambridge sets each line after marking, based on how hard that set of papers turned out to be. You cannot know that in advance, so plan for the highest line, not the lowest.

## How do Paper 1 and Paper 2 combine?

Both syllabuses have two compulsory papers. These are the facts from the 2027 to 2029 syllabuses:

| Paper | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Paper 1: Multiple Choice | 1 hour 30 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2: Structured Written Paper | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 70% |

Paper 2 has five compulsory questions worth 20 marks each. From 2027, Paper 1 grows from 35 to 40 questions and from 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes. Paper 2 does not change.

The thresholds above are from the old 35 question Paper 1. Cambridge scaled it so Paper 1 made up 30% of the 143 and Paper 2 made up 70%. The exact scaling below is my estimate, worked back from the published tables. It matches the published totals to within half a mark:

- each Paper 1 mark was worth about 1.23 weighted marks
- each Paper 2 mark was worth about 1.00 weighted mark

So Paper 2 decides your grade, because it carries 70% of the total. Paper 1 still matters: one multiple choice mark is worth slightly more than one Paper 2 mark.

The syllabus also gives the weighting of the skills it tests. On Paper 2, knowledge and understanding is 60%, analysis 25% and evaluation 15%. Evaluation (judging information and reaching a conclusion) does not appear on Paper 1 at all. In my experience, that 15% is where A grade students stop short of A\*.

## What does an A\* look like in real marks?

A student once asked online whether about 70 out of 100 on Paper 2 and 27 out of 35 on Paper 1 would be an A\*. It is a good question to work through, because it shows why a raw percentage can mislead.

**Step 1: weight each paper, using my estimated factors.**

| Paper | Raw mark | Weighted mark |
|---|---:|---:|
| Paper 1 | 27 out of 35 | 27 × 1.2257 = 33.1 |
| Paper 2 | 70 out of 100 | 70 × 1.001 = 70.1 |
| Total | | 103.2 out of 143 |

**Step 2: compare with the A\* lines.** A total of about 103 clears the A\* line of 100 (June 2026 variants 1 and 3), 99 (June 2025 variants 1 and 3) and 93 (June 2024 variant 1). It falls short of 105, 107, 109 and 111 on the other variants.

So, on this estimate, the same performance was an A\* in 5 of the 11 IGCSE combinations and an A in the other 6. That is the honest answer to "is 70% an A\*?": sometimes.

**Step 3: find the safe target.** The highest line was 111. With 30 out of 35 on Paper 1 (36.8 weighted) and 75 out of 100 on Paper 2 (75.1 weighted), the total is about 111.9. That clears every A\* line in the table. With 29 on Paper 1 and 75 on Paper 2, the total is about 110.6, too close to 111 to rely on.

From 2027, Paper 1 is out of 40, so the exact weighting will change. The percentages still give a fair target: about 86% on Paper 1 (34 or 35 out of 40) and 75% on Paper 2.

## Where do A grade students lose the marks that cost them an A\*?

Students aiming for A\* usually know the content. The marks go on small, repeated slips. These come from the June 2025 and March 2026 examiner reports for 0452.

| Mistake | What examiners saw | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Abbreviations in accounts | Short forms such as bbd, NP and MV were not accepted | Write "Balance b/d", "Profit for the year" and every account name in full |
| No clear recommendation | Discuss answers listed points but did not decide | End with one sentence: "I would advise ... because ..." |
| A memorised answer | General statements that did not relate to the scenario given | Use the names, figures and situation in the question |
| Missing opening balance | Left out of supplier accounts and the provision for depreciation account | Start every ledger account with the balance given |
| Contra entry missed | A transfer between cash and bank recorded on one side only | Record it in both the cash and bank columns |
| Cash with a credit balance | Students did not notice an impossible result | Treat a credit cash balance as a sign of an error, and find it |
| Discount worked out wrongly | Cash discount was calculated incorrectly in the cash book | Calculate the discount first, then post the net amount |
| Untidy or unclear work | Some scripts were very untidy and hard to read | Neat columns, labelled workings, a note if you continue on another page |

The June 2025 report said many candidates did not know how to prepare an opening journal entry. That topic is small but it is in the syllabus. A\* students cover every topic, because every question on both papers is compulsory.

On Paper 1, the March 2026 report noted that candidates did less well when the wording was less familiar. Two traps from the reports: a trial balance includes opening inventory, not closing inventory. And when an entry is posted to the wrong side, the correction adds the two amounts. It does not subtract them.

This is where I see most students get stuck. They can do a full set of accounts, but they lose four or five marks across the paper on layout and on discuss answers that never reach a decision.

On the site's tagged 0452 Paper 2 questions from 2022 to 2026, two topics appear most often: books of prime entry (2.3) and correction of errors (3.2). Manufacturing accounts, limited companies and partnerships come next. On Paper 1, questions are spread across the whole syllabus.

## How should you answer Paper 1?

You have 90 minutes for 40 questions from 2027, which is 2 minutes 15 seconds each. That is enough time if you work, rather than guess.

- Do the calculation in the margin before you read the options. The wrong options are usually built from common slips.
- For "which entry" questions, write the debit and credit yourself first.
- Read the last line of the question twice. "Which is **not** correct" catches many students.
- Never leave a question blank.

An A\* student should treat it as a paper to win, not survive. I give the full method and practice questions in my [Paper 1 multiple choice guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-paper-1-mcq-tips).

## How should you answer Paper 2?

Paper 2 gives you 105 minutes for 100 marks. That is about a minute a mark, or 21 minutes for each 20 mark question.

1. **Read all the data before you start.** The June 2025 report asked candidates to read every piece of information and to be clear on what each question requires.
2. **Set out the full format.** Headings, dates, account names and totals all carry marks.
3. **Show workings next to the answer.** A wrong final figure with a clear working can still earn marks.
4. **Answer discuss questions in three parts.** Points for, points against, then a decision with a reason. Give the number of points the question asks for, which is often two each.
5. **Use the scenario in every point.** Name the business, use its figures, and say what the change means for that owner.
6. **Check your balances.** If something cannot be true, such as cash overdrawn, go back.

A discuss answer for an A\* is short but complete. Here is the shape I teach for a five mark question:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Points for | Each advantage, explained for this business |
| Points against | Each disadvantage, explained for this business |
| Decision | "I would advise ... because ..." |

<Tip title="One decision, one reason">
Never write "it depends" as your conclusion. Choose, then give the single reason that matters most in this business.
</Tip>

## What does a 12 week plan to A\* look like?

This plan assumes you have studied the whole syllabus once and have about 12 weeks before your exam. The [revision planner](/tools/revision-planner) can turn it into a daily timetable for your own dates.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Double entry, books of prime entry, the trial balance (topics 1.1 to 3.1) |
| 2 | Correction of errors, bank reconciliation, control accounts (3.2 to 3.4) |
| 3 | Depreciation, accruals and prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory (4.1 to 4.5) |
| 4 | Sole trader financial statements with all adjustments (5.1) |
| 5 | Partnerships and limited companies (5.2, 5.3) |
| 6 | Manufacturing accounts, clubs, incomplete records (5.4 to 5.6) |
| 7 | Ratios, interpretation, users of accounts, concepts, ethics, technology (6.1 to 7.3) |
| 8 | One full Paper 1 and one full Paper 2, timed, marked with the mark scheme |
| 9 | Two timed papers; redo every question below 70% |
| 10 | Two timed papers; practise discuss answers from your weakest topics |
| 11 | Two timed papers on the newest sessions you have |
| 12 | Formats, theory answers, one last paper early in the week, then rest |

For weeks 1 to 7, finish each day with at least one past paper question on that topic. Keep a mistakes list. Before every timed paper, read it.

When you mark a paper, record Paper 1 and Paper 2 separately. Then work out your weighted total as in the example above. If one paper is far below the other, the next week goes there.

## Practice questions

**Question 1.** A student scores 25 out of 35 on Paper 1 and 64 out of 100 on Paper 2 under the old format. Using the June 2026 variant 1 lines (A\* 100, A 80), what grade is this, and roughly how many more Paper 2 marks would reach A\*?

**Answer.** Paper 1: 25 × 1.2257 = 30.6. Paper 2: 64 × 1.001 = 64.1. Total about 94.7, which is an A. The student needs 100, so the gap is about 5.3 weighted marks. Each Paper 2 mark is worth about one weighted mark, so around 6 more Paper 2 marks are needed. This is an estimate, so aim for 7 to be safe.

**Question 2.** A customer, Imran, paid $320. The bookkeeper debited Imran's account with $320. What entry corrects Imran's account?

**Answer.** Credit Imran's account with $640. The first $320 cancels the wrong debit and the second $320 records the payment correctly.

**Question 3.** Hamid is a tailor who keeps all his accounting records by hand in paper books. He is thinking of moving them to a digital application that stores his data with a cloud service, for a monthly fee. Advise Hamid whether to change. Give two points for and two points against. (5 marks)

**Answer (model).** For: the application adds up and posts the entries, so Hamid should make fewer arithmetic errors. A cloud copy also means his records survive a fire or theft at the shop. Against: the monthly fee is a new expense that reduces his profit. Hamid would also need time to learn the system and enter his existing records. Decision: I would advise Hamid to change, because losing his only paper records would harm the business far more than the monthly fee.

## FAQ

### Is 70% an A\* in IGCSE Accounting?

Sometimes. In the last four sessions the A\* line ran from 93 to 111 out of 143, roughly 65% to 78% of the weighted total. A weighted 70% cleared some variants and missed others.

### Are the 0452 and 7707 thresholds the same?

In the sessions above, yes: each 7707 variant had the same A\* and A lines as the matching 0452 variant. 7707 is graded A\* to E, while 0452 goes down to G.

### Which paper matters more for an A\*?

Paper 2. It is 70% of the total and has the discuss questions, where I see A grade students lose the marks that separate A from A\*.

### Will the thresholds change in 2027?

Expect them to differ, because Paper 1 changes from 35 to 40 marks. Cambridge sets the new lines after the exams, so aim at the highest recent line as a percentage.

### How many past papers should I do for an A\*?

There is no fixed number. In my plan above, you sit timed papers every week for the last five weeks, and you mark each one properly with the mark scheme.

### Can I get an A\* if Paper 1 goes badly?

It is possible but hard. A lost Paper 1 mark is worth about 1.23 weighted marks, so a five mark drop on Paper 1 needs about six extra marks on Paper 2.
