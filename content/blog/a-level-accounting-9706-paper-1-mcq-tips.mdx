AS Level Accounting 9706 Paper 1 is 30 multiple choice questions in 1 hour, worth 30 marks and 28% of the AS Level. That is two minutes a question. It tests AS content only: 22 questions on financial accounting and 8 on costing. To score well, work out each answer on the question paper before you read the options.

## How long should each question take?

Sixty minutes for 30 questions is two minutes each. In practice, the questions are not equal. A definition or concept question takes under a minute. A suspense account or partnership question can take three.

This is the pacing I give my students:

1. **First pass, about 45 minutes.** Answer everything you can do with confidence. If a question will clearly take more than three minutes, circle it and move on.
2. **Checkpoint.** Aim to reach question 15 by about the 25 minute mark. If you are behind, speed up on the short questions, not the long ones.
3. **Second pass, about 10 minutes.** Go back to the circled questions.
4. **Last 5 minutes.** Check that every answer on the sheet is in the right row. Fill any blanks with your best guess.

## Which question types come up, and how do I answer each one?

Almost every question is one of the types below. For each one, I give you the working to write before you look at the options. The wrong options are built from common slips, so if you look first, a wrong answer that seems familiar will pull you in.

### Calculations from incomplete records

These give you some figures and ask for a missing one: revenue, purchases, cost of sales or closing inventory.

1. Write the cost of sales layout: opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory.
2. Fill in what you know.
3. If a mark-up or margin is given, write which one it is before you use it.
4. Mark-up is on cost of sales. Margin is on revenue. A 25% mark-up is a 20% margin.

### Errors and suspense accounts

1. For each error, ask: did it make the debit and credit totals different? If not, it does not go through the suspense account.
2. For each error that does, write the correcting journal (debit and credit) in the margin.
3. Post only the suspense side of each journal into a small T account.
4. Balance the T account. The balancing figure is the original suspense balance.

Errors of omission, commission, principle, original entry, reversal and compensating errors do not affect the trial balance. They never touch the suspense account.

### Bank reconciliation

1. Draw two lists: "update the cash book" and "only in the reconciliation".
2. Bank charges, interest, direct debits, standing orders and credit transfers the business did not know about go in the cash book list.
3. Unpresented cheques, deposits not yet credited and errors made by the bank go in the reconciliation list.
4. Read which figure the question wants. The balance shown in the statement of financial position is the updated cash book balance.

### Control accounts

1. Draw the T account: sales ledger control has the opening balance and credit sales on the debit side; purchases ledger control has them on the credit side.
2. Cross out any item that does not belong. Cash sales, cash purchases and returns that belong to the other ledger are common extras.
3. Add up each side and find the balance.

### Adjustments to the financial statements

Accruals, prepayments, irrecoverable debts, allowances for irrecoverable debts, depreciation and inventory valuation.

1. Write the figure **for the year**, not the amount paid or received.
2. For an "effect" question, write two short lines: the effect on profit, then the effect on assets or liabilities.
3. For depreciation and disposals, write each year on its own line with the date, so you know which years have been charged.

### Partnerships and limited companies

1. For partnerships, list the order: profit for the year, then interest on drawings added, then salaries, interest on capital and the residual share.
2. Interest on a partner's loan is an expense. It comes off before the appropriation account.
3. If there is no partnership agreement, the Partnership Act 1890 applies: profits shared equally, no salaries, no interest on capital, no interest charged on drawings, and 5% a year on partners' loans.
4. For share issues, write three figures: number of shares × nominal value, number of shares × premium, and total cash.

### Ratios

1. Write the formula from the syllabus appendix in words.
2. Put the numbers in.
3. Check the units: %, times, days or a ratio such as 1.5 : 1.

There is a full section on the appendix below.

### Costing questions

1. **Absorption against marginal:** write the change in inventory in units, then multiply by the fixed overhead per unit. That is the difference in profit.
2. **Break-even:** write contribution per unit first (selling price − variable cost), then fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit.
3. **Overhead apportionment:** write the basis next to each overhead (floor area for rent, value of machinery for insurance) before you calculate.
4. **Limiting factor:** rank products by contribution for each unit of the scarce resource. Do not rank by profit, or by contribution per unit of product.

### Which statement is correct?

Some questions give two to four numbered statements, and the options are combinations such as "1 and 2 only".

1. Mark each statement ✓ or ✗ on the question paper on its own, before you read the options.
2. Then find the option that matches your ticks exactly.
3. Be suspicious of absolute words such as "eliminate", "ensure" and "always". A bank reconciliation helps detect fraud. It cannot remove it.

<Tip title="Write the working, then look">
Cover the four options with your hand or ruler while you work. Uncover them only when you have a figure or a ✓ and ✗ for every statement. If your answer is not there, recheck your working, not the options.
</Tip>

## Where do candidates lose marks in 9706 Paper 1?

I read the Paper 1 sections of every 9706 examiner report on file from 2022 to 2026: March, June and November 2022, March, June and November 2023, March 2025 and March 2026. The 2022 reports come from the last year of the previous syllabus, so I only use the patterns that still match the current topics.

Where the reports give a mean score, it was between 14 and 18 out of 30 for each variant. So the average candidate lost between 12 and 16 marks, and the same topics come up again and again.

### Mark-up and margin mixed up

This appears in June 2022 and twice in March 2026. Students apply a mark-up when the question gives a margin, or the other way round. In March 2026 it caught students in a gross profit question and in a selling price question. The key messages of that report asked candidates to understand the difference and be able to apply it in different ways.

**The fix:** before you calculate, write "on cost" or "on revenue" next to the percentage.

### Suspense accounts going the wrong way

Suspense account questions feature in March 2022, June 2022, March 2023 and March 2026. The common errors:

- Putting an error of omission through the suspense account, when it does not affect the trial balance at all.
- Getting the direction of the correction wrong. If a debit balance was entered as a credit, the correction is double the amount.
- Forgetting that a bank overdraft is a credit balance.

### Bank reconciliations and which balance to use

Bank reconciliation questions were reported as hard in March 2023, June 2023, November 2023 and March 2026. Students adjust the cash book for unpresented cheques, or for errors the bank made in its own records. Others give the bank statement balance when the question wants the figure for the statement of financial position. That figure is the updated cash book balance.

### Words like "eliminate" and "ensure"

In November 2023, more than half of candidates on one variant chose an option saying a bank reconciliation would remove the chance of staff fraud. March 2025 had the same trap with computerised accounting. Reconciliations and computer checks reduce errors and fraud. They cannot make sure there are none. The March 2026 report warned that "some options are only partially correct".

### Partnership profit shares

Partnership questions are discussed in the June 2022, June 2023 and November 2023 reports. The traps:

- Treating interest on a partner's loan as an appropriation. It is an expense, taken off before profit is shared.
- Giving only the residual share when the question asks for a partner's **total** share, which also includes their salary and interest on capital.
- Not knowing the Partnership Act 1890 rules, especially the 5% interest on loans and no interest on capital.
- Forgetting that a credit balance on a partner's current account is money the business owes the partner.

### Non-current assets and the wrong year

Several reports describe students charging depreciation for the wrong number of years before a disposal, or answering for the wrong financial year. In March 2026, many missed that a fully depreciated asset has no more depreciation charged, even while it is still used. In November 2023, students classed replacing parts of an asset as capital spending. Replacement or repair is revenue spending. Only spending that improves the asset is capital.

### Limited company entries

- A bonus issue does not change net assets. It moves reserves into share capital.
- For shares issued at a premium, the cash received is the number of shares × (nominal value + premium), not just the premium.
- Return on capital employed uses profit from operations, so add back debenture interest to profit for the year. This was the common error in March 2022.

### Costing

Cost and management accounting is only 8 questions, but the reports single it out often:

- **Absorption against marginal profit** (November 2022, March 2025). When inventory rises, absorption costing carries some fixed overhead into closing inventory, so absorption profit is higher.
- **Contribution and margin of safety** (November 2022, March 2026). When sales volume changes, rework the profit from variable cost per unit. Do not reuse the old contribution to sales ratio. A margin of safety of 40% means break-even revenue is 60% of the sales revenue.
- **Cost behaviour graphs** (March 2026). Total variable cost is zero at zero output.
- **Overhead apportionment** (November 2022). Choose the basis that fits the cost, such as value for insurance.
- **Ranking products** (June 2022). Most candidates ranked on profit figures. The ranking needed contribution, measured against the scarce resource.

### Not reading the question

Almost every report repeats this message. Check whose books you are in (the customer's or the supplier's), which year is asked for, and whether every figure given is actually needed. Examiners include figures that are not needed.

## How do I use the ratio formulae appendix?

The 9706 syllabus has an appendix that lists the ratio formulae. The syllabus says Paper 1 candidates must use these formulae to get their answers. It also says ratios are not given in the question paper. So you must learn the appendix versions by heart.

Here are the AS Level ratios from the appendix:

| Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|
| Gross profit margin (%) | Gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Mark-up (%) | Gross profit ÷ cost of sales × 100 |
| Profit margin (%) | Profit for the year ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Return on capital employed (%) | Profit from operations ÷ capital employed × 100 |
| Expenses to revenue (%) | Expenses ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Operating expenses to revenue (%) | Operating expenses ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ current liabilities, as a ratio |
| Acid test ratio | (Current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities, as a ratio |
| Non-current asset turnover (times) | Net revenue ÷ total net book value of non-current assets |
| Trade receivables turnover (days) | Trade receivables ÷ credit sales × 365 |
| Trade payables turnover (days) | Trade payables ÷ credit purchases × 365 |
| Inventory turnover (days) | Average inventory ÷ cost of sales × 365 |
| Rate of inventory turnover (times) | Cost of sales ÷ average inventory |

Capital employed is issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.

Three rules from the appendix matter in multiple choice:

- **Use year-end figures** unless the question tells you to use averages. Note that the two inventory formulae are written with average inventory.
- **Round to the decimal places the question asks for.** Options often differ only in the last digit.
- **Watch the denominator.** Receivables use credit sales. Payables use credit purchases. Margin uses revenue. Mark-up uses cost of sales.

### How do I work out ROCE in a multiple choice question?

A company has profit for the year of $42,000, after debenture interest of $6,000. Its issued shares are $200,000, reserves $80,000 and 6% debentures $100,000. There is no tax in this example.

- Profit from operations = $42,000 + $6,000 = $48,000.
- Capital employed = $200,000 + $80,000 + $100,000 = $380,000.
- ROCE = $48,000 ÷ $380,000 × 100 = 12.6%.

The options would likely include 11.1% (profit for the year used, $42,000 ÷ $380,000) and 17.1% (debentures left out, $48,000 ÷ $280,000). Both use the wrong formula.

## Which practice questions should I try?

These are my own questions, written in the Paper 1 style. Cover each answer and try the question first. Give yourself 20 minutes for all ten.

**Question 1.** A trader's opening inventory was $18,000, purchases were $96,000 and closing inventory was $14,000. All goods are sold at a mark-up of 25%. What was the revenue for the year?

A $120,000  
B $125,000  
C $133,333  
D $160,000

**Answer: B.** Cost of sales = $18,000 + $96,000 − $14,000 = $100,000. A 25% mark-up is on cost, so revenue = $100,000 × 1.25 = $125,000. Option C treats 25% as a margin ($100,000 ÷ 0.75). Option A applies the mark-up to purchases and ignores inventory.

**Question 2.** A trial balance did not agree, and the difference was entered in a suspense account. These errors were then found:

1. The sales account was undercast by $600.
2. Discount allowed of $150 was credited to the discount allowed account. The customer's account was correctly credited.
3. Office equipment costing $2,000 was debited to the purchases account.
4. Rent paid of $900 was entered in the cash book but not posted to the rent account.

What was the original balance on the suspense account?

A $600 debit  
B $600 credit  
C $450 debit  
D $2,600 debit

**Answer: A.** Error 3 is an error of principle and does not affect the trial balance. There are three corrections. Credit sales $600 and debit suspense $600. Debit discount allowed $300 and credit suspense $300: this cancels the $150 credit and records the $150 debit. Debit rent $900 and credit suspense $900. The suspense account has $600 debit and $1,200 credit, so it must have started with a debit balance of $600. Option C uses $150 instead of $300 for error 2. Option D puts error 3 through suspense.

**Question 3.** A business's cash book shows a debit balance of $4,300. When it is compared with the bank statement, these items are found:

- bank charges of $85 not entered in the cash book
- a credit transfer of $640 from a customer not entered in the cash book
- unpresented cheques of $1,200
- a deposit of $900 not yet credited by the bank
- a cheque for $250 wrongly debited to the business's account by the bank

What bank balance should be shown in the statement of financial position?

A $3,745  
B $4,555  
C $4,605  
D $4,855

**Answer: D.** Only the bank charges and the credit transfer go in the cash book: $4,300 − $85 + $640 = $4,855. The unpresented cheques, the deposit and the bank's error belong in the reconciliation only. Option A reverses the two adjustments. Option B also adjusts for the cheques and the deposit. Option C deducts the bank's error.

**Question 4.** X and Y are partners. Their agreement gives Y a salary of $8,000 and interest on capital at 5%. Capitals are X $80,000 and Y $40,000. Remaining profit is shared equally. The draft profit of $60,000 was calculated before charging 5% interest on a $10,000 loan from X. What is Y's total share of the profit?

A $22,750  
B $24,750  
C $32,750  
D $33,000

**Answer: C.** Loan interest is an expense: $10,000 × 5% = $500, so profit for the year is $59,500. Interest on capital is $4,000 for X and $2,000 for Y. Residual profit = $59,500 − $8,000 − $6,000 = $45,500, so each partner gets $22,750. Y's total = $8,000 + $2,000 + $22,750 = $32,750. Option A is the residual share only. Option B leaves out the salary. Option D forgets the loan interest.

**Question 5.** A machine was bought on 1 January 2023 for $40,000. It is depreciated at 25% a year on the reducing balance, with a full year's charge in the year of purchase and none in the year of sale. The financial year ends on 31 December. The machine was sold on 30 June 2025 for $21,000. What was the profit or loss on disposal?

A $9,000 loss  
B $1,500 loss  
C $1,000 profit  
D $4,125 profit

**Answer: B.** 2023: $40,000 × 25% = $10,000, net book value $30,000. 2024: $30,000 × 25% = $7,500, net book value $22,500. No charge in 2025. $21,000 − $22,500 = a loss of $1,500. Option A charges only one year. Option C uses straight-line depreciation for two years. Option D also charges 2025.

**Question 6.** A business paid $6,000 to repaint its office building. The amount was debited to the buildings account. Ignoring depreciation, what is the effect of this error?

A no effect on profit; non-current assets overstated by $6,000  
B profit understated by $6,000; non-current assets overstated by $6,000  
C profit overstated by $6,000; non-current assets understated by $6,000  
D profit overstated by $6,000; non-current assets overstated by $6,000

**Answer: D.** Repainting is a repair, so it is revenue spending and should be an expense. Because it was not charged, profit is $6,000 too high. Because it was added to buildings, non-current assets are $6,000 too high.

**Question 7.** A company issues 200,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each at $1.20 per share. All the cash is received. By how much does the share premium account increase?

A $100,000  
B $140,000  
C $240,000  
D $340,000

**Answer: B.** The premium is $1.20 − $0.50 = $0.70 a share. 200,000 × $0.70 = $140,000. Option A is the increase in share capital (200,000 × $0.50). Option C is the total cash received. Option D adds the nominal value to the issue price.

**Question 8.** In its first year, a business produced 10,000 units and sold 8,000. Fixed production overheads were $50,000, absorbed on units produced. Profit using marginal costing was $30,000. What was the profit using absorption costing?

A $20,000  
B $30,000  
C $40,000  
D $80,000

**Answer: C.** Fixed overhead per unit = $50,000 ÷ 10,000 = $5. Closing inventory of 2,000 units carries 2,000 × $5 = $10,000 of fixed overhead into next year. So absorption profit is $10,000 higher: $40,000. Option A takes the $10,000 off instead of adding it. Option D adds the whole fixed overhead.

**Question 9.** A product sells for $25 a unit with a variable cost of $15 a unit. Fixed costs are $60,000. Budgeted sales are 8,000 units. What is the margin of safety as a percentage of budgeted sales?

A 25%  
B 33.3%  
C 40%  
D 75%

**Answer: A.** Contribution per unit = $25 − $15 = $10. Break-even = $60,000 ÷ $10 = 6,000 units. Margin of safety = 8,000 − 6,000 = 2,000 units, and 2,000 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = 25%. Option B divides by break-even sales. Option C is the contribution to sales ratio. Option D is break-even as a percentage of budget.

**Question 10.** A business had opening inventory of $22,000 and closing inventory of $18,000. Cost of sales was $160,000. Using the syllabus formula, what is the inventory turnover in days, to one decimal place?

A 8.0 days  
B 41.1 days  
C 45.6 days  
D 50.2 days

**Answer: C.** Average inventory = ($22,000 + $18,000) ÷ 2 = $20,000. Inventory turnover = $20,000 ÷ $160,000 × 365 = 45.6 days. Option B uses closing inventory and option D uses opening inventory. Option A is the rate of inventory turnover in times, 8, given the wrong unit.

<Tip title="One figure apart">
Sometimes two options differ by exactly one figure from the question, such as the $250 bank error in Question 3. The question is testing whether that figure belongs. Decide that one item and you have the answer.
</Tip>

## How should I revise with past papers?

Practise by topic first, then by full paper. The [9706 past papers](/past-papers/a-level-accounting-9706) on this site are sorted by session, and the [9706 topic pages](/past-papers/a-level-accounting-9706/topics) group questions by syllabus topic. The current syllabus says textbooks endorsed for examination from 2023 are still suitable, so papers from 2023 onwards match the current content. Older papers are still useful, but check each question against the topic list.

A plan for the eight weeks before the exam:

| Weeks | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Topic sets. Start with [reconciliation and verification](/past-papers/a-level-accounting-9706/topics/1-4-reconciliation-and-verification), then partnerships, companies and costing. Write the full working for every question |
| 3 to 6 | Two full Paper 1s a week, timed at 1 hour. Mark each one with the [past paper MCQ checker](/tools/past-paper-mcq) |
| 7 | Redo every question you got wrong, without looking at the answer first |
| 8 | One final timed paper, then light review of your error log |

Keep an error log. For each wrong answer, write the topic, the question type from this article, and the reason: misread, wrong formula, wrong year, or did not know. After four or five papers, most lost marks usually sit in two or three types. Those are the ones to drill.

Do not let Paper 1 take over your revision. Paper 2 is 72% of the AS Level, so most of your time should still go on full written questions. Paper 1 practice is short, which makes it a good warm-up before a Paper 2 session. For depreciation and disposals, my [depreciation questions article](/blog/a-level-accounting-depreciation-questions) has full worked examples. To see how Paper 1 marks feed into a grade, check the [9706 grade thresholds](/grade-thresholds/a-level-accounting-9706).
