Short answer: no, AS and A Level Accounting 9706 does not change for 2027. If you sit in 2027, you use the same syllabus as students who sat in 2026, and the next syllabus, for 2029, has no significant changes either. So this article is the full syllabus explained instead: the four papers, the weightings, the topics, the rules that are easy to miss, and where the marks come from.

## Does anything change in 2027?

Here is what Cambridge publishes for 9706, and what each document says.

| Document | Covers exams in | What Cambridge says |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 to 2028 syllabus, version 2 (December 2025) | 2026, 2027, 2028 | "There are no significant changes which affect teaching." The only change in version 2 is an updated weblink on page 34. Specimen materials were not updated. |
| 2029 syllabus, version 1 (September 2026) | 2029 | "There are no significant changes which affect teaching." |

The last real revision was for examination from June 2023, and Cambridge says textbooks endorsed for exams from 2023 are still suitable. In practical terms:

- **Sitting AS in 2027 and A Level in 2028:** one syllabus all the way through.
- **Resitting in 2027:** same syllabus as your first attempt in 2026.
- **Using a senior's notes from 2023 onwards:** they match the current content, though always check them against the syllabus itself.

## The four papers

| Paper | Marks and time | Questions | Content | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Multiple Choice | 30 marks, 1 hour | 30 multiple choice questions | Sections 1 and 2 | 28% of AS, 14% of A Level |
| 2 Fundamentals of Accounting | 90 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes | Four structured questions | Sections 1 and 2 | 72% of AS, 36% of A Level |
| 3 Financial Accounting | 75 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes | Three structured questions | Section 3, with AS knowledge assumed | 30% of A Level |
| 4 Cost and Management Accounting | 50 marks, 1 hour | Two structured questions | Section 4, with AS knowledge assumed | 20% of A Level |

Calculators are essential for all papers. AS Level is graded a to e. A Level is graded A\* to E.

### Three routes to the qualification

1. **AS Level only:** Papers 1 and 2 in the same exam series.
2. **A Level, staged over two years:** Papers 1 and 2 in year 1, then Papers 3 and 4 in year 2. Your AS result is carried forward, subject to the rules and time limits in the Cambridge Handbook.
3. **A Level in one series:** all four papers together.

If you take the staged route, check the carry-forward rules with your exams officer before you enter for year 2.

## What you study

AS students study topics 1.1 to 2.2. A Level students study everything, 1.1 to 4.4.

| | Financial accounting | Cost and management accounting |
|---|---|---|
| **AS Level** | 1.1 Types of business entity, 1.2 The accounting system, 1.3 Accounting for non-current assets, 1.4 Reconciliation and verification, 1.5 Preparation of financial statements, 1.6 Analysis and communication of accounting information | 2.1 Costs and cost behaviour, 2.2 Traditional costing methods |
| **A Level** | 3.1 Preparation of financial statements, 3.2 Regulatory and ethical considerations, 3.3 Business acquisition and merger, 3.4 Computerised accounting systems, 3.5 Analysis and communication of accounting information | 4.1 Activity based costing, 4.2 Standard costing, 4.3 Budgeting and budgetary control, 4.4 Investment appraisal |

### What the AS topics ask of you

- **Non-current assets:** straight-line and reducing balance depreciation, the cost model and the revaluation model, disposals, and the effect of treating capital spending as revenue spending (or the reverse).
- **Verification:** errors that do and do not affect the trial balance, suspense accounts, bank reconciliation statements, and sales and purchases ledger control accounts.
- **Financial statements:** sole traders, partnerships (including the Partnership Act 1890 rules) and limited companies, with year-end adjustments.
- **Ratios:** profitability, liquidity and efficiency, and how to improve each.
- **Costing:** FIFO and AVCO inventory valuation (perpetual and periodic), just in time, absorption costing with overhead absorption rates, marginal costing, break-even, and reconciling marginal and absorption profits.

### What the A Level topics add

- **Partnership changes:** goodwill and revaluation on a change in profit-sharing ratio, admission, retirement and dissolution, plus realisation and revaluation accounts.
- **Clubs and societies, and manufacturing businesses**, including unrealised profit on unsold inventory.
- **Limited companies:** the statement of cash flows, the statement of changes in equity and the schedule of non-current assets, prepared in line with international accounting standards.
- **Nine standards by number:** IAS 1, 2, 7, 8, 10, 16, 36, 37 and 38.
- **Ethics and audit:** the five fundamental principles, external and internal audit, qualified and unqualified audit reports, and stewardship.
- **Mergers and acquisitions**, including goodwill on acquisition.
- **More ratios:** earnings per share, price/earnings ratio, dividend per share, dividend yield, dividend cover, interest cover, gearing and the working capital cycle.
- **Management accounting:** activity based costing, standard costing variances (including fixed overhead capacity and efficiency sub-variances), budgets and flexible budgets, and investment appraisal using payback, ARR, NPV and IRR.

### What will not be set

The syllabus includes notes on what Cambridge will not examine. They are worth knowing so you do not waste revision time:

- Questions on Limited Liability Partnerships will not be set.
- Questions will not be set on preference shares.
- You will not be asked to prepare a break-even chart, though you must interpret one.
- Questions on discounted payback will not be set.
- Questions involving a residual value at the end of an investment project will not be set.
- For a bonus issue of shares, the revaluation reserve is not to be used.
- No knowledge of specific accounting software is required.

## What examiners reward

These are the assessment objective weightings from the syllabus:

| Assessment objective | AS Level | A Level | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 3 | Paper 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AO1 Knowledge and understanding | 63% | 55% | 70% | 60% | 54% | 44% |
| AO2 Analysis | 25% | 30% | 30% | 23% | 33% | 28% |
| AO3 Evaluation | 12% | 15% | 0% | 17% | 13% | 28% |

Three things stand out.

**Knowledge still carries the most weight.** Over half of the A Level rewards preparing statements and calculations correctly, in the expected format. Cambridge's Teacher Guidance document sets out the formats it expects for the standard financial statements, so practise those layouts until they are automatic.

**Paper 4 is the evaluation paper.** Evaluation is 28% of Paper 4, the highest share on any paper. AO3 asks you to "make informed recommendations and decisions" and to draw conclusions "based on financial and non-financial data". Standard costing, budgeting and investment appraisal all list "the significance of non-financial factors" as content, so build one into every recommendation.

**Ratio formulas are fixed.** For both the AS and A Level ratios, the syllabus says you must use the formulae in its appendix, and that these are the only formulae accepted. If you learned a different version of gearing or ROCE from another source, replace it.

<Tip>
In any question that asks you to advise or recommend, finish with a clear decision in one sentence, then give the two strongest figures from your own workings that support it, then one non-financial factor. A balanced discussion with no decision does not do what AO3 asks.
</Tip>

## How to plan your two years

- **Year 1 (AS):** Paper 2 is 72% of the AS grade, so most of your practice should be full structured questions under time: 1 hour 45 minutes for four questions. Use Paper 1 practice for speed: 30 questions in an hour.
- **Year 2 (A Level):** Paper 3 assumes all the AS financial accounting, and Paper 4 assumes all the AS costing. Keep a short weekly AS review going while you learn the new topics.
- **Past papers from 2023 onwards** match the current syllabus. Older papers are still useful practice, but check each question against the current topic list.

## What grade do you need?

In June 2026, an AS grade a needed 85 out of 125 weighted marks on all three variants. At A Level, an A\* needed 192 out of 250 on variants 1 and 3, and 189 on variant 2. These numbers move every session.
