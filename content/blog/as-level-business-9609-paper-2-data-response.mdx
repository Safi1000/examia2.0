AS Business 9609 Paper 2 gives you 60 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes: two cases, each with six parts worth 30 marks in all. Most parts reward facts from the case, not business in general. The last part of each case is a 12 mark evaluate question, and its top marks need a clear judgement built on the case's own facts.

## What is the format of 9609 Paper 2?

The 9609 syllabus for 2026 to 2028 sets the paper as two compulsory questions of 30 marks each, based on the AS content (topics 1.1 to 5.5). Each question gives you a text with written, numerical or diagram data, and each has six parts. The syllabus says the last part of each question should show evaluation.

The marks and command words below come from the papers themselves. Every Paper 2 from March 2023 to June 2026, all variants, and the specimen paper for examination from 2023 used exactly this pattern, in both questions.

| Part | Command word | Marks | Must you use the case? |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a)(i) | Identify | 1 | No |
| (a)(ii) | Explain the term | 3 | No, an example of your own is fine |
| (b)(i) | Calculate | 3 | Yes, the data given |
| (b)(ii) | Explain one | 3 | Yes |
| (c) | Analyse (two points, or one point on each side) | 8 | Yes |
| (d) | Evaluate | 12 | Yes |

The last column comes from the mark schemes and reports. The June 2026 Paper 21 mark scheme allowed the application in 1(a)(ii) to use the case or any other business, but said the application in 1(b)(ii) had to be about the case business, so a general answer would not do. The March 2025 examiner report made the same point for both (a)(ii) and (b)(ii) parts.

Paper 2 is 60% of the AS Level and 30% of the full A Level.

If you practise on 2022 papers, the layout was different. In the 2022 papers, (a)(i) was a 2 mark define question and (d) was worth 11 marks, sometimes with "recommend" as the command word. June 2022 Papers 21 and 22 also split the middle marks differently. The skills carry over; the mark split does not.

### Which skills does Paper 2 test?

The syllabus gives the Paper 2 weightings: knowledge and understanding 30%, application 30%, analysis 20% and evaluation 20%. In my words:

| Skill | What it means in your answer |
|---|---|
| Knowledge (AO1) | You know the term or the point and state it correctly |
| Application (AO2) | You tie the point to this business, using a fact only this case has |
| Analysis (AO3) | You show a chain: this happens, so this follows, which leads to that |
| Evaluation (AO4) | You weigh the arguments and reach a judgement for this business |

Evaluation is 20% of 60 marks, which is 12 marks. In the June 2026 Paper 21 mark scheme, evaluation marks sit only in the two (d) parts, 6 marks each.

## What does "using the case" actually mean?

Application is not writing the business's name. The June 2023 Paper 22 report made this point: a name alone does not count, so your knowledge must fit something specific about that business.

The best context is usually a number. The November 2023 Paper 22 report rated figures from the case highly as context, partly because they take little time to use. The March 2026 report said many answers did not make full use of the numbers in the case.

Three habits help:

1. **Read the whole case before you answer anything.** The June 2022 Paper 22 report said five minutes of reading at the start of each case is not too much.
2. **Mark the facts that point to answers.** The June 2022 and November 2023 Paper 22 reports both said the data signposts the most likely answers. If the case mentions an overdraft, a rival's price or a rating, it is there for a reason.
3. **Use the fact, do not copy it.** The November 2023 Paper 21 report said case facts should be built into your own points, not copied out.

### A sample case: FreshFold

I wrote this short case to practise on. The business and figures are invented.

> **FreshFold** is a laundry and dry cleaning business in a large city. It is a partnership owned by two sisters, Sana and Mehr, and it has one shop in a busy market and six employees. Most customers are office workers who drop off clothes on their way to work. FreshFold has a rating of 4.8 out of 5 on a local review website, and many reviews praise its careful handling of wedding and formal clothes.
>
> FreshFold plans a home collection service. A driver will collect bags of clothes from homes and return them clean within 48 hours. Two large competitors already offer this service at $6 a bag. Mehr wants to launch at $4 a bag for the first three months, using penetration pricing, and then raise the price. Sana is worried. FreshFold's bank overdraft is already $3 000, a van will be leased for $500 a month, and FreshFold's machines have spare capacity for only about 700 extra bags a month.

**Table 1: Mehr's forecasts for home collection**

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Variable cost per bag (detergent, energy, driver's pay per bag) | $2.50 |
| Forecast bags per month at $4 | 900 |
| Forecast bags per month at $6 | 400 |
| Van lease per month | $500 |

## How do I answer the 1 mark and 3 mark questions?

These four parts are worth 10 of the 30 marks on each case, so keep them short. The June 2023 Paper 21 report described a significant minority who wrote a page for a 3 mark answer, then gave the 8 and 12 mark questions only a few lines. The November 2023 Paper 23 report said this happened often.

### Identify (1 mark)

Give one word or phrase and move on. The March 2025 report said identify needs only knowledge, and gave a one word answer that would have earned the mark on that paper. The June 2026 Paper 21 mark scheme told examiners to mark only the first answer if a candidate gives more than one, so put your best answer first and stop.

### Explain the term (3 marks)

In the mark schemes I checked, the split is 1 knowledge mark and 2 application marks. The March 2023 report warned that a long definition uses up the time you need for the 2 application marks. Give a precise meaning in different words from the term, then show it working in a business.

Sample: *Explain the term penetration pricing. [3]*

> Penetration pricing is setting a low price when a product is launched, so that it wins customers and market share quickly. The business plans to raise the price once it has a base of loyal buyers. For example, a new mobile network might offer cheap monthly plans in its first year to pull customers away from older networks.

My marking: 1 for the meaning, 1 for the plan to raise the price later, 1 for the example. Do not define a term with its own words: the June 2023 Paper 21 report said such answers are not rewarded.

The June 2023 Paper 21 and November 2023 Paper 23 reports said that, on those papers, no credit went to (a) answers built only from case examples or lifted case text, since these showed no grasp of the term. So give the meaning in your own words first. An example can then come from the case or any business, as the June 2026 Paper 21 scheme allows, but it cannot replace the meaning.

### Calculate (3 marks)

Sample: *Refer to Table 1. Calculate FreshFold's forecast monthly contribution from home collection at $4 a bag. [3]*

> Contribution per bag = $4.00 − $2.50 = $1.50
>
> Monthly contribution = $1.50 × 900 bags = **$1 350**

On its 1(b)(i), the June 2026 Paper 21 mark scheme gave full marks for a correct answer even with no working and no $ sign. But the March 2022 report said a wrong final answer with no working scores nothing, while setting out the formula, the figures and the answer may cost just one mark if only the last step is wrong. The mark schemes also use the own figure rule: if an early step is wrong, later steps done correctly with your figure can still earn marks. Always show each step and put a box round the final answer, as the March 2025 report suggested.

### Explain one (3 marks)

Again 1 knowledge mark and 2 application marks, but now the application must be about the case business.

Sample: *Explain one benefit to FreshFold of its high review rating. [3]*

> A strong reputation means customers trust a business, so they are more willing to buy from it. FreshFold's rating is 4.8 out of 5, and reviews praise its care with formal clothes. This means office workers may choose FreshFold for home collection even if it charges close to the $6 that its two rivals charge.

My marking: 1 for knowledge (trust from a strong reputation), then 2 for application. The schemes differ on how those 2 are earned. The June 2026 Paper 21 scheme gave one for explaining the point and one for a case fact. The June 2023 Paper 21 and November 2023 Paper 23 reports asked for two pieces of case information. Using two facts, as here, covers both. There is no analysis mark, so keep to explaining. The March 2025 report expected about two or three sentences here.

## How do I answer the 8 mark analyse question?

The (c) part is marked on levels. In the June 2026 Paper 21 mark scheme, the split was 2 knowledge marks, 2 application marks and 4 analysis marks. Application was capped at one mark per point, so each of your two points needs its own case fact.

The analysis marks depend on the length of your chain. In that scheme, a single step of cause and effect is limited analysis; two or more steps are developed analysis. Developed analysis on both points reaches the top of the analysis marks.

The March 2025 report said some candidates jump straight from the start of the chain to the end, for example from motivation straight to profit. Each step needs to be on the page.

Sample: *Analyse one advantage and one disadvantage to FreshFold of leasing the van rather than buying it. [8]*

> **Advantage.** Leasing means FreshFold pays to use the van without buying it. This avoids a large payment at the start, when FreshFold's overdraft is already $3 000. So FreshFold does not need to borrow more, which keeps its interest costs down, and cash is left to pay for detergent and the driver while home collection orders build up.
>
> **Disadvantage.** The lease is a fixed cost of $500 that must be paid every month, even in a month when few bags are collected. In the first months of the service, orders may be well below Mehr's forecast. FreshFold could then make a loss on the service, which would push the overdraft higher and leave less cash for the shop.

My marking: 2 knowledge (what leasing avoids, the fixed monthly cost), 2 application (the $3 000 overdraft, the $500 lease) and developed analysis on both points, so 8. Notice the question says "rather than buying it". Keep that comparison in every point.

## How is the 12 mark evaluate question marked?

The (d) mark schemes I checked (March 2023, June 2024 Paper 22, November 2025 Paper 23, March 2026, June 2026 Paper 21) and the specimen mark scheme all use the same split: 2 knowledge marks, 2 application marks, 2 analysis marks and 6 evaluation marks. Half the question is evaluation.

Here is the evaluation part of the level table, in my own words, from the June 2026 Paper 21 scheme:

| Level | Evaluation marks | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5 to 6 | Your verdict is reasoned and backed up, you compare the strongest points for and against, and both use this business's own situation |
| 2 | 3 to 4 | The same reasoned verdict and weighing, but without this business's situation |
| 1 | 1 to 2 | A verdict with little reason behind it, or a first try at showing both sides |

The scheme calls the top level "Developed evaluation in context". That last phrase is the gap between 4 and 6 marks. A judgement that would fit any business stops at Level 2.

The knowledge, application and analysis marks are only 2 each here. Two well chosen arguments, each with a case fact and a short chain, are enough to collect them. On the 11 mark version used in 2022, the November 2022 Paper 21 report said full marks did not need many arguments: two good ones and a justified recommendation could be enough. The rest of your time belongs to judgement.

### What do examiners say about evaluation?

- **Judge as you go, then conclude.** The March 2025 report said the best evaluation usually comes from answers that "make an evaluative point after each analytical point".
- **Pick a side.** The June 2023 Paper 21 report warned that "by saying both are important, the answer is not specific enough".
- **There is no one right answer.** The November 2023 Paper 22 report said any judgement can work if it is justified in context.

<Tip title="Find the deciding fact first">
Before you write a 12 mark answer, find the one fact in the case that could change the decision: a capacity limit, a debt, a rival's move. Build your judgement around it.
</Tip>

## What does a top 12 mark answer look like?

Sample: *Evaluate whether Mehr's pricing plan is the right way for FreshFold to start its home collection service. [12]*

> Penetration pricing means launching at a low price to win customers quickly, then raising the price later. At $4, FreshFold would be $2 cheaper than its two large rivals, and Mehr forecasts 900 bags a month instead of 400 at $6. More office workers would try the service, and if they form a habit, many may stay when the price rises after three months. This would raise FreshFold's revenue in the long term.
>
> However, the low price cuts the contribution on each bag from $3.50 to $1.50. At the forecasts, FreshFold earns $1 350 of contribution a month at $4, against $1 400 at $6. FreshFold would wash more than twice as many bags for less money. So on the forecasts alone, penetration pricing does not pay, unless most customers stay after the price rises.
>
> The bigger problem is capacity. FreshFold's machines can handle only about 700 extra bags a month, so at $4 it could not serve about 200 of the bags customers want. Late returns or refused orders could lead to poor reviews and damage the 4.8 rating. That rating is FreshFold's main strength, and losing it could also cost FreshFold its shop customers.
>
> FreshFold should not use penetration pricing. If it only accepts the 700 bags it can wash, the service's profit after the van lease is $550 a month at $4 ($1 050 − $500), against $900 at $6 ($1 400 − $500). With a $3 000 overdraft to repay, FreshFold needs the higher profit. Its rating also gives customers a reason to pay a rival's price, so FreshFold does not need to be the cheapest. A price close to $6 is better for FreshFold.
>
> This depends on Mehr's forecasts. If demand at $6 is far below 400 bags, a short discount for existing shop customers would be a safer start, because it fills spare capacity without promising more than FreshFold can deliver.

I would place this at Level 3 for evaluation. That is my judgement against the published table, not an official mark.

| Skill | Where it is in the answer |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | The meaning of penetration pricing; contribution |
| Application | The $4 and $6 prices, the 900 and 400 forecasts, the 700 bag capacity, the 4.8 rating, the overdraft |
| Analysis | Habit leads to customers staying, then higher revenue; over capacity leads to late returns, poor reviews, then lost shop customers |
| Evaluation | A mini judgement after the contribution point; a clear verdict using two case facts; what the verdict depends on and a safer option |

The figures: 900 × $1.50 = $1 350; 400 × $3.50 = $1 400; 700 × $1.50 = $1 050. The June 2022 Paper 22 report pointed out that your own (b)(i) answer can give you context for later parts.

## Where do students lose marks on Paper 2?

The middle column is my summary of each examiner report.

| Mistake | What the report said | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Giving the verdict in the first line and nothing more | June 2023 Paper 21 and November 2023 Paper 23: many scripts opened with a verdict of one line; placed before any analysis, it could earn no more than 1 evaluation mark | Build the judgement from your analysis and give the reason that decides it |
| Spending the 12 mark answer on analysis | March 2023 and June 2023 Paper 22: too much time went on analysis in many answers; in June 2023 many proposed a change to all four Ps and had no time left to judge them | After two arguments, stop analysing and start weighing |
| Writing few evaluative comments | November 2023 Paper 22: many answers made only a handful of judgements, though evaluation is 6 of the 12 marks | Add a mini judgement after each argument |
| Repeating the analysis as a conclusion | March 2026: some conclusions repeated earlier analysis; June 2023 Paper 23 and November 2023 Paper 21 warned against restating earlier points in the conclusion | Your conclusion should add a reason or a condition you have not used yet |
| Generic answers | June 2022 and November 2022 Paper 22: an answer that ignores the context is always held back | Use a fact only this case has, ideally a number |
| Leaving numbers unused | March 2022: the numbers in one case were poorly used; March 2026: many answers did not make full use of the numbers | Quote at least one figure in each of (c) and (d) |
| Answering a nearby question | March 2026: answers occasionally drifted, for example to demand factors instead of supply factors, profit instead of cash flow, or the business instead of a stakeholder | Underline who and what the question is about before you write |
| Using every piece of data | November 2022 Paper 22: on Question 2(d), some answers tried to cover all the data, relevant or not | Choose the two or three facts that matter most for the decision |

One more from the March 2025 report: if you answer the questions out of order, label each answer clearly. That report named mixing up the two cases as the most common slip among students who change the order.

## How long should I spend on each part?

The paper gives you 90 minutes for 60 marks, so 45 minutes for each case. This is my advice for splitting each 45 minutes, not a Cambridge rule.

| Part | Marks | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Read the case and mark key facts | none | 5 |
| (a)(i) | 1 | 1 |
| (a)(ii) | 3 | 4 |
| (b)(i) | 3 | 4 |
| (b)(ii) | 3 | 4 |
| (c) | 8 | 11 |
| (d) | 12 | 16 |

That adds up to 45 minutes. Answer both cases. The June 2022 Paper 21 and November 2022 Paper 23 reports each found a significant minority who attempted only Question 1.

<Tip title="Plan the 12 mark answer while you read">
Read the (d) question before the case. Then sort each fact you mark as for, against, or deciding. By the time you reach (d), your plan is ready.
</Tip>

## Practice questions with answers

All three use the FreshFold case above. The answers show the key steps; the marking is my judgement.

### Practice question 1

*Refer to Table 1. Calculate how many bags a month FreshFold must collect at $4 to cover the van lease. [3]*

**Answer.** Contribution per bag = $4.00 − $2.50 = $1.50. Break even = $500 ÷ $1.50 = 333.3 bags. FreshFold must collect **334 bags** a month. At 333 bags, contribution is only $499.50.

### Practice question 2

*Explain one reason why FreshFold's overdraft might rise during the launch of home collection. [3]*

**Answer.** Knowledge: a business uses its overdraft more when cash going out is greater than cash coming in. Application: FreshFold must pay the $500 van lease from the first month, while orders may start well below the 900 bag forecast. Application: with only $1.50 of contribution on each bag at $4, slow early orders may not cover the lease, so the gap adds to the $3 000 overdraft.

### Practice question 3

*Analyse two ways FreshFold's large competitors might respond to its launch, and the effect of each on FreshFold. [8]*

**Answer outline.**

- **They cut their price to match $4.** FreshFold would lose its price advantage, so fewer customers switch, so orders fall below the 900 bag forecast, so contribution falls and the lease is harder to cover. Application: the $6 price gap and the 900 forecast.
- **They advertise faster returns than 48 hours.** Office workers who need clothes quickly may stay with the rivals, so FreshFold's orders grow more slowly, so it takes longer to fill its spare capacity and recover the $500 monthly lease. Application: the 48 hour promise and the office worker customers.

## FAQ

### How many marks is 9609 Paper 2?

60 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes. There are two compulsory questions of 30 marks, each with six parts.

### How much of the AS Level is Paper 2?

The syllabus gives Paper 2 as 60% of the AS Level and 30% of the full A Level.

### Do I need to use the case in every answer?

Not in (a)(i), which needs only knowledge, or in (a)(ii), where an example from any business can earn the application marks. From (b)(i) onwards, every part needs facts or numbers from the case.

### How many points should I make in the 12 mark question?

Two well developed arguments, one on each side, are usually enough for the knowledge, application and analysis marks. Spend the rest of your time on the judgement, which carries 6 marks.

### Can I write "it depends" as my conclusion?

Only after a clear judgement. Say what FreshFold should do and why, then say what the decision depends on. "It depends" on its own, or saying every option matters equally, does not answer the question.
