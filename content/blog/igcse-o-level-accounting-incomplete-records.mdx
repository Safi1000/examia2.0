Incomplete records means a sole trader has not kept full double entry books, so you rebuild the missing figures. Find profit from the change in capital: closing capital minus opening capital, plus drawings, minus capital introduced. Or find sales and purchases from trade receivables and trade payables accounts, use mark-up or margin for cost of sales, then prepare the financial statements.

## What does the 2027 to 2029 syllabus say about incomplete records?

Incomplete records is section 5.6 of the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-5.6) and of the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus. The list is the same in both. You need to know:

1. Why businesses keep other types of records instead of full double entry.
2. The advantages and disadvantages of not keeping a full set of records.
3. How to prepare opening and closing statements of affairs.
4. How to calculate profit or loss for the year from changes in capital.
5. How to calculate sales, purchases, gross profit, trade receivables, trade payables and other figures from incomplete information.
6. How to prepare the statement of profit or loss and the statement of financial position from incomplete records.
7. The sole trader adjustments from section 5.1.
8. How to use mark-up, margin and inventory turnover to find missing figures.

The syllabus sets one limit. Questions are only about **sole traders**, never about service or manufacturing businesses.

The syllabus says statement of profit or loss. Older past papers and mark schemes say income statement. I use both names here.

## Why do some traders not keep full records?

A small trader often keeps only a bank record, some invoices and a list of who owes money. The usual reasons are:

- **Cost.** A bookkeeper has to be paid.
- **Time.** The owner is busy buying and selling.
- **Knowledge.** The owner may not know double entry.
- **Size.** A business with few transactions may not feel the need.

Records can also be lost, for example in a fire. These are the points I teach for each side.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Cheaper: no bookkeeper to pay | No trial balance, so errors are harder to find |
| Saves the owner's time | Fraud and theft are harder to spot |
| Simple to keep | Profit is only found at the year end, by rebuilding figures |

The June 2025 Paper 2 asked whether a trader should employ a bookkeeper. The mark scheme gave up to 2 marks for points in favour, up to 2 for points against and 1 for a recommendation. So learn both columns, and always end with a clear decision.

## How do you calculate profit from the change in capital?

A **statement of affairs** is a list of a trader's assets and liabilities on one date. It finds **capital**, what the business owes the owner:

**Capital = Total assets − Total liabilities**

Profit raises capital. So does money the owner puts in. Drawings reduce it. That gives the formula:

**Profit for the year = Closing capital − Opening capital + Drawings − Capital introduced**

Add drawings back because they made closing capital smaller but are not a business expense. Take off capital introduced because it was not earned by trading. A negative answer is a loss.

### What does a statement of affairs look like?

Sana does not keep full records. Her opening statement of affairs at 1 January 2026 showed capital of $20 200. During 2026 she took drawings of $14 000, paid in $3 000 of her own savings and took out a bank loan of $5 000. Her closing statement uses her assets and liabilities at 31 December 2026.

**Sana: Statement of affairs at 31 December 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Non-current assets** | | |
| Equipment | | 10 800 |
| **Current assets** | | |
| Inventory | 5 600 | |
| Trade receivables | 3 900 | |
| Bank | 4 100 | |
| Cash | 400 | 14 000 |
| **Total assets** | | **24 800** |
| **Capital and liabilities** | | |
| Capital (balancing figure) | | 16 700 |
| **Non-current liabilities** | | |
| Bank loan | | 5 000 |
| **Current liabilities** | | |
| Trade payables | | 3 100 |
| **Total capital and liabilities** | | **24 800** |

Capital is 24 800 − 5 000 − 3 100 = 16 700.

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Closing capital | 16 700 |
| Add drawings | 14 000 |
| Less capital introduced | (3 000) |
| Less opening capital | (20 200) |
| **Profit for the year** | **7 500** |

Sana's capital fell by $3 500, yet she made a profit of $7 500. Her drawings were bigger than her profit. The bank loan is a liability, not capital introduced.

Use this layout, the same as a statement of financial position. The June 2022 examiner report said candidates should not present it as a calculation or as a trial balance.

## How do you find missing sales and purchases?

Most questions give receipts and payments, not sales or purchases. Build a total account, enter every figure you know, and the missing figure is the one that makes both sides equal.

| Account | Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables | Opening balance, **credit sales** | Receipts from customers, discount allowed, sales returns, irrecoverable debts, closing balance |
| Trade payables | Payments to suppliers, discount received, purchases returns, closing balance | Opening balance, **credit purchases** |

Then add cash sales to credit sales to get revenue. Add cash purchases to credit purchases. Only trade items belong in these accounts. The full example below shows both.

### How do you find cash sales or drawings from a cash or bank summary?

A bank summary lists all money in and out of the bank for the year, like the bank columns of a cash book. The missing figure is often drawings. A cash summary does the same for the till.

Kiran started the year with $200 in the till and ended with $300. From the till he paid expenses of $1 300, took drawings of $2 400 and banked $18 000.

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 200 | Expenses | 1 300 |
| Cash sales (missing figure) | 21 800 | Drawings | 2 400 |
| | | Bank | 18 000 |
| | | Balance c/d | 300 |
| | **22 000** | | **22 000** |

Cash sales are 22 000 − 200 = **$21 800**. Every use of the till money is **added**, because all of it came from sales.

## How do you use mark-up and margin to find missing figures?

**Mark-up** is gross profit as a percentage of **cost of sales**. **Margin** is gross profit as a percentage of **revenue**.

Write each one as three numbers. A mark-up of 25% means cost 100, profit 25, revenue 125. A margin of 20% means revenue 100, profit 20, cost 80. These two describe the same goods, because 25 ÷ 125 = 20%.

| You know | You want | Calculation (25% mark-up) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Cost of sales | Revenue × 100/125 |
| Cost of sales | Revenue | Cost of sales × 125/100 |
| Cost of sales | Gross profit | Cost of sales × 25/100 |
| Revenue | Gross profit | Revenue × 25/125 |

**Goods taken by the owner** are drawings at cost. If the question gives their selling price of $1 500, the cost is 1 500 × 100/125 = **$1 200**. Taking 25% off the selling price gives $1 125, which is wrong.

**Inventory turnover** works the same way. Rate of inventory turnover = cost of sales ÷ average inventory. So if average inventory is $5 000 and it turns over 8 times, cost of sales is 5 000 × 8 = $40 000. My [Accounting ratios guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-ratios-formulas) lists every formula.

<Tip title="Write the three numbers first">
Before any calculation, write "cost 100, profit 25, revenue 125" beside the question. The June 2022 examiner report noted "Far too often 25 per cent was calculated on the selling price".
</Tip>

### How do you calculate inventory lost in a fire or theft?

A fire destroyed most of Farah's inventory on 31 March 2026. Since 1 January her opening inventory was $8 000, purchases $30 000 and revenue $40 000. Her mark-up is 25%. Goods costing $2 500 were saved.

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Opening inventory | 8 000 |
| Add purchases | 30 000 |
| Goods available for sale | 38 000 |
| Less cost of sales (40 000 × 100/125) | (32 000) |
| Inventory that should be left | 6 000 |
| Less inventory saved | (2 500) |
| **Inventory destroyed** | **3 500** |

Find what should be there, take off what is there, and the gap is the loss.

## In what order do you prepare the final statements?

1. Find opening capital, if it is not given.
2. Complete the bank or cash summary to find drawings or a missing balance.
3. Find credit sales from the trade receivables account. Add cash sales.
4. Find credit purchases from the trade payables account. Add cash purchases.
5. Adjust expenses for accruals and prepayments at both ends of the year.
6. Calculate depreciation.
7. Prepare the income statement, using my [income statement format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format).
8. Prepare the statement of financial position. The layout is on my [Accounting formats page](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-formats).
9. Check that total assets equal total capital and liabilities.

This is where students get stuck. They can do each working alone, but lose track of which figure goes where. Number every working (W1, W2) and refer to it in the statements.

## How do you answer a full incomplete records question?

Imran runs a shop selling household goods. He keeps only a bank record. His year ends on 30 June 2026.

**Balances**

| Item | 1 July 2025 $ | 30 June 2026 $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fixtures | 15 000 | 17 100 (valuation) |
| Inventory | 6 000 | 7 400 |
| Trade receivables | 4 200 | 5 100 |
| Rent prepaid | 300 | 400 |
| Bank | 3 500 | 11 400 |
| Trade payables | 3 800 | 4 500 |
| Electricity owing | 0 | 250 |

**Bank receipts and payments for the year**

| Receipts | $ | Payments | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| From credit customers | 52 000 | To credit suppliers | 41 000 |
| Cash sales (all banked) | 18 000 | Rent | 3 600 |
| Capital introduced | 5 000 | Wages | 9 800 |
| | | Electricity | 1 500 |
| | | New fixtures | 4 000 |
| | | Drawings | not recorded |

Discount allowed was $600 and discount received was $400. Imran also took goods costing $500 for his family.

### W1: opening capital

Assets are 15 000 + 6 000 + 4 200 + 300 + 3 500 = 29 000. Capital at 1 July 2025 is 29 000 − 3 800 = **$25 200**. If the question asks for a statement of affairs, set this out like Sana's.

### W2: bank summary to find drawings

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 3 500 | Trade payables | 41 000 |
| Trade receivables | 52 000 | Rent | 3 600 |
| Cash sales | 18 000 | Wages | 9 800 |
| Capital | 5 000 | Electricity | 1 500 |
| | | Fixtures | 4 000 |
| | | Drawings (missing figure) | 7 200 |
| | | Balance c/d | 11 400 |
| | **78 500** | | **78 500** |

The known credits total 71 300, so cash drawings are 78 500 − 71 300 = 7 200. Total drawings = 7 200 + 500 goods = **$7 700**.

### W3: credit sales and revenue

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 4 200 | Bank | 52 000 |
| Credit sales (missing figure) | 53 500 | Discount allowed | 600 |
| | | Balance c/d | 5 100 |
| | **57 700** | | **57 700** |

Revenue = 53 500 + 18 000 cash sales = **$71 500**.

### W4: credit purchases

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Bank | 41 000 | Balance b/d | 3 800 |
| Discount received | 400 | Credit purchases (missing figure) | 42 100 |
| Balance c/d | 4 500 | | |
| | **45 900** | | **45 900** |

### W5: expenses and depreciation

| Expense | Working | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Rent | 3 600 paid + 300 opening prepaid − 400 closing prepaid | 3 500 |
| Electricity | 1 500 paid + 250 owing | 1 750 |
| Depreciation | 15 000 + 4 000 new − 17 100 valuation | 1 900 |

The fixtures are valued at the year end, so depreciation is the fall in value. This is the revaluation method.

### The income statement

**Imran: Income statement for the year ended 30 June 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | | 71 500 |
| Opening inventory | 6 000 | |
| Purchases (42 100 − 500 goods for own use) | 41 600 | |
| | 47 600 | |
| Less closing inventory | (7 400) | |
| Cost of sales | | (40 200) |
| **Gross profit** | | **31 300** |
| Add discount received | | 400 |
| | | 31 700 |
| Rent | 3 500 | |
| Wages | 9 800 | |
| Electricity | 1 750 | |
| Discount allowed | 600 | |
| Depreciation of fixtures | 1 900 | (17 550) |
| **Profit for the year** | | **14 150** |

The new fixtures, the capital introduced and the drawings are **not** here. They are not expenses.

### The statement of financial position

**Imran: Statement of financial position at 30 June 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Non-current assets** | | |
| Fixtures at valuation | | 17 100 |
| **Current assets** | | |
| Inventory | 7 400 | |
| Trade receivables | 5 100 | |
| Other receivables (rent prepaid) | 400 | |
| Bank | 11 400 | 24 300 |
| **Total assets** | | **41 400** |
| **Capital and liabilities** | | |
| Capital at 1 July 2025 | | 25 200 |
| Add capital introduced | | 5 000 |
| Add profit for the year | | 14 150 |
| | | 44 350 |
| Less drawings (7 200 + 500) | | (7 700) |
| Capital at 30 June 2026 | | 36 650 |
| **Current liabilities** | | |
| Trade payables | 4 500 | |
| Other payables (electricity owing) | 250 | 4 750 |
| **Total capital and liabilities** | | **41 400** |

It balances. Now test the profit with the capital formula: 36 650 − 25 200 + 7 700 − 5 000 = **14 150**. Both methods agree.

## How is an incomplete records question marked?

Paper 2 has five compulsory questions of 20 marks each in both 0452 and 7707. Two 0452 Paper 22 mark schemes gave a whole question to incomplete records.

| Paper 22 | Part | Marks |
|---|---|---:|
| June 2022 | Bank account to find drawings | 4 |
| | Total drawings (cash plus goods at cost) | 2 |
| | Closing statement of affairs | 5 |
| | Profit from the change in capital | 4 |
| | Should the trader sell on credit? | 5 |
| June 2025 | Purchases ledger control account | 4 |
| | Income statement using a mark-up | 8 |
| | Closing trade receivables | 3 |
| | Should the trader employ a bookkeeper? | 5 |

Three things follow:

- **Many marks are own figure (OF) marks.** If your purchases figure is wrong, you can still score for using it correctly later. Never leave a later part blank.
- **Marks are given line by line.** In the June 2022 bank account, four separate figures earned one mark each: total receipts, total payments, drawings and the balance brought down. Clear workings let the examiner find them.
- **Both questions ended with a 5-mark decision.** Give points on both sides, then recommend.

The topic also appears in Paper 1, the multiple choice paper. The June 2023 and June 2025 reports both discuss incomplete records questions there.

In June 2022 many candidates rebuilt a whole income statement to find profit. The report says that could still earn the marks, but the capital formula is the approach it recommends. It is also much quicker.

## What mistakes do examiners report?

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Entering the closing bank balance as the opening balance, or leaving out general expenses (June 2022) | Opening balance is b/d. Tick off every receipt and payment as you use it |
| Taking a 25% mark-up as 25% of the selling price (June 2022) | Write cost 100, profit 25, revenue 125 first |
| Not adding goods taken to cash drawings (June 2022) | Total drawings = cash drawings + goods at cost |
| Subtracting a fall in liabilities from a rise in assets (June 2025, Paper 1) | Both raise capital. Add them |
| Subtracting payments and drawings when finding cash sales (June 2025, Paper 1) | Add every use of the till money |
| Putting expenses or the bank balance in the purchases account (June 2025) | Only payables balances, payments to suppliers, discounts and returns belong there |
| Using total receipts from customers as revenue (June 2025) | Find credit sales through the receivables account |
| Using cash paid to suppliers as purchases, or putting a new vehicle in the trading account (7707, November 2023) | Use purchases from the payables account. A vehicle is a non-current asset |

On the income statement, the June 2025 report noted "Discount received was almost universally omitted". Add it below gross profit.

The 7707 November 2023 report called rebuilding a trading account from a mark-up "unknown territory" for many candidates. Practise it until it is routine.

<Tip title="Receipts are not revenue">
If a question gives "total receipts from customers", do not write it as revenue. Put it in the trade receivables account and find credit sales as the missing figure.
</Tip>

## Can you answer these practice questions?

Try each one on paper before you read the answer.

### Practice question 1

On 1 April 2025 Hira's business had total assets of $30 000 and total liabilities of $6 000. On 31 March 2026 it had total assets of $41 500 and total liabilities of $9 000. Hira took drawings of $11 000 and paid in $4 000 from an inheritance. Calculate her profit for the year.

**Answer.** Opening capital = 30 000 − 6 000 = 24 000. Closing capital = 41 500 − 9 000 = 32 500. Profit = 32 500 − 24 000 + 11 000 − 4 000 = **$15 500**.

### Practice question 2

Yusuf's trade receivables were $6 300 at the start of the year and $7 100 at the end. Customers paid $48 500. Discount allowed was $700 and an irrecoverable debt of $400 was written off. Cash sales were $12 000. His trade payables were $4 900 at the start and $3 600 at the end. He paid suppliers $36 200 and received discount of $500. All purchases were on credit. Calculate (a) revenue and (b) purchases.

**Answer.**

(a) **Trade receivables account**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 6 300 | Bank | 48 500 |
| Credit sales (missing figure) | 50 400 | Discount allowed | 700 |
| | | Irrecoverable debts | 400 |
| | | Balance c/d | 7 100 |
| | **56 700** | | **56 700** |

Revenue = 50 400 + 12 000 = **$62 400**.

(b) **Trade payables account**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Bank | 36 200 | Balance b/d | 4 900 |
| Discount received | 500 | Purchases (missing figure) | 35 400 |
| Balance c/d | 3 600 | | |
| | **40 300** | | **40 300** |

Purchases = **$35 400**.

### Practice question 3

Mehreen's revenue was $90 000 and her gross margin is 30%. Opening inventory was $11 000 and closing inventory was $9 500. Calculate (a) cost of sales, (b) purchases and (c) her mark-up.

**Answer.** Revenue 100, profit 30, cost 70.

(a) Cost of sales = 90 000 × 70/100 = **$63 000**.

(b) Purchases = cost of sales − opening inventory + closing inventory = 63 000 − 11 000 + 9 500 = **$61 500**.

(c) Gross profit is 90 000 − 63 000 = 27 000. Mark-up = 27 000 ÷ 63 000 × 100 = **42.86%**.

## FAQ

### What is incomplete records in accounting?

It is the topic where a trader has not kept full double entry records. You rebuild the missing figures, then find the profit and prepare the financial statements.

### What is the formula for profit in incomplete records?

Profit for the year = closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced. Each capital figure is total assets minus total liabilities.

### What is the difference between a statement of affairs and a statement of financial position?

They use the same layout. A statement of affairs is not based on full double entry records, and capital is its balancing figure.

### How do you find sales in incomplete records?

Find credit sales as the missing figure in a trade receivables account. Then add cash sales, which may come from a cash summary.

### What is the difference between mark-up and margin?

Mark-up is gross profit as a percentage of cost of sales. Margin is gross profit as a percentage of revenue. A 25% mark-up equals a 20% margin.

### Is incomplete records tested for service or manufacturing businesses?

No. The 2027 to 2029 syllabus for 0452 and 7707 says questions are set only on sole traders, not on service or manufacturing businesses.
