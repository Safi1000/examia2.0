Students call them "evaluate" questions, but the 0450 papers rarely use that word. The long questions say things like "Consider three ways... Which would be the best? Justify your answer." That one instruction is where a strong answer and a middling answer split apart. Below is how those questions are marked and the plan I teach for them.

## The facts (from the Cambridge syllabus)

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Short Answer and Data Response | Case Study |
| Time | 1 hour 30 minutes | 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Marks | 80 | 80 |
| Weighting | 50% | 50% |
| Questions | Four, with short answers and structured data responses | Four, based on a case study in a separate insert |
| Evaluation (AO4) share of the paper | 10% | 20% |

Across the whole qualification, evaluation (AO4) is 15% of the marks, analysis (AO3) is 25%, application (AO2) is 20% and knowledge (AO1) is 40%. Paper 2 is where evaluation counts most, so that is where this method earns you the most.

The syllabus defines **justify** as supporting a case with evidence or argument. In other words, saying which option is best is not enough. You have to show why it beats the others.

## How the long question is marked

Take Question 2(b) from the June 2024 Paper 21 (0450/21). It asked candidates to consider three ways the business in the case could increase sales, and to justify which would be the best. It was worth 12 marks, marked in levels:

| Level | Marks | What it needed |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 9 to 12 | Detailed discussion of two or more of the ways, and a well-justified recommendation |
| 2 | 5 to 8 | Detailed discussion of at least one way, and some justification of the choice |
| 1 | 1 to 4 | Limited discussion, little or no explanation |

Two things in that table matter more than anything else:

- **You need at least two options discussed in detail** to reach the top level. One brilliant paragraph is capped.
- **The recommendation is marked on its justification**, not on which option you pick. There is usually no single right answer.

The examiner report for that session said a lack of analysis and evaluation kept answers in the lower levels, and that the recommendation should be applied to the case.

## A plan for the 12-mark question

Spend the first two minutes on a plan in the margin, not on writing.

**1. One paragraph per option.** For each one:

- **Say what it is in this business.** Use a name, a product or a figure from the case.
- **Give one advantage, and follow it through.** Do not stop at "more customers". Take it to what happens next: more customers, so higher revenue, so the business can afford the new equipment the case mentions.
- **Give one drawback, also followed through.** Cost, time, risk, or something in the case that makes this option harder.

**2. The recommendation, as its own paragraph.** This is where most of the 12 marks are decided. A strong recommendation does three things:

- **Chooses.** "Option B is the best choice for this business."
- **Compares.** Says why B beats A, and why B beats C, using the case. In the bakery example below: "Unlike social media advertising, emailing offers reaches customers who already buy from the bakery, and most of its sales come from regulars."
- **Says what it depends on.** "This works only if the email list is up to date. If it is not, a website would reach more new customers in the long run."

<Tip>
If your recommendation could be copied into an answer about a different business, it is not applied. Every sentence in the final paragraph should use something from the case: a figure, a product, an objective or a problem the owner has.
</Tip>

## A model recommendation (my own example)

Here is a final paragraph I wrote for a made-up case. A family bakery sells mostly to regular customers, has a small budget, and wants to raise sales before a new competitor opens next month.

> I recommend emailing special offers to existing customers. It costs very little, which matters because the bakery's budget is small, and it can start this week, before the competitor opens. Social media advertising would reach more new customers, but it costs more and the results are slower to arrive. A website would take the longest to set up and would not be ready in a month. Emailing offers is the best choice only if the bakery keeps its regulars, so it should also check what the competitor charges for the same products.

Look at what it does. It chooses one option, uses two facts from the case (budget, timing), rejects the other two with a reason each, and ends with what the decision depends on.

## The mistakes that cap your marks

| Mistake | The fix |
|---|---|
| Listing all three options with one line each | Fewer options, followed through further. Two in detail beat three in outline. |
| Recommending without comparing | Name the rejected options in your final paragraph and say why they lose. |
| A conclusion that repeats the paragraphs above | Add something new: a comparison, a condition, a short or long term effect. |
| "It depends" with no decision | Make the choice first, then say what it depends on. |
| Generic answers | Use a fact from the case in every paragraph. |

## Sitting in 2027? The code changes

Cambridge is replacing 0450 with **Cambridge IGCSE Business (0264)**, first assessed in March 2027. The four assessment objectives stay the same: knowledge, application, analysis and evaluation. The weighting moves towards application: in 0264, Paper 1 is 40% knowledge, 30% application, 20% analysis and 10% evaluation, and Paper 2 is 30%, 30%, 25% and 15%.

For you, that means the plan above still works, and applying every point to the case matters even more. Check the 0264 specimen papers on the Cambridge website to see the new question styles.
