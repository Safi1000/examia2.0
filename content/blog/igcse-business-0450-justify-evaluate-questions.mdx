The longest questions on the 0450 case study paper rarely say "evaluate". On the June 2024 Paper 21, a 12-mark question gave three ways the business could raise its sales, asked which would be best, and told candidates to justify the answer. Most students can describe three options. Far fewer can show why one beats the other two, and that is where the top band is decided.

## The two papers

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Short Answer and Data Response | Case Study |
| Time | 1 hour 30 minutes | 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Marks | 80 | 80 |
| Weighting | 50% | 50% |
| Questions | Four, short answers and structured data responses | Four, on a case study in a separate insert |
| Share of marks for evaluation (AO4) | 10% | 20% |

Evaluation is worth twice as much on Paper 2 as on Paper 1. Across the qualification, the split is knowledge 40%, application 20%, analysis 25% and evaluation 15%.

The syllabus defines **justify** as supporting a case with evidence or argument. Naming the best option earns little. The marks are for the reasons it is better than the others.

## How the 12 marks are shared out

Question 2(b) on 0450/21, June 2024, was marked in three levels:

| Level | Marks | What it needed |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 9 to 12 | Detailed discussion of two or more of the ways, and a well-justified recommendation |
| 2 | 5 to 8 | Detailed discussion of at least one way, and some justification of the choice |
| 1 | 1 to 4 | Limited discussion, little or no explanation |

- **Level 3 needs at least two options in detail.** One excellent paragraph on a single option cannot get past Level 2.
- **The choice itself earns nothing on its own.** There is rarely one right answer. The marks go to the justification.

The examiner report for that session said a lack of analysis and evaluation kept many answers in the lower levels, and that the recommendation should be applied to the case.

## Your time

Paper 2 gives you 90 minutes for 80 marks, a little over a minute a mark. A 12-mark part deserves about 13 to 14 minutes: two minutes to plan, about eight on the options and three or four on the recommendation. If you have spent 20 minutes on one, stop, write the recommendation, and move on.

## A paragraph plan

**For each option you discuss**

1. **Place it in the business.** A name, a product or a number from the case.
2. **One advantage, followed through to the end.** Not "more customers", but more customers, so more revenue, so the loan the owner is worried about gets paid off sooner.
3. **One drawback, followed through.** Cost, time, risk, or a fact in the case that makes the option harder.

**The recommendation, as its own paragraph**

1. **Choose.** "Option B is the best choice for this business."
2. **Compare.** Why B beats A, and why B beats C, each with a reason from the case.
3. **Say what it depends on.** The one condition that would change your answer.

<Tip>
Test your final paragraph by swapping in a different business. If it still reads correctly, it is not applied, and it will not reach Level 3. Every sentence should use something only this case has: a figure, a product, an objective or a problem the owner faces.
</Tip>

## A weak paragraph, rebuilt

A made-up case: a family bakery sells mostly to regular customers, has a small marketing budget, and a competitor opens on the same street next month. One option is advertising on social media.

**Weak (Level 1):**

> Social media advertising is good because lots of people use social media, so the bakery will get more customers and more sales. But it can be expensive.

It is true, and it would work for any business on earth. Nothing in it belongs to the bakery.

**Strong (Level 3 quality):**

> Social media advertising could reach families in the area who have never visited, which matters because the new competitor will be fighting for exactly those customers. More first-time buyers would mean more revenue before the competitor is established. The drawback is cost: the bakery's budget is small, and paid adverts take weeks to build an audience, so most of the spending would happen before the competitor opens but the results would arrive after it.

Same idea. This time it uses three facts from the case (new competitor, small budget, one month), and both the advantage and the drawback are followed through to what they mean for the business.

## A recommendation, start to finish

> I recommend emailing special offers to existing customers. It costs very little, which suits the bakery's small budget, and it can start this week, before the competitor opens. Social media advertising would reach more new customers, but it costs more and the results arrive too late to matter this month. A website would take the longest to set up and would not be ready in time. Emailing offers is the best choice as long as the bakery's regulars are its main source of sales. If the competitor undercuts its prices, the bakery may need to review its pricing as well.

## Mistakes that cap the mark

| Mistake | The fix |
|---|---|
| Three options, one line each | Two options in detail beat three in outline. |
| A choice with no comparison | Name each rejected option in the final paragraph and say why it loses. |
| A conclusion that repeats earlier points | Add something new: a comparison, a condition, a short-term or long-term effect. |
| "It depends" with no decision | Choose first, then give the condition. |
| Answers that fit any business | Use a fact from the case in every paragraph. |

## Sitting in 2027? The code changes

Cambridge is replacing 0450 with **Cambridge IGCSE Business (0264)**, first assessed in March 2027. The four skills stay the same, but application gains weight. In 0264, Paper 1 is 40% knowledge, 30% application, 20% analysis and 10% evaluation, and Paper 2 is 30%, 30%, 25% and 15%. Applying every point to the case was always good technique. Under 0264 it carries more marks. The 0264 specimen papers on the Cambridge website show the new question styles.
