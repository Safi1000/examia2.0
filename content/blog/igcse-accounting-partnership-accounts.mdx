A sole trader keeps all of the profit. A partnership has to split it, and the partnership agreement says how. Most partnership questions are really one question: follow the agreement, in the right order, and show where every dollar went. Once you see the order, the appropriation account and the current accounts almost write themselves.

## What the 0452 syllabus asks for

The Cambridge IGCSE Accounting syllabus expects you to:

- explain the purpose of an appropriation account
- record interest on partners' loans, interest on capital, interest on drawings, partners' salaries and the division of the balance of profit or loss
- explain the uses of, and the differences between, partners' capital accounts and current accounts
- prepare capital and current accounts in ledger form and as part of the statement of financial position

It leaves out a partner joining or leaving, dissolving a partnership and changing the profit-sharing ratio, so you will not be asked about those.

## The three accounts you need

| Account | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Appropriation account | How the profit for the year is shared between the partners. It comes straight after the income statement. |
| Capital accounts | The money each partner put into the business. Usually fixed: the balance does not change unless a partner adds or takes out capital. |
| Current accounts | Everything each partner earns from the business (their share of the profit) and everything they take out (drawings). |

Keeping capital fixed and putting everything else through the current account is why a partnership needs both accounts.

## The order of the appropriation account

1. Start with the **profit for the year** from the income statement.
2. **Add interest on drawings.** Partners are charged interest on what they took out, so it increases the profit available to share.
3. **Less partners' salaries.**
4. **Less interest on capital.**
5. What is left is the **residual profit**. Share it in the **profit-sharing ratio**.

A salary to a partner is not an expense in the income statement. It is a share of the profit, so it belongs in the appropriation account. This is one of the most common mistakes I see in mock papers.

## Worked example

Amina and Bilal are in partnership. Their agreement says:

- Interest on capital at 5% a year.
- Bilal receives a salary of $8,000 a year.
- Interest is charged on drawings: Amina $600, Bilal $400.
- Remaining profit is shared Amina 3 : Bilal 2.

Information for the year ended 31 March 2026:

| | Amina | Bilal |
|---|---|---|
| Capital account (fixed) | $60,000 | $40,000 |
| Current account at 1 April 2025 | $2,500 credit | $1,200 debit |
| Drawings during the year | $20,000 | $18,000 |

Profit for the year was $48,000.

### Step 1: the workings

- Interest on capital: Amina 5% × $60,000 = **$3,000**. Bilal 5% × $40,000 = **$2,000**.
- Profit to share: $48,000 + $1,000 interest on drawings − $8,000 salary − $5,000 interest on capital = **$36,000**.
- Amina's share: 3/5 × $36,000 = **$21,600**. Bilal's share: 2/5 × $36,000 = **$14,400**.

Check: $21,600 + $14,400 = $36,000.

### Step 2: the appropriation account

**Amina and Bilal: appropriation account for the year ended 31 March 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Profit for the year | | 48,000 |
| Add interest on drawings: Amina | 600 | |
| Bilal | 400 | 1,000 |
| | | 49,000 |
| Less salary: Bilal | | (8,000) |
| Less interest on capital: Amina | 3,000 | |
| Bilal | 2,000 | (5,000) |
| | | 36,000 |
| Share of profit: Amina (3/5) | 21,600 | |
| Bilal (2/5) | 14,400 | 36,000 |

Every dollar is accounted for: the $49,000 available goes $8,000 in salary, $5,000 in interest on capital and $36,000 in shares of profit.

### Step 3: the current accounts

Everything a partner **earns** goes on the credit side. Everything a partner **takes or is charged** goes on the debit side.

**Current account: Amina**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drawings | 20,000 | Balance b/d | 2,500 |
| Interest on drawings | 600 | Interest on capital | 3,000 |
| Balance c/d | 6,500 | Share of profit | 21,600 |
| | 27,100 | | 27,100 |
| | | Balance b/d | 6,500 |

**Current account: Bilal**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balance b/d | 1,200 | Salary | 8,000 |
| Drawings | 18,000 | Interest on capital | 2,000 |
| Interest on drawings | 400 | Share of profit | 14,400 |
| Balance c/d | 4,800 | | |
| | 24,400 | | 24,400 |
| | | Balance b/d | 4,800 |

Bilal started the year with a **debit** balance. That means Bilal had taken out more than they had earned, so the opening balance goes on the debit side. Students who put it on the credit side end up $2,400 out.

### Step 4: the statement of financial position

| | Amina | Bilal | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| | $ | $ | $ |
| Capital accounts | 60,000 | 40,000 | 100,000 |
| Current accounts | 6,500 | 4,800 | 11,300 |
| | | | 111,300 |

## Where marks go

| Mistake | The fix |
|---|---|
| Salary in the income statement | It is an appropriation of profit, not an expense. |
| Interest on drawings subtracted | It is added in the appropriation account and debited in the current account. |
| Interest on capital on the wrong figure | Use the capital account balance the question gives, and the rate in the agreement. |
| A debit opening balance put on the credit side | Read "debit" or "overdrawn" in the question and place it on the debit side. |
| Shares that do not add back to the residual profit | Add the shares before you move on. It takes five seconds. |

<Tip>
Write the workings first, on one line each, before you draw any account. If the shares add back to the residual profit, the appropriation account will balance, and the current accounts take their figures straight from it.
</Tip>
