A bank reconciliation statement explains why the bank balance in your cash book is different from the balance on the bank statement. You do it in two steps. First, update the cash book for items only the bank knew about, such as bank charges and direct debits. Then list the timing differences, unpresented cheques and uncredited deposits, until the two balances agree.

## What does the syllabus say about bank reconciliation?

Bank reconciliation is section 3.3 of both the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-3.3) and the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The list is the same in both. You need to understand four things:

1. The use and purpose of a bank statement.
2. How to update the cash book for bank charges, bank interest paid and received, correction of errors, credit transfers, direct debits and standing orders.
3. The purpose and preparation of a bank reconciliation statement, including bank errors, uncredited deposits and unpresented cheques.
4. How digital business transactions affect the bank reconciliation process.

It sits in topic 3, "Verification of accounting records", with the trial balance, correction of errors and control accounts. The examiner reports show it tested in both Paper 1 (multiple choice) and Paper 2 (structured written questions).

## Why do the cash book and bank statement show different balances?

The cash book is your record of the bank account. The bank statement is the bank's record of the same account. They are written by different people at different times, so they differ for three reasons:

- **Items the bank has recorded but you have not.** You usually find out only when the statement arrives.
- **Timing differences.** You have recorded something, but it has not reached the bank yet.
- **Errors.** Either you or the bank has made a mistake.

### Which items update the cash book?

These appear on the bank statement first. Once you see them, you record them in the cash book.

| Item | What it is | Cash book side |
|---|---|---|
| Bank charges | Fees the bank takes for running the account | Credit (money out) |
| Bank interest paid | Interest charged on an overdraft or loan | Credit (money out) |
| Standing order | A fixed amount the bank pays for you on fixed dates, such as rent | Credit (money out) |
| Direct debit | A payment the receiver collects from your account. The amount and date can vary, such as electricity | Credit (money out) |
| Dishonoured cheque | A customer's cheque you paid in that their bank refused to pay | Credit (money out) |
| Credit transfer | Money paid straight into your account, often by a customer | Debit (money in) |
| Bank interest received | Interest the bank pays you on a positive balance | Debit (money in) |

Section 3.3 does not name dishonoured cheques. The syllabus lists them under control accounts (3.4). I teach them here because a November 2022 Paper 2 question put one in the cash book update.

A standing order is a fixed amount on fixed dates. A direct debit can vary in amount and date. The November 2022 examiner report said many candidates found this difference hard, so learn it in those words.

In the cash book, use the name of the other account, not the method of payment. Write "Rent", not "Standing order". Write "Bilal" (the customer), not "Dishonoured cheque". The same report picked out this labelling error.

### Which items go in the statement?

Timing differences are already in your cash book. The bank has not caught up yet. You do **not** touch the cash book for them.

| Item | What has happened |
|---|---|
| Unpresented cheques | You wrote a cheque and recorded it, but the supplier has not paid it into their bank yet |
| Uncredited deposits (also called outstanding lodgements) | You paid money in and recorded it, but the bank has not added it by the statement date |

### Where do errors go?

- **An error in the cash book** is corrected in the cash book. For example, a cheque for $370 written in the cash book as $730.
- **An error by the bank** is not your record to change. It goes in the reconciliation statement, and you ask the bank to fix it.

## Is a bank statement a debit or a credit balance?

In your cash book, money in the bank is a **debit** balance, because it is an asset to you. On the bank statement it is a **credit** balance, because the bank owes that money to you. The two records are mirror images:

| Position | Cash book | Bank statement |
|---|---|---|
| Money in the account | Debit balance | Credit balance |
| Overdraft | Credit balance | Debit balance |

The March 2022 examiner report noted that some candidates did not see that the bank statement balance would be a credit balance.

## How do you prepare a bank reconciliation statement step by step?

This is the method I teach. Do it in this order every time.

1. **Tick the matching items.** Tick every item that appears in both the cash book and the bank statement.
2. **Find the unticked items on the bank statement.** Bank charges, interest, standing orders, direct debits, credit transfers and dishonoured cheques go in the cash book. A bank error goes in the statement.
3. **Find the unticked items in the cash book.** Payments are unpresented cheques. Receipts are uncredited deposits. These go in the statement.
4. **Check for cash book errors.** Correct them in the cash book.
5. **Update the cash book and balance it.** This new balance is the correct bank figure for the statement of financial position.
6. **Prepare the bank reconciliation statement** from the updated balance, using one of the two layouts below.
7. **Check the last line** equals the bank statement balance given in the question.

Steps 5 and 6 are linked. The November 2022 report said many candidates could not make this link between the two parts of the question.

## What are the two layouts for a bank reconciliation statement?

You can start from either balance. The November 2022 mark scheme showed both presentations. Read the question, because it often tells you which balance to start from.

**Layout A: start from the updated cash book balance**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Balance as per updated cash book | X |
| Add unpresented cheques | X |
| | X |
| Less uncredited deposits | (X) |
| Balance as per bank statement | X |

Why add the unpresented cheques? You have already taken them off in the cash book, but the bank has not. Adding them back moves you to the bank's figure.

**Layout B: start from the bank statement balance**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Balance as per bank statement | X |
| Add uncredited deposits | X |
| | X |
| Less unpresented cheques | (X) |
| Balance as per updated cash book | X |

Here you do the opposite. The deposit is not on the statement yet, so you add it. The cheques have not left the bank yet, so you take them off.

A bank error is adjusted in whichever direction undoes the bank's mistake. If the bank wrongly took money out, the bank figure is too low. Starting from the bank statement, you add it back.

### How does an overdraft change the signs?

An overdraft is money you owe the bank. The safest method is to treat it as a **negative number** and keep the same layout. Write it in brackets at the top, then add and subtract as normal.

For example, an overdraft of $500 with unpresented cheques of $800 is (500) + 800 = 300. The answer is $300 in the bank, not an overdraft.

Some students treat the overdraft as a positive amount and flip every sign. Mixing the two methods in one statement gives a wrong answer, so pick one.

## Worked example: updating the cash book and reconciling

Leila's cash book shows a bank balance of $3 450 (debit) on 31 March 2026. Her bank statement on the same date shows $2 740 (credit). After ticking the matching items, these are left:

**On the bank statement only**

- Bank charges $45
- Standing order for rent $600
- Direct debit for electricity $230
- Credit transfer from a customer, Omar, $520
- Bank interest received $15
- Cheque from a customer, Bilal, $310, dishonoured

**In the cash book only**

- Cheque No. 104 to Asif $780, not yet presented
- Cheque No. 106 to Zain $410, not yet presented
- $1 250 paid in on 31 March, not yet credited by the bank

### Step 1: update the cash book

**Leila: Cash book (bank column) at 31 March 2026**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 3 450 | Bank charges | 45 |
| Omar | 520 | Rent | 600 |
| Interest received | 15 | Electricity | 230 |
| | | Bilal | 310 |
| | | Balance c/d | 2 800 |
| | **3 985** | | **3 985** |
| Balance b/d | 2 800 | | |

Check the arithmetic. Debits: 3 450 + 520 + 15 = 3 985. Credits before the balance: 45 + 600 + 230 + 310 = 1 185. So the balance is 3 985 − 1 185 = 2 800.

The correct bank figure for Leila's statement of financial position is $2 800, not $3 450 and not $2 740.

### Step 2: the bank reconciliation statement (layout A)

**Leila: Bank reconciliation statement at 31 March 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance as per updated cash book | | 2 800 |
| Add unpresented cheques: Asif (104) | 780 | |
| Zain (106) | 410 | 1 190 |
| | | 3 990 |
| Less uncredited deposit | | (1 250) |
| **Balance as per bank statement** | | **2 740** |

The last line is $2 740, the same as the bank statement. Layout B gives the same result in reverse: 2 740 + 1 250 − 1 190 = 2 800.

Notice what is missing from the statement: bank charges, rent, electricity, Omar, interest and Bilal. They were dealt with in the cash book, so they do not appear again. The November 2022 report noted items such as rates being wrongly included in the statement.

<Tip title="Every item goes in one place only">
Before you write the statement, label each item "CB" (cash book) or "BRS" (statement). No item gets both labels. The June 2022 report noted that many candidates thought one item would affect both records at once.
</Tip>

## Worked example: an overdraft and two errors

Tariq's cash book shows an **overdraft** of $1 620 (credit balance) on 30 June 2026. His bank statement shows an overdraft of $435 (debit balance). The differences are:

- Bank charges $85 and overdraft interest $40, only on the bank statement
- Direct debit for insurance $150, only on the bank statement
- Credit transfer from a customer, Hina, $900, only on the bank statement
- A cheque paid to a supplier, Raza, for $370 was entered in the cash book as $730
- The bank wrongly charged a $200 cheque written by another business to Tariq's account
- Unpresented cheques: No. 311 to Kamal $640 and No. 314 to Sadia $460
- $700 paid in on 30 June, not yet credited by the bank

### Step 1: update the cash book

The Raza error is in the cash book. Tariq recorded $730 going out when only $370 left. That overstates payments by $730 − $370 = $360. To correct it, debit the cash book with $360. The bank error does **not** go in the cash book.

**Tariq: Cash book (bank column) at 30 June 2026**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Hina | 900 | Balance b/d | 1 620 |
| Raza (correction) | 360 | Bank charges | 85 |
| Balance c/d | 635 | Bank interest | 40 |
| | | Insurance | 150 |
| | **1 895** | | **1 895** |
| | | Balance b/d | 635 |

Check. Credits: 1 620 + 85 + 40 + 150 = 1 895. Debits before the balance: 900 + 360 = 1 260. The balance is 1 895 − 1 260 = 635. The credit side is bigger, so the updated balance is still an overdraft: $635, brought down on the credit side.

### Step 2: the bank reconciliation statement (overdraft as a negative)

**Tariq: Bank reconciliation statement at 30 June 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Overdraft as per updated cash book | | (635) |
| Add unpresented cheques: Kamal (311) | 640 | |
| Sadia (314) | 460 | 1 100 |
| | | 465 |
| Less uncredited deposit | | (700) |
| | | (235) |
| Less cheque wrongly charged by bank | | (200) |
| **Overdraft as per bank statement** | | **(435)** |

Read it as plain maths: −635 + 1 100 = 465. Then 465 − 700 = −235. Then −235 − 200 = −435. That is an overdraft of $435, matching the bank statement.

Why take off the bank error? The bank took out $200 that it should not have, so its figure is $200 worse than the true figure.

## Why is a bank reconciliation statement prepared?

The syllabus asks for the purpose of the statement. A November 2022 Paper 2 question asked for two reasons, and the report called answers such as "find errors" vague. Say what is checked and where. These are specific enough:

- It **finds errors or missing items in the cash book**, so they can be corrected.
- It **finds errors on the bank statement**, so the bank can be asked to correct them.
- It **brings the cash book up to date** and gives the correct bank balance for the statement of financial position.
- It **shows unpresented cheques and uncredited deposits**, so the trader can follow up any that are delayed.
- It helps to **discover unauthorised payments** from the account, which reduces the risk of fraud.

Two facts also come up in Paper 1. A bank reconciliation statement is **not part of the double entry system**. It is a check on the cash book, which is. And it is **prepared by the business**, not by the bank. The March 2026 examiner report gave both points.

### What is a bank statement used for?

The June 2025 report said many answers described what a bank statement contains instead of what it is for. It also said "locates errors" was incomplete without saying where the errors are. Good uses are: to find why the cash book balance and the bank's balance differ, and to find errors or missing items in the cash book or the bank's records.

### How do digital transactions affect the process?

The syllabus names this point but does not say what answer it expects. The examiner reports I used do not comment on it. So this part is my own reasoning as a teacher, not Cambridge wording.

More payments are now made online, by card and by bank transfer. These often reach the bank account before the trader records them. So I would expect more items on the statement that must be added to the cash book.

Fewer cheques should also mean fewer unpresented cheques. Online banking lets a trader see the account every day, so the cash book can be checked more often and errors found sooner. The method itself stays the same. If Cambridge publishes guidance on this point, follow that.

## How is a bank reconciliation question marked?

Paper 2 has five questions worth 20 marks each, and bank reconciliation is usually one part of a question. In the November 2022 mark scheme, the cash book update carried five marks and the statement four. Each correct line earned its own mark. The mark for the opening line of the statement was given on the candidate's own updated balance.

So one wrong figure does not cost you the whole answer.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the 0452 and 7707 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Putting unpresented cheques or uncredited deposits into the cash book (Nov 2022) | They are already recorded. They belong in the statement only |
| Using the payment method as the label, such as "Standing order" (Nov 2022) | Use the other account's name: "Rent", "Insurance", the customer's name |
| Leaving timing differences out of the statement, or using your own layout (Nov 2022) | Follow the standard layout. The statement holds timing differences and bank errors only |
| Adding unpresented cheques and deducting uncredited deposits when starting from the bank statement (June 2025, Paper 1) | From the bank statement: add deposits, subtract cheques |
| Deducting the unpresented cheque when working towards the bank statement balance (March 2026) | From the cash book balance, unpresented cheques are added |
| Using the original bank balance instead of the corrected one (March 2026) | Always update first, then work from the updated figure |
| Preparing a full reconciliation statement when only a correct bank balance was asked for (June 2025, 7707 Nov 2023) | Answer the question set. If it asks for a balance, calculate the balance |

<Tip title="Start with what the question names">
If the question says "starting with the bank statement balance", use layout B. If it gives you a cash book to update, update it first and start the statement from your updated balance. If an earlier part went wrong, still start from your own figure. The June 2025 report shows a mark was given for doing this.
</Tip>

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper before you read the answer.

### Practice question 1

Noor's cash book shows a bank balance of $1 900 (debit) on 30 April. The bank statement shows bank charges of $35, a credit transfer from a customer of $410 and a standing order for rates of $250. None of these are in the cash book. Unpresented cheques total $620. A deposit of $380 has not yet been credited by the bank.

(a) Calculate the updated cash book balance.
(b) Calculate the balance on the bank statement.

**Answer**

(a) Updated cash book: 1 900 − 35 + 410 − 250 = **$2 025** (debit).

(b) Using layout A: 2 025 + 620 unpresented cheques − 380 uncredited deposit = **$2 265** (credit on the bank statement).

### Practice question 2

For each item, say whether it goes in the cash book (CB) or in the bank reconciliation statement (BRS).

1. Interest received from the bank, shown only on the bank statement.
2. A cheque paid in on the last day of the month, not yet on the statement.
3. A customer's cheque returned by the bank as dishonoured.
4. A cheque sent to a supplier that has not yet been presented.
5. The bank charged another customer's direct debit to this account.
6. A receipt of $95 was entered in the cash book as $59.

**Answer**

| Item | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CB, debit | Only the bank knew about it |
| 2 | BRS | Uncredited deposit, a timing difference |
| 3 | CB, credit | The money never arrived, so reverse the receipt |
| 4 | BRS | Unpresented cheque, a timing difference |
| 5 | BRS | Bank error, the bank must correct it |
| 6 | CB, debit $36 | Cash book error: 95 − 59 = 36 too little recorded |

### Practice question 3

On 31 October the bank statement shows an overdraft of $1 280. Unpresented cheques total $540 and a deposit of $900 has not yet been credited. Bank charges of $60 and a direct debit of $120 appear only on the bank statement.

(a) Calculate the updated cash book balance.
(b) Calculate the cash book balance before it was updated.

**Answer**

(a) Start from the bank statement and treat the overdraft as negative: (1 280) + 900 uncredited deposit − 540 unpresented cheques = **(920)**. The updated cash book shows an overdraft of $920, a credit balance.

(b) The updated balance already includes the $60 charges and $120 direct debit, which made the overdraft bigger. Remove them to go back: (920) + 60 + 120 = **(740)**. The original cash book showed an overdraft of $740.

## FAQ

### What is a bank reconciliation statement in simple words?

It is a short statement that explains the difference between the bank balance in your cash book and the balance on your bank statement. Done correctly, it starts at one balance and ends exactly at the other.

### Do you update the cash book or the bank statement first?

Update the cash book first. Then prepare the reconciliation statement from the updated cash book balance. You never change the bank statement yourself.

### Why are unpresented cheques added to the cash book balance?

Because the cash book has already deducted them, but the bank has not paid them yet. Adding them back takes you from your figure to the bank's figure.

### How do you show an overdraft in a bank reconciliation statement?

Write it in brackets as a negative number and use the normal layout. If the final figure is in brackets, it is an overdraft on the bank statement.

### Which balance goes in the statement of financial position?

The updated cash book balance. In Leila's example that is $2 800 as a current asset. In Tariq's it is an overdraft of $635 as a current liability.
