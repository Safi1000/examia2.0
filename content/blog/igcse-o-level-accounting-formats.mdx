In Paper 2, a correct figure in the wrong place often earns nothing. The maths is usually fine. The marks go on a missing heading, a total in the wrong column, or "less" written where it should say "add". Learn the layouts below once, properly, and those marks stop leaking. Every layout here is filled in with figures that add up, so you can check your own version line by line.

## Before you start: the names Cambridge uses

Cambridge uses international terms, and some names change in the syllabus for exams from 2027. The layouts stay the same. The 2026 syllabus states that "candidates will not lose marks for using different terms", but the safest habit is to use the newest names.

| Syllabus for exams in 2026 | Syllabus for exams from 2027 |
|---|---|
| Income statement | Statement of profit or loss |
| Provision for depreciation | Accumulated depreciation |
| Provision for doubtful debts | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Profit for the year | Profit for the year |

Older textbooks say "trading and profit and loss account", "net profit", "bad debts" and "long-term liabilities". Those are the same ideas under old names.

Two rules apply to every format:

- **Heading in three parts**: the business name, the statement name, and the period or date. An income statement is "for the year ended …". A statement of financial position is "at …".
- **Use the columns properly**: items in the inner columns, subtotals in the outer column. Brackets show a figure being deducted.

### What the examiner checks

- **Carriage inwards** goes in cost of sales. **Carriage outwards** is an expense. These two are easy to swap under pressure.
- **Closing inventory** comes off the cost of sales. It then appears again as a current asset.
- **Increase in the provision** is an expense. A decrease would be added to gross profit as income.
- For a **service business** (a tutor, a cleaner), there is no cost of sales and no gross profit. Start with fees or revenue, add other income, then deduct expenses.

### What the examiner checks

- **Current assets in order of liquidity**: inventory, trade receivables, other receivables, bank, cash.
- **Prepaid expenses** are other receivables (an asset). **Accrued expenses** are other payables (a liability).
- A **bank overdraft** is a current liability, never a negative current asset.
- A loss for the year is **deducted** from capital, in the same place the profit was added.

## 3. Partnership: appropriation account and current accounts

Ali and Bina share profits and losses in the ratio 3:2. Their agreement gives interest on capital at 5%, a salary of $8 000 to Bina, and interest on drawings. Capitals are Ali $40 000 and Bina $30 000. Profit for the year from the income statement is $42 000.

**Ali and Bina: Appropriation account for the year ended 30 June 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Profit for the year | | 42 000 |
| Add interest on drawings: Ali | 300 | |
| Bina | 200 | 500 |
| | | 42 500 |
| Less interest on capital: Ali | 2 000 | |
| Bina | 1 500 | |
| Partner's salary: Bina | 8 000 | (11 500) |
| Residual profit | | 31 000 |
| Share of profit: Ali (3/5) | 18 600 | |
| Bina (2/5) | 12 400 | 31 000 |

The current accounts are usually asked in columnar ledger form. Debits reduce what the business owes a partner. Credits increase it.

**Current accounts**

| Dr | Ali $ | Bina $ | Cr | Ali $ | Bina $ |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 Jul 2025 Balance b/d | | 600 | 1 Jul 2025 Balance b/d | 1 500 | |
| 30 Jun 2026 Drawings | 15 000 | 12 000 | 30 Jun 2026 Interest on capital | 2 000 | 1 500 |
| Interest on drawings | 300 | 200 | Salary | | 8 000 |
| Balance c/d | 6 800 | 9 100 | Share of profit | 18 600 | 12 400 |
| | 22 100 | 21 900 | | 22 100 | 21 900 |
| | | | 1 Jul 2026 Balance b/d | 6 800 | 9 100 |

In the statement of financial position, show capital accounts (Ali $40 000, Bina $30 000, total $70 000) and current accounts (Ali $6 800, Bina $9 100, total $15 900) separately. Together they give $85 900.

<Tip>
Interest on a **partner's loan** is not an appropriation. It is an expense in the income statement, because the loan is a debt of the business. Interest on **capital** goes in the appropriation account. If the question gives a partner's loan, look for its interest in the expenses first.
</Tip>

### What the examiner checks

- **Prime cost** is direct costs only: materials consumed, direct wages, direct expenses.
- **Three inventories, three places**: raw materials and work in progress in the manufacturing account, finished goods in the trading section.
- Costs shared between factory and office (rent, insurance) are split. Only the factory share goes here. The office share goes to the income statement expenses.

## 5. Club: income and expenditure account

A club does not make a "profit". It has a **surplus** (income more than expenditure) or a **deficit**. Its capital is called the **accumulated fund**. The income and expenditure account follows the same accruals rules as an income statement, so subscriptions must be adjusted first.

Green Valley Cricket Club received $5 600 in subscriptions during the year. At the start, members owed $300 and had paid $200 in advance. At the end, members owed $400 and had paid $500 in advance.

**Subscriptions account**

| Dr | $ | Cr | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Sep 2025 Balance b/d (owing) | 300 | 1 Sep 2025 Balance b/d (in advance) | 200 |
| 31 Aug 2026 Income and expenditure | 5 400 | Bank | 5 600 |
| 31 Aug 2026 Balance c/d (in advance) | 500 | 31 Aug 2026 Balance c/d (owing) | 400 |
| | 6 200 | | 6 200 |

The refreshments bar is a revenue-generating activity, so it gets its own small calculation: sales $4 200, less cost of refreshments ($300 opening inventory + $2 400 purchases less $200 closing inventory = $2 500), gives a profit of $1 700.

**Green Valley Cricket Club: Income and expenditure account for the year ended 31 August 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Income** | | |
| Subscriptions | 5 400 | |
| Profit on refreshments | 1 700 | |
| Donations | 500 | 7 600 |
| **Expenditure** | | |
| Groundsman's wages | 3 600 | |
| Rent of ground | 1 800 | |
| Electricity | 650 | |
| Depreciation of equipment | 900 | |
| Insurance | 350 | 7 300 |
| **Surplus for the year** | | **300** |

With an accumulated fund of $15 000 at the start, the closing accumulated fund is $15 000 + $300 = $15 300.

### Receipts and payments account or income and expenditure account?

| Receipts and payments account | Income and expenditure account |
|---|---|
| A summary of the cash book | Like an income statement |
| Shows all money in and out, including capital items | Revenue items only, adjusted for accruals and prepayments |
| Ends with a cash or bank balance | Ends with a surplus or deficit |
| No depreciation | Includes depreciation |
