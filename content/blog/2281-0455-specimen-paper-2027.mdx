The 2027 specimen papers for O Level Economics 2281 and IGCSE Economics 0455 are identical. Paper 1 has 40 multiple choice questions in 1 hour. Paper 2 has 80 marks in 2 hours: one compulsory 20 mark data question, then three 20 mark questions chosen from four. Below is every question in my own words, and how each one is marked.

For the full list of syllabus changes, read my [2281 syllabus 2027 article](/blog/o-level-economics-2281-syllabus-2027-changes). This article is about the papers themselves.

## Where can you download the 2027 specimen papers?

The syllabus points you to the Cambridge syllabus page for your code, 2281 or 0455. Both are linked at the end of this article. You need four files: specimen Paper 1, its mark scheme (the answer key), specimen Paper 2 and its mark scheme.

The syllabus also lists specimen paper answers among the resources on Cambridge's School Support Hub, which your school logs into. Ask your teacher for those.

One warning. My copy of the Paper 2 mark scheme is labelled v3, while the other three files are labelled v1. Download a fresh copy rather than using one passed around on WhatsApp.

### Are the 2281 and 0455 specimen papers the same?

Yes. I extracted the text of all eight files and compared them line by line. The only differences are the qualification name and the syllabus code in the headers. Every question, option, mark and line of the mark schemes is identical. So whichever code you sit, everything below applies to you. My [0455 syllabus 2027 article](/blog/igcse-economics-0455-syllabus-2027-changes) covers the IGCSE changes in detail.

## What does the specimen Paper 1 look like?

Paper 1 is 40 questions, 40 marks, 1 hour, with four options per question. That is still 90 seconds a question, the same pace as the old 30 question paper. Calculators are allowed.

### Which topics does Paper 1 test?

I sorted all 40 questions by the six sections of the 2027 syllabus. A few questions touch two topics, so I placed each one where its main idea sits.

| Syllabus section | Questions | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 The basic economic problem | 1 to 3 | 3 |
| 2 The allocation of resources | 4 to 10, 21 | 8 |
| 3 Microeconomic decision makers | 11 to 20 | 10 |
| 4 Government and the macroeconomy | 22 to 33 | 12 |
| 5 Economic development | 34 to 37 | 4 |
| 6 International trade and globalisation | 38 to 40 | 3 |
| **Total** | | **40** |

Three things stand out.

**Sections 3 and 4 carry more than half the paper.** Banking, households, workers, firms, costs and market structures, then fiscal, monetary and supply side policy, growth, unemployment and inflation. If your Paper 1 practice is weak anywhere, fix these first.

**Numbers matter.** Nine questions give you figures to work with: questions 6, 8, 23, 24, 30, 33, 35, 39 and 40. They cover PED, PES, adding up tax revenue, a budget balance, reading a data table, CPI weights, GDP per head, an exchange rate change and percentages of trade. Five more (4, 5, 10, 14 and 19) are built around a diagram.

**The answers are spread evenly.** In the answer key, A is correct 11 times, B 8 times, C 11 times and D 10 times. There is no pattern to guess from.

### Which 2027 changes show up in Paper 1?

The specimen tests several things that are new or more explicit in the 2027 syllabus:

- **Perfectly inelastic supply**, one of the newly named PES terms (question 8)
- **Age as an influence on saving** (question 12)
- **Types of merger**, now with definitions in the syllabus (question 16)
- **Advantages of competitive markets** (question 17)
- **Average total cost diagrams** (question 19)
- **Environmental protection as a macroeconomic aim**, and growth versus the environment (questions 22 and 28)
- **Causes of recession** (question 29)
- **Reasons for trade restrictions**, including sustainability (question 38)

Neither specimen paper has a question on deflation, fixed exchange rates, population pyramids or the claimant count. The syllabus lists all four as removed or cut back, and the specimen matches.

For a method for each type of multiple choice question, see my [Paper 1 MCQ tips for 2281 and 0455](/blog/o-level-igcse-economics-paper-1-mcq-tips).

## What does the specimen Paper 2 look like?

| Feature | Specimen Paper 2 for 2027 |
|---|---|
| Time and marks | 2 hours, 80 marks, 70% of the qualification |
| Section A | Question 1, compulsory, 20 marks, parts (a) to (f) |
| Section B | Answer any three of Questions 2 to 5, 20 marks each |
| Section B parts | (a) 2, (b) 4, (c) 6, (d) 8 in every question |

That gives you about a minute and a half per mark: roughly 30 minutes for Section A and 30 minutes for each Section B question.

### Section A: what does Question 1 ask?

Question 1 is a data response question about a real economy, Algeria. The extract covers climate change, the oil and gas industry, farming, productivity in services, life expectancy and migration, and the idea of a carbon tax. It includes a short fact file and a table comparing six countries. The paper tells you that you "must use the information provided", and the mark scheme enforces that.

| Part | Command word and task | Marks | How it is marked |
|---|---|---:|---|
| (a) | Calculate one sector's output from GDP and its share | 2 | 2 for the right answer; 1 for a correct method with a wrong answer |
| (b) | Identify two capital goods | 2 | 1 each; they must come from the extract |
| (c) | Explain one reason productivity rose in services | 2 | 1 for the reason, 1 for explaining it |
| (d) | Draw a demand and supply diagram for a change in taste | 4 | Labelled axes, original curves, the shift, old and new equilibrium; nothing for writing |
| (e) | Analyse the relationship between two columns of the table | 4 | Marks for the expected relationship, evidence from the table, why it happens, and an exception |
| (f) | Discuss whether the government should impose a carbon tax | 6 | Point by point, up to 4 marks for each side |

A few points the mark scheme makes clear:

- **Part (a) asks for working.** When a question tells you to show your working, a correct method earns a mark even if you slip on the final figure.
- **Part (b) is a reading test.** Capital goods you invent from your own knowledge score nothing here, even if they are correct economics.
- **Part (d) is all diagram.** Four marks, four features. Labels are where most of these marks are won or lost.
- **Part (e) rewards spotting an exception.** State the general pattern, quote countries from the table as evidence and explain why the pattern exists. Naming a country that breaks the pattern, and suggesting why, earns marks too.
- **Part (f) is the only discussion question in Section A,** and it is marked by counting developed points on each side, not by levels. One side only caps you at 4 out of 6.

The 2027 link is plain: the whole extract is built on environment and sustainability, which Cambridge has now embedded across the syllabus, and the syllabus now spells out the reasons for taxation, one of which is encouraging environmental sustainability.

### Section B: what do Questions 2 to 5 ask?

Each question opens with a short paragraph about a real country. You may use it, or bring in your own examples.

| Question | Context | The four parts |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Sweden: trade, unemployment, current account surplus | (a) define international trade; (b) explain two effects on firms of falling unemployment; (c) analyse two policies to cut unemployment; (d) discuss whether a bigger current account surplus raises living standards |
| 3 | Grenada and Venezuela: sugar, food prices | (a) identify how external cost differs from social cost; (b) explain two causes of a rise in PED for a staple food; (c) analyse how maximum and minimum prices cause disequilibrium; (d) discuss whether a government should subsidise a health product |
| 4 | Norway: electric cars, saving, jobs | (a) define division of labour; (b) explain two reasons saving may fall; (c) analyse two reasons fewer people choose one profession; (d) discuss whether consumers gain from more firms competing |
| 5 | Laos: exchange rate, university places, inflation | (a) identify two reasons a government may want a lower exchange rate; (b) explain how opportunity cost shapes a school leaver's choice; (c) analyse how lower investment can increase poverty; (d) discuss whether attracting foreign MNCs will reduce inflation |

Notice the spread. Questions 2 and 5 jump between syllabus sections: Question 2 alone runs from trade to unemployment policy to the balance of payments. If you skip whole sections of the syllabus, you may not find three questions you can answer fully.

The 2027 changes show here too. Question 4(d) is the new content on the advantages and disadvantages of competitive markets. Question 5(d) is about multinational companies, which sit beside the new globalisation content.

One caution: the specimen does **not** directly test the new causes and effects of globalisation (changes in trade restrictions, transport costs, communication costs and the movement of MNCs). A specimen is one sample paper, not a list of what will come up. Learn the whole topic.

## How does the new mark scheme reward analysis and evaluation?

The syllabus says the layout and wording of the Paper 2 mark scheme have changed, but that overall "the marking criteria stay largely the same". The specimen mark scheme shows what the new layout looks like in practice. There are two ways of marking.

### How is point marking used in Section A and parts (a) to (c)?

Every part of Question 1, and parts (a), (b) and (c) of each Section B question, is marked point by point. Each correct, relevant point earns a mark. Three rules in the mark scheme matter:

- A key term on its own earns nothing unless that is all the question asks for. You must show you understand it.
- Repeating a point you already made, or simply stating its opposite, earns nothing extra.
- In several questions that ask for two items, the guidance tells examiners to consider only the first three you give. Put your best points first.

For the 6 mark questions that ask you to analyse two things, the specimen gives up to 2 marks for naming two relevant ideas and the remaining marks for the chain of reasoning. So the naming is the easy part. The marks are in the links: how one thing leads to the next, until you reach the outcome the question asks about.

### How is part (d) in Section B marked with levels?

Each 8 mark part (d) is marked with a levels table printed in the mark scheme as Table A. In my own words:

| Level | Marks | What the answer needs |
|---|---:|---|
| 3 | 6 to 8 | Both sides argued and developed, with clear reasoning and accurate economics. The two sides need not be equally deep. |
| 2 | 3 to 5 | Sound reasoning about the issue, but thinner, or developed on one side only. |
| 1 | 1 to 2 | Correct terms or points that are stated and left unexplained. |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worth a mark. |

The ceiling is the lesson. **An answer that argues only one side cannot get past 5 out of 8,** however good it is. Level 3 also mentions pointing out that outcomes are uncertain, which is where words like "it depends on" earn their place.

### Where did the evaluation marks go?

Evaluation (AO3) is now 15% of Paper 2. Fifteen per cent of 80 marks is 12 marks. Yet the discussion questions you answer add up to 30 marks: 6 in Question 1(f) plus 8 in each of your three part (d) answers.

Cambridge does not label each mark with an objective, but the arithmetic tells you something important. More than half of the marks in those discussion questions must be rewarding knowledge and analysis, not judgement. The Level 3 description points the same way: it asks for clear analysis and accurate economics, not only for two sides.

So a strong part (d) answer is mostly economics. Two or three well developed points for, two or three against, each a full chain of cause and effect. Then a short judgement that says what the answer depends on. A long conclusion with thin analysis above it scores less than the reverse.

### When does the other side earn a mark?

Here is a question of my own, in the Section A style: *Discuss whether a government should tax sugary drinks.*

- **Point for:** the tax raises the price, so consumption falls, so fewer people suffer tooth decay and obesity, which cuts healthcare spending. This is a developed point.
- **Mirror statement (no extra credit):** without the tax, people keep drinking as much as before. This is the first point turned around. It adds nothing new.
- **Genuine point against:** demand for sugary drinks may be price inelastic because buyers are used to them, so consumption hardly falls. The tax then mostly raises revenue, and it takes a bigger share of income from poorer households.

The third statement uses a different idea, PED, and that is what makes it creditable. Every "on the other hand" needs its own reason.

## Where do students lose marks on the new papers?

There are no examiner reports for the new papers yet, because nobody has sat them. These rows come straight from the guidance in the specimen mark schemes.

| Mistake | What the mark scheme does |
|---|---|
| Section A answers from memory instead of the extract | Question 1(b) only credits capital goods named in the source |
| Writing a paragraph under the Question 1(d) diagram | No marks for written analysis; only the diagram's features score |
| Unlabelled axes or curves, or no new equilibrium | Each missing feature costs one of the four marks |
| Listing five reasons when two are asked for | Only the first three are considered |
| Using a term without showing what it means | A key term alone earns nothing |
| The opposite of a point offered as the other side | Mirror statements get no extra credit |
| Arguing one side only in part (d) | Capped at Level 2, so 5 out of 8 at most |
| Wrong final answer with no working shown | The method mark is lost too |

## How should you use the specimen in revision?

The specimen is the only paper written to the 2027 format, so use it carefully. Do not waste it in week one.

1. **Learn the format first from past papers.** Old 2281 and 0455 Paper 2s use the same (a) 2, (b) 4, (c) 6, (d) 8 pattern in Section B. Practise on those topic by topic using [2281 past papers](/past-papers/o-level-economics-2281), and skip questions on removed content.
2. **Sit the specimen as a full mock, timed.** Paper 1 in 60 minutes. Paper 2 in 120 minutes, about 30 minutes per question.
3. **Mark it with the official mark scheme, not your own sense of what is good.** For each part (d), decide honestly which level your answer reached, and why.
4. **Rewrite your weakest part (d).** Add the missing side, or extend each point by one more link.
5. **Check your Paper 1 errors against the topic table above.** Two wrong answers in the same section means that section needs work, not just those questions.
6. **Fill the gaps the specimen does not cover.** Use the [2281 syllabus checklist](/syllabus/o-level-economics-2281) to tick off every topic, including globalisation, and the [most repeated 2281 topics](/past-papers/o-level-economics-2281/most-repeated-topics) to see what old papers have tested most often.

For the full method on each command word, with model answers for 2, 4, 6 and 8 mark questions, see my guide on [how to answer IGCSE and O Level Economics questions](/blog/how-to-answer-igcse-o-level-economics-questions).

<Tip title="Use the Section A minutes for Section A">

## Can you answer three questions in the new style?

These are my own questions, built to match the specimen's command words and marks.

**1. Calculate (2 marks).** A country's GDP is $80bn. The secondary sector makes up 35% of GDP. Calculate the value of secondary sector output. Show your working.

*Answer:* $80bn × 35 ÷ 100 = **$28bn**. The method earns 1 mark even if the final figure is wrong, so always write it down.

**2. Explain two (4 marks).** Explain two reasons why the price elasticity of supply of a manufactured good may increase.

*Answer:* Firms may gain spare capacity (1), so they can raise output quickly when price rises (1). Firms may hold more stocks (1), so they can release extra goods onto the market at once (1). A longer time period would also work: more time lets firms hire workers and buy machinery.

**3. Discuss (8 marks).** Discuss whether a government should subsidise public transport.

*Answer outline:* For: a subsidy lowers fares, so more people use buses and trains; fewer cars mean less congestion and pollution, an external cost; workers can reach more jobs, which helps geographical mobility. Against: there is an opportunity cost, as the money could fund schools or hospitals; if demand is price inelastic, few people switch from cars; operators may become inefficient if the subsidy covers their losses. Judgement: it depends on how many drivers would switch, and on whether the government can afford it without cutting other spending. Two developed sides plus that judgement is Level 3.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the 2281 specimen paper the same as the 0455 specimen paper?

Yes. Apart from the title and the syllabus code, the question papers and mark schemes for 2027 are identical, word for word.

### How many questions are in Economics Paper 1 from 2027?

Forty multiple choice questions in 1 hour, each worth one mark. It is 30% of the qualification, the same weighting as before.

### How many questions do you answer in the new Paper 2?

Four. Question 1 in Section A is compulsory and worth 20 marks. Then you choose three of the four questions in Section B, each worth 20 marks, for 80 marks in total.

### Is there still evaluation in 2281 and 0455 from 2027?

Yes, but less of it. Evaluation is 10% of the qualification and 15% of Paper 2. In the specimen it appears in Question 1(f) and in every part (d) in Section B.

### How is part (d) marked in the new Paper 2?

With three levels. Level 3 (6 to 8 marks) needs a developed discussion of both sides. An answer that covers only one side can reach Level 2, which is 5 marks at most.

### Are old past papers still useful for the 2027 exam?

Yes, for practice by topic and for Section B technique, since the (a) to (d) pattern is the same. Skip questions on removed topics, and remember the old Section A was worth 30 marks, not 20.
