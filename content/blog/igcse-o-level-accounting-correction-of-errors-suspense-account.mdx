To correct an accounting error, write a journal entry: debit one account and credit another. If the error made the trial balance disagree, one side of the entry goes to a suspense account. This temporary account holds the difference. Once every error is corrected, the suspense account clears to zero. Then adjust the draft profit for each error.

## What does the syllabus say about correction of errors?

Correction of errors is section 3.2 of the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-3.2) and of the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. It covers five things:

1. Correcting errors with journal entries.
2. The suspense account as a temporary way to balance the trial balance.
3. Correcting errors through the suspense account.
4. Adjusting the profit or loss after the corrections.
5. The effect of the corrections on the statement of financial position.

The six errors that do not affect the trial balance are listed in section 3.1, the trial balance. Questions use both sections together.

Paper 1 is 40 multiple choice questions in 1 hour 30 minutes. Paper 2 is five compulsory questions of 20 marks each in 1 hour 45 minutes. The syllabus says Paper 2 may ask you to "prepare suspense accounts and journal entries". In the 2022 to 2025 examiner reports the topic came up in both papers.

## Which six errors do not affect the trial balance?

In these six errors the debits and credits are still equal. So the trial balance agrees, even though the books are wrong. My [trial balance guide](/blog/igcse-accounting-trial-balance) explains why. The figures below are my own.

| Error | Example | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Omission**: not recorded at all | A credit sale of $600 to Danish was never entered | Debit Danish $600, credit sales $600 |
| **Commission**: right type of account, wrong person | $350 received from Farah was credited to Fahad | Debit Fahad $350, credit Farah $350 |
| **Principle**: wrong type of account | Office equipment bought for $1 200 was debited to purchases | Debit office equipment $1 200, credit purchases $1 200 |
| **Original entry**: wrong amount on both sides | An invoice from Kamran for $870 was entered as $780 | Debit purchases $90, credit Kamran $90 |
| **Complete reversal**: right accounts, sides swapped | Cash sales of $250 banked were debited to sales and credited to bank | Debit bank $500, credit sales $500 |
| **Compensating**: two errors cancel out | The sales and wages accounts were both overcast by $100 | Debit sales $100, credit wages $100 |

"Overcast" means added up too high. "Undercast" means added up too low.

Two of these corrections catch students out. For an error of original entry you correct only the difference, here $90. For a complete reversal you double the amount. The first $250 cancels the wrong entry and the second $250 makes the right one.

## Which errors go through the suspense account?

An error goes through the suspense account only if it left the debits and credits unequal. These are the usual ones:

- **One side missing.** Wages paid were credited to bank but never debited to wages.
- **Both entries on the same side.** An expense was credited in its own account as well as in the bank.
- **Different amounts on each side.** Bank shows $300 received but the income account shows $30.
- **An account added up wrongly.** The sales account was undercast by $500.
- **A balance left out of the trial balance**, or written on the wrong side.

When the trial balance disagrees and the errors cannot be found straight away, the difference is put in a suspense account. The totals now agree, so draft financial statements can be prepared. Each error is corrected later by a journal entry with one side in suspense.

### Which side does the difference go on?

It goes on the side of the trial balance that is **short**. Label it "Difference on trial balance".

| Trial balance | Suspense account opens with |
|---|---|
| Credit total bigger than debit total | A debit |
| Debit total bigger than credit total | A credit |

## How do you write a correcting journal entry?

This is the method I teach. It works with or without a suspense account.

1. **Write what should have happened.** The correct debit and credit.
2. **Write what did happen.** The entries actually made.
3. **Compare the two, account by account.** Each account gets whatever moves it from "did" to "should".
4. **Send any unmatched side to suspense.** This happens when an entry was missing or doubled up.
5. **Check that total debits equal total credits.**

Here it is on a hard one. Electricity of $90 was paid by bank. Bank was credited correctly, but electricity was also credited with $90.

- Should: debit electricity $90, credit bank $90.
- Did: credit electricity $90, credit bank $90.
- Bank is right. Electricity must move from a credit of $90 to a debit of $90, so debit electricity $180.
- Nothing matches that debit, so credit suspense $180.

Use the exact name of each ledger account: "Wages", "Sales returns", "Omar". Never write the book the entry came from, such as "sales journal".

A narrative is a short note under a journal entry saying why it was made. Write one whenever the question asks for it. The mark schemes I checked for this topic set the journal as a table with no narrative column and gave no mark for one. So follow the layout on the paper.

<Tip title="Double it or not?">
Double the amount when the entry was on the wrong side of an account. Correct only the difference when the side was right but the figure was wrong. The 2022 and 2023 examiner reports list both mistakes.
</Tip>

Most students get stuck on which side the suspense entry goes. Write "should" and "did" every time and the side falls out of the comparison.

## Worked example: how do you clear a suspense account and correct the profit?

Nadia's trial balance at 31 March 2026 did not agree. The debit side totalled $84 120 and the credit side $84 600. She opened a suspense account and calculated a draft profit for the year of $18 500. These errors were then found. Ignore depreciation.

1. Wages of $380 paid by bank were not posted to the wages account.
2. Equipment bought for $2 400 was debited to the purchases account.
3. Discount received of $70 was entered correctly in the supplier's account but debited to the discount received account.
4. The sales account was overcast by $500.
5. A cheque for $260 from a customer, Sami, was entered in the bank account but not in Sami's account.
6. Goods costing $300 taken by Nadia for her own use were not recorded.

### Step 1: which errors affect the trial balance?

Errors 1, 3, 4 and 5 left the debits and credits unequal, so each has an entry in suspense. Error 2 is an error of principle and error 6 is an error of omission. They do not touch the suspense account.

### Step 2: the journal

Error 3 needs doubling. Discount received was debited with $70 when it should have been credited with $70.

**Nadia: Journal**

| Details | Debit $ | Credit $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1. Wages | 380 | |
| Suspense | | 380 |
| 2. Equipment | 2 400 | |
| Purchases | | 2 400 |
| 3. Suspense | 140 | |
| Discount received | | 140 |
| 4. Sales | 500 | |
| Suspense | | 500 |
| 5. Suspense | 260 | |
| Sami | | 260 |
| 6. Drawings | 300 | |
| Purchases | | 300 |
| **Total** | **3 980** | **3 980** |

Check: 380 + 2 400 + 140 + 500 + 260 + 300 = 3 980 on each side.

### Step 3: the suspense account

The debit side of the trial balance was short by 84 600 − 84 120 = $480. So the account opens with a debit.

**Nadia: Suspense account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Difference on trial balance | 480 | Wages | 380 |
| Discount received | 140 | Sales | 500 |
| Sami | 260 | | |
| | **880** | | **880** |

Debits: 480 + 140 + 260 = 880. Credits: 380 + 500 = 880. The account clears, so there is no balance to bring down. Each entry names the other account in its journal entry.

### Step 4: the corrected profit for the year

Take each error in turn and ask: did it change an income or an expense?

| Item | Effect | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Draft profit for the year | | 18 500 |
| Error 1: wages understated | − | (380) |
| Error 2: purchases overstated | + | 2 400 |
| Error 3: discount received understated | + | 140 |
| Error 4: sales overstated | − | (500) |
| Error 5: no effect | | 0 |
| Error 6: purchases overstated | + | 300 |
| **Corrected profit for the year** | | **20 460** |

Check: 18 500 + 2 400 + 140 + 300 = 21 340. Then 21 340 − 380 − 500 = 20 460.

Error 5 is between a customer's account and suspense, so profit does not change. Error 6 raises profit because goods taken for own use reduce purchases.

### Step 5: the statement of financial position

| Item | Effect |
|---|---|
| Equipment (non-current assets) | Increases by $2 400 |
| Trade receivables (Sami) | Decrease by $260 |
| Suspense account | The $480 debit balance is removed |
| Capital | Increases by $1 660 |

Profit rises by 20 460 − 18 500 = $1 960 and drawings rise by $300. So capital rises by 1 960 − 300 = $1 660. Check: 2 400 − 260 − 480 = $1 660, the same change on the assets side.

### Step 6: the corrected trial balance total

Some questions ask for the trial balance total after the corrections. Start from the original total and adjust only for balances that change on that side.

- Debit side: 84 120 + 380 (wages) − 260 (Sami) = $84 240.
- Credit side: 84 600 − 500 (sales) + 140 (discount received) = $84 240.

Errors 2 and 6 change nothing here. Each one moves an amount between two debit balances.

## How do corrections change profit and the statement of financial position?

An error changes profit only if it involves an income or an expense account. The suspense account is neither, so it never appears in the profit calculation.

| The error made | Draft profit is | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Income understated, or an expense overstated | Too low | Add |
| Income overstated, or an expense understated | Too high | Subtract |
| A non-current asset or drawings recorded as an expense | Too low | Add |
| An expense recorded as a non-current asset | Too high | Subtract |
| Only assets, liabilities, capital or personal accounts involved | Correct | None |
| One expense recorded as another expense | Correct | None |

The last row needs care. If carriage inwards is recorded as carriage outwards, profit for the year is correct but gross profit is not. Carriage inwards is part of cost of sales.

For the statement of financial position, look at the other side of each correction. It changes an asset, a liability or drawings. Every change in profit changes capital by the same amount.

## What harder versions do examiners set?

Three variations are worth practising. The figures are my own.

**Returns in the wrong returns account.** Goods returned to a supplier, $210, were debited correctly to the supplier but also debited to sales returns. Two accounts are wrong. Credit sales returns $210 to remove the entry. Credit purchases returns $210 to record it properly. Debit suspense $420. Profit rises by $420, shown as two lines of $210. The November 2023 7707 report said many candidates showed only one of the two returns figures.

**An expense debited to a liability.** Loan interest of $150 paid by bank was debited to the bank loan account. Debit loan interest $150 and credit bank loan $150. The entry was on the correct side of the wrong account, so do not double it. Profit falls by $150 and the loan rises by $150.

**Closing capital.** Some questions ask for capital after the corrections. Take opening capital, add the corrected profit and subtract the corrected drawings. In Nadia's case drawings include the extra $300.

## How is a correction of errors question marked?

I checked three Paper 2 mark schemes: 0452 March 2023, 0452 March 2025 and 7707 November 2023. In each one nearly all of a 20-mark question was on this topic. The pattern was the same:

- **Journal entries:** one mark for each correct line. In March 2023, five errors carried 10 marks.
- **Suspense account:** one mark for each correct entry. In November 2023 this was 5 marks, with an own figure mark for the difference.
- **Corrected profit:** one mark for each correct adjustment, plus an own figure mark for the final profit.
- **Other parts:** March 2025 asked for each error's effect on profit and on the bank balance. It also asked for the corrected trial balance total and the type of error.

An own figure mark rewards a correct method even if an earlier figure was wrong. So show one labelled line for each error, with a clear + or −.

In Paper 1, the reports describe questions on the opening entry in a suspense account and its balance before corrections. Others asked for the journal entry for one error, or the corrected profit.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the examiner reports. November 2023 is the 7707 report and the rest are 0452. The 7707 reports for June 2022 and June 2023 make the same points.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Not doubling when an entry was on the wrong side (June 2022, March 2023, June 2023) | Cancel the wrong entry, then make the right one |
| Doubling, or using the full amount, when only the difference was needed (March 2023, June 2023) | Right side, wrong figure: correct the difference only |
| Naming a book or a made-up account, such as "sales journal" or "cash drawings" (June 2022, November 2023, March 2026) | Write the ledger account name: "Sales", "Drawings" |
| Putting errors that do not affect the trial balance into suspense (March 2023, June 2023, November 2023) | Only errors with unequal debits and credits go to suspense |
| Opening difference on the wrong side, or a balance brought down on the suspense account (June 2022, June 2023, March 2025) | Put the difference on the short side. The account then clears |
| Leaving out the draft profit, adjusting for errors with no profit effect, or reversing the signs (March 2023, June 2023, November 2023) | Start from the draft profit. Adjust only for income and expense errors |
| Adjusting the trial balance total for every error, or leaving out the original total (March 2025) | Start from the original total. Adjust only for balances that change |
| Calling an error of commission an error of principle (March 2022) | Commission: wrong person. Principle: wrong type of account |

The November 2023 7707 report was firm about the opening line of the suspense account. It must be labelled as the difference on the trial balance, and "it is not a balance b/d".

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper first. All figures are my own.

### Practice question 1

Give the journal entry to correct each error, and its effect on profit for the year.

1. Repairs to machinery of $340 were debited to the machinery account.
2. A credit purchase of $1 560 from Lina was entered in both accounts as $1 650.
3. Stationery of $75 paid by bank was debited to bank and credited to stationery.

**Answer**

| Error | Journal entry | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Principle | Debit machinery repairs $340, credit machinery $340 | Falls by $340 |
| 2. Original entry | Debit Lina $90, credit purchases $90 | Rises by $90 |
| 3. Complete reversal | Debit stationery $150, credit bank $150 | Falls by $150 |

Net effect on profit: 90 − 340 − 150 = a fall of $400.

### Practice question 2

Bilal's trial balance did not agree, and the difference was entered in a suspense account. His draft profit for the year was $12 000. These errors were found:

1. Carriage inwards of $120 was debited to the carriage outwards account.
2. Rent paid of $640 was entered correctly in the bank but as $460 in the rent account.
3. Commission received of $210 was debited to the bank and also debited to the commission received account.

(a) Prepare the journal entries. (b) Prepare the suspense account. (c) Calculate the corrected profit for the year.

**Answer**

(a)

| Details | Debit $ | Credit $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1. Carriage inwards | 120 | |
| Carriage outwards | | 120 |
| 2. Rent | 180 | |
| Suspense | | 180 |
| 3. Suspense | 420 | |
| Commission received | | 420 |
| **Total** | **720** | **720** |

(b) The difference is the balancing figure: 420 − 180 = $240 on the credit side. So the debit side of the trial balance was $240 bigger.

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Commission received | 420 | Difference on trial balance | 240 |
| | | Rent | 180 |
| | **420** | | **420** |

(c) Corrected profit: 12 000 − 180 + 420 = $12 240. Error 1 lowers gross profit by $120, but profit for the year does not change.

### Practice question 3

Mehreen's draft profit for the year was $15 300. These errors were found:

1. Cash of $500 taken by Mehreen for her own use was debited to the wages account.
2. Office furniture bought for $1 800 was debited to office expenses.
3. Cash sales of $240 were not recorded at all.

(a) Calculate the corrected profit for the year. (b) State the effect of the corrections on the statement of financial position.

**Answer**

(a) Each error understated profit. Corrected profit: 15 300 + 500 + 1 800 + 240 = $17 840.

(b)

| Item | Effect |
|---|---|
| Office furniture (non-current assets) | Increases by $1 800 |
| Cash | Increases by $240 |
| Capital | Increases by $2 040 |

Profit rises by $2 540 and drawings rise by $500, so capital rises by $2 040. That equals the rise in assets: 1 800 + 240 = $2 040.

## FAQ

### What is a suspense account in simple words?

It is a temporary account that holds the difference when a trial balance does not agree. Once every error is corrected, its balance is zero.

### Which errors go through the suspense account?

Only errors that left the debits and credits unequal. The six errors that do not affect the trial balance never use it.

### Why do you double the amount for a complete reversal?

The wrong entry must be cancelled and then the right entry made. Each step is the full amount.

### Does correcting an error always change profit?

No. Only errors involving an income or expense account change profit. A correction between two personal accounts has no effect.

### Is a narrative needed in a correcting journal entry?

Write one if the question asks for it. The mark schemes I checked set the journal as a table with no narrative column.

### What if my suspense account does not clear to zero?

One of your corrections is wrong, or you included an error that does not affect the trial balance. Check the side of the opening difference too.
