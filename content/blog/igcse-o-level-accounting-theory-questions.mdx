Accounting theory questions are the written parts of Paper 2: define a term, state reasons, explain how a concept is applied, or advise the owner. Each mark goes to one separate, correct point. "Explain" needs a reason or a result added to the point, and "advise" needs points on both sides plus a clear decision. Learn the definitions exactly, then tie every point to the business in the question.

## How many marks are theory questions worth in Paper 2?

About 40 of the 100, by my count. Paper 2 in both 0452 and 7707 has five compulsory questions of 20 marks each, 100 marks in 1 hour 45 minutes. I went through the 0452 specimen Paper 2 for 2027 and counted every part answered in words rather than figures. The grouping below is mine, not Cambridge's.

| Type of written part | Specimen parts | Marks |
|---|---|---:|
| Define, state or give | 1(a), 2(a), 2(b), 3(a), 4(d), 5(a), 5(b) | 15 |
| Name the concept or ethical principle | 2(e), 2(f) | 4 |
| Explain or suggest | 1(c), 2(g), 5(e) | 6 |
| Advise, with arguments for and against | 1(e), 3(d), 5(f) | 15 |
| **Total** | | **40** |

The syllabus weighting points the same way. In Paper 2, 60% of the marks test knowledge and understanding, 25% analysis and 15% evaluation. The advise parts are where most of that evaluation sits.

So strong layouts are not enough on their own. A student who is weak on words gives away a large share of the paper.

Any point can, because the questions are based on all the subject content. These are the ones that ask you to explain, list or judge rather than calculate. | 3.1 Trial balance | Purposes and limitations; the six errors it does not reveal |

## How is each command word marked?

The command word tells you how much to write. These are Cambridge's command words in my own plain wording, with how the specimen mark scheme and recent examiner reports show them being rewarded.

| Command word | What it asks for | How marks are usually given |
|---|---|---|
| State, Give, Identify | A short fact or name | 1 mark per correct point, up to the number asked |
| Define | The precise meaning | Often 2 marks: the basic idea, then the detail that separates it from a similar term |
| Explain | Reasons, or how and why, with support | 1 mark for the point, often 1 more for developing it |
| Suggest | Apply what you know to this case, where several answers could be right | 1 mark per sensible reason that fits the data given |
| Advise, Discuss, Justify | Advise: a suggested course of action. Discuss: the issue in depth. Justify: a case backed by argument | In the specimen: points for and against, then 1 mark for a decision |

Four marking rules sit behind every written part. I have paraphrased them from the general marking principles at the front of the mark scheme.

- **Your own words are fine.** An answer worded differently from the mark scheme earns the mark if it means the same thing.
- **A key term alone is not enough.** Writing "prudence" earns nothing unless the question only asks you to name it. You must show you understand it.
- **Repeating a point earns nothing.** "Profit rises" and "losses fall" count as one point, not two.
- **Contradictions lose the mark.** If you write two opposite answers to cover yourself, neither is credited.

## How do I answer a define or state question?

Write a complete sentence that would make sense to someone who has never seen the question. For a 2 mark definition, give the basic idea, then the detail that makes it different from its nearest neighbour.

**Define an error of omission (2 marks).** A transaction that has been left out of the books completely (1), so neither the debit nor the credit entry was made (1).

The second half is what separates it from a single entry error, which would make the trial balance disagree. For the other five errors, my [correction of errors guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-correction-of-errors-suspense-account) gives a definition for each.

For "state two reasons" questions, write two points that are clearly different. Number them. A second point that only says the first one again in other words scores nothing.

The June 2025 examiner report found that defining equity "proved to be a very difficult question". Short definitions of everyday terms are easy marks to drop, so practise each one until you can write it without stopping to think.

## How do I explain how an accounting concept is applied?

Examiner reports keep flagging this question type. When a question asks how a concept is applied to a particular item, many students write the general definition of the concept instead. That does not answer the question.

Use this three step frame:

1. Name the item from the question.
2. Say how it is treated in the accounts.
3. Say what would be wrong if it were not treated that way, usually that profit or assets would be overstated or understated.

**Explain how the prudence concept is applied to irrecoverable debts (2 marks).** The debt that will not be paid is written off as an expense and removed from trade receivables (1). This stops profit for the year and the value of trade receivables being overstated (1).

Here are all ten concepts in 7.1, each with the idea and one application you can adapt.

| Concept | The idea in my words | A typical application |
|---|---|---|
| Business entity | The business and its owner are treated as separate | Goods taken by the owner are recorded as drawings, not as a business expense |
| Consistency | The same methods are used from one year to the next | The same depreciation method each year, so profits can be compared |
| Duality | Every transaction has two equal effects, a debit and a credit | Buying a van on credit increases vehicles and increases the amount owed to the seller |
| Going concern | The business is expected to carry on trading | Assets are shown at cost less depreciation, not at what they would fetch if the business closed |
| Historic cost | Assets and expenses are recorded at what they actually cost | Premises bought for $80 000 stay at $80 000 in the accounts even if their market value rises |
| Matching (accruals) | Income and expenses belong to the period they relate to, not the period of payment | Only this year's rent goes to the statement of profit or loss; rent paid in advance is a prepayment |
| Materiality | Small items are not worth separate treatment | A $15 stapler is treated as an expense, even though it will last for years |
| Money measurement | Only items that can be measured in money are recorded | The skill of the staff does not appear in the accounts |
| Prudence | Profits and assets must not be overstated | Inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value |
| Realisation | Revenue is recorded when goods or services pass to the customer | A credit sale is revenue on the day the goods are delivered, not when the cash arrives |

<Tip title="Define only when asked to define">
If the question says "how" or "why", your first sentence should mention the item in the question. That might be the rent, the inventory or the owner's drawings. If your answer would fit any question about that concept, it is a definition, and it will not earn the marks.
</Tip>

The 7.1 questions in past papers are the best practice for this. You can work through the past paper questions on [accounting concepts for 0452](/past-papers/igcse-accounting-0452/topics/7-1-accounting-concepts) one after another. Check each against its mark scheme.

Reciting the ten definitions is the easy part. Applying them to an item you have not seen before, under time pressure, is the skill these questions test.

## How do I answer ethics questions?

Section 7.2 has three parts: why accountants need an ethical framework, the five fundamental principles, and why applying them matters to stakeholders and society. The specimen asks you to name the principle shown in a short situation, then explain why the framework matters.

Learn each principle with a situation of your own. A list of names will not help you when the situation is unfamiliar. The syllabus names the five principles but does not define them, so the meanings and situations below are my own wording.

| Principle | What it means | A situation that shows it |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity | Being straightforward and honest | An accountant refuses to leave a cash shortage out of the accounts |
| Objectivity | Not letting bias, a conflict of interest or pressure from others affect judgement | An accountant turns down an expensive gift from a client whose accounts she is checking |
| Professional competence and due care | Having the knowledge and skill the work needs, and doing it carefully | An accountant checks every figure against the source documents before she signs off a client's accounts |
| Confidentiality | Not sharing client information without permission | An accountant will not tell a friend how much profit a client made |
| Professional behaviour | Obeying the law and avoiding anything that harms the profession's name | An accountant does not post rude comments about clients online |

One case needs care. In the specimen Paper 2, an accountant will not advise on a complex issue until she has done some training and research. The mark scheme gives **professional behaviour** as the answer. From the names of the principles alone, I would have expected professional competence and due care, and many students will too.

My advice is my own judgement, not a Cambridge ruling. For a situation about refusing work until trained, give the specimen's answer. Use professional competence and due care when the situation is about working carefully or having the right skills.

**Explain why an ethical framework matters to the people who use a business's accounts (2 marks).** Lenders and investors did not prepare the accounts, so they need to trust that the accountant was honest and careful (1). With that trust they can make sound decisions, such as whether to lend to the business (1).

## How do I answer technology and storage questions?

Section 7.3 is practical. The syllabus says you do not need to know any particular accounting software. You need to know why data must be stored safely and sustainably, the risks if it is not, and the four types of storage. The syllabus lists the four types but not their advantages and disadvantages, so the points and examples below are my own.

| Storage type | One advantage | One disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Manual (paper files) | No equipment or internet needed | Easily lost or destroyed by fire or water, and slow to search |
| Data storage devices (USB drive, external hard drive) | Cheap, and the business holds the data itself | Can be lost, stolen or damaged, and needs regular backups |
| Cloud services | Records can be reached from any device and are backed up off site | Needs internet access, usually has a regular fee and depends on a strong password |
| Other digital services (for example online banking or accounting apps) | Transactions can be recorded automatically and quickly | The business relies on another company to keep its data secure |

For "state ways to store records securely", give separate actions, one per line. The specimen mark scheme credits actions such as strong passwords, regular backups and limiting who can use the computer.

## How do I write a five mark advise answer?

Paper 2 usually includes three of these. The examiner reports for March 2025 and June 2025 both describe them as three five mark questions with points for and against and a recommendation. In the specimen, the advise parts are marked like this:

- Up to 3 marks for arguments for, and up to 3 for arguments against.
- No more than 4 marks in total for the arguments.
- 1 mark for a recommendation based on at least one argument on each side.

So the full mark shape is two points for, two points against and a decision. A fifth argument earns nothing, and a decision without both sides earns nothing either. Some questions set the shape for you, such as one advantage and one disadvantage of each of two options. Follow whatever the question asks.

### Worked example: should Bilal offer a cash discount?

This is my own question. Bilal sells phone accessories to shops on credit. His credit sales are $108 000 a year and his customers owe him $18 000 at the year end. His profit for the year is $25 000. He is thinking of offering a 2% cash discount to customers who pay within 10 days. Advise Bilal whether he should offer the discount. (5 marks)

First, the figures that make the answer specific to Bilal:

| Working | Figure |
|---|---|
| Trade receivables turnover: $18 000 ÷ $108 000 × 365 | 60.8, about 61 days |
| Discount if every customer takes it: 2% × $108 000 | $2 160 a year |
| Profit if every customer takes it: $25 000 − $2 160 | $22 840 |

In my judgement you do not need these calculations for the marks. But figures from the question make each point specific.

**A weak answer.** For: customers will pay faster. Cash flow will be better. Against: costs will increase. Profit will go down. He should offer it.

I would give this 2 marks: one for faster payment and one for profit falling. "Cash flow will be better" is the same idea as paying faster with no reason added. "Costs will increase" is too vague, and the decision does not refer to any argument.

**A full mark answer.**

1. **For:** customers who take the discount pay within 10 days instead of about 61, so Bilal has cash sooner to pay his own suppliers. (1)
2. **For:** money is owed to him for a shorter time, so fewer debts are likely to become irrecoverable. (1)
3. **Against:** discount allowed is an expense. If every customer takes it, it costs $2 160 a year and profit falls from $25 000 to $22 840. (1)
4. **Against:** customers who already pay promptly will now get a discount without changing anything. (1)
5. **Decision:** Bilal should offer the discount, because getting his cash about 50 days earlier and cutting irrecoverable debts is worth the fall in profit. He should check after six months whether customers are really paying sooner. (1)

Every point names Bilal's situation. Each one could only have been written for this question.

<Tip title="Plan the five lines before you write">
Spend 30 seconds writing "F, F, A, A, D" in the margin and a word next to each. The examiner reports keep asking students to plan these answers so the points come in a logical order and the decision is clear.
</Tip>

## What are the best model answers for purposes and limitations?

Each of these is a syllabus point, so it pays to have a ready answer. The points are my own wording, not mark scheme text. Learn the idea, then fit it to the question.

| Question | Points that earn marks |
|---|---|
| Purposes of a trial balance | Checks the arithmetic accuracy of the double entry; gives a summary of balances for preparing the financial statements |
| Limitation of a trial balance | It can agree even when there are errors: commission, compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry and principle |
| Purposes of control accounts | Check the accuracy of the sales or purchases ledger; give a quick total of trade receivables or payables; help find errors and fraud |
| Why provide for depreciation | Spreads the cost of the asset over its useful life (matching); shows the asset at a more realistic value |
| Why keep an allowance for irrecoverable debts | Some customers may not pay (prudence); shows trade receivables at the amount likely to be received |
| Limitations of accounting statements | Assets at historic cost, not current value; different accounting policies make comparison hard; nothing shown for things like staff skill, location or the economic climate |
| Why profit and cash differ | Credit sales and purchases; depreciation is not a cash payment; capital spending and drawings are not expenses; accruals and prepayments |

Calculations can also turn into theory. If a question asks why a ratio worries the owner, each mark is a fact from the figures plus what it leads to. "Trade receivables turnover rose from 30 to 38 days, so more debts may become irrecoverable" is one full point. The [ratios guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-ratios-formulas) shows the interpretation for every ratio on the list.

## Where do students lose marks on theory questions?

Every row comes from a Cambridge examiner report for 0452 or 7707.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Defining the concept when the question asks how it is applied to an item (7707, November 2023, prudence and matching) | Start with the item in the question and say how it is treated |
| Stopping at a general statement, such as writing only that the debt should be treated as an expense (June 2023) | Link the item to the result: the debt is written off so that profit and assets are not overstated |
| General statements not linked to the case, such as "costs will increase" (March 2026) | Say which cost, and why it rises in this business |
| One standard answer written out and repeated across all three advise questions (June 2025, Paper 22) | Read each scenario and use its details: rented premises, a new supplier, an existing discount |
| Answering a different question, such as discussing trade discount when the case is about cash discount (March 2026) | Underline the exact change being proposed before you start |
| No clear recommendation (June 2025, March 2026) | End with a separate sentence: "He should..." |
| A vague point, such as saying a bank statement finds errors without saying where (June 2025) | Finish the thought: errors in the cash book, found by comparing it with the bank statement |
| Duality described only as entering everything twice (March 2026) | Add that each transaction has a debit and a credit of equal value |

## Practice questions

Write your answer before you read mine.

**1. Explain how the business entity concept is applied when the owner takes goods from the business for his own use. (2 marks)**

The goods are recorded as drawings, which reduce the owner's capital, and are taken out of purchases (1). They are not treated as a business expense, so profit for the year is not understated (1).

**2. State two disadvantages of storing accounting records only on a USB drive. (2 marks)**

The drive can easily be lost or stolen, taking the records with it (1). If the drive fails and there is no backup, the records are gone for good (1).

**3. Ayesha runs a bakery and uses paper records. She is thinking of buying accounting software for $600 a year. Advise her whether she should buy it. (5 marks)**

For: totals and balances are worked out automatically, so she makes fewer arithmetic mistakes (1). For: she can produce invoices and her statement of profit or loss quickly, saving her time to run the bakery (1). Against: the $600 a year is an extra expense that reduces her profit (1). Against: she will need time to learn the software, and her records are at risk if she does not back them up (1). Decision: Ayesha should buy the software, because the time saved and greater accuracy are worth $600 a year, provided she backs up her data regularly (1).

## FAQ

### Are there theory questions in Paper 1 as well?

Yes, as multiple choice. The 2027 specimen Paper 1 has questions on an accounting concept, an ethical principle, and technology and storage. You choose an answer instead of writing one, so the same knowledge helps in both papers.

### Do I need to memorise definitions word for word?

No. The mark scheme accepts any wording with the same meaning. But a definition you know exactly is quicker to write and less likely to miss the detail that earns the second mark.

### How long should a theory answer be?

Use the marks as your guide, as the syllabus itself advises. For a 2 mark part, two clear sentences are enough. For a 5 mark advise part, five short points.

### What is the difference between explain and state?

"State" wants a short fact. "Explain" wants the fact plus a reason or a result, usually linked with "so" or "because". If an explain answer has no "so" or "because" in it, it is probably a state answer.

### Is theory enough to get an A\*?

No. Most of Paper 2 is still figures and layouts. But by my count about 40 of the 100 marks are written, so they matter at every grade. My [A\* guide for 0452 and 7707](/blog/how-to-get-a-star-igcse-o-level-accounting) shows how both papers combine.
