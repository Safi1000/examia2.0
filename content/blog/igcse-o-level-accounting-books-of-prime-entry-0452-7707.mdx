Books of prime entry are where a transaction is first recorded, from its business document, before it reaches the ledger. IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 name seven: the sales, purchases, sales returns and purchases returns journals, the general journal, the cash book and the petty cash book. Credit sales and purchases go in the journals, money goes in the cash books, and everything else goes in the general journal.

## What does the syllabus ask you to know?

Books of prime entry are section 2.3 of the 0452 and 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029, with business documents in section 2.2. Here is what section 2.3 asks for, in my words.

| Syllabus point | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Process data in the seven books | Write up each book from documents or a list of transactions |
| Post from the books to the ledger | Know which account each line and each total goes to |
| Trade discount and cash discount | Treat each one correctly |
| Dual function of the cash book | Explain that it is a book of prime entry and the bank and cash ledger accounts |
| Ways of paying and receiving | Record cash, cheques, debit and credit cards, online and bank transfers |
| The imprest system | Explain its purpose and operate it |
| Keeping cash at the business | Give benefits and limitations |
| Manual and digital original entry | Give benefits and limitations of each |

On our past paper pages, 108 questions from the 0452 and 7707 papers of 2022 to 2026 carry the 2.3 tag, as main or second tag (0452: 68, 7707: 40). Question 1 of Paper 2 often opens with a cash book, a journal or a petty cash book.

## Which book of prime entry records which transaction?

| Book | What goes in it | Source document |
|---|---|---|
| Sales journal | Goods sold on credit | Copy of the sales invoice |
| Purchases journal | Goods for resale bought on credit | Invoice received from the supplier |
| Sales returns journal | Goods returned by credit customers | Credit note sent to the customer |
| Purchases returns journal | Goods returned to credit suppliers | Debit note sent, or credit note received |
| Cash book | All money in and out of the bank and the cash till | Receipts, cheque counterfoils, paying-in slips, bank statements |
| Petty cash book | Small cash payments, such as postage | Petty cash vouchers |
| General journal | Anything not in the other six: opening entries, buying non-current assets on credit, writing off irrecoverable debts, correcting errors | Internal records |

The November 2022 0452 Paper 13 report described the general journal this way: it records transactions that do not go in any other book of prime entry. It gave interest on overdue accounts and irrecoverable debts as examples.

Two traps. **Cash sales and cash purchases never go in the sales or purchases journal.** They go in the cash book. The March 2025 0452 Paper 22 report listed purchases made by bank transfer, wrongly entered in a purchases journal, as a common error. And **credit sales and credit purchases never go in the cash book**, because no money has moved yet. The June 2022 Paper 22 report for both codes called this a common error, and the June 2023 Paper 22 reports said weaker answers made it.

## How do the sales and purchases journals work?

Each journal is a simple list: date, name of the customer or supplier, and the amount after trade discount. At the end of the month, you total it and post the total to one account.

### What is the difference between trade discount and cash discount?

| | Trade discount | Cash discount |
|---|---|---|
| Given for | Buying in bulk, or to a trade customer | Paying early, within the agreed time |
| Where it is shown | On the invoice, deducted from the list price | In the discount columns of the cash book |
| In the ledger? | Never | Yes: discount allowed or discount received |

The June 2022 Paper 12 report for both 0452 and 7707 said trade discount is never recorded in the ledger. The journals record the invoice price after it has been taken off.

**Work out cash discount on the price after trade discount.** The November 2022 0452 Paper 23 report said many candidates worked out cash discount as a percentage of the list price instead.

### Worked example: Mehwish Stationers

Here is my own example, with every number checked.

> Mehwish Stationers buys and sells office supplies on credit. In May 2026:
>
> - May 4: bought goods from Rizwan Supplies, list price $1,200, less 25% trade discount
> - May 9: sold goods to Bright School, list price $1,500, less 10% trade discount
> - May 15: bought goods from Kohinoor Paper, $640
> - May 18: returned goods costing $40 to Kohinoor Paper
> - May 22: sold goods to Alif Traders, $480
> - May 27: bought goods from Rizwan Supplies, list price $800, less 25% trade discount

**Purchases journal**

| Date and supplier | $ |
|---|---|
| May 4 Rizwan Supplies ($1,200 less $300) | 900 |
| May 15 Kohinoor Paper | 640 |
| May 27 Rizwan Supplies ($800 less $200) | 600 |
| May 31 Transfer to purchases account | 2,140 |

**Sales journal**

| Date and customer | $ |
|---|---|
| May 9 Bright School ($1,500 less $150) | 1,350 |
| May 22 Alif Traders | 480 |
| May 31 Transfer to sales account | 1,830 |

**Purchases returns journal**

| Date and supplier | $ |
|---|---|
| May 18 Kohinoor Paper | 40 |
| May 31 Transfer to purchases returns account | 40 |

The 2027 specimen Paper 2 for 0452 set out a purchases journal the same way: each supplier with the trade discount taken off, then a total labelled with the account it is posted to.

### How are the journals posted to the ledger?

| From | Each line goes to | The monthly total goes to |
|---|---|---|
| Purchases journal | Credit each supplier's account | Debit purchases account |
| Sales journal | Debit each customer's account | Credit sales account |
| Purchases returns journal | Debit each supplier's account | Credit purchases returns account |
| Sales returns journal | Credit each customer's account | Debit sales returns account |

So Rizwan Supplies' account gets two credits, $900 and $600, but the purchases account gets one debit of $2,140. The November 2023 7707 Paper 13 report said a significant proportion of candidates thought the sales journal total was debited to the sales account. It is credited.

Label the total with the account name. The March 2025 0452 Paper 22 report said the total of the journal was often labelled incorrectly, and dates were often left out. The June 2022 Paper 21 report for both codes said some candidates added debit and credit columns and tried to write general journal entries instead of a simple list.

## How does the three column cash book work?

The cash book has two sides, each with three money columns: discount, cash and bank. It is a book of prime entry and, at the same time, the ledger account for cash and the ledger account for bank. That is its dual function.

Continuing my example, Mehwish Stationers' money in May 2026:

> - May 1: balances, cash $250, bank $3,600
> - May 6: cash sales $420
> - May 10: paid $300 of cash into the bank
> - May 12: paid rent by bank transfer, $700
> - May 20: paid Rizwan Supplies the May 4 invoice of $900 by bank, less 2% cash discount
> - May 25: Bright School paid its $1,350 invoice by bank transfer, less 4% cash discount
> - May 28: bought goods for cash, $150
> - May 30: withdrew $200 from the bank for use as office cash

Cash discount: 2% of $900 = $18, so Mehwish paid $882. 4% of $1,350 = $54, so Bright School paid $1,296.

**Cash book, debit side (money in)**

| Date and details | Discount ($) | Cash ($) | Bank ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| May 1 Balance b/d | | 250 | 3,600 |
| May 6 Sales | | 420 | |
| May 10 Cash (contra) | | | 300 |
| May 25 Bright School | 54 | | 1,296 |
| May 30 Bank (contra) | | 200 | |
| Totals | 54 | 870 | 5,196 |
| Jun 1 Balance b/d | | 420 | 3,414 |

**Cash book, credit side (money out)**

| Date and details | Discount ($) | Cash ($) | Bank ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| May 10 Bank (contra) | | 300 | |
| May 12 Rent | | | 700 |
| May 20 Rizwan Supplies | 18 | | 882 |
| May 28 Purchases | | 150 | |
| May 30 Cash (contra) | | | 200 |
| May 31 Balance c/d | | 420 | 3,414 |
| Totals | 18 | 870 | 5,196 |

Check: credit cash 300 + 150 + 420 = 870; credit bank 700 + 882 + 200 + 3,414 = 5,196. Both match the debit totals.

The credit sales of May 9 and May 22 are not in the cash book. Neither are the credit purchases. Only money that moved is here.

### What are the rules for each column?

- **Contra entries.** Moving money between cash and bank needs both a debit and a credit inside the cash book. On May 10, cash goes down (credit cash) and bank goes up (debit bank). The March 2023 0452 Paper 22 report listed treating the contra as money taken for personal use among the common errors. The June 2025 0452 Paper 22 report called it a common error to miss that a transfer from bank into cash needs both a debit and a credit entry.
- **Discount columns are not balanced.** Each is totalled and posted: $54 to the debit of discount allowed, $18 to the credit of discount received. The June 2022 Paper 22 report for both codes called balancing off the discount received column a common error. The November 2022 0452 Paper 12 report said the discount columns are not part of the double entry: they are just a convenient place to note discounts, which are posted to the ledger at the end of each month.
- **Cash cannot have a credit balance.** You cannot hold less than no cash. The June 2023 Paper 22 reports for both codes, the June 2025 0452 Paper 22 report and the March 2026 0452 Paper 22 report all warned about this. If your cash column shows money out greater than money in, look for a missed entry.
- **Bank can have a credit balance.** That is an overdraft.
- **Details name the other account.** Write "Rent", not "paid rent by transfer". The March 2023 0452 Paper 22 report listed details such as "telephone transfer" as a common error, because they are not account names.

<Tip title="Check cash first">
After balancing, look at the cash column before anything else. A credit balance on cash is impossible, so it tells you at once that an entry is missing or on the wrong side.
</Tip>

### How do payment methods change the entry?

The 2027 syllabus asks you to record cash, cheques, debit and credit cards, online and bank transfers. The rule is simple: if the money goes through the bank account, use the bank column; if it is notes and coins, use the cash column. A debit card payment, a bank transfer and a cheque all go in the bank column. The name of the method never goes in the details column; the account name does.

## How does the petty cash book and the imprest system work?

A petty cash book records small cash payments, so the main cash book is not filled with tiny amounts. Under the **imprest system**, the petty cashier starts each period with a fixed amount, the imprest. At the end of the period, the main cashier gives back exactly what was spent, so the float returns to the imprest.

The June 2022 Paper 11 report for both codes explained it this way: the amount needed to restore the imprest equals the net amount spent, which is total payments less any receipts.

### Worked example: petty cash for June

> Mehwish Stationers uses an imprest of $120. On 1 June 2026, the petty cash balance is $120. Payments in June: Jun 3 postage $14; Jun 8 cleaning $25; Jun 15 Kohinoor Paper, a credit supplier, $30; Jun 22 stationery $18; Jun 27 postage $9.

| Date and details | Total paid ($) | Analysis column |
|---|---|---|
| Jun 3 Postage | 14 | Postage 14 |
| Jun 8 Cleaning | 25 | Cleaning 25 |
| Jun 15 Kohinoor Paper | 30 | Ledger accounts 30 |
| Jun 22 Stationery | 18 | Stationery 18 |
| Jun 27 Postage | 9 | Postage 9 |
| Total paid | 96 | |

The analysis columns total: postage $23, cleaning $25, stationery $18, ledger accounts $30. Together they make $96, which must equal the total paid column.

- Received: Jun 1 Balance b/d $120.
- Balance c/d on 30 June: $120 minus $96 = $24.
- Restoration on 1 July: Balance b/d $24, then Bank $96, which brings the float back to $120.

At the end of the month, each analysis total is debited to its expense account (postage $23, cleaning $25, stationery $18), and the $30 is debited to Kohinoor Paper's account in the purchases ledger.

In real papers, the June 2025 0452 Paper 21 scheme for a petty cash book was worth 11 marks: one for each entry, one for the dates, one for totalling the analysis columns, and marks for bringing down the balance and restoring the imprest. The June 2025 report listed the common errors: leaving out the opening balance, wrong wording for payments, putting the balance carried down before the total so the columns did not agree, and missing or wrongly worded restoration of the imprest. The June 2023 Paper 21 reports for both codes listed the same opening balance and restoration errors.

### What are the advantages of the imprest system?

The March 2022 0452 Paper 22 report accepted reducing fraud and controlling small cash spending. The November 2022 0452 Paper 23 report, asked about petty cash books in general, accepted fewer entries in the main cash book and the ledger, division of labour, and training for junior staff. It added that the imprest system may help to reduce fraud, but simply keeping a petty cash book will not. Keep those two questions apart.

## How does the general journal work?

The general journal lists the account debited, then the account credited, with the amounts, and a narrative: a short note saying why. For example:

| Date and account | Debit ($) | Credit ($) |
|---|---|---|
| Jun 10 Fixtures and fittings | 1,100 | |
| FitRite Limited | | 1,100 |
| Narrative: purchase of shelving on credit | | |

The shelving is a non-current asset, so it is not debited to purchases. The June 2022 Paper 21 report for both codes made exactly this point about equipment bought on credit, and said not all candidates included the narrative the question asked for.

### What is an opening journal entry?

When a business starts keeping double entry records, the general journal lists every asset as a debit, every liability as a credit, and capital as the balancing credit. The June 2025 0452 Paper 21 report said many candidates were unfamiliar with it, and some only showed assets minus liabilities as a sum. The November 2023 7707 Paper 23 report said a very common error was to leave out the capital altogether.

## What are the benefits and limitations of keeping cash at the business?

The June 2023 0452 Paper 21 question asked whether a trader should stop using cash. The scheme's points, in my words:

| Stopping using cash: for | Stopping using cash: against |
|---|---|
| Safer: no cash on the premises or carried to the bank | Some customers and suppliers prefer cash |
| Less risk of fraud | Bank transfers can take longer to arrive |
| No petty cash book needed | Some cash is useful for emergencies and small expenses |
| Saves time handling and banking cash | Extra bank charges |
| | Bank reconciliation may be harder |

Turn it round for "keeping cash": its benefits are the right column, its limitations the left.

### Manual or digital records?

The 2027 specimen Paper 2 for 0452 asked whether a trader should use a computerised accounting system. The scheme credited, for: greater accuracy, automatic double entry, speed, extra features such as inventory and credit control, and documents and statements produced automatically. Against: cost of hardware and software, training, running both systems in parallel at first, risk of data loss or hacking, and the cost of breakdowns. It capped the arguments at 4 marks and gave 1 for a recommendation based on both sides.

## What do examiners say students get wrong?

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Credit purchases or sales in the cash book (a common error, June 2022 P22; weaker responses, June 2023 P22) | Only money in or out goes in the cash book |
| Trade discount left in the journal or posted to the ledger (one customer's entries invariably wrong, November 2023 7707 P22) | Record the price after trade discount |
| Cash discount worked on the list price (many, November 2022 0452 P23) | Work it on the invoice price after trade discount |
| Discount received column balanced off (a common error, June 2022 P22) | Total the discount columns and post them |
| Contra missed or treated as drawings (March 2023 0452 P22; June 2025 0452 P22) | Two entries inside the cash book |
| Credit balance on the cash column (March 2026 0452 P22; November 2023 7707 P23) | Cash can never be overdrawn: find the error |
| Restoration of the imprest left out (June 2023 P21; June 2025 0452 P21) | Bank in on the first day of the next period |
| Journal total not labelled with an account (March 2025 0452 P22) | "Transfer to purchases account", with the date |

## Practice questions with answers

Each question is my own. Try it on paper before reading the answer.

### Practice question 1

> Name the book of prime entry for each transaction.
>
> (a) A credit customer returns damaged goods.
>
> (b) A delivery van is bought on credit.
>
> (c) The owner pays wages from the business bank account.
>
> (d) Bus fares of $6 are paid from the petty cash. [4]

**Answer.** (a) Sales returns journal. (b) General journal. (c) Cash book. (d) Petty cash book.

### Practice question 2

> Goods with a list price of $2,000 are bought on credit, less 20% trade discount. The supplier offers 3% cash discount for payment within 14 days, and the trader pays on day 10.
>
> (a) Calculate the amount entered in the purchases journal. [1]
>
> (b) Calculate the cash discount and the amount paid. [2]

**Answer.** (a) $2,000 less $400 = $1,600. (b) 3% of $1,600 = $48 discount received; amount paid $1,552.

### Practice question 3

> A petty cashier has an imprest of $200. During the month, payments total $143 and a member of staff repays $15 for a personal phone call. Calculate the amount needed to restore the imprest. [2]

**Answer.** The net amount spent is $143 minus $15 = $128. The balance before restoring is $200 minus $128 = $72, so $128 is paid in to bring it back to $200.

The marks here are my own judgement for practice, not a Cambridge scheme.

## FAQ

### What are the seven books of prime entry?

The sales journal, purchases journal, sales returns journal, purchases returns journal, general journal, cash book and petty cash book.

### Why is the cash book both a book of prime entry and a ledger?

Receipts and payments are first recorded in it, which makes it a book of prime entry. Its cash and bank columns are also the cash account and bank account, so no separate ledger accounts are kept for them.

### Is trade discount recorded in the books?

Not in the ledger. It is shown on the invoice and taken off before the amount is written in the journal. Only cash discount is posted, as discount allowed or discount received.

### What is the imprest system?

The petty cashier starts each period with a fixed float. At the end, the amount spent is paid back in, so the float always returns to the same figure.

### Where does the total of the sales journal go?

To the credit of the sales account. Each customer's account is debited with the individual invoice.

### Are books of prime entry the same in 0452 and 7707?

Yes. Section 2.3 lists the same seven books and the same points in both syllabuses for 2027 to 2029.
