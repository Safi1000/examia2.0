Your O Level method stops working because AS Level pays for judgement, not recall. The long answers ask you to assess or evaluate, and an answer that only explains loses the evaluation marks. In AS Economics 9708, evaluation is 25% of the marks. In AS Business 9609 it is 20%. More notes will not fix that. A different way of answering will.

## Why does a strong O Level student struggle at AS?

One of my reels carried the line "This is where most students quietly lose their grades." The point was simple: they take an O Level method into an A Level exam.

Most of these students are not weak. They revise the way that worked at 16: learn the notes, repeat the past papers, explain each point clearly. The AS exam wants a decision as well.

The change is written down. Cambridge publishes how the marks are split, and every number below comes from its syllabuses.

## What actually changes in the marks?

Every Cambridge syllabus splits its marks into assessment objectives (AOs). These are the skills the examiner rewards: knowledge, application (using it on the business or data in front of you), analysis (explaining causes and effects step by step) and evaluation (judging and deciding).

**Check which O Level figures these are.** They come from the new O Level and IGCSE syllabuses, first examined in 2027. If you sat your exams in 2026 or earlier, you sat the older syllabuses. Cambridge says the new Economics and Business syllabuses give evaluation less weight than the old ones did, so your own evaluation figures were higher. The AS and A Level figures are from the syllabuses for 2026 to 2028.

### How do the marks change in Economics (2281 and 0455 to 9708)?

| Skill | O Level 2281 / IGCSE 0455 (2027) | AS Level 9708 | 9708 Paper 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Knowledge and understanding | 43% | 35% | 33% |
| Analysis | 47% | 40% | 37% |
| Evaluation | 10% | 25% | 30% |

In the 2027 O Level syllabus, Paper 1 has no evaluation marks and Paper 2 has 15%. In the older 2281 syllabus, examined up to 2026, evaluation was 20% of the qualification, as my [guide to the 2281 changes](/blog/o-level-economics-2281-syllabus-2027-changes) shows.

Either way, AS asks for more. A quarter of your grade is judgement, and much of it sits inside two 20 mark essays.

### How do the marks change in Business (7081 and 0264 to 9609)?

| Skill | O Level 7081 / IGCSE 0264 (2027) | AS Level 9609 | Full A Level 9609 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Knowledge and understanding | 35% | 30% | 25% |
| Application | 30% | 30% | 25% |
| Analysis | 22% | 20% | 25% |
| Evaluation | 13% | 20% | 25% |

At AS the shift looks gentle. What matters is where the evaluation marks sit. In 9609 Paper 1, the second part of the essay must show evaluation. In Paper 2, the last part of each data response question must. In Paper 3, in the second year, evaluation is 30% of the paper and knowledge is only 20%.

### How do the marks change in Accounting (7707 and 0452 to 9706)?

| Skill | O Level 7707 / IGCSE 0452 (2027) | AS Level 9706 | Full A Level 9706 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Knowledge and understanding | 66% | 63% | 55% |
| Analysis | 24% | 25% | 30% |
| Evaluation | 10% | 12% | 15% |

Accounting changes least on paper, and that fools students. AS Paper 1, the multiple choice, has no evaluation marks. So all the evaluation sits in Paper 2, where it is 17% of the paper. In Paper 4, in the second year, it is 28%.

One rule does not change. Both syllabuses accept only the ratio formulae printed in the syllabus. What is new at AS is the content and the size of the questions. Question 4 of Paper 2 is 30 marks of cost and management accounting. Absorption costing and marginal costing are not in the O Level syllabus.

## How are the AS papers different from O Level papers?

The papers are not longer. The questions are fewer and bigger.

| Subject | O Level / IGCSE written paper (2027) | AS Level written papers |
|---|---|---|
| Economics | Paper 2: 2 hours, 80 marks. One compulsory data question, then three structured questions from four | 9708 Paper 2: 2 hours, 60 marks. One data response, one micro essay and one macro essay, 20 marks each. Each essay has two parts |
| Business | Paper 1 (short answers and data response) and Paper 2 (case study): 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks each | 9609 Paper 1: 1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks. Four questions of 5 marks, then one 20 mark essay. Paper 2: 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks. Two data response questions of 30 marks, six parts each |
| Accounting | Paper 2: 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks. Five compulsory questions of 20 marks | 9706 Paper 2: 1 hour 45 minutes, 90 marks. Four questions of 30, 15, 15 and 30 marks |

Three things matter here.

- **One essay is a third of the paper.** Each 9708 Paper 2 essay carries 20 of the 60 marks. The June 2023 examiner report said even the 8 mark part (a) keeps up to 2 marks for evaluation.
- **The Business case study is not an AS paper.** It arrives in the second year. 9609 Paper 3 is five questions on a case study. Paper 4 is two essays on a case study.
- **Accounting adds two papers in the second year.** Paper 3 is Financial Accounting: three questions of 25 marks. Paper 4 is Cost and Management Accounting: two questions of 25 marks in 1 hour. Both assume your AS content.

## What do evaluate, discuss and assess actually ask for?

Look at the command words each syllabus lists. O Level Economics 2281 and IGCSE 0455 list "discuss", but not "evaluate" or "assess". O Level Business 7081 and IGCSE 0264 list "justify", but none of "evaluate", "discuss" or "assess". At AS, "assess" and "evaluate" are listed in all three subjects.

| Command word | Listed at AS in | What it needs from you |
|---|---|---|
| Assess | 9706, 9708, 9609 | Weigh the arguments and decide. The 9708 syllabus defines it as "make an informed judgement" |
| Evaluate | 9706, 9708, 9609 | Judge how good, how important or how large something is. Both sides, then a decision with a reason |
| Discuss | 9706, 9708 | A full, organised answer. In practice: arguments for, arguments against, conclusion |
| Advise | 9706, 9609 | Tell the person in the question what to do, and why |
| To what extent | 9609 | Not yes or no. How far is it true, and what does it depend on? |

None of these can be answered by describing. Each one ends in a decision.

A judgement that earns marks has three parts:

1. **A direct answer** to the exact question ("The subsidy is likely to reduce congestion, but only a little").
2. **The reason** it beats the other side ("because the fare is a small part of why people drive").
3. **What it depends on** ("it depends on how close a substitute the bus is for the car in this city").

This is where most of my new AS students get stuck. They know the content. They write two good sides. Then they stop, or they write "it depends on many factors". That sentence earns nothing, because it does not say which factor matters or why.

## What does an A Level answer look like next to an O Level answer?

Here is one question answered twice in each subject. The questions and figures are my own, not Cambridge's.

### Economics: will a bus fare subsidy cut congestion?

**Question:** A city government plans to subsidise bus fares. Assess whether this will reduce traffic congestion. [12]

**O Level style answer:**

> A subsidy is money paid by the government to producers. It lowers the cost of running buses, so fares fall. More people will travel by bus because it is cheaper, so fewer people drive. This reduces congestion. It also reduces pollution. Therefore the subsidy will reduce congestion.

This is a correct chain of reasoning. At AS it is one side with no judgement. The June 2023 9708 examiner report gave a warning about part (b) essay answers that cover one side only. They are unlikely to pass the middle of Level 2 for analysis, and they get no evaluation marks.

**A Level style answer (outline):**

> A subsidy shifts the supply curve for bus services to the right. The fare falls from P1 to P2 and bus journeys rise from Q1 to Q2 (labelled diagram). Buses and cars are substitutes, so cross elasticity of demand is positive: a lower bus fare reduces demand for car journeys. Fewer cars means less congestion, which is an external cost of driving.
>
> However, the size of the effect depends on how close the substitutes are. If drivers value speed, comfort or door-to-door travel, cross elasticity is low and few will switch. If buses are already full or slow, a lower fare attracts few drivers. The subsidy also has an opportunity cost: the money could have paid for more buses.
>
> Judgement: on its own, the subsidy is likely to reduce congestion only a little, because the fare is a small part of why people drive. It works better alongside more frequent buses. The deciding factor is how close a substitute the bus is for the car in this city.

Count the differences: a diagram, the theory named, a second side, and a judgement that names the deciding factor.

### Business: should a bakery open a second shop?

**Question:** A family bakery has one shop, a profit margin of 12% and a waiting list for celebration cakes. The owners plan to open a second shop with a bank loan. Evaluate whether this is the right decision. [12]

**O Level style answer:**

> Opening a second shop will increase sales and profit. The bakery can reach more customers and gain economies of scale. However, the loan must be repaid with interest. Overall, it is a good decision because profit will increase.

These are real business ideas, but they would fit any business in the world. The March 2026 9609 Paper 1 examiner report described this weakness: generic answers that do not use the context.

**A Level style answer (outline):**

> The waiting list shows unmet demand for celebration cakes. A second shop with its own ovens could turn those lost orders into revenue. At the current 12% margin, every 100,000 of extra sales would add 12,000 of profit.
>
> However, the new shop will not earn 12% at first, because it adds rent and loan interest before sales build up. The waiting list may also reflect a shortage of skilled bakers, not of shop space. If so, a second shop adds cost without removing the real limit on output. The owners also cannot be in two shops at once.
>
> Judgement: expansion is right only if the bakery can hire and train bakers first. If people are the bottleneck, extending the current kitchen is a cheaper first step. The deciding factor is what is causing the waiting list.

The margin is used in a calculation, the waiting list is interpreted, and the judgement could only belong to this bakery.

### Accounting: should a trader buy more inventory on credit?

In accounting, the two answers often contain the same ratios. You already calculated the current ratio and the acid test ratio at O Level. The difference is what you do with them.

**Question:** A trader's records show the figures below. A supplier offers a further $10,000 of inventory on credit. Advise the trader whether to accept. [8]

| Item | Last year ($) | This year ($) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Inventory | 18,000 | 36,000 |
| Trade receivables | 21,000 | 24,000 |
| Bank | 9,000 | nil |
| **Total current assets** | **48,000** | **60,000** |
| Trade payables | 24,000 | 32,000 |
| Bank overdraft | nil | 8,000 |
| **Total current liabilities** | **24,000** | **40,000** |

**O Level style answer:**

> The current ratio has fallen from 2:1 to 1.5:1. The acid test ratio has fallen from 1.25:1 to 0.6:1. Liquidity has worsened, so the trader should not buy more inventory.

Both ratios are right:

| Ratio | Last year | This year |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | 48,000 ÷ 24,000 = 2:1 | 60,000 ÷ 40,000 = 1.5:1 |
| Acid test ratio | (48,000 less 18,000) ÷ 24,000 = 1.25:1 | (60,000 less 36,000) ÷ 40,000 = 0.6:1 |

But "liquidity has worsened" only repeats the numbers. The answer gives no cause, no other side and no reason for the decision.

**A Level style answer (outline):**

> The acid test ratio has fallen from 1.25:1 to 0.6:1. Without selling inventory, the trader has $24,000 of receivables to cover $40,000 of current liabilities. The cause is clear in the figures. Inventory has doubled from $18,000 to $36,000, while $9,000 in the bank has become an $8,000 overdraft. Cash is tied up in unsold goods.
>
> On the other hand, the current ratio of 1.5:1 is still above 1:1. The supplier is offering credit, so no cash leaves today. If the goods are for a busy season and sell quickly, the cash will come back.
>
> Accepting would raise inventory to $46,000 and current liabilities to $50,000. The acid test ratio would fall to 0.48:1 ($24,000 ÷ $50,000).
>
> Decision: do not accept now. The trader already holds twice last year's inventory and relies on an overdraft. Sell the existing inventory and collect receivables first. Accept only if these goods are selling fast, because that is what decides whether the cash returns in time.

The 9706 syllabus expects a recommendation to rest on reasons from both sides, with a justification. That is the second answer: a cause, the other side, the effect of the choice, and a justified decision.

## What do examiners say AS candidates miss?

These patterns come from Cambridge's examiner reports on the AS written papers. Each one is an O Level habit carried into AS.

| What candidates did | Where it was reported |
|---|---|
| Listed advantages and disadvantages but never reached a clear decision | 9706 Paper 22, March 2026 |
| Gave short answers where explanation was needed. The report said "One or two-word answers will seldom suffice" | 9706 Paper 21, June 2023 |
| Abbreviated labels in financial statements, or left out workings | 9706 Paper 21, June 2023 |
| Ignored the command word, treating "assess" like "explain" | 9708 Paper 21, June 2023 |
| Made judgements without explaining the reasoning behind them | 9708 Paper 22, March 2026 |
| Left out diagrams that were asked for, or drew them without labels | 9708 Paper 21, June 2023 |
| Wrote generic answers that ignored the context. The report said "A one-line statement is insufficient" as a conclusion | 9609 Paper 12, March 2026 |
| Answered a nearby question, such as demand factors when the question asked about supply | 9609 Paper 22, March 2026 |

Almost none of these are about missing knowledge. They are about the shape of the answer.

<Tip title="The one-sentence test">
Before you write any long answer, write your final judgement in one sentence at the top of your plan. If you cannot, you do not yet know what you are arguing. Every paragraph after that should support the judgement or test it.
</Tip>

## How should a new AS student reset in four weeks?

Use this plan in your first month of AS, or as soon as a test goes badly.

**Week 1: learn the new rules**

- Read the assessment overview and command words in your own syllabus. Write the AO weightings for your papers at the top of your notes.
- Take one O Level answer you were proud of. Underline every sentence that only describes.
- Accounting: copy out the ratio formulae from the 9706 syllabus exactly as written. Use no other version.

**Week 2: build chains, not lists**

- For every point, write three linked steps: cause, effect, final outcome for the business or the economy.
- Economics: draw one labelled diagram a day and write two sentences explaining the shift.
- Business: for every theory you revise, write one sentence applying it to a business, with a figure in it.

**Week 3: practise judgement**

- Answer only the evaluation parts this week: part (b) of the 9708 Paper 2 essays, the last part of each 9609 Paper 2 question, and the advise or discuss parts of 9706 Paper 2.
- End every answer with the three-part judgement: direct answer, reason, what it depends on.
- Swap answers with a classmate and ask one question: would this conclusion fit a different business or country? If yes, make it more specific.

**Week 4: work under time**

- Do one full question per subject in timed conditions. 9708 Paper 2 gives 2 minutes a mark, so a 20 mark essay gets 40 minutes.
- In Economics and Business, mark it against the level descriptions in the mark scheme, not just the list of points.
- Write down your two most common errors and check them against the examiner table above.

<Tip title="Plan before you write">
Spend the first few minutes of a long answer on a plan: two points for, two against, and the judgement. The March 2026 9708 examiner report linked weak planning to answers that lost focus on the question.
</Tip>

## Can you rewrite these O Level answers?

Try each one, then compare with the outline.

**1. Economics.** "A tax on sugary drinks raises the price, so people buy less and become healthier." Turn this into an AS answer to: Assess whether a tax on sugary drinks will improve health.

*Outline:* the tax shifts supply to the left and raises the price (diagram). The fall in quantity depends on price elasticity of demand. If demand is inelastic, consumption falls only a little and the tax mainly raises revenue. Consumers may also switch to other unhealthy goods. Judgement: the effect is likely to be small unless the tax is large or the revenue is spent on health education.

**2. Business.** "Training motivates workers and improves quality." Turn this into an AS answer to: Evaluate whether a hotel with high staff turnover should spend more on training.

*Outline:* training raises skills and service quality, which matters in a hotel because guests deal with staff directly. But if staff leave because of low pay, trained staff may still leave and take their skills to a competitor. Judgement: training helps only if the hotel also deals with the main reason staff leave.

**3. Accounting.** A trader's trade receivables turnover has risen from 30 days to 55 days. "This means customers are paying more slowly." Turn this into advice on whether the trader should offer a cash discount for early payment.

*Outline:* slower collection ties up cash and raises the risk of irrecoverable debts. A cash discount may speed up payment, but it reduces profit on every sale where it is taken. Decision: offer the discount if the trader is short of cash and the discount costs less than borrowing. First check whether a few large customers are causing the delay.

## FAQ

### Is A Level much harder than O Level?

The content is deeper, but the bigger change is the skill being tested. More marks go to judgement in long answers. A student who only memorises will find AS much harder than one who practises deciding.

### Can I take AS Business if I did not study it at O Level?

Yes. The 9609 syllabus says Cambridge does not expect learners to have studied business before. For 9706 and 9708, Cambridge recommends a mathematics course at IGCSE or O Level standard first.

### How much study time does AS Level need compared with O Level?

Cambridge designs each O Level and IGCSE syllabus for about 130 guided learning hours. Each AS Level is about 180 hours, and a full A Level is about 360.

### Is there an A\* at AS Level?

No. AS Level grades run from a to e. The A\* exists only for the full A Level.

### Are my O Level notes still useful?

Yes, as a base. Definitions and basic methods carry over. What they lack is the second side and the judgement, so add those as you revise.

### I got a low grade in my first AS test. Is it too late?

No. Look at where you lost the marks. If it was on the assess, evaluate or advise parts, the method is the problem, and the four-week plan above targets it.
