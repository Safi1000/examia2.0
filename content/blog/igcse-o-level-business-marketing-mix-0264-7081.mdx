The marketing mix is the set of four decisions a business makes to sell a product: product, price, place and promotion, often called the 4Ps. In IGCSE Business 0264 and O Level Business 7081 it is syllabus section 3.3. You need to know each P, then recommend and justify the right mix for the business in the question.

## What is the marketing mix in 0264 and 7081?

Each P is one decision. **Product** is what you sell, including its brand and packaging. **Price** is how much you charge and how you set it. **Place** is how the product reaches customers, called the distribution channel. **Promotion** is how you tell customers about it and persuade them to buy.

The 0264 and 7081 syllabuses use the same wording for section 3.3, so everything below applies to both. The syllabus only lists what to learn. The definitions, advantages and disadvantages in this article are standard textbook points, explained the way I teach them. The section has seven parts.

| Part | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 3.3.1 Product | Brand image, packaging, new products, product life cycle, extension strategies |
| 3.3.2 Price | Five pricing methods, their advantages and disadvantages |
| 3.3.3 Place | Four distribution channels |
| 3.3.4 Promotion | Reasons for promotion, sales promotion, advertising |
| 3.3.5 Ecommerce | Examples, and advantages and disadvantages for businesses and customers |
| 3.3.6 Marketing strategy | Why each element matters, and choosing a mix for a situation |
| 3.3.7 Entering new markets in other countries | Advantages and disadvantages as a method of growth |

Notice how many lines start with "recommend and justify". There are six of them in this section. That tells you how the topic is examined: knowing the 4Ps earns a few marks, and choosing between options for a named business earns the rest.

## What do I need to know about product?

The syllabus groups product into five areas.

- **Brand image**: the picture customers have in their minds of the business or product. A strong brand image can let a business charge a higher price and keep customers coming back.
- **Packaging**: what the product comes in. Its roles include protecting the product, giving information, attracting attention on the shelf and supporting the brand image.
- **Developing new products**: this can bring new sales and spread risk, but research and launch costs are high and many new products fail.
- **The product life cycle** and **extension strategies**, which have their own sections below.

### What are the stages of the product life cycle?

The syllabus names four stages. You must use these exact names.

| Stage | What happens to sales | Usual focus |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low, starting to rise | High promotion to raise awareness |
| Growth | Rising quickly | Build brand loyalty, widen distribution |
| Maturity | High but levelling off | Hold market share against competitors |
| Decline | Falling | Cut costs, use an extension strategy or stop the product |

You may be asked to interpret a product life cycle diagram. Read it like a line graph: sales on the vertical axis, time along the bottom. The curve rises slowly, then steeply, flattens at the top and then falls. Say which stage a product is in from the shape of its sales, then say what that means for the business.

The June 2022 examiner report for 0450 and 7115 pointed out that saturation is a point within maturity, not a separate stage. Words like start, peak or end are not stage names either.

### Which extension strategies can a business use?

The syllabus lists four:

1. Find new markets, such as selling to a new age group or a new country.
2. Find new uses for the product.
3. Adapt the product or its packaging.
4. Increase advertising or sales promotion.

Each costs money, and none is certain to work. In an evaluation question, compare two strategies for the business in front of you. The 7115 November 2022 report noted that the better answers compared adding new features with changing the packaging, which gave them a basis for evaluation.

### How does promotion change over the product life cycle?

Name the stage first, then say how the level or purpose of promotion changes. Do not switch to price or list advertising methods. The June 2022 and November 2023 reports both describe candidates struggling with this question type. Practice question 2 below shows a full answer.

## Which pricing methods do I need to know?

The syllabus names five methods. Learn each with one advantage, one disadvantage and the situation it suits.

| Method | What it means | Suits | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost plus | Add a fixed percentage to the cost of making each unit | Businesses that must cover costs on every sale | Ignores competitors' prices and demand |
| Competitive | Set the price close to what competitors charge | Markets with many similar products | Less profit if costs are higher than rivals' |
| Penetration | A low price to enter a market and win customers quickly | New products in a crowded market | Low profit per unit; hard to raise the price later |
| Skimming | A high price at launch, lowered later | New, unique or high quality products with no close rival | Few sales if customers will not pay; attracts competitors |
| Dynamic | The business keeps moving its price up or down as market demand at that moment changes | Products where demand changes, such as tickets or rides | Customers may feel the price is unfair |

In the specimen mark scheme, a bare statement that prices move with demand earned only 1 of the 2 marks for defining dynamic pricing. Give the fuller meaning.

The June 2022 and June 2025 reports for 0450 also mention promotional pricing, and June 2022 mentions predatory pricing. They are not in the 0264 and 7081 list of five, so build your answers around the five above.

### How do I calculate a cost plus price?

Find the average cost of one unit, then add the markup. The syllabus formula for average cost is total cost divided by the number of units.

> A bakery makes 500 cakes a week. Fixed costs are $400 a week and the variable cost is $2 per cake. It adds a 50% markup.
>
> Total cost = $400 + ($2 × 500) = $1,400
> Average cost = $1,400 ÷ 500 = $2.80
> Markup = 50% of $2.80 = $1.40
> Price = $2.80 + $1.40 = $4.20

Check the answer the other way: $4.20 is 1.5 times $2.80. The bakery earns $1.40 on each cake sold. Notice that this is profit per cake, not total profit. The March 2026 report noted that some candidates missed this point.

## Which distribution channel is best?

Place in 0264 and 7081 means choosing a distribution channel. The syllabus lists four.

| Channel | Advantage for the producer | Disadvantage for the producer |
|---|---|---|
| Direct to customers | Keeps the full price and talks to customers directly | Has to pay for its own selling and delivery |
| Through retailers | Wider distribution; retailers may promote the product | Retailer adds its own margin, so the final price rises |
| Wholesalers | Buy in bulk, which cuts storage and transport costs | Less control over where and how the product is sold |
| Agents | Local knowledge, useful in a new country | Commission must be paid; less control |

Always answer from the point of view of the producer, unless the question says otherwise.

## What is the difference between sales promotion and advertising?

Promotion has two branches in the syllabus. The syllabus also asks for reasons for promotion without listing them. The usual textbook reasons are raising awareness of a new product, increasing sales and building loyalty.

| | Sales promotion | Advertising |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A short term offer that pushes customers to buy now | A paid message that informs and persuades |
| Syllabus examples | Vouchers, reward schemes, competitions, special offers or discounts | Social media, direct or targeted emails, leaflets, billboards |
| Typical aim | Quick rise in sales | Awareness and brand image over time |

## Where do ecommerce and new markets fit?

Ecommerce means buying and selling online. The syllabus examples are mobile phone or internet banking, online shopping and online ticketing. You need its advantages and disadvantages for businesses and for customers separately. For a business, it can reach customers all day and in more places, but it needs a website, delivery and online security. For a customer, it is convenient and easy to compare prices, but they cannot see or try the product first. In November 2023, better answers on selling through a website or through shops used this point about trying a product on.

Entering new markets in other countries sits at the end of 3.3. The syllabus examples of advantages are increasing sales and spreading risk. The disadvantages are cultural differences, lack of knowledge and legal requirements. Explain the effect, not just the label. Cultural differences matter because an advert or product that works at home may not suit customers abroad.

## How do I choose the right marketing mix for a business?

This is syllabus point 3.3.6, and it is the skill behind most of the long questions. I teach it in five steps.

1. **Find the target customer.** Who buys this product? Age, income, location? Underline the facts in the case.
2. **Find the competition.** Is the product unique, or are there close rivals? This decides price more than anything else.
3. **Match each P to those two facts.** A unique, high quality product for high income customers points to skimming, retailers with the right image and targeted advertising. A similar product in a crowded market points to competitive or penetration pricing and sales promotion.
4. **Check the four Ps agree.** A premium price with cheap packaging and discount vouchers sends mixed messages.
5. **Decide, then say what it depends on.** Give one clear choice and one condition that would change it.

Do not skip the last step. In March 2024, a question asked whether a marketing mix was suitable. The report said many candidates discussed one element and never said whether the mix was suitable, so very few reached Level 3. Whatever the question asks you to decide, decide it in your final paragraph, in its own words.

Knowing all five pricing methods and all four channels is not enough. The marks come from choosing between them, which is the habit the model answer below shows.

## What does a full mark answer look like?

This is my own question, written in the style of the 12 mark part (b) questions on Paper 2.

> Glow Ltd is a private limited company with 6 employees. It makes scented candles by hand and sells them through its own website and two gift shops. Its brand image is "natural and calm". Glow plans to launch a new range of soy wax candles. Fixed costs for the range will be $2,000 a month and the variable cost is $3 per candle. Two competitors already sell soy candles, at $9 and $11.
>
> | Pricing method | Price per candle | Estimated monthly sales |
> |---|---|---|
> | Cost plus (60% markup) | $8 | 1,000 |
> | Penetration | $7 | 1,400 |
> | Skimming | $12 | 600 |
>
> Consider the following three pricing methods Glow could use for its new range. Which method should Glow use? Justify your answer. [12]

### First, work out the profit for each option

You are not told to calculate, but in my view using the data is the strongest application you can show. Profit is total revenue minus total costs.

| Method | Revenue | Total costs | Profit a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost plus | $8 × 1,000 = $8,000 | $2,000 + $3,000 = $5,000 | $3,000 |
| Penetration | $7 × 1,400 = $9,800 | $2,000 + $4,200 = $6,200 | $3,600 |
| Skimming | $12 × 600 = $7,200 | $2,000 + $1,800 = $3,800 | $3,400 |

The cost plus price checks out too. Average cost at 1,000 candles is $5,000 ÷ 1,000 = $5. Adding 60% gives $8.

### The model answer

> **Cost plus.** Cost plus pricing adds a percentage to the cost of each unit. Glow's average cost is $5, so a 60% markup gives a price of $8. The advantage is that every candle sold covers its cost and earns $3, which is safe for a small business with only 6 employees. However, cost plus ignores what competitors charge. At $8, Glow is $1 below the cheapest rival, so it may be giving away profit it did not need to lose. Its estimated profit of $3,000 a month is the lowest of the three.
>
> **Penetration.** Penetration pricing sets a low price to win customers quickly. At $7, Glow would be $2 below the cheapest competitor, so customers comparing soy candles in the two gift shops are likely to switch. Estimated sales of 1,400 give a profit of $3,600 a month, the highest of the three, because the extra sales more than make up for the lower price. The risk is that $7 could damage Glow's "natural and calm" image, as customers may link a low price with low quality. It would also be hard to raise the price later without losing the new customers.
>
> **Skimming.** Price skimming charges a high price at launch. This suits a handmade product with a premium image, and each candle earns $12 minus $3, or $9, towards fixed costs. However, skimming works best when a product is new to the market. Two competitors already sell soy candles at $9 and $11, so at $12 Glow is the most expensive. Sales are estimated at only 600, and if customers choose the cheaper rivals, even that may not be reached.
>
> **Recommendation.** Glow should use penetration pricing. The range is not new to the market, since two rivals already sell soy candles, so skimming is unlikely to work. Penetration gives the highest estimated profit, $600 a month more than cost plus. It also uses the website and gift shops to win customers before the competitors respond. This depends on the estimate of 1,400 sales being accurate. If sales reached only 1,000 at $7, profit would fall to $2,000, so Glow should review the price after the first three months and move towards $8 once it has loyal customers.

### Why I would place this in the top band

This is my judgement as a teacher, not a Cambridge mark. The specimen level descriptors for these questions put an answer in Level 3 (9 to 12 marks) when it covers at least two options in depth, uses the case, and backs its recommendation with reasons. The top of the band goes to answers that cover all three and say why the others were turned down.

| What the examiner looks for | Where it is in the answer |
|---|---|
| Knowledge of each method | The first sentence of each paragraph |
| A different fact from the case in each paragraph | 6 employees and $5 cost; gift shops and $9 rival; handmade and two rivals |
| A chain of reasoning, not a list | Price compared with rivals, then sales, then profit |
| Advantage and disadvantage for each option | Every paragraph has both |
| A decision that compares | The recommendation rejects skimming and cost plus with reasons |
| What it depends on | The 1,400 estimate, and what happens at 1,000 |

The extra calculation in the recommendation is new information, not repetition. At 1,000 candles and $7, revenue is $7,000 and total costs are $5,000, so profit is $2,000. Reports from March 2022, June 2023 and June 2025 all noted that weak recommendations simply repeated earlier points.

## How are marketing mix questions marked?

The patterns below come from the 0264 specimen mark schemes for Paper 1 and Paper 2. The 7081 Paper 2 specimen carries the same packaging question.

| Question type | Marks | How the marks split |
|---|---|---|
| Define, such as "Define dynamic pricing" (Paper 1) | 2 | 2 for a clear meaning, 1 for some understanding |
| Explain one advantage and one disadvantage (Paper 1) | 6 | For each: 1 knowledge, 1 application, 1 analysis |
| Explain one advantage and one disadvantage (Paper 2 part a) | 8 | 1 for each point, then up to 3 for each explanation, one of which must be applied |
| Consider three options, which should it use? (Paper 2 part b) | 12 | Levels: 1 to 4, 5 to 8, 9 to 12 |

In the specimen papers, marketing mix appears as a definition of dynamic pricing, the advantages and disadvantages of leaflets for a new nursery, and the advantages and disadvantages of environmentally friendly packaging for a café business. Each paper has 80 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes.

The specimen guidance says a reference to the business only earns application when it fits the point you are making. Dropping in the business name is not enough. ## What do examiners say students get wrong?

These come from the 0450 and 7115 examiner reports, the papers that 0264 and 7081 replace. Sessions in brackets are where each warning appears.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Mixing up the product life cycle and the business cycle, such as giving boom and slump as stages (June 2022, June 2023, June 2025; the reverse mix up in March 2022) | Use introduction, growth, maturity and decline only |
| Swapping penetration and skimming (November 2023, March 2024, March 2025, June 2025) | Penetration is the low price to enter. Skimming is the high price at launch |
| Writing "lower than competitors" for competitive pricing (June 2022) | The report's meaning: level with competitors' prices or only a little under |
| Giving free samples and competitions as advertising (some candidates, June 2022), or advertising and pricing methods as sales promotion (many, June 2025) | Sales promotion is an offer; advertising is a paid message |
| Explaining brand image with no link to packaging (June 2023) | Show how the packaging creates or supports the image |
| Writing about the benefit to the retailer, wholesaler or customer, not the producer (many in June 2023, some in November 2023) | Take the producer's side unless told otherwise |
| Using the same fact from the case for both points (November 2022, November 2023, March 2025) | A different fact for each point |
| Adding new knowledge points instead of developing one (November 2023, March 2026) | Follow each point through to sales, costs or profit |

## Practice questions with answers

Try each on paper before reading the answer.

### Practice question 1

> Fresh Squeeze is a sole trader selling bottled fruit juice at a stall near two schools. Juices are made each morning with no preservatives. Most customers are students aged 12 to 18.
>
> Outline two roles of packaging for Fresh Squeeze. [4]

**Answer**

- To protect the product **[k]**, because the juice has no preservatives and must stay sealed and fresh until a student drinks it **[app]**.
- To attract customers **[k]**, as bright, colourful bottles can stand out to students aged 12 to 18 walking past the stall **[app]**.

Two roles, two different facts: no preservatives, and the age of the customers.

### Practice question 2

> Bright Bites Ltd makes healthy snack bars. Its new Oat Crunch bar was launched last month. Its Fruit Bar has sold well for six years, but sales have stopped rising.
>
> Explain the level of promotion Bright Bites should use for each of its two bars. [6]

**Answer**

The Oat Crunch bar is in the introduction stage **[k]**, as it was launched only last month **[app]**. It needs a high level of promotion so customers know it exists, which should help sales rise quickly **[an]**.

The Fruit Bar is in the maturity stage **[k]**, because sales have stopped rising after six years **[app]**. Promotion can be lower and aimed at reminding existing customers, to protect market share while keeping costs down **[an]**.

### Practice question 3

> Hill Honey Ltd produces honey on a farm 40 km from the nearest city. It currently sells only at its farm shop. It is considering selling through supermarkets in the city.
>
> Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to Hill Honey Ltd of selling through supermarkets. [6]

**Answer**

- Advantage: wider distribution **[k]**. City customers who would never drive 40 km to the farm shop could buy the honey **[app]**. More customers should mean higher sales and revenue **[an]**.
- Disadvantage: the supermarket adds its own margin **[k]**, so honey that sells at a set price in the farm shop will cost more in the city **[app]**. Customers may choose cheaper brands next to it on the shelf, so sales may be lower than expected **[an]**.

Notice that both answers take Hill Honey's side, not the supermarket's.

## FAQ

### What are the 4Ps of the marketing mix?

Product, price, place and promotion. Together they describe what a business sells, what it charges, how the product reaches customers and how customers find out about it.

### How many pricing methods are in the 0264 and 7081 syllabus?

Five: cost plus, competitive, penetration, skimming and dynamic. You need the advantages and disadvantages of each, and to recommend one for a given situation.

### What is the difference between penetration pricing and price skimming?

Penetration sets a low price to enter a market and win customers quickly. Skimming sets a high price at launch, usually for a new or unique product, and lowers it later.

### Is the marketing mix the same in 0264 and 7081?

Yes. Section 3.3 uses the same wording in both syllabuses, from product through to entering new markets in other countries.

### Is ecommerce part of the marketing mix?

In 0264 and 7081, yes. It is part 3.3.5 of the marketing mix section. You need examples and the advantages and disadvantages for businesses and customers.

### Can I use old 0450 and 7115 papers to practise the marketing mix?

Yes, the questions test the same skills. Check each pricing method against the new list of five, since older reports also mention promotional pricing.
