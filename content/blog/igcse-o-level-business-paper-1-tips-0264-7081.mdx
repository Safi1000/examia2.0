Business Paper 1 for IGCSE 0264 and O Level 7081 is 80 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes: four compulsory questions, each worth 20 marks. In the specimen each question has four parts worth 2, 4, 6 and 8 marks. Give each question about 22 minutes. Marks come from four skills: knowledge, application to the business, analysis and a justified decision. Most lost marks come from missing application and weak justification.

## What is on 0264 and 7081 Paper 1?

These facts are from the 2027 to 2029 syllabuses. Paper 1 is the same for 0264 and 7081.

| Paper 1 fact | From 2027 |
|---|---|
| Name | Short Answer and Data Response |
| Time | 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Marks | 80 |
| Weighting | 50% of the qualification |
| Questions | Four, all compulsory |
| Calculator | Allowed (specimen cover) |

Each question starts with stimulus material about a business. The syllabus says this may include tables, graphs and images, and that some questions need you to use it. Paper 2, the case study, is the other 50%.

The syllabus also gives the skill weightings for Paper 1: knowledge and understanding 40%, application 30%, analysis 20% and evaluation 10%. Application is worth almost a third of the paper, which is why I spend so much class time on it.

### What changed from 0450 and 7115?

The changes page says each Paper 1 question now has four parts instead of five, and the breakdown of marks has changed. It also says the weighting of application has gone up. Here is how the parts line up, from the 0450 June 2025 Paper 12 question paper and the 0264 specimen:

| Part | Old 0450 and 7115 | New 0264 and 7081 (specimen) |
|---|---:|---:|
| (a) | 2 | 2 |
| (b) | 2 | 4 |
| (c) | 4 | 6 |
| (d) | 6 | 8 |
| (e) | 6 | none |

Both formats total 20 marks a question and 80 for the paper. My guide on [using 0450 and 7115 past papers for 0264](/blog/igcse-business-0450-past-papers-for-0264) explains which old questions still work for practice.

## How is each part of a Paper 1 question marked?

This comes from the 0264 specimen Paper 1 mark scheme. The 7081 Paper 1 specimen mark scheme on disk has the same questions. The 7081 Paper 1 question paper itself is not on disk, so I have only checked its mark scheme.

| Part | Typical command | How the specimen awards marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) 2 marks | Define, calculate, identify | Define: 2 for clear understanding, 1 for some. Calculate: method and answer. Identify: 1 per point |
| (b) 4 marks | Outline two, identify four, or two short tasks | Outline: for each point, 1 knowledge and 1 application. Identify four: 1 per point. Question 2 splits (b) into a 2 mark calculation and a 2 mark identify task |
| (c) 6 marks | Explain two, or one advantage and one disadvantage | For each point, 1 knowledge, 1 application and 1 analysis |
| (d) 8 marks | Explain two and choose, or advantages against disadvantages | Up to 2 each for knowledge, application, analysis and a justified decision |

Two things in the table trip people up.

First, **not every 4 mark part has application**. In specimen Question 4, part (b) asks you to identify four reasons, and its scheme gives 1 mark per reason, with no application. Question 4(a) is also identify only. Read the command word before you write.

Second, **the old 6 mark (e) part had no application**. The 0450 June 2025 Paper 12 scheme for 1(e) gave 2 marks each for points, development and a justified decision, and the question asked about "a business" in general. The new 8 mark (d) part adds 2 application marks. So when you practise old (e) questions, link your points to a business anyway.

### What counts as application?

Application means using a fact from the stem to make your point fit this business. The specimen scheme lists the words it accepts for each question, such as the product, the number of employees or a figure from the table. It also says the reference must make sense with the point you are making. So I teach that naming the business alone is not enough. My full guide on [application marks in 0264 and 7081](/blog/igcse-o-level-business-application-marks) shows how to pick the right fact.

### What counts as a justified decision?

In the specimen (d) parts, the 2 evaluation marks are for a justified decision. The guidance says the justification should not repeat earlier points. Recent 0450 reports make the same point. The June 2025 report (Paper 12) said a decision alone, or a summary of earlier points, is not evaluation. The March 2026 report (Paper 12) said effective evaluation was rare, and advised explaining why your choice is better than the alternative.

## How long should you spend on each part?

My advice: 90 minutes for 80 marks is 1.125 minutes a mark. Each question is 20 marks, so it gets 22.5 minutes. I rounded the part timings to the nearest half minute.

| Part | Marks | Time (my plan) |
|---|---:|---:|
| (a) | 2 | 2 minutes |
| (b) | 4 | 4.5 minutes |
| (c) | 6 | 7 minutes |
| (d) | 8 | 9 minutes |
| **One question** | **20** | **22.5 minutes** |

Four questions take 90 minutes. There is no spare time, so do not write extra points. The specimen mark scheme for 4(a) and 4(b) says only the first two, or first four, answers are marked. Extra points cost time and earn nothing.

## Worked example: a full Paper 1 question with model answers

Here is a question I wrote in the specimen style, with an answer for each part and where each mark comes from. The marking labels are my judgement, built on the pattern of the specimen mark scheme. They are not Cambridge's marking.

> **Fresh Squeeze (FS)** is a sole trader business that sells fresh fruit juices from a stall in a city shopping centre. Its owner, Hina, employs 6 workers. Last year FS's sales revenue was $96 000. Total sales of all juice businesses in the city were $1.2 million. The price of fruit has been rising. Hina buys all her fruit from one local supplier and is deciding whether to open a second stall in another shopping centre.

### (a) Calculate FS's market share. Show your working. [2]

> Market share = FS's sales revenue ÷ total market sales × 100
> = 96 000 ÷ 1 200 000 × 100 = **8%**

One mark for the method, one for the correct answer. Write the % sign. The March 2025 0450 report (Paper 12) said a common mistake was to leave the × 100 out of a profit margin formula, or the % sign off the answer, and reminded candidates to include the right units.

### (b) Outline two ways a rise in the price of fruit might affect FS. [4]

> **Way 1.** Higher costs of raw materials [k] because every juice FS sells is made from fresh fruit [app].
>
> **Way 2.** Hina may have to raise her prices [k], and some shoppers in the shopping centre may buy cheaper drinks from other stalls [app].

Each way has one point of knowledge and one reference to FS. Outline does not need analysis, so I stop there.

### (c) Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to FS of buying all its fruit from one supplier. [6]

> **Advantage.** A close link with one supplier can mean quick, reliable deliveries [k], which matters because FS's juices are made fresh at the stall [app]. FS is less likely to run out of fruit, so it loses fewer sales [an].
>
> **Disadvantage.** FS depends on that supplier's prices [k]. With the price of fruit already rising [app], Hina cannot easily switch to a cheaper source, so her costs go up and her profit falls [an].

Each side has knowledge, a different piece of application and its own analysis. The second analysis is not a copy of the first.

### (d) Do you think the advantages to FS of opening a second stall are greater than the disadvantages? Justify your answer. [8]

> **Advantages.** A second stall in another shopping centre reaches new customers [k] who do not visit FS's current site [app]. FS's sales revenue would rise above $96 000, which could increase its 8% market share and its profit [an].
>
> **Disadvantages.** Hina must pay for a second stall and more workers on top of her 6 [app]. As a sole trader she has unlimited liability [k], so if the new stall makes a loss she could lose her personal possessions to pay its debts [an].
>
> **Decision.** I think the disadvantages are greater for now. With fruit prices rising, the profit FS makes on each juice is already falling, so a second stall may not cover its extra costs [eval]. The risk falls on Hina personally, so she should first protect the profit of her current stall, perhaps by raising prices slightly, before taking on a second site [eval].

The decision gives a reason that was not used earlier: the rising price of fruit squeezing the profit on each juice. It then says what Hina should do instead. That is what moves a conclusion from a summary to a justified decision.

<Tip title="One fact, one point">
Use a different fact from the stem for each point. The June 2025 0450 report (Paper 12) said a different contextual reference is needed for each point. Two points tied to the same fact earn one application mark, not two.
</Tip>

## Where do students lose marks on Paper 1?

These come from 0450 and 7115 Paper 1 examiner reports. The old papers are not the same format, but the skills are. The middle column is my summary.

| Mistake | What to do instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Definitions that are vague or only partly right | Learn a precise definition for each key term; it need not match the textbook word for word | 0450 June 2025 Papers 11 and 12; 0450 March 2026 Paper 12; 7115 June 2022 Paper 12 |
| No application, or the same fact used for both points | Use a different fact from the stem for each point | 0450 June 2025 Paper 12; 0450 March 2026 Paper 12 (many reused the same application); 7115 June 2022 Paper 12 (many missed application) |
| Writing "product" instead of the real product | Name what the business sells | 0450 and 7115 November 2023 Paper 11 |
| The same analysis for both points | Develop each point with its own consequence | 0450 June 2025 Paper 12; 0450 November 2023 Paper 12; 7115 November 2023 Paper 12 |
| A decision with no reason, or a summary as the conclusion | Give a reason for the choice and say why it beats the alternative | 0450 June 2025 Papers 11 and 12; 0450 March 2026 Paper 12; 0450 November 2023 Paper 11 |
| Twisting the question to fit what you know | Answer the question set; revise every topic | 0450 June 2023 Paper 11; 7115 June 2022 Paper 11 |
| A missing minus sign in a cash flow answer | Show negative values in brackets or with a minus sign | 0450 June 2025 Paper 11, Question 3(b) (many correct answers lost a mark); 0450 June 2025 Paper 13, Question 2(b) |
| Dividing instead of subtracting in margin of safety | Margin of safety = actual sales minus break even sales | 0450 March 2026 Paper 12, Question 3(b) (many) |

The June 2023 0450 report (Paper 12) and the June 2022 7115 report (Paper 12) both reminded candidates that questions can be set on any area of the syllabus. The 0450 report added that some candidates did not know topics such as why markets are competitive. Paper 1 covers the whole syllabus, so a skipped chapter is a real risk.

## How should you practise for Paper 1?

How I would prepare, as my advice:

1. **Learn definitions word perfect in meaning.** Two of the four (a) parts in the specimen are definitions, and 2 marks need the full meaning, not an example. The specimen scheme for 1(a) says examples do not define the term.
2. **Learn the formulas list.** The 0264 syllabus lists the formulas and ratios, including market share, labour productivity, break even and margin of safety. Write the formula, then the figures.
3. **Practise old papers by topic.** Use the [0450 past papers by topic](/past-papers/igcse-business-studies-0450/topics), and turn each old (e) part into an 8 mark (d) answer by adding application.
4. **Do the specimen in timed conditions.** My [0264 and 7081 specimen paper guide](/blog/0264-7081-business-specimen-paper-2027) goes through it question by question.
5. **Mark with the scheme and label each mark** k, app, an or eval. You will see quickly which skill you are missing.

You can see how many marks past grades needed on the [0450 grade thresholds page](/grade-thresholds/igcse-business-0450). The 0264 lines will be new from 2027.

## Can you answer these practice questions?

All three are my own. Marks follow the specimen pattern, and the split is my judgement.

> **TopTiles (TT)** makes floor tiles in a factory with 120 workers. TT made 2 400 000 tiles last year. TT exports 30% of its tiles. Managers are thinking about replacing some workers with machines.

**1.** Calculate TT's labour productivity. Show your working. [2]

**Answer.** Labour productivity = output ÷ number of employees = 2 400 000 ÷ 120 = **20 000 tiles per employee** (2). One mark for the correct method if the answer is wrong.

**2.** Outline two reasons why TT might want to increase its labour productivity. [4]

**Answer.** Lower cost per unit (1) because each of the 120 workers makes more tiles (1). More competitive prices abroad (1), which matters because 30% of TT's tiles are exported (1).

**3.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to TT of replacing some workers with machines. [6]

**Answer.** Advantage: machines can work faster and for longer than people (1), so the factory could make more than 2 400 000 tiles a year (1), lowering the cost of each tile and raising profit (1). Disadvantage: machines are expensive to buy (1), and TT would need enough of them to do the work of some of its 120 workers (1), so it may have to borrow and pay interest, which reduces profit (1).

## FAQ

### How many questions are on 0264 Paper 1?

Four compulsory questions, each worth 20 marks, for a total of 80 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes.

### Is Paper 1 the same for IGCSE 0264 and O Level 7081?

The syllabuses give the same Paper 1 for both, and the 7081 specimen Paper 1 mark scheme has the same questions as the 0264 one.

### Is there a 12 mark question on Paper 1?

Not in the specimen. The largest Paper 1 part is the 8 mark (d) part. Paper 2 is the case study paper.

### Do definitions need application?

No. In the specimen, (a) parts that ask you to define are marked on knowledge only: 2 marks for clear understanding, 1 for some.

### Can I still use 0450 and 7115 Paper 1 past papers?

Yes, for practice by topic. Each old question has five parts and the 6 mark (e) part had no application marks, so add application when you practise it.

### How long should I spend on the 8 mark part?

About 9 minutes, by my plan. That is 1.125 minutes a mark, based on 80 marks in 90 minutes.
