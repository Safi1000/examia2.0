Ethics, technology and sustainability are the two new topics in IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 from 2027: section 7.2 Ethical considerations and section 7.3 Technology and sustainability. You need the five fundamental ethical principles, why they matter to stakeholders, and how accounting data is stored safely and sustainably. Both codes word these two sections the same way, and both are first examined in 2027.

## What is new in 0452 and 7707 for 2027?

Topic 7 has a new title, Accounting concepts and modern practice. It keeps 7.1 Accounting concepts and adds two short sections. The 2027 specimen papers for 0452 and 7707 use the same questions.

| Section | What the syllabus asks you to understand |
|---|---|
| 7.2 Ethical considerations | Why accounting needs an ethical framework; the five fundamental principles; why applying the framework matters to stakeholders and society |
| 7.3 Technology and sustainability | Digital applications for all or part of the records; storing data safely and sustainably; the risks of not doing so; four types of storage and their advantages and disadvantages |

The syllabus adds one helpful note under 7.3: you do not need to know, or have access to, any particular accounting software. Questions are about ideas and judgement, not about which buttons to press.

There are no past paper questions and no examiner reports on these sections yet. The first reports can only come after the 2027 exams. Everything below about how the topics are tested comes from the syllabus and the 2027 specimen papers and mark schemes.

## Why does accounting need an ethical framework?

An ethical framework is a set of principles that tells accountants how to behave when no rule covers the exact situation. Accounting needs one because other people rely on accounts they did not prepare.

Think about who uses a set of accounts. The owner uses them to decide whether to expand. A bank uses them to decide whether to lend. The tax authority uses them to work out tax. None of these people can check every figure themselves. They trust the accountant.

Accountants also face pressure. A client may want profit to look higher before asking for a loan. A friend may ask about another client's business. A boss may want a job finished faster than it can be done properly. The framework gives the accountant a clear standard to follow under that pressure.

## What are the five fundamental ethical principles?

The syllabus names five principles but does not define them. The meanings below are my own wording, and each example is my own.

| Principle | What it means | Example of the principle being applied |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity | Being honest and straightforward in all work | An accountant refuses to record a fake sale that would make profit look higher |
| Objectivity | Not letting bias, a conflict of interest or pressure from others affect judgement | An accountant declines to check the accounts of her brother's business |
| Professional competence and due care | Having the knowledge and skill the work needs, keeping it up to date, and working carefully | An accountant checks every balance against the bank statement before finishing the accounts |
| Confidentiality | Not sharing information about a client without permission, unless the law requires it | An accountant will not tell a supplier how much a client owes the bank |
| Professional behaviour | Obeying laws and regulations and avoiding anything that would damage the good name of the profession | An accountant does not criticise clients by name on social media |

### How do I tell integrity and objectivity apart?

These two are the easiest to mix up. Integrity is about **honesty**: is the accountant telling the truth? Objectivity is about **fairness of judgement**: could a personal interest or relationship bend the accountant's view?

A quick test helps. If the problem is a lie, it is integrity. If the problem is a gift, a relative, a friendship or pressure from someone powerful, it is objectivity.

### What about competence and professional behaviour?

Professional competence and due care is about skill and care: knowing enough to do the job, and doing it carefully. Professional behaviour is about the law and the reputation of the profession.

One specimen answer needs care. In specimen Paper 2, an accountant will not advise on a complex issue until she has done some training and research. The mark scheme gives professional behaviour as the answer, not professional competence and due care. I would have expected competence from the names alone. My advice, as my own judgement and not a Cambridge rule: for a situation about refusing work until trained, give the specimen's answer. Use competence when the situation is about care, checking or having the right skills.

## Why does applying the framework matter to stakeholders and society?

Stakeholders are the people and groups with an interest in a business, such as owners, lenders, employees, suppliers and the government. The syllabus asks why it matters to them, and to society, that accountants follow the framework.

The specimen mark scheme rewards three kinds of point: stakeholders can trust the accountant's work, they can make sound decisions with it, and the reputation of the accounting profession is protected. Here is how that looks for each group.

| Stakeholder | Why ethical accounting matters to them |
|---|---|
| Owners and shareholders | They can trust the profit figure when deciding on drawings, dividends or expansion |
| Banks and other lenders | They can judge whether a loan will be repaid |
| Suppliers | They can judge whether to offer credit |
| Employees | They can judge whether their jobs and wages are secure |
| Government and tax authorities | Tax is worked out on honest figures |
| Society | People keep trusting businesses and accountants, so lending and trade continue |

The specimen "explain" question on this gives 1 mark for each correct point, up to 2. So make two separate points, for example trust and decisions. My advice is to tie each point to a stakeholder: the bank can trust the figures, and the owner can decide whether to expand.

## What does the syllabus mean by digital applications?

Digital applications are programs or apps used to keep accounting records on a computer, tablet or phone. The syllabus says a business may use them for **all or part** of its records. A shop might keep its sales on a till system and its wages in a spreadsheet, yet still keep some records by hand.

The specimen tests this through a five mark "advise" question on whether a business owner should use a computerised accounting system. The mark scheme points fall into the groups below. The wording is mine.

| Arguments for a computerised system | Arguments against |
|---|---|
| The system adds up totals and balances, so fewer arithmetic errors | Buying the computer and the program costs money |
| Each transaction is typed once and the system posts both sides | The owner or staff must learn the system |
| Transactions are entered faster | For a while, manual and computer records may need to run side by side |
| It can also keep track of inventory and of customers who owe money | Data may be lost, or stolen by hackers |
| The system can run checks for you, such as a trial balance or a bank reconciliation | Repairs, updates and help add to running costs |
| Invoices and year end statements come straight from the data | A power cut or breakdown can stop work |

One idea from the specimen Paper 1 is worth learning: a computer system only produces digital statements from data entered into it. Manual records do not turn into digital statements by themselves.

## What does storing data safely and sustainably mean?

These are two different ideas, and the specimen tests whether you can tell them apart.

**Safely** means the data is protected from loss, damage, theft and people who should not see it. Passwords, backups and antivirus software are safety measures.

**Sustainably** means storing data in a way that does not waste resources, such as electricity, equipment and paper. Keeping only the data the business actually needs, and choosing storage methods that use less power, are sustainability measures.

A password protects data, so it is a safety measure, but it does nothing for sustainability. Keeping every record twice, once on paper and once online, may feel safe, but it doubles the resources used. Read the question word carefully: "securely" or "safely" wants protection; "sustainably" wants less waste.

### How do I store computerised records securely?

The specimen Paper 2 asks for four ways to store computerised records securely, one mark each. Points the mark scheme accepts include:

- protect the computer with antivirus software
- make backup copies at regular times
- set passwords that are hard to guess
- lock the screen before leaving the desk
- let only chosen people log in
- clear all data off old equipment before it is sold or thrown away

Write each way as a separate action, starting with a verb. "Make a backup copy every week" is a clear point. "Be careful with data" is vague.

### What are the risks of not storing data safely and sustainably?

The syllabus lists the risks as a point to learn but does not name them. These are the risks I teach.

| Risk | What happens to the business |
|---|---|
| Records are lost or destroyed (fire, flood, a broken device) | It cannot prove what it is owed or what it owes, and cannot prepare accurate statements |
| Data is stolen or hacked | Customer and supplier details are exposed, which breaks confidentiality and damages trust |
| Records are changed without permission | The accounts become wrong, and fraud may go unnoticed |
| Storage wastes resources | Running costs rise, and the business uses more power and materials than it needs |

## What are the four types of storage system?

The syllabus names four types: manual, data storage devices, cloud services and other digital services. It does not list their advantages and disadvantages, so the points below are my own. They are the kind of ordinary, sensible points the specimen mark schemes reward.

| Storage type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Manual (paper files and books) | No equipment, power or internet needed; cheap to start | Easily destroyed by fire or water; slow to search; takes up space; uses paper |
| Data storage devices (USB drive, external hard drive) | Cheap; the business keeps the data in its own hands; works without internet | Small and easily lost or stolen; can fail without warning; must be backed up |
| Cloud services (data kept on another company's servers over the internet) | Reachable from any device; the provider usually keeps backup copies away from the business | Needs internet access; usually a regular fee; a weak password puts all the data at risk |
| Other digital services (for example online banking and online accounting apps) | Transactions can be recorded quickly, sometimes automatically | The business depends on another company to keep its data secure and available |

The syllabus does not define "other digital services". I teach it as online services beyond plain cloud storage, such as online banking records and accounting apps. If a question gives you a setting, use the example it gives.

<Tip title="Match the advantage to the business in the question">
In an "advise" question, an advantage counts most when it fits the case. A trader who works from three shops gains from records reachable anywhere. A trader in an area with poor internet loses from relying on the cloud. Use the details the question gives you.
</Tip>

## Worked example: a full question on 7.2 and 7.3

This is my own question, written in the style of the specimen. It is not a Cambridge question.

*Imran runs a hardware shop. He keeps his accounts on one laptop in the shop. His accountant, Zara, prepares his financial statements each year.*

**(a) State which fundamental ethical principle Zara applies in each situation. (2 marks)**

1. Imran asks Zara to leave a large unpaid bill out of the accounts so the bank will lend to him. Zara refuses.
2. Zara's cousin plans to open a shop nearby and asks to see Imran's sales figures. Zara refuses.

**Answer.**

1. Integrity (1). Leaving the bill out would make the accounts dishonest.
2. Confidentiality (1). The sales figures belong to Imran, and he has not given permission to share them.

**(b) Explain why it is important that Zara applies the ethical framework. (2 marks)**

**Answer.** The bank will rely on Imran's accounts to decide whether to lend, so it needs to trust that Zara's figures are honest (1). If accountants could not be trusted, the reputation of the whole profession would suffer and stakeholders would stop relying on accounts (1).

**(c) State two risks of keeping all the accounting records on one laptop. (2 marks)**

**Answer.** If the laptop is stolen or breaks, all the records are lost at once (1). Anyone who uses the laptop in the shop could see or change the records (1).

**(d) State two ways Imran could store his records more sustainably. (2 marks)**

**Answer.** Keep only the records the business needs, and delete old files it no longer has to keep (1). Use storage that uses less electricity, rather than running extra devices all day (1).

**(e) Advise Imran whether he should move all his records to a cloud service. Justify your answer. (5 marks)**

**Answer.**

- For: the records are kept away from the shop, so a theft or fire there does not destroy them (1).
- For: Imran and Zara can both reach the records from their own devices, which saves time at the year end (1).
- Against: Imran would pay a regular fee, which reduces his profit (1).
- Against: if the shop's internet stops, he cannot reach his records (1).
- Recommendation: Imran should move to the cloud service, because having all his records on one laptop is the bigger risk, and the fee is a small cost for protecting them (1).

That is 13 marks in total. In part (e), there are two points on each side and a decision that refers back to them. Under the specimen mark scheme, at most 3 marks go to one side and at most 4 to the arguments in total, and the recommendation earns its mark only when you have given at least one argument on each side.

## How are 7.2 and 7.3 marked?

The specimen mark schemes show four patterns:

- **"State" questions** give 1 mark per correct point. A list of separate short points is best.
- **"Explain" questions** in the specimen give 1 mark for each correct point. My advice is to add the reason or the result, so that no point reads as a single vague word.
- **"Advise" questions** give up to 4 marks for arguments for and against (no more than 3 on one side) and 1 mark for a recommendation supported by at least one point each way.
- **Lists end with "Accept all valid responses."** Any correct point in your own words earns the mark, so you do not need the mark scheme's wording.

For the matching style of the advise question, see my guide to [answering theory questions in Paper 2](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-theory-questions), which also covers 7.1 Accounting concepts.

## Where will students lose marks?

There are no examiner reports on 7.2 and 7.3 yet. This list is my own judgement, based on the specimen mark scheme rules.

| Mistake | Why it costs marks | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Writing "honesty" instead of a principle name | The question asks for the principle, and there are five set names | Learn the five names exactly as the syllabus gives them |
| Mixing up integrity and objectivity | They test different things | Lie means integrity; gift, relative or pressure means objectivity |
| Giving a safety measure when asked about sustainability | Passwords protect data but do not reduce waste | Check the key word: safely or sustainably |
| One vague point stretched over three lines | Each mark needs a separate point | One clear action or reason per line |
| Only advantages in an advise answer | Arguments are capped at 3 per side, and the decision needs both sides | Two for, two against, then decide |
| No clear decision at the end | The recommendation is a separate mark | End with a sentence that starts "He should" |
| Naming a software brand | The syllabus says no software knowledge is needed | Describe what the system does, not what it is called |
| Treating "other digital services" as the same as cloud | The syllabus lists them as separate types | Give online banking or an accounting app as the example |

## Practice questions

These are my own questions, not Cambridge questions. Try each one before reading the answer.

**1. State which fundamental ethical principle is applied in each situation. (2 marks)**

(a) Hassan finds he has made an error in a client's accounts last year. He tells the client and corrects it.
(b) Sana is asked to prepare the accounts of a company in which she owns shares. She asks another accountant to do the work.

**Answer.** (a) Integrity (1): he is honest about his own error. (b) Objectivity (1): owning shares in the company could affect her judgement.

**2. State two disadvantages of keeping accounting records on a USB drive. (2 marks)**

**Answer.** Any two: it is small and easily lost or stolen (1); it can stop working without warning (1); anyone who finds it may be able to read the records (1).

**3. Farah has a tailoring business with two branches. Her records are on a spreadsheet saved on one external hard drive, which she carries between the branches. Advise Farah whether she should keep the hard drive as her only store of records. Justify your answer. (5 marks)**

**Answer.** For: the drive is cheap and has no regular fee (1); it works without internet, and the records stay in her own hands (1). Against: a drive carried every day is easily lost, stolen or damaged, and every record goes with it (1); only one branch can use the records at a time (1). Recommendation: she should not rely on the drive alone, because losing it would lose every record, which matters more than saving a fee. She should keep a second copy, for example with a cloud service (1).

## How should I revise these two topics?

1. Learn the five principle names word for word, each with one example of your own.
2. Learn the integrity and objectivity test until you can apply it in seconds.
3. Make one table of the four storage types with two advantages and two disadvantages each.
4. Keep a separate list of six security steps and a short list of sustainability steps.
5. Practise one five mark advise answer a week on a technology decision.
6. Do the 2027 specimen papers under timed conditions, since past papers do not cover these sections.

## FAQ

### Are ethics and technology new in 0452 and 7707 for 2027?

Yes. Sections 7.2 Ethical considerations and 7.3 Technology and sustainability are new in the 2027 to 2029 syllabus for both codes, and are first examined in 2027.

### Will ethics come up in Paper 1 or Paper 2?

It can come up in either, because both papers test the whole syllabus. In the 2027 specimens, ethics appears in one Paper 1 question and in two short parts of Paper 2 Question 2.

### Do I need to know accounting software like QuickBooks?

No. The syllabus says you do not need knowledge of or access to any specific accounting software package. You need to understand what digital systems do and their advantages and disadvantages.

### Are there past paper questions on 7.2 and 7.3?

No. These sections are new, so the only official questions are in the 2027 specimen papers. There are no examiner reports on them yet.

### What is the difference between safe and sustainable storage?

Safe storage protects data from loss, theft and misuse, for example with passwords and backups. Sustainable storage avoids waste, for example by keeping only the data the business needs and using storage that uses less power.

### Does this topic affect my grade much?

In the specimens it is worth 16 of 140 marks. A real paper may differ, but marks like these matter near a grade boundary. Check the [0452 grade thresholds](/grade-thresholds/igcse-accounting-0452) to see the gaps between grades.
