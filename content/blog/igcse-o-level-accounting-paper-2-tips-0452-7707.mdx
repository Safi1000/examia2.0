IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 Paper 2 gives you 1 hour 45 minutes for five compulsory questions of 20 marks each. My advice is to give each question 20 minutes and keep 5 minutes to check. Inside a question, spend about a minute a mark and move on when the 20 minutes are up. Always show your workings, so a slip early on does not cost you the marks that follow.

## What is in 0452 and 7707 Paper 2?

The [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452) and the O Level 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029 describe Paper 2 in the same words.

| Paper 2 fact | 0452 and 7707 |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Questions | 5, all compulsory |
| Marks | 20 per question, 100 in total |
| Share of the qualification | 70% |
| Calculators | Allowed |
| Changes for 2027 | None |

Every question starts with **stimulus material**, the story and figures about a business, followed by several tasks. The syllabus lists the kinds of task: calculations, completing a cash book, completing a statement that has been started for you, preparing accounts from the information, and preparing suspense accounts and journal entries.

The syllabus also tells you how the marks split by skill. In Paper 2, 60% of the marks test knowledge and understanding, 25% test analysis and 15% test evaluation (judging information and reaching a decision). Paper 1 has no evaluation at all, so the written judgement parts live here.

The syllabus itself says to use the marks for each question as a guide to how much to write and how to manage your time. The time plan below is my way of doing that.

## How long should each question take?

Cambridge sets no time per question. My advice is a fixed plan you practise until it feels normal.

| Time from the start | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 20 minutes | Question 1 |
| 20 to 40 minutes | Question 2 |
| 40 to 60 minutes | Question 3 |
| 60 to 80 minutes | Question 4 |
| 80 to 100 minutes | Question 5 |
| 100 to 105 minutes | Check totals, labels and anything you left |

Before the exam, write the five finishing times on your rough paper or at the top of the question paper. If you start at 9:00, those are 9:20, 9:40, 10:00, 10:20 and 10:40. Then you never have to work out how you are doing. You just look at the clock and the list.

### How do I split the 20 minutes inside a question?

Use about a minute a mark, then adjust by task type. This is how I teach it:

- **State, give or define parts** (1 or 2 marks): about 30 seconds a mark. Write the answer and stop.
- **Format parts** (a ledger account, a journal, an income statement): about a minute a mark, sometimes a little more. Headings, dates and labels all take writing time.
- **Calculations** (2 to 4 marks): a minute a mark, with the working written out.
- **Advise parts** (5 marks): 5 minutes. Plan for 1 minute, write for 4.

The time you save on short parts pays for the long format part. Write a paragraph for a 2 mark "state" question and you take that time from question 5.

## Which topics come up in each Paper 2 question?

The syllabus says questions can come from any part of the subject content. In practice, each question number tends to favour certain topics. I counted the topic tags on the 49 Paper 2 papers from 2022 to 2026 on this site (31 for 0452 and 18 for 7707). These are this site's own tags, not Cambridge's, and a question can carry more than one.

| Question | Topics tagged most often (papers out of 49) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Books of prime entry (39), double entry (13), bank reconciliation (7) |
| 2 | Manufacturing accounts (10), sole trader statements (9), correction of errors (8) |
| 3 | Correction of errors (19), accruals and prepayments (10), trial balance (7) |
| 4 | Correction of errors (12), limited companies (10), ratio calculation (7) |
| 5 | Limited companies (11), ratio calculation (11), manufacturing accounts (8) |

Across the whole paper, books of prime entry appeared in 44 of the 49 papers and correction of errors in 40. The 2027 specimen Paper 2 for 0452 has a similar shape. Question 1 is a purchases journal and a supplier account, question 2 is correction of errors and question 3 is a manufacturing business. Question 4 is a club and question 5 is a limited company with ratios.

Two warnings. First, these are patterns, not promises. All five questions are compulsory, and the March 2022 report reminded candidates that this means knowing every topic. Second, the 2027 syllabus adds ethics, technology and sustainability topics that have no past papers yet. ## What should I do in the first two minutes of each question?

Read the whole question before you write anything. The March 2022, March 2023 and June 2025 reports all advise reading each question carefully so no data is missed. In my experience, the missed item is often a note at the bottom: an accrual, a depreciation rate, a drawing of goods.

My routine for every question:

1. Read the last line of each part first, so you know what you are building.
2. Read the data, and tick each figure in the margin when you use it.
3. Note the mark for each part and write its time next to it.
4. Underline any instruction about form: "to two decimal places", "balance the account", "show your workings".

Step 4 matters more than it looks. The March 2026 report noted that some candidates did not follow the instruction to balance an account, and that weaker answers did not give a ratio to two decimal places.

## What do I do if I fall behind?

At each finishing time, stop and move to the next question, even mid part. Leave a few lines blank and come back in the last 5 minutes. A part you never start scores nothing, and in my experience the first marks on a new question are often the easiest to earn.

If you come back and need more space than you left, write the answer on a blank page and put a note where the answer should be, such as "continued on page 14". The March 2022, June 2023 and June 2025 reports all say a note like this stops the extra work being missed. If you try a part twice, cross out the version you do not want marked, as the June 2023 report advised.

When you are stuck on a calculation, write the format anyway. Marks are given point by point, so correct labels, dates and figures in a partly finished statement can still score.

## How is Paper 2 marked?

The mark schemes use **point marking**: each correct step, label or figure earns its own mark. Two rules from the mark scheme instructions, and one warning from the examiner reports, decide how many marks you keep.

| Rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Correct answer, no working | Full credit, unless the question asks for working |
| Wrong answer, no working | No marks, because there is no evidence of your method (June 2023 report, Paper 21) |
| Own figure rule | If you use your own wrong figure from an earlier part with a correct method, you can still earn full marks for that later part |

The own figure rule is the most useful thing on this page. The 0452 June 2025 Paper 21 mark scheme shows it at work. A depreciation total gave marks for the yearly charges along the way. The final total was marked OF, meaning it was credited on the candidate's own figures. So one early slip does not wipe out the rest of a question, as long as the examiner can follow you.

Calculations are a real share of the paper. The June 2022 report for Paper 22 said almost a quarter of the questions on that paper needed calculations of 2 to 6 marks. It also warned that netting off figures, combining several numbers into one without showing them, risked losing all 5 marks on one part.

### How are the five mark advise questions marked?

The March 2025 and June 2025 reports both say the paper had its usual three five mark questions, each needing points for, points against and a recommendation. I checked three mark schemes: 0452 June 2025 Paper 21, 0452 March 2026 Paper 22 and 7707 June 2024 Paper 21. In each, the split was 4 marks for the points, with a limit on each side, and 1 mark for the recommendation. My [theory questions article](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-theory-questions) shows the full method with a model answer. In short: two points for, two points against, each tied to the business in the question, then one clear decision.

## Worked example: how do I time one full question?

Here is a question I have written to show the time plan in action.

**Hira owns a shop.** For the year ended 31 December 2026: revenue $48,000, inventory at 1 January 2026 $5,000, purchases $30,000, carriage inwards $400, inventory at 31 December 2026 $6,000.

| Part | Task | Marks | My time |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | State two reasons why Hira prepares an income statement | 2 | 1.5 minutes |
| (b) | Explain the difference between gross profit and profit for the year | 2 | 1.5 minutes |
| (c) | Prepare the trading section of Hira's income statement | 6 | 7 minutes |
| (d) | Calculate the gross margin to two decimal places | 2 | 2 minutes |
| (e) | Last year's gross margin was 42.00%. Suggest two reasons for the change | 3 | 3 minutes |
| (f) | Advise Hira whether to open a second shop | 5 | 5 minutes |
| | **Total** | **20** | **20 minutes** |

The short parts (a) and (b) get 3 minutes together. That leaves 7 minutes for the format in (c), the largest part of the question.

### Part (c): the trading section

Hira
Income statement (extract) for the year ended 31 December 2026

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | | 48,000 |
| Less Cost of sales | | |
| Inventory at 1 January 2026 | 5,000 | |
| Purchases | 30,000 | |
| Carriage inwards | 400 | |
| | 35,400 | |
| Less Inventory at 31 December 2026 | 6,000 | |
| | | 29,400 |
| Gross profit | | 18,600 |

Check: 5,000 + 30,000 + 400 = 35,400. Then 35,400 less 6,000 = 29,400. Then 48,000 less 29,400 = 18,600. The heading, every label and both totals are written in full: no "COGS", no "GP".

### Part (d): the gross margin

Gross margin = gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 = 18,600 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = **38.75%**.

Now suppose a student added the closing inventory in (c) instead of taking it away. Their cost of sales becomes 5,000 + 30,000 + 400 + 6,000 = 41,400, and their gross profit is 48,000 less 41,400 = 6,600. Part (c) loses marks. But in part (d) they write:

Gross margin = 6,600 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = 13.75%

The method is correct and the working is shown, so under the own figure rule part (d) can still earn its marks. If they had written only "13.75%", the examiner would see a wrong answer with no method, and part (d) would score nothing. Same mistake, different result, decided by one line of working.

### Part (f): the advise plan

In my 5 minutes, the first minute is a plan in the margin:

- For: more customers and more sales; a second area for the business.
- Against: extra rent and wages; Hira's gross margin has already fallen from 42.00% to 38.75%, so costs need watching.
- Decision: wait, for example, until she finds out why the margin fell.

Then I write it out in full sentences, using Hira's own figures. A rehearsed answer about "expansion" in general would miss the point the figures make.

<Tip title="Working on the line before the answer">
Write the formula with your numbers in it on the line above every final figure. It costs ten seconds and it is the only thing that lets the own figure rule work for you.
</Tip>

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong in Paper 2?

These come from the Paper 2 sections of the 0452 and 7707 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026.

| What goes wrong | Where the reports say it |
|---|---|
| A piece of data is missed because the question was not read in full | 0452 March 2022, March 2023 and June 2025 |
| A wrong figure with no working, so no marks can be given | 0452 and 7707 June 2023, Paper 21 |
| Figures netted off into one number, losing the process marks | 0452 and 7707 June 2022, Paper 22 |
| Abbreviations such as COGS, GP, NP or "bbd" for Balance b/d | 0452 March 2022, June 2023 and June 2025, 7707 November 2023 |
| Handwriting or overwritten figures that cannot be read | 0452 November 2022 (Paper 23), June 2023 and June 2025 |
| Ratios without the right decimal places or without %, times or : 1 | 0452 March 2023 and March 2026 |
| Advise answers that are general and not about the business in the question | 0452 March 2026, June 2025 (Paper 22) and 7707 November 2023 |
| Advise answers that cover only one side, or give no clear recommendation | 0452 November 2022 (Paper 23), March 2023 and June 2025 |

The June 2023 report for Paper 22 put the advise problem bluntly. Too often, it said, candidates give "a rehearsed answer to a question that is not there". The fix is the same each time. Read the scenario, then use its names and figures in every point.

<Tip title="Labels are marks">
In ledger accounts and statements, the words carry marks as well as the numbers. Write "Balance b/d", "Cost of sales" and "Profit for the year" in full, with dates where the account needs them.
</Tip>

## How should I practise for Paper 2?

Practise the paper the way you will sit it, but build up to the full timing.

1. **Single questions, timed.** Take one Paper 2 question, set 20 minutes, and stop when the time ends. Mark it with the mark scheme, including the own figure marks.
2. **Topic sets.** Group past questions by question number. Doing ten "question 1" papers in a row makes books of prime entry automatic. The topic filter on the past papers pages makes this quick.
3. **Full papers.** In the last few weeks, sit full papers in 1 hour 45 minutes with the finishing times written down.
4. **Mark the losses by type.** For each lost mark, write the cause: time, missing working, label, misread data or method. After three papers the pattern is usually clear.

For the formats you will write most often, keep my [accounting formats page](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-formats) open while you practise. To check ratio answers quickly, use the [ratio calculator](/tools/ratio-calculator). If you want to see how Paper 2 scores combine with Paper 1 into a grade, the [0452 grade thresholds](/grade-thresholds/igcse-accounting-0452) and my [A\* guide](/blog/how-to-get-a-star-igcse-o-level-accounting) explain it. Paper 1 has its own method, which is on my [Paper 1 tips page](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-paper-1-mcq-tips).

## Practice questions

**Question 1.** Your exam started at 2:00. It is now 3:06. You have finished question 1 and question 2, and you are halfway through question 3. What should you do?

**Answer.** Question 3 should have ended at 3:00, so you are 6 minutes behind. Finish only the part you are on if it is nearly done, otherwise leave a few lines and start question 4 now. Come back to question 3 in the 5 minutes at the end.

**Question 2.** In part (b), a student worked out current assets of $18,400. The correct figure was $17,900. Current liabilities were $8,000. Part (c) asks for the current ratio to two decimal places. What should the student write, and can they earn the marks?

**Answer.** They should write: current ratio = 18,400 ÷ 8,000 = 2.30 : 1. The correct answer is 17,900 ÷ 8,000 = 2.24 : 1 (2.2375 rounded). With the working shown, the own figure rule lets part (c) earn its marks even though 2.30 : 1 is not the correct figure. Without the working, "2.30 : 1" alone would score nothing.

**Question 3.** Rewrite these lines from a student's answer so they would earn their label marks: "bbd 1,200", "COGS 29,400", "GP 18,600", "NP 7,250", "Current ratio 2.3".

**Answer.** "Balance b/d 1,200", "Cost of sales 29,400", "Gross profit 18,600", "Profit for the year 7,250" and "Current ratio 2.30 : 1". Each abbreviation here is one the examiner reports have said is not acceptable, and a ratio needs its unit.

## FAQ

### How long is IGCSE Accounting 0452 Paper 2?

It is 1 hour 45 minutes for 100 marks, with five compulsory questions of 20 marks each. It is worth 70% of the qualification. O Level 7707 Paper 2 has the same format.

### Is Accounting Paper 2 changing in 2027?

No. The 0452 and 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029 both say there are no changes to Paper 2. Of the two papers, only Paper 1 changes, to 40 questions in 1 hour 30 minutes.

### Can I use a calculator in Accounting Paper 2?

Yes. Both syllabuses say calculators may be used. Still write the working on paper, because a wrong answer with no working earns no marks.

### Which topics come up most in Paper 2?

On the 49 Paper 2 papers from 2022 to 2026 on this site, books of prime entry appeared in 44 and correction of errors in 40. Question 1 was tagged books of prime entry in 39 of them.

### If I get an early figure wrong, do I lose the whole question?

Usually not. The own figure rule means later parts that use your wrong figure with a correct method can still earn full marks, as long as your working is shown.

### How many advise questions are in Paper 2?

Usually three. The March 2025 and June 2025 reports both mention three five mark questions where you weigh up a situation and say what the business should do. Plan each one for a minute before you write.
