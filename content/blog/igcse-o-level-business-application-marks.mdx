You earn an application mark when you use a fact about the business in the question to support your point: a figure, a product, a customer group or a problem it faces. Use a different fact for each point, because the same fact is credited once. In IGCSE Business 0264 and O Level Business 7081, application is about 30% of each paper.

## What does application mean in 0264 and 7081?

Both syllabuses mark four skills, called assessment objectives (AOs). Application is AO2. The syllabus describes it as applying business knowledge "to a given business". In plain words: knowing what a bank loan is earns knowledge. Showing why a bank loan suits this business earns application.

| Skill | What you do | Share of 0264 and 7081 |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 Knowledge | Show you know the term or idea | 35% |
| AO2 Application | Use it for the business in the question | 30% |
| AO3 Analysis | Explain the effect, step by step | 22% |
| AO4 Evaluation | Make a justified decision | 13% |

The syllabus calls these weightings approximate. Paper 1 is 40% knowledge, 30% application, 20% analysis and 10% evaluation. Paper 2 is 30%, 30%, 25% and 15%. Each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 80 marks.

Application is the only skill that gained weight. Both new syllabuses say plainly that the weighting of application has increased and the weightings of the other three skills have decreased. You can see the full topic list in the [0264 syllabus checklist](/syllabus/igcse-business-0264) and the [7081 syllabus checklist](/syllabus/o-level-business-7081).

## Which questions carry application marks on Paper 1?

Paper 1 (Short Answer and Data Response) has four questions. Each one opens with a short description of a business, called the stem. In the 2027 specimen mark scheme, application marks sit in the (b), (c) and (d) parts that ask about that business.

| Part | Typical command | Marks | How they split |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Define | 2 | Knowledge only |
| (b) | Outline two | 4 | 2 knowledge, 2 application |
| (c) | Explain two | 6 | 2 knowledge, 2 application, 2 analysis |
| (d) | Do you think, or explain two, then justify | 8 | 2 of each skill |

Not every part follows the pattern. In the specimen, some (a) and (b) parts are calculations or "identify" questions with no application. Read the command word and the business name in the question. If the question names the business, it almost always wants application.

By my own count, 20 marks are labelled as application across ten parts of the specimen Paper 1. That is a quarter of the paper, a little under the syllabus's approximate 30%. Examiner reports on the old papers say most candidates miss many of these marks.

If you practise with old 0450 or 7115 papers, notice one difference. Old Paper 1 questions had five parts, and application sat mainly in parts (c) and (d). The new papers have four parts. The skill is the same.

## Which questions carry application marks on Paper 2?

Paper 2 is the case study. You get a separate insert about one business, with appendices of data. Every question is about that business.

**The (a) questions, 8 marks.** The syllabus says every part (a) on Paper 2 is now an applied question. In the specimen, you earn a mark for each advantage or benefit, then more marks for explaining it. The explanation must be applied to the case. In one specimen question, each explanation was worth one mark and had to be in context. In another, each explanation was worth up to three marks, and one of the three had to be applied.

**The (b) questions, 12 marks.** These are marked in levels, and every level description says "in context". In my judgement, an answer that could describe any business stays near the bottom, however good the theory. I cover these 12 mark recommendation questions in detail in [how to answer justify and discuss questions](/blog/igcse-business-0450-justify-evaluate-questions).

**Calculations and "state" parts.** These carry no application marks. Work them out and move on.

## Will my point score if it is not on the mark scheme?

Yes, if it is correct and relevant. The marking principles in the specimen mark schemes tell examiners to credit correct alternative answers that are not written in the mark scheme. Most answer lists end with an instruction to accept all valid responses.

The same is true of application. The mark scheme lists facts from the stem that could be used, then accepts other valid examples. But there is one condition, and it is the one students miss. The specimen guidance says the reference "must make sense in relation to the point being made". A fact dropped in for decoration does not count.

## How do I use the case in every point?

This is the method I teach. It works for any Outline, Explain or (d) question.

1. **Underline the facts in the stem.** Numbers, products, customers, location, type of business, objectives, problems. Aim for five or six.
2. **Pick your two points.** Knowledge first: what are the two advantages, reasons or factors?
3. **Give each point its own fact.** Match one underlined fact to each point. Cross it off once used.
4. **Write the point and the fact in one sentence.** The fact should explain why the point matters here.
5. **Add the "so what".** For Explain and (d) questions, follow the effect through: costs, revenue, profit, customers, workers.
6. **Swap test.** Replace the business with a different one in your head. If the sentence still works, it is not applied.

<Tip title="One fact, one point">
Before you write, put a small number next to each fact you underlined. Point 1 uses fact 1. Point 2 uses fact 2. Examiners can credit the same fact only once in a question, and this habit stops you reusing it without noticing.
</Tip>

### What counts as a fact from the case?

| Type of fact | Example from a made up stem |
|---|---|
| A number | 4 mechanics, 60% of revenue from repairs |
| The product or service | bicycle repairs and bicycle hire |
| The customers or location | students at the nearby university |
| The type of business | a sole trader |
| An objective or a problem | a new bike sharing scheme opened last month |

A name on its own is risky. "Zara's shop" shows you read the question, but it does not show why your point matters to this business. In my marking experience, a fact is always safer than a name.

Most students who reach this point understand the idea and still lose the marks in the exam room, because they write the point first and look for a fact afterwards. If that sounds like you, the rewrite below is the habit to copy.

## What does an applied answer look like?

This is my own question, written in the style of Paper 1.

> Zara is a sole trader. She runs a bicycle repair and hire shop near a large university. She employs 4 mechanics. 60% of her revenue comes from repairs. A new bike sharing scheme opened in the city last month. Zara plans to introduce an online booking system for repairs.
>
> Explain two advantages to Zara of introducing an online booking system. [6]

The mark scheme pattern for "Explain two" is one knowledge mark, one application mark and one analysis mark per advantage. So 3 + 3 = 6.

### The generic answer

> An online booking system saves time, so the business becomes more efficient. Customers can also book at any time, which is convenient for customers, so the business gets more sales.

How I would mark it against the pattern:

| Mark | Earned? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge, point 1 (saves time) | Yes | A valid advantage |
| Application, point 1 | No | Nothing about Zara's shop |
| Analysis, point 1 | Weak | "More efficient" is vague: efficient at what? |
| Knowledge, point 2 (book at any time) | Yes | A valid advantage |
| Application, point 2 | No | "Customers" fits every business |
| Analysis, point 2 | Possibly | "More sales" is a simple effect |

That is about 2 or 3 out of 6. Every sentence would work for a dentist, a hair salon or a cinema.

### The applied answer

> One advantage is that fewer phone calls need to be answered **[k]**, so Zara's 4 mechanics can spend more of the working day repairing bicycles **[app]**. This raises the number of repairs done each day without hiring more staff, which increases revenue while wages stay the same **[an]**.
>
> A second advantage is that customers can book a repair at any time, even when the shop is closed **[k]**. Students at the nearby university can book between classes **[app]**, so Zara can plan the week's work in advance and fill quiet days. This should win back repair customers who might otherwise switch to the new bike sharing scheme **[an]**.

Now count the facts. Point 1 uses "4 mechanics". Point 2 uses "students at the nearby university" and the bike sharing scheme. No fact is used twice. Each fact explains why the point matters here. I would give this answer 6 out of 6.

Notice what did not change. The two advantages are the same as in the generic answer. The knowledge was never the problem. The marks were lost in the link to the business and the follow through.

### What if it were a (d) question?

Suppose the question asked: "Do you think the advantages of an online booking system are greater than the disadvantages? Justify your answer." [8]

The specimen pattern gives 2 marks each for knowledge, application, analysis and a justified decision. Your two points (one advantage, one disadvantage) still need two different facts. Your decision needs a fact as well, and it should not just repeat earlier points. For example: the advantages are greater because 60% of Zara's revenue depends on repairs, so filling repair slots matters more than the cost of setting up the system.

## What do examiners say students get wrong?

These come from the 0450 and 7115 examiner reports, the papers that 0264 and 7081 replace. The skill has not changed, so the warnings still apply.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Using the same fact for both points, credited only once (0450 June 2025, 0450 March 2026, 7115 June 2023) | Give each point a different fact from the stem |
| Answers with no link to the business at all (0450 March 2026) | Put at least one fact into every point in a question that names the business |
| Using a word already in the question as the application, such as "factory" when the question was about the factory (0450 March 2026) | Find a fact the question did not hand you |
| Copying data from the case without comment, which earned nothing (7115 November 2022) | Say what the figure means for the business |
| Applying answers in a question with no application marks (0450 June 2025) | Check the command word. Definitions, calculations and "state" parts need no context |
| Repeating the knowledge point instead of developing it (0450 June 2025) | After the fact, explain the effect: costs, sales, profit, workers |
| Calling something easy with no explanation (0450 March 2026) | Say what is easier and why, for this business |

The June 2025 report put it simply: "Application does not have to be difficult". Any relevant fact that supports your point can earn the mark. The same report noted that candidates missed many of the 16 application marks on one Paper 1 variant.

## How much should I write for each mark?

Paper 1 gives you 90 minutes for 80 marks, so a little over a minute per mark. The times and lengths below are my own guide, not a Cambridge rule.

| Part | Marks | Rough time | Rough length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outline two | 4 | 4 to 5 minutes | Two sentences, one per point |
| Explain two | 6 | 6 to 7 minutes | Two short paragraphs |
| Do you think, justify | 8 | 9 to 10 minutes | Two paragraphs and a decision |

The syllabus tells candidates to use the marks as a guide to length. An Outline answer of two lines can earn full marks if each line has a point and a fact. Length does not earn application. Facts do.

## Practice questions with answers

Use this stem for all three. Try each one on paper first.

> Hassan Uniforms Ltd is a private limited company. It makes school uniforms and sells them to 35 schools. It employs 120 workers. 70% of its sales happen in July and August. The directors are considering buying automated cutting machines.

### Practice question 1

Outline two problems for Hassan Uniforms Ltd of making most of its sales in two months of the year. [4]

**Answer**

- Cash flow problems **[k]**, because 70% of the cash from sales arrives in July and August but costs are paid all year **[app]**.
- Workers may have little to do for much of the year **[k]**, which is a problem for a business paying 120 workers **[app]**.

Two points, two different facts: 70% of sales, and 120 workers.

### Practice question 2

Explain two advantages to Hassan Uniforms Ltd of buying automated cutting machines. [6]

**Answer**

- Machines can cut fabric faster than workers **[k]**, so the business can produce more uniforms before the July and August peak **[app]**. This means it can meet more orders when demand is highest, raising revenue **[an]**.
- Machines cut more accurately **[k]**, so uniforms ordered by 35 different schools are the same size and shape every time **[app]**. Fewer faulty uniforms means less wasted fabric, lowering costs, and schools are more likely to reorder **[an]**.

### Practice question 3

A student wrote this answer to Practice question 2. Mark it out of 6 and rewrite the weaker point.

> Machines are faster, so the business can make more uniforms. Machines also make fewer mistakes, so the business can make better uniforms.

**Answer**

How I would mark it: knowledge 2 (faster, fewer mistakes), application 1 (the word "uniforms" is used twice, so credited once), analysis 0 (more and better uniforms is not a developed effect). That is about 3 out of 6.

A better second point: Machines make fewer cutting mistakes **[k]**, which matters when supplying 35 schools that each expect the same uniform **[app]**. Fewer rejected uniforms means lower fabric costs and a better reputation with the schools **[an]**.

## FAQ

### What is application in IGCSE Business?

Application means using your business knowledge for the particular business in the question. You show it by linking each point to a fact from the stem or case, such as a figure, a product or a problem the business faces.

### Can I use the same fact from the case for two points?

You can, but it will only be credited once. All four 0450 and 7115 examiner reports I used for this article repeat the warning. Use a different fact for each point.

### Do definition questions need application?

No. In the specimen Paper 1, the 2 mark definitions are knowledge only. Adding the business name will not hurt, but it will not earn anything and it costs time.

### Is application worth more in 0264 and 7081 than in 0450?

Yes. Both new syllabuses state that the weighting of application has increased, to about 30%, and that knowledge, analysis and evaluation have all lost some weight.

### Do I have to quote the case word for word?

No. A short, relevant reference is enough, as long as it supports your point. Copying data without saying what it means earned no credit in the 7115 November 2022 report.

### Does the business name count as application?

On its own, it is a weak link, because it does not show why your point matters to this business. Use a fact from the case instead: a number, product, customer group or problem.
