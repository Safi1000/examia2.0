Yes, IGCSE Accounting 0452 changes for 2027, but the changes are small. Paper 1 grows from 35 to 40 multiple choice questions, with 15 more minutes. Two short topics are added: ethics, and technology and sustainability. The ratio list is updated and the international standards list is removed. Paper 2 does not change at all, so your written practice still counts.

Below is every change Cambridge lists, the full 2027 paper structure, the topic list, the ten ratios you must know, and a plan for getting ready. Everything comes from Cambridge's 0452 syllabus for 2027, 2028 and 2029. Where the syllabus does not say something, I say so rather than guess.

## Does 0452 change for 2027? The short answer

Cambridge says the syllabus "has been reviewed and revised for first examination in 2027". In plain terms:

- **The big one:** Paper 1 is longer. 40 questions and 40 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes.
- **New content:** 7.2 Ethical considerations and 7.3 Technology and sustainability.
- **Renamed:** Topic 7 is now "Accounting concepts and modern practice".
- **Updated:** the accounting ratios. The syllabus now prints ten ratios with their formulas.
- **Removed:** the international standards list.
- **Unchanged:** Paper 2, word for word. Five compulsory questions, 20 marks each, 1 hour 45 minutes.

If you have been studying from older notes, most of them still work. You need to add two short topics, rewrite your ratio sheet, and practise Paper 1 at the new length.

## The 2027 paper structure

All candidates take both papers. There are no options and no choice of questions.

| | Paper 1 Multiple Choice | Paper 2 Structured Written Paper |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 1 hour 30 minutes | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Marks | 40 | 100 |
| Weighting | 30% | 70% |
| Questions | 40 multiple choice questions, all compulsory | Five compulsory questions of 20 marks each |
| Question types | Based on all the subject content. Some questions need calculations | Stimulus material followed by tasks: calculations, completing a cash book, completing an incomplete statement of accounts, preparing accounts from information given, preparing suspense accounts and journal entries |
| Assessment objectives | AO1 and AO2 | AO1, AO2 and AO3 |
| Calculator | Allowed | Allowed |

Grades run from A\* to G. Exams are available in the June and November series, and in the March series in India.

### What the numbers mean in practice

- **Paper 1 time per question:** 90 minutes for 40 questions is 2 minutes 15 seconds each. The old paper gave about 2 minutes 9 seconds. A small gain, but useful for the calculation questions.
- **Each Paper 1 mark is worth a little less:** 30% over 40 marks is 0.75% of your final grade per mark. Cambridge lists no change to the 30% weighting, only to the number of questions and the time.
- **Paper 2 pacing:** 105 minutes for 100 marks is about a minute a mark, or 21 minutes per question. Keep to that and you will not run out of time on question 5.
- **Paper 2 is where the grade is decided.** It carries 70% of the qualification. Neat formats and correct adjustments matter more than anything else.

For Paper 1 technique, see my [Paper 1 MCQ tips for IGCSE and O Level Accounting](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-paper-1-mcq-tips).

## Assessment objectives and weightings

The objectives tell you what the examiner is rewarding.

- **AO1 Knowledge and understanding:** know the facts, terms, concepts, policies, procedures and techniques, and apply them to accounting situations, presenting the result in the right form.
- **AO2 Analysis:** select, calculate and interpret data, analyse financial information, and communicate the result in the most suitable form.
- **AO3 Evaluation:** interpret and evaluate accounting information and draw conclusions.

| Assessment objective | Whole qualification | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| AO1 Knowledge and understanding | 66% | 80% | 60% |
| AO2 Analysis | 24% | 20% | 25% |
| AO3 Evaluation | 10% | 0% | 15% |

Two things stand out. First, two thirds of the qualification rewards knowing the method and applying it correctly. That means the right format, the right headings and accurate arithmetic. Second, evaluation only appears on Paper 2, at 15%. Those are the "advise", "evaluate" and "justify" parts at the end of a question, where you make a decision using the figures.

### The command words for 2027

The list of command words has been updated. These are the ones the 2027 syllabus prints:

| Command word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Advise | Write down a suggested course of action in a given situation |
| Analyse | Examine in detail to show meaning, identify elements and the relationship between them |
| Calculate | Work out from given facts, figures or information |
| Compare | Identify or comment on similarities and/or differences |
| Define | Give precise meaning |
| Demonstrate | Show how or give an example |
| Describe | State the points of a topic, give characteristics and main features |
| Discuss | Write about issues or topics in depth in a structured way |
| Evaluate | Judge or calculate the quality, importance, amount or value of something |
| Explain | Set out purposes or reasons, make relationships clear, say why and/or how, with evidence |
| Give | Produce an answer from a given source or from memory |
| Identify | Name, select or recognise |
| Justify | Support a case with evidence or argument |

"Prepare" is the most common command word in Accounting. It means a full format with headings, not a list of figures.

## Topic-by-topic walkthrough

Cambridge lists what was added, renamed and removed. It does not list every reworded line. So below, for each topic, I separate what Cambridge confirms as a change from what the 2027 text simply asks of you.

### Topic 7: the biggest change

**Renamed.** Topic 7 is now "Accounting concepts and modern practice". The new introduction says it covers the main accounting concepts, the need to work within an ethical framework, and the role technology can play in modern accounting.

**7.1 Accounting concepts.** Ten concepts: business entity, consistency, duality, going concern, historic cost, matching / accruals, materiality, money measurement, prudence and realisation.

*What this means for you:* learn each concept with a one-line definition and one example of it in a question. "Prudence" and "matching" come up again and again in adjustments.

**7.2 Ethical considerations (new).** You need:

- the need for an ethical framework in accounting
- five fundamental principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, and professional behaviour
- why applying an ethical framework matters to stakeholders and to society

*What this means for you:* learn the five principles as a list, in that order, each with a short example. For example, confidentiality means not sharing a client's financial details. This is a compact topic and quick to learn.

**7.3 Technology and sustainability (new).** You need:

- the use of digital applications for all or part of the accounting records
- the need to store accounting data safely and sustainably, and the risks of not doing so
- the types of storage system: manual, data storage devices, cloud services and other digital services
- the advantages and disadvantages of each type

The syllabus adds that you do not need to know any specific accounting software.

*What this means for you:* make a small table. Four storage types down the side, advantages and disadvantages across the top. That one table covers most of what can be asked.

**Removed: the international standards list.** Cambridge confirms this list has gone from the syllabus.

*What this means for you:* you no longer need to learn a list of named international accounting standards. Note that 6.5 still lists "application of accounting policies" as a limitation of accounting statements, so keep that point.

### Topic 6: Analysis and interpretation

**6.1 Ratios (updated).** Cambridge says the accounting ratios have been updated. The full list is in the next section. The syllabus also says: "These are the only formulas accepted in candidate responses."

**6.2 Interpretation.** You must compare results across years, interpret the ratios, suggest ways to improve profitability, liquidity and working capital, and explain the difference between cash and profit. It also covers how gross profit is affected by inventory valuation, sales quantity and changes in buying and selling prices.

**6.3 Inter-business comparison.** The factors that may affect two businesses' ratios, and the problems of comparing them.

**6.4 Interested parties.** The 2027 list is: owners, managers, employees, banks, investors and lenders, suppliers, customers, governments / tax authorities, club members, and other parties such as public and environmental bodies.

**6.5 Limitations of accounting statements.** Historic cost, application of accounting policies, and non-financial aspects such as the skill of the workforce, the location of the business and the economic climate.

*What this means for you:* Topic 6 is where most AO3 evaluation marks sit. Practise writing a short judgement after every ratio: is it better or worse, why, and what should the owner do?

### Topic 5: Preparation of financial statements

Sole traders, partnerships, limited companies, manufacturing accounts, clubs and societies, and incomplete records. Cambridge does not list a content change here beyond general rewording and reordering.

The 2027 text keeps these limits, which save you revision time:

- **Partnerships:** no questions on dissolution, changes to a profit-sharing ratio or limited liability partnerships. No financial statements on the admission or departure of a partner.
- **Incomplete records:** sole traders only. No incomplete records questions on service or manufacturing businesses.

*What this means for you:* this topic is the heart of Paper 2. Know each format cold. My [IGCSE and O Level accounting formats guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-formats) lays them out, and my [partnership accounts guide](/blog/igcse-accounting-partnership-accounts) covers the appropriation account and current accounts.

### Topic 4: Accounting procedures

Capital and revenue items, depreciation (straight-line, reducing balance and revaluation methods) and disposals, accruals and prepayments, irrecoverable debts and the allowance for them, and inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value. No change is listed beyond rewording.

*What this means for you:* these are the year-end adjustments that appear inside almost every Paper 2 financial statements question. Practise them as journal entries and as ledger accounts. The [depreciation calculator](/tools/depreciation-calculator) is handy for checking your workings.

### Topic 3: Verification of accounting records

Trial balance, correction of errors, bank reconciliation and control accounts. No change is listed beyond rewording.

The 2027 text names six errors that do not affect the trial balance: commission, compensating, complete reversal, omission, original entry and principle. It also notes that you do not need to reconcile control account balances with the sales and purchases ledger balances.

*What this means for you:* Paper 2 tasks named in the syllabus include suspense accounts and journal entries, and both depend on this topic. My [trial balance worked example](/blog/igcse-accounting-trial-balance) is a good place to start.

### Topics 1 and 2: Fundamentals, sources and recording

The accounting equation, double entry, business documents and books of prime entry. No change is listed beyond rewording and reordering.

Several lines in the 2027 text mention digital methods: ledger accounts kept digitally, business documents produced manually or digitally, and the benefits and limitations of manual and digital methods for original entry. Topic 3 also asks about the impact of digital transactions on bank reconciliation and control accounts. Cambridge does not say which of these lines are new, so check them against your own notes. The text also says you do not need to explain or use folio columns.

*What this means for you:* be ready for a short written question on manual versus digital record-keeping. It links neatly to 7.3.

## The ten ratios you must know in 2027

The syllabus gives every formula in full. Learn them exactly as printed.

| Group | Ratio | Formula | Answer as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profitability | Gross profit margin | Gross profit ÷ Revenue × 100 | % |
| Profitability | Mark-up | Gross profit ÷ Cost of sales × 100 | % |
| Profitability | Profit margin | Profit for the year ÷ Revenue × 100 | % |
| Profitability | Return on capital employed (ROCE) | Profit for the year before interest ÷ Capital employed × 100 | % |
| Liquidity | Current (working capital) ratio | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities | Ratio, e.g. 1.8 : 1 |
| Liquidity | Acid test (liquid) ratio | (Current assets − Inventory) ÷ Current liabilities | Ratio |
| Efficiency | Rate of inventory turnover | Cost of sales ÷ Average inventory | Times |
| Efficiency | Inventory turnover | Average inventory ÷ Cost of sales × 365 | Days |
| Efficiency | Trade receivables turnover | Trade receivables ÷ Credit sales × 365 | Days |
| Efficiency | Trade payables turnover | Trade payables ÷ Credit purchases × 365 | Days |

Capital employed = issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.

5.6 also asks you to use mark-up, margin and inventory turnover to find missing figures in incomplete records questions. You can check your practice answers with the [ratio calculator](/tools/ratio-calculator).

<Tip>
For every ratio, write three lines: the formula, the figures put in, and the answer with its unit. It shows the examiner your method, and it stops you mixing up gross profit margin (over revenue) and mark-up (over cost of sales).
</Tip>

## Who is affected

- **Sitting 0452 in June or November 2027, 2028 or 2029** (or March, in India): you are on the new syllabus. This includes resits.
- **Sitting in November 2026:** you are still on the old syllabus. The changes in this article do not apply to you.
- **Started the course on the old syllabus but sitting in 2027:** the new syllabus applies. Cambridge's rule is to use the syllabus for the year the candidate takes the exam.
- **Private candidates** can enter for 0452.

### How 0452 relates to O Level 7707

O Level Accounting 7707 is the sister syllabus. Its 2027 changes follow the same pattern: a 40-question Paper 1 in 1 hour 30 minutes, an unchanged Paper 2, and the same two new topics. I cover it in my [O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus 2027 article](/blog/o-level-accounting-7707-syllabus-2027).

Two differences matter. 0452 grades run from A\* to G, while 7707 runs from A\* to E. And the 0452 syllabus says you cannot take 0452 in the same exam series as O Level 7707 or IGCSE (9–1) Accounting 0985. Cambridge treats IGCSE and O Level as the same level.

## A term-by-term prep plan for a 2027 candidate

This plan assumes you sit in June 2027 and are reading this in late 2026. If you sit in November 2027, stretch each block by a few months. If you are starting the course for 2028, use the same order across the full course.

### Term 1 (December to January): close the gaps

1. Print the 2027 topic list and tick off each subtopic you have finished.
2. Learn 7.2 and 7.3. They are short, and they are new, so older notes will not have them.
3. Rewrite your ratio sheet using the ten formulas above, word for word.
4. Rebuild your formats: statement of profit or loss, statement of financial position, appropriation account, statement of changes in equity, manufacturing account, income and expenditure account.

### Term 2 (February to March): past papers by topic

1. Do Paper 2 questions topic by topic, starting with Topic 5. Every past Paper 2 is still the right practice, because Paper 2 has not changed.
2. Mark each answer against the mark scheme. Note where you lost marks: format, adjustment or arithmetic.
3. Do Paper 1 questions in topic sets of 10 to 15, timed at about 2 minutes 15 seconds each.
4. Find questions by topic on the [0452 past papers page](/past-papers/igcse-accounting-0452).

### Term 3 (April to the exam): full papers under time

1. Do full Paper 2s in 1 hour 45 minutes. Aim for 21 minutes a question.
2. Build Paper 1 practice up to the new length: 40 questions in 90 minutes. Older papers have 35 questions, so add five questions from another paper to make a full set.
3. Use the specimen papers for the 2027 syllabus. Cambridge tells teachers to use the updated specimen materials alongside the syllabus. Ask your teacher for them.
4. Redo every question you got wrong in Terms 1 and 2.

### Which old past paper questions to skip

Only one area is confirmed as removed: the international standards list. Skip old questions that ask you to name or describe a specific international accounting standard. Everything else in older papers is still worth doing, but look out for:

- old Topic 7 questions on accounting policies: check them against 6.5 and 7.1 before spending time on them
- old ratio questions using a formula that differs from the 2027 list: do the question, but use the 2027 formula

Past papers will not test 7.2 or 7.3, because those topics are new. Use the specimen papers and your own short notes for those.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using the wrong ratio formula.** The syllabus accepts only its own formulas. Learn them as printed, including ROCE using profit before interest.
- **Practising Paper 1 at the old length.** Sets of 35 in 75 minutes will not prepare you for 40 in 90. Build your stamina to the full length.
- **Skipping 7.2 and 7.3 because they are short.** Both papers are based on all the subject content, so they can come up anywhere. They are also quick marks once learned.
- **Answering "prepare" questions without a format.** No title, no headings, figures in the wrong column. These lose AO1 marks even when the arithmetic is right.
- **Writing lists instead of decisions in evaluation parts.** For "advise" or "evaluate", make a clear choice and support it with figures from the question.
- **Mixing up the answer forms.** Margins, mark-up and ROCE are percentages. Current and acid test are ratios. Turnover ratios are times or days.
- **Revising excluded content.** Partnership dissolution, changes to profit-sharing ratios, and incomplete records for service or manufacturing businesses are not set.

## FAQ

### Does IGCSE Accounting 0452 change in 2027?

Yes. Paper 1 becomes 40 questions in 1 hour 30 minutes, two new topics are added (7.2 and 7.3), the ratios are updated and the international standards list is removed. Paper 2 does not change.

### Is the 2027 syllabus harder?

Not much. Paper 1 has five more questions but also 15 more minutes. The new topics are short lists you can learn quickly. Paper 2, worth 70%, is unchanged.

### Can I still use old past papers?

Yes. Every past Paper 2 is still the right practice. For Paper 1, the question style is the same, but practise at 40 questions in 90 minutes. Skip questions on named international accounting standards.

### My child started the course on the old syllabus. Does that matter?

What matters is the year of the exam. If your child sits in 2027 or later, the 2027 to 2029 syllabus applies. They need to add 7.2, 7.3 and the updated ratios to what they already know.

### Do I need to learn accounting software for 7.3?

No. The syllabus says candidates do not need knowledge of, or access to, any specific accounting software.

### How many ratios do I need for 0452 in 2027?

Ten: four profitability, two liquidity and four efficiency ratios. Use the formulas exactly as the syllabus prints them.

### What is the difference between 0452 and O Level 7707?

The papers and content are very similar for 2027. The main differences are the grade range (A\* to G for 0452, A\* to E for 7707) and that you cannot take both in the same exam series.

### Has the weighting of Paper 1 and Paper 2 changed?

No. Paper 1 is 30% and Paper 2 is 70%. Cambridge lists changes only to Paper 1's number of questions and its time.
