An accrual is an amount that belongs to this year but has not been paid or received yet. A prepayment is an amount paid or received now that belongs to next year. For IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 you adjust every expense and income to the amount for the year. Accrued expenses are other payables. Prepaid expenses and accrued income are other receivables.

## What does the syllabus say about accruals and prepayments?

This is section 4.3, "Other payables and other receivables", in the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452) and the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. You need to understand:

1. Why costs and revenues must be matched to the right year.
2. How accruals and prepayments put the matching (accruals) concept into practice.
3. Journal entries and ledger accounts for expenses that are accrued or prepaid.
4. Journal entries and ledger accounts for income that is accrued or prepaid.

Section 5.1 then asks you to make these adjustments when you prepare financial statements. Matching and accruals is also one of the concepts listed in section 7.1.

### Which names do past papers use?

The syllabus heading says other payables and other receivables. Its bullet points say accruals, prepayments, accrued and prepaid.

The 0452 and 7707 Paper 2 questions from 2022 to 2026 mostly describe the item in plain words: rent "owing", insurance "prepaid", income "in advance". Some newer papers also use the labels. The November 2023 7707 Paper 23 listed rent prepaid as "Other receivables". Some Paper 1 questions use "accrued income" and "prepaid income".

The mark schemes use the labels in the statement of financial position. The November 2022 Paper 23 scheme put insurance prepaid and commission accrued under other receivables. The March 2025 Paper 22 scheme listed other payables, then income prepaid as a separate line. The June 2026 Paper 22 scheme also accepted prepayments and accruals as labels.

Past papers say "income statement". The 2027 to 2029 syllabus says statement of profit or loss. The entries are the same. I use "income statement" below because that is what you will practise on.

## What are the four types of accrual and prepayment?

Every adjustment is one of four. Learn them as a pair of pairs: expenses and income, each either late or early.

| Item | What it means | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Expense for this year not yet paid | Other payables, current liability |
| Prepaid expense | Expense paid this year for next year | Other receivables, current asset |
| Accrued income | Income for this year not yet received | Other receivables, current asset |
| Income received in advance | Income received this year for next year | Current liability, on its own line |

Ask one question: who owes whom? If the business owes someone, it is a liability. If someone owes the business, or the business has paid for something it has not used yet, it is an asset.

The June 2022 0452 report explained a multiple choice question this way. Loan interest owed to a trader was accrued income. It added to profit, and because the trader was owed it, it was a current asset.

## Why do we adjust? The matching principle

The income statement must show the expenses and income **for the year**, not the cash that happened to move in the year. Rent for June is an expense of June even if you pay it in July. That is the matching principle, also called accruals.

Without the adjustment, profit is wrong. So are the ratios built on it, and any decision based on them.

Two kinds of question test this, and they need different answers:

- **"Explain why matching is important."** In November 2022 Paper 23, many candidates gave a definition instead. The mark scheme rewarded consequences. In my words: the year's income and expenses are shown at their true amounts, so the profit is right and the ratios and decisions built on it can be trusted.
- **"State how matching is applied to this account."** The November 2023 7707 Paper 23 report also found many definitions. The scheme wanted the application: only the rent for the current year goes to the income statement.

In both cases, do not stop at the definition. Say what the principle does to the figures in front of you.

## How do you work out the figure for the income statement?

Start with the cash, then adjust for each balance. This is how I teach it.

| Expense | Income |
|---|---|
| Cash paid in the year | Cash received in the year |
| Less accrued at the start | Less accrued at the start |
| Add accrued at the end | Add accrued at the end |
| Add prepaid at the start | Add received in advance at the start |
| Less prepaid at the end | Less received in advance at the end |

The logic is the same each time. An opening accrual was last year's cost, paid this year, so take it out. A closing accrual is this year's cost, not paid yet, so put it in. Prepayments work the other way round.

Examiners also work these questions backwards. In June 2023, the 7707 Paper 11 report set out rent received as the rent due for the year less the opening prepayment less the rent still owed. Same logic, different unknown.

When the yearly amount is fixed, such as rent at so much a month, I teach students to draw a twelve month timeline. Mark which months were paid, when, and which are still owing. Then the charge for the year is simply twelve months' worth.

<Tip title="Due for the year, not cash for the year">
In a March 2022 0452 multiple choice question on club subscriptions, the report suggested some candidates wrongly adjusted for members who had not paid and members who paid early. The figure needed was what all members owed for that year. Always ask what belongs to the year first.
</Tip>

## How do you record accruals and prepayments in the ledger?

Each expense or income has its own account. During the year, payments and receipts go in from the cash book. At the year end you do two things:

1. Transfer the amount for the year to the income statement.
2. Carry down whatever is left as a balance, which becomes the accrual or prepayment.

In the Paper 2 mark schemes I checked, the accrual or prepayment stays in the same account as a balance. It is not moved to a separate accrual account.

### Which side does each balance go on?

| Balance at the year end | Brought down on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Credit side | The business owes it: a liability |
| Prepaid expense | Debit side | The business has paid ahead: an asset |
| Accrued income | Debit side | Someone owes the business: an asset |
| Income received in advance | Credit side | The business owes the service: a liability |

In an expense account, payments are on the debit side and the transfer to the income statement is on the credit side. An income account is the mirror image: receipts on the credit side, the transfer on the debit side.

### Which journal entries do you need?

The syllabus asks for journal entries as well as ledger accounts. I did not find a journal for this topic in the Paper 2 mark schemes I checked, so this is how I teach it: the year end transfers go through the general journal.

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer the expense for the year | Income statement | Expense account, for example rent payable |
| Transfer the income for the year | Income account, for example rent receivable | Income statement |

Add a narrative to each one, for example "Rent payable for the year transferred to the income statement". The cash payments and receipts during the year are recorded in the cash book, not the journal.

This is where students get stuck. The calculation is easy, but the account has six or seven lines, two of them balances, and each one has to be on the right side with the right label. One reversed line loses marks all the way through to the statement of financial position.

## A worked example: rent payable and rent receivable

Sana runs a tailoring shop. Her year ends on 30 June. She rents the shop for $750 a month and sublets a back room to a tenant for $350 a month. All figures are my own.

| Date | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 July 2025 | July 2025 rent for the shop had been paid in advance, $750 |
| 1 July 2025 | The tenant had paid July 2025 rent in advance, $350 |
| 2 September 2025 | Paid shop rent by bank, $2 250 |
| 4 October 2025 | Received rent from the tenant by bank, $1 400 |
| 1 December 2025 | Paid shop rent by bank, $2 250 |
| 6 February 2026 | Received rent from the tenant by bank, $1 400 |
| 3 March 2026 | Paid shop rent by bank, $2 250 |

Prepare the rent payable account and the rent receivable account for the year ended 30 June 2026. Show the journal entries for the year end transfers, and the extracts from the income statement and the statement of financial position.

### Step 1: what belongs to the year?

Shop rent for the year is 12 × 750 = $9 000. Rent from the tenant for the year is 12 × 350 = $4 200.

| Shop rent | $ |
|---|---:|
| July, paid last year | 750 |
| August to April, paid this year (3 × 2 250) | 6 750 |
| May and June, not yet paid | 1 500 |
| **Rent payable for the year** | **9 000** |

| Rent from the tenant | $ |
|---|---:|
| July, received last year | 350 |
| August to March, received this year (2 × 1 400) | 2 800 |
| April to June, not yet received | 1 050 |
| **Rent receivable for the year** | **4 200** |

Check with the method from the earlier table. Rent payable: 6 750 + 750 + 1 500 = $9 000. Rent receivable: 2 800 + 350 + 1 050 = $4 200.

At 30 June 2026, Sana owes $1 500 of shop rent. That is an accrued expense. The tenant owes Sana $1 050. That is accrued income.

### Step 2: the ledger accounts

**Sana: Rent payable account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2025 Jul 1 Balance b/d | 750 | 2026 Jun 30 Income statement | 9 000 |
| Sep 2 Bank | 2 250 | | |
| Dec 1 Bank | 2 250 | | |
| 2026 Mar 3 Bank | 2 250 | | |
| Jun 30 Balance c/d | 1 500 | | |
| | **9 000** | | **9 000** |
| | | 2026 Jul 1 Balance b/d | 1 500 |

The account opens with a debit balance, a prepayment, and closes with a credit balance, an accrual. Debit side: 750 + 2 250 + 2 250 + 2 250 + 1 500 = $9 000.

**Sana: Rent receivable account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Jun 30 Income statement | 4 200 | 2025 Jul 1 Balance b/d | 350 |
| | | Oct 4 Bank | 1 400 |
| | | 2026 Feb 6 Bank | 1 400 |
| | | Jun 30 Balance c/d | 1 050 |
| | **4 200** | | **4 200** |
| 2026 Jul 1 Balance b/d | 1 050 | | |

This one opens with a credit balance, income received in advance, and closes with a debit balance, accrued income. Credit side: 350 + 1 400 + 1 400 + 1 050 = $4 200.

### Step 3: the journal entries

| Date | Account | Debit ($) | Credit ($) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2026 Jun 30 | Income statement | 9 000 | |
| | Rent payable | | 9 000 |
| Jun 30 | Rent receivable | 4 200 | |
| | Income statement | | 4 200 |

Narratives, in order: "Rent payable for the year transferred to the income statement" and "Rent receivable for the year transferred to the income statement".

### Step 4: the financial statements

**Sana: Extract from the income statement for the year ended 30 June 2026**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Other income: rent receivable | 4 200 |
| Expenses: rent payable | 9 000 |

Rent receivable is other income, added to gross profit. It is not part of revenue.

**Sana: Extract from the statement of financial position at 30 June 2026**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Current assets | |
| Other receivables (rent receivable accrued) | 1 050 |
| Current liabilities | |
| Other payables (rent payable accrued) | 1 500 |

Notice the gap between cash and the true figures. Sana paid $6 750 in shop rent but the expense is $9 000. She received $2 800 from the tenant but earned $4 200.

## How are accruals and prepayments marked?

I checked five Paper 2 mark schemes where this topic was a whole question part.

| Paper | Part | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 0452 November 2022, variant 23 | Insurance account and commission receivable account | 8 |
| | Statement of financial position extract | 2 |
| | Why matching is important | 3 |
| 0452 March 2025, variant 22 | Current liabilities section | 4 |
| 0452 June 2025, variant 21 | Rental income account | 4 |
| 0452 June 2025, variant 22 | Rent and rates account | 6 |
| | Principle behind the prepaid rent adjustment | 1 |
| 7707 November 2023, variant 23 | Rent payable account | 6 |
| | How matching applies to the rent account | 1 |

What the marks were for, inside the accounts:

- Each opening balance and each bank entry usually earned a mark. In November 2022 and June 2025 variant 22, the transfer to the income statement was marked as an own figure, so a correct method still scored after a slip.
- The balance brought down after the year end earned a mark. November 2022 gave one mark for both accounts together; June 2025 variant 22 gave one for each. None of these schemes gave a mark for the balance carried down.
- The June 2025 variant 21 scheme gave one mark for the two receipts from the tenant together.
- The 7707 November 2023 scheme gave a separate mark for dates.
- In the November 2022 extract, one mark was for the "Current assets" heading. The scheme accepted either one line of other receivables or the two items listed separately.

In the March 2025 current liabilities section, other payables, income prepaid and the bank overdraft each had a mark, with a further own figure mark for the total.

<Tip title="Finish the account">
The June 2025 Paper 22 report made this point: even if the question tells you the closing figures, full marks still need those balances brought down on the first day of the new year. Always write the balance b/d line.
</Tip>

## Where else do accruals and prepayments appear?

This topic rarely stays in its own question. Expect it in these places.

**Full income statements.** Accruals and prepayments are part of the usual adjustments. The June 2022 report (0452 variants 22 and 23, and 7707 variant 22) said that, on the whole, the expenses with an accrual or a prepayment were handled correctly. The November 2022 variant 23 report said most candidates adjusted the expenses correctly for a service business. But in March 2025 variant 22, wrong adjustments for accruals and prepayments were among the errors that occurred regularly. My [income statement format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format) shows where every adjustment sits.

**Effect on profit.** Multiple choice questions ask what happens when an adjustment is made or missed. On November 2022 Paper 12, the 0452 report said candidates were unsure about this. Its answer: a prepayment adjustment makes the expense smaller, so profit goes up, and current assets go up too. The June 2022 report worked through a question where accrued commission and a prepaid expense had both been left out, so the profit was understated.

| Adjustment left out | Profit for the year | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Accrued expense | Overstated | Current liabilities understated |
| Prepaid expense | Understated | Current assets understated |
| Accrued income | Understated | Current assets understated |
| Income received in advance | Overstated | Current liabilities understated |

**Two items in one account.** The June 2025 variant 22 question combined rent and rates in one account. Rent was prepaid and rates were unpaid, so the account had a balance on **both** sides at the start and at the end. The scheme gave a mark for each of the four balances brought down. Treat each balance on its own and put it on its own side.

**Clubs and societies.** Subscriptions in arrears are accrued income and subscriptions in advance are income received in advance. The March 2025 variant 22 report listed wrong classification of subscriptions and expenses prepaid and accrued as a common error when calculating the accumulated fund. The June 2023 report (0452 variants 22 and 23, and 7707 variant 22) said opening balances in the subscriptions account were often on the wrong side or labelled as accruals and prepayments instead of balances. My [clubs and societies guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-clubs-and-societies) covers the subscriptions account.

**The statement of financial position.** Other receivables sit in current assets after trade receivables. Other payables sit in current liabilities after trade payables. The [statement of financial position format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-statement-of-financial-position-format) shows the full layout.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the examiner reports. Reports marked 7707 are O Level; the rest are 0452.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Rental income added to sales or taken off expenses (March 2025, Paper 22) | Show it as other income below gross profit |
| Accrued expenses left out or labelled wrongly; income received in advance left out, added into other payables or labelled wrongly (March 2025, Paper 22) | Show accrued expenses as other payables and income received in advance on its own labelled line, as that scheme did |
| Commission receivable owing shown as a current liability (November 2022, Paper 23) | Accrued income is a current asset, part of other receivables |
| Prepaid insurance left out of current assets (November 2023 7707, Paper 22) | List it with the other current assets |
| Wrong details in the account: words like owing or accrual where the balance belongs, or a description of the payment where Bank belongs (November 2022, Paper 23); accrued b/d or prepayments b/d as labels (June 2025, Paper 22) | Use Bank, Income statement, Balance c/d and Balance b/d |
| Rental income account fully reversed, opening rent accrued left out or put on the credit side, tenant's name written for Bank (June 2025, Paper 21) | Receipts on the credit side; opening accrued income on the debit side |
| Two payments shown as one entry, dates left out, "rent charge" written for income statement (November 2023 7707, Paper 23) | One line per payment, with its own date |
| A definition of matching given when the question asked why it matters or how it is applied (November 2022, Paper 23; November 2023 7707, Paper 23) | Apply it to the figures in the question |

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper first. All figures are my own.

### Practice question 1

Kiran's year ends on 31 March. At 1 April 2025, $265 was owing for electricity. During the year Kiran paid $3 180 for electricity. At 31 March 2026, $340 was owing. Calculate the electricity expense for the year and state how the closing amount appears in the statement of financial position.

**Answer**

Electricity for the year: 3 180 − 265 + 340 = $3 255.

The $340 owing is an accrued expense. It appears as other payables of $340 under current liabilities.

### Practice question 2

Divya earns commission from a supplier. Her year ends on 31 December. At 1 January 2025, $150 of commission had been received in advance. During 2025 she received $1 870 by bank. At 31 December 2025, $420 of commission was owing to her. Prepare the commission receivable account for the year ended 31 December 2025. Balance the account and bring down the balance.

**Answer**

Commission for the year: 1 870 + 150 + 420 = $2 440.

**Divya: Commission receivable account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2025 Dec 31 Income statement | 2 440 | 2025 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 150 |
| | | Dec 31 Bank | 1 870 |
| | | Dec 31 Balance c/d | 420 |
| | **2 440** | | **2 440** |
| 2026 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 420 | | |

The $420 debit balance is accrued income, shown under other receivables.

### Practice question 3

Omar's draft profit for the year ended 30 September 2026 was $18 450. No adjustment had been made for these items at 30 September 2026:

1. Insurance prepaid, $320.
2. Wages accrued, $610.
3. Interest receivable owing to Omar, $85.

Calculate the corrected profit for the year. State the totals for other receivables and other payables.

**Answer**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Draft profit | 18 450 |
| Add insurance prepaid | 320 |
| Less wages accrued | 610 |
| Add interest receivable accrued | 85 |
| **Corrected profit** | **18 245** |

Other receivables: 320 + 85 = $405. Other payables: $610.

## FAQ

### Is an accrued expense an asset or a liability?

A liability. The business owes the money, so it appears under current liabilities as other payables.

### Is a prepaid expense an asset?

Yes. The business has paid for something it has not used yet, so it is a current asset, shown as other receivables.

### Where does accrued income go?

In the income statement it is part of that income for the year. In the statement of financial position it is a current asset, under other receivables.

### Is income received in advance a liability?

Yes. The business has been paid for something it has not provided yet. The March 2025 Paper 22 scheme, the only one I found with this item, showed it under current liabilities on its own labelled line.

### What is the difference between accruals and other payables?

An accrual is an expense owed at the year end. Other payables is the label for accrued expenses in the statement of financial position, separate from trade payables to suppliers.

### Do I need to bring down the balance on an expense account?

Yes. In the mark schemes I checked, the balance brought down on the first day of the next year earned a mark. The June 2025 Paper 22 report said this applies even when the question gives the closing figure.
