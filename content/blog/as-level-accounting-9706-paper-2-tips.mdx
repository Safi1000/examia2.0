AS Level Accounting 9706 Paper 2 gives you 1 hour 45 minutes for four compulsory structured questions worth 90 marks: two of 30 and two of 15. Marks are given point by point, and the own figure rule protects later parts when you show working. My advice: about 1.1 minutes a mark, full labels, net figures and a clear decision in every advise answer.

## What is in 9706 Paper 2?

The [9706 syllabus for 2026 to 2028](/syllabus/a-level-accounting-9706) sets out Paper 2, called Fundamentals of Accounting, like this.

| Paper 2 fact | What the syllabus says |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 hour 45 minutes |
| Marks | 90 |
| Questions | 4 structured questions, all compulsory, answered on the question paper |
| Question 1 | Financial accounting, 30 marks |
| Questions 2 and 3 | Financial accounting, 15 marks each |
| Question 4 | Cost and management accounting, 30 marks |
| Share of the grade | 72% of the AS Level, 36% of the full A Level |
| Calculators | The syllabus calls them essential |

The questions use AS Level content only, from sections 1 and 2 of the syllabus. Paper 1 is the other AS paper: 30 multiple choice questions for the remaining 28%. My [9706 Paper 1 guide](/blog/a-level-accounting-9706-paper-1-mcq-tips) covers that one.

The syllabus also splits Paper 2 by skill. 60% of the marks test knowledge and understanding, 23% test analysis and 17% test evaluation, which means judging information and making a recommendation. Paper 1 has no evaluation at all, so every judgement mark at AS Level sits in Paper 2.

Three more rules from the syllabus decide marks on this paper:

- **Ratio formulae.** Use the formulae in the appendix to section 3 of the syllabus. No other version is accepted.
- **Terminology.** You are expected to use international accounting terms and formats.
- **Recommendations.** When asked for a decision, you need reasons on both sides and a justified conclusion.

## What topics come up in each question?

Any AS topic can appear. Still, the examiner reports describe what each question covered, and the pattern is useful. These are the Paper 22 variants, taken from the reports and put in my words. The report for March 2026 does not name the topics, so that row comes from its mark scheme.

| Session (Paper 22) | Question 1 (30 marks) | Questions 2 and 3 (15 each) | Question 4 (30 marks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 2022 | Incomplete records of a sole trader | Limited company equity; partnership | Absorption and marginal costing, budgeting |
| June 2022 | Partnership accounts | Non-current assets; ratios | Absorption and marginal costing |
| November 2022 | Limited company accounts | Sales ledger control account; ratios | Marginal costing and break-even |
| March 2023 | Partnership accounts | Depreciation and capital expenditure; ratios | Marginal costing |
| June 2023 | Limited company accounts | Bank reconciliation; incomplete records | Absorption and marginal costing |
| November 2023 | Limited company accounts | Non-current assets; control accounts | Absorption and marginal costing |
| March 2025 | Partnership accounts | Sales ledger control account; limited company equity | Overheads, inventory valuation, limiting factor |
| March 2026 | Sole trader without full records | Non-current assets; limited company equity | Marginal costing |

Question 1 is usually a main financial statement for one kind of business, with short written parts and often an advise part. In all eight sessions, Question 4 included overheads, marginal costing or both, and each time it had an advise part. If limited company statements are your weak area, work through my [9706 limited company accounts guide](/blog/a-level-accounting-9706-limited-company-accounts) first, because they turn up in Question 1 and in Questions 2 and 3.

These are patterns from eight sessions, not promises. You must answer all four questions, so every AS topic needs to be ready.

## How long should each question take?

Cambridge sets no time per question. Here is my advice, worked out from the syllabus figures. 105 minutes for 90 marks is about 1.17 minutes a mark. I round that down to 1.1 minutes a mark and trim each 15 mark question from 16.5 to 16 minutes. That leaves 7 minutes spare for checking.

| Time from the start | What you do | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 33 minutes | Question 1 | 30 |
| 33 to 49 minutes | Question 2 | 15 |
| 49 to 65 minutes | Question 3 | 15 |
| 65 to 98 minutes | Question 4 | 30 |
| 98 to 105 minutes | Check totals, labels and anything left blank | |

Write the finishing times on your question paper before you start. If the exam begins at 9:00, they are 9:33, 9:49, 10:05, 10:38 and 10:45.

Why protect Question 4? Because in all eight of those sessions it included an advise part, worth 5 or 7 marks in the schemes I checked, and those are easy marks to lose by running out of time. The reports disagree on how often this happens. The March 2025 report said the final advise task was well answered, and read that as a sign that few candidates were short of time. The March 2026 report said some candidates left the final task unfinished, and suggested time was the reason. Both are about Paper 22 in those sessions only.

### How do I split the time inside a question?

This is how I teach it:

- **State or define parts** (1 or 2 marks): under a minute a mark. Write the point and stop.
- **Explain parts** (2 to 4 marks): a minute a mark. Every point needs a second sentence that develops it.
- **Financial statements** (the largest parts, 14 marks in March 2026 Paper 22): a little over a minute a mark. Workings first, then the statement.
- **Advise parts** (5 or 7 marks): 1 minute to plan in the margin, then write.

If a part runs over, leave a few lines and move on. Come back in the last 7 minutes.

## How is Paper 2 marked?

The mark schemes use point marking. Each correct figure, label or step earns its own mark, and marks are never taken away for errors. The March 2026 Paper 22 mark scheme sets out the general rules on its opening pages, and these are the ones that matter most for calculations.

| Rule in the mark scheme | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| A correct answer earns full credit without working, unless the question asks for working | Working is not always required, but it is your insurance |
| A different valid method earns the same marks at the same stages | You do not have to copy the textbook method |
| Own figure rule: a wrong earlier figure, used with a correct and complete method, can still earn full marks | One early slip need not wipe out the rest of a question |

The examiner reports add the other half of the picture. The November 2022 Paper 22 report said workings will always earn credit when valid, but "in their absence, candidates will not get any credit". The June 2022 and November 2023 Paper 21 reports said the same. My reading of the two together: a correct answer alone is fine, but a wrong answer alone is worth nothing, because the examiner cannot see a method to reward.

### What do OF, W and CF mean in a mark scheme?

The March 2026 Paper 22 scheme lists its own shorthand. In my words:

| Mark scheme note | What it means |
|---|---|
| OF | Own figure. The mark is given if you used your own earlier figure correctly |
| W | Working. The calculation is shown under the answer, and marks can sit inside it |
| CF | Correct figure. Only the exact right figure earns this mark |
| fb | One mark that needs both figures |
| row | Every figure in the row must be right for the mark |

The same page says marks for figures depend on the correct sign or direction. So a correct number entered on the wrong side, or added when it should be taken away, does not score.

Look at how a statement is marked in that scheme. In Question 1(b), a 14 mark statement of profit or loss, the gross profit mark and the profit for the year mark were both OF marks. Several expenses carried 2 marks: one for a step in the working and one for the final figure. A candidate with one wrong expense could still earn the profit for the year mark on their own figures.

### How are the advise questions marked?

Every scheme I checked gave the decision its own single mark, with the rest for the points you make. The split, though, changes from scheme to scheme.

| Mark scheme | Part and marks | How the marks split |
|---|---|---|
| Specimen Paper 2 (2023) | 1(d)(ii), 5 marks | Up to 3 per option, 4 at most for both, 1 for the decision |
| Specimen Paper 2 (2023) | 4(d) and 4(e), 5 marks each | 2 for, 2 against, 1 for the decision |
| June 2024 Paper 22 | 1(e), 7 marks | Up to 3 for points, up to 3 for developing them, 1 for the advice |
| March 2026 Paper 22 | 1(e), 7 marks | Up to 3 advantages, up to 3 disadvantages, 1 for the decision |
| June 2025 Paper 21 | 1(d), 5 marks | Up to 2 per option, 1 for the decision |

So the safe habit is the same everywhere: points on both sides, each tied to the business, then one decision with a reason. The syllabus asks for exactly this balance. One more detail is worth knowing. In the March 2026 Paper 22 scheme, Question 4(e) gave a point about which option made more profit as an OF mark. Even your evaluation can be credited on your own figures.

## Worked example: how does the own figure rule save marks?

Here is a short question I have written in the style of Question 4. The marks next to each part, and the way I mark it below, are my own scheme, laid out the way the Cambridge schemes do it.

**Kiran Limited makes ceramic lamps.** Each lamp sells for $48. Variable costs per lamp are direct materials $14, direct labour $9 and variable overheads $3. Sales staff earn commission of 5% of the selling price. Fixed costs are $27,600 a month. Budgeted sales are 2,000 lamps a month.

| Part | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Calculate the contribution per lamp | 3 |
| (b) | Calculate the monthly break-even point in units | 2 |
| (c) | Calculate the margin of safety in units | 1 |
| (d) | Calculate the budgeted profit for one month | 2 |
| (e) | The directors plan a 10% price cut, expecting sales of 2,400 lamps a month. Calculate the new monthly profit | 2 |
| (f) | Advise the directors whether they should cut the price. Justify your answer | 5 |
| | **Total** | **15** |

### The full answer

**(a)** Commission = 5% × $48 = $2.40 (1). Variable cost = $14 + $9 + $3 + $2.40 = $28.40 (1). Contribution = $48 less $28.40 = **$19.60** (1) OF.

**(b)** Break-even = $27,600 ÷ $19.60 (1) OF = 1,408.16, rounded up to **1,409 lamps** (1) OF. You round up because 1,408 lamps would still leave a small loss.

**(c)** Margin of safety = 2,000 less 1,409 = **591 lamps** (1) OF.

**(d)** Total contribution = 2,000 × $19.60 = $39,200 (1) OF. Profit = $39,200 less $27,600 = **$11,600** (1) OF.

**(e)** New price = $48 less 10% = $43.20. Commission = 5% × $43.20 = $2.16 (1). Variable cost = $26.00 + $2.16 = $28.16. Contribution = $43.20 less $28.16 = **$15.04**. Profit = 2,400 × $15.04 = $36,096, less $27,600 = **$8,496** (1) OF.

Checks: 14 + 9 + 3 + 2.40 = 28.40. Then 19.60 × 1,409 = 27,616.40, just above the fixed costs, while 19.60 × 1,408 = 27,596.80 is just below. Then 39,200 less 27,600 = 11,600, and 36,096 less 27,600 = 8,496.

### The same question with an early slip

Now take a student who forgets the commission in part (a). It is a real kind of slip. The November 2022 Paper 22 report said several candidates did not treat a sales commission as a variable cost.

| Part | Correct answer | Student's answer, working shown | My marks for the student |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | $19.60 | Variable cost $26.00, contribution $48 less $26 = $22.00 | 1 of 3 (the OF contribution mark) |
| (b) | 1,409 lamps | $27,600 ÷ $22.00 = 1,254.55, so 1,255 lamps | 2 of 2 (OF) |
| (c) | 591 lamps | 2,000 less 1,255 = 745 lamps | 1 of 1 (OF) |
| (d) | $11,600 | 2,000 × $22 = $44,000, less $27,600 = $16,400 | 2 of 2 (OF) |
| (e) | $8,496 | Contribution $43.20 less $26 = $17.20; 2,400 × $17.20 = $41,280, less $27,600 = $13,680 | 1 of 2 (OF profit mark only) |

The student scores 7 of the 10 calculation marks with one wrong idea. In part (e) my scheme gives the first mark for working out the commission on the new price. That is a fresh step, not a figure carried forward, and the student leaves it out, so I give no mark. This is my judgement as the setter, not a Cambridge rule. A real scheme shows which marks are OF, and it may be more generous. The profit mark is still OF, because the step from their contribution to their profit is correct.

Now picture the same student writing only the answers: "1,255", "745", "$16,400", "$13,680". Each is wrong, and nothing shows a method, so those parts risk earning nothing. With working, the slip costs 3 of the 10 calculation marks. With bare answers, it can cost all 10.

<Tip title="One line of working above every answer">
Write the formula with your own numbers in it on the line above each final figure, and finish with one net figure. That line is what lets the examiner give you an OF mark.
</Tip>

### Part (f): a 5 mark advise answer

I plan for one minute in the margin, using the figures I have just worked out:

- **For the cut:** sales rise from 2,000 to 2,400 lamps, and a lower price may win customers from rivals, which could help sales of other lamps later.
- **Against the cut:** monthly profit falls from $11,600 to $8,496, a drop of $3,104, because contribution per lamp falls from $19.60 to $15.04. The new break-even is $27,600 ÷ $15.04 = 1,835.1, so 1,836 lamps. The margin of safety becomes 2,400 less 1,836 = 564 lamps, down from 591, so the business is closer to a loss.
- **Decision:** do not cut the price, because profit falls by $3,104 a month and risk rises. Rivals may also cut their prices, so the extra 400 lamps a month are not certain.

On my scheme that earns 2 for, 2 against and 1 for a decision with a reason. Notice that every point uses Kiran Limited's own figures. A general paragraph about "price cuts increase sales" would fit any question and earn very little.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong in Paper 2?

Every row comes from the Paper 2 sections of the 9706 examiner reports from March 2022 to March 2026. Each row names the sessions and variants where I found the comment.

| What goes wrong | Where the reports say it |
|---|---|
| A wrong answer with no working, so nothing can be credited | June 2022 Paper 21, November 2022 Papers 22 and 23, November 2023 Papers 21 and 22 |
| A calculation written as a bracket of several figures with no single net figure, so no calculation mark | June 2022 Paper 23, November 2022 Paper 21, June 2023 Paper 23 |
| Abbreviated labels such as COS, or old terms such as net profit instead of profit for the year | June 2023 Paper 22, November 2023 Paper 22, March 2023 Paper 22 |
| Ledger labels that describe the transaction instead of naming the other account (receipts from customers instead of bank) | June 2023 Paper 21, March 2025 Paper 22, March 2026 Paper 22 |
| Missing dates, missing labels or no balance carried down in ledger accounts | March 2023 Paper 22, November 2023 Paper 22, March 2025 Paper 22 |
| Instructions ignored: rates not to two decimal places, no per machine hour or per labour hour basis, break-even not rounded up | March 2022 Paper 22, November 2022 Paper 22, March 2023 Paper 22, June 2023 Papers 21 and 22 |
| Advise answers on one option only, with no decision, or repeating the question without development | November 2022 Paper 21, June 2022 Paper 23, March 2023 Paper 22, March 2026 Paper 22 |
| Mark up confused with margin when setting a selling price | November 2022 Papers 21 and 23 |

Two of these deserve a closer look.

**Labels.** Several reports put the rule the same way: a mark needs the right figure with the right label. The March 2025 report gave clear examples from a sales ledger control account. Each entry is labelled with the account that holds the other half of the double entry. So you write sales, not credit sales, and bank, not a phrase about money coming in. In ledger accounts and statements, the words are marks too.

**Advise answers.** The November 2023 Paper 22 report noted that some candidates lost marks by making one point and then repeating it in reverse for the other option. Under the March 2026 Paper 22 mark scheme principles, a mirror statement like this is not credited twice. Give each option its own reasons.

<Tip title="Labels are marks">
Write "Balance b/d", "Cost of sales" and "Profit for the year" in full, and name the other account in every ledger entry. All eight reports I checked warn about labels in some form.
</Tip>

## How should I practise for Paper 2?

Practise in the shape of the real paper, then build up to full timing.

1. **Question by question, timed.** Take one Question 1 and give it 33 minutes. Stop when the time ends, then mark it with the scheme, including the OF marks.
2. **By topic.** Use the [9706 past papers by topic](/past-papers/a-level-accounting-9706/topics) to do several statements of the same business type in a row, then several Question 4 costing tasks.
3. **Full papers.** In the last few weeks, sit complete papers from the [9706 past papers page](/past-papers/a-level-accounting-9706) in 1 hour 45 minutes, with your finishing times written down.
4. **Sort your lost marks.** For each lost mark, note the cause: time, no working, label, instruction or method. After three papers, the pattern is usually clear.

To check break-even answers quickly while you practise, use the [break-even calculator](/tools/break-even-calculator). To see how a Paper 2 mark combines with Paper 1 into a grade, look at the [9706 grade thresholds](/grade-thresholds/a-level-accounting-9706). If you are aiming for the top grade across the full A Level, my [A\* marks article](/blog/a-level-a-star-marks-9706-9708-9609) explains how that line is set.

## Practice questions

**Question 1.** Your Paper 2 starts at 1:30 pm. Using my time plan, when should you finish Question 3, and how long do you then have for Question 4?

**Answer.** Question 3 ends 65 minutes in, at 2:35 pm. Question 4 gets 33 minutes, so it runs to 3:08 pm. That leaves 7 minutes to check before the paper ends at 3:15 pm.

**Question 2.** A trader's credit sales were $146,000 and revenue was $169,000. Trade receivables at the year end were $16,900. A student calculated trade receivables turnover using revenue and wrote 37 days. Later in the question, they subtracted last year's figure of 40 days to find the change. What did they lose, and what can they keep?

**Answer.** The syllabus formula is trade receivables ÷ credit sales × 365. That gives 16,900 ÷ 146,000 × 365 = 42.25, so 43 days, because the March 2023 report said these days ratios are rounded up to the next whole day. The student's 16,900 ÷ 169,000 × 365 = 36.5 used the wrong formula, so the syllabus says it is not credited. But the syllabus also says the resulting figure is credited as their own figure in following calculations. So their change of 3 days (40 less 37) can still be credited.

**Question 3.** Rewrite these lines so they earn their label marks: in a sales ledger control account, "Receipts from customers 8,400" and "Credit sales 12,600"; in a statement, "COS 38,000" and "Net profit 9,500"; and an absorption rate of "14.6".

**Answer.** "Bank 8,400", "Sales 12,600", "Cost of sales 38,000", "Profit for the year 9,500" and "$14.60 per machine hour" (or per labour hour, whichever basis the question uses, and to two decimal places when the question asks). Each fix matches a problem the reports named: account names in ledgers, no abbreviations, current terms, decimal places and the basis of the rate.

## FAQ

### How long is AS Level Accounting 9706 Paper 2?

It is 1 hour 45 minutes for 90 marks. The syllabus for 2026 to 2028 gives it four compulsory structured questions.

### How many questions are on 9706 Paper 2, and what are they worth?

Four. Question 1 is worth 30 marks and Questions 2 and 3 are worth 15 each, all on financial accounting. Question 4 is cost and management accounting, worth 30 marks.

### How much of the AS Level is Paper 2 worth?

72% of the AS Level and 36% of the full A Level. Paper 1, the multiple choice paper, makes up the rest of the AS Level.

### Do I have to show workings in 9706 Paper 2?

The mark scheme gives full credit for a correct answer without working unless the question asks for it. But several examiner reports say that without workings, candidates get no credit, so always show them.

### What is the own figure rule in 9706?

If you use your own wrong figure from an earlier part with a correct and complete method, later parts can still earn full marks. The syllabus applies the same idea to ratios worked out with a wrong formula.

### Can I use a calculator in 9706 Paper 2?

Yes. The syllabus says calculators are essential for all papers. Write your working on the paper anyway, because the calculator's steps are invisible to the examiner.
