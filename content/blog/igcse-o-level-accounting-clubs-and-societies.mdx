A club or society does not trade for profit, so its accounts use different names. The receipts and payments account is a summary of the cash book. The income and expenditure account matches income and expenses to the year and ends with a surplus or deficit. Subscriptions are adjusted through a subscriptions account, and the club's capital is called the accumulated fund.

## What does the syllabus say about clubs and societies?

Clubs and societies is section 5.5 of the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-5.5) and of the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The list is the same in both. You need to know:

1. What receipts and payments accounts and income and expenditure accounts are.
2. How to prepare a receipts and payments account.
3. How to prepare accounts for activities that earn money, such as refreshments and subscriptions.
4. How to prepare an income and expenditure account and a statement of financial position.
5. The sole trader adjustments from section 5.1: accruals, prepayments, depreciation and irrecoverable debts.
6. How to define and calculate the accumulated fund.

Point 5 matters most. Everything you learned for a sole trader's year end adjustments is used again here.

## How are a club's accounts different from a business's?

A club exists to serve its members, not to make money for an owner. So the ideas are the same, but the names change.

| Sole trader | Club or society |
|---|---|
| Cash book | Receipts and payments account |
| Statement of profit or loss (income statement) | Income and expenditure account |
| Profit for the year | Surplus (income more than expenditure) |
| Loss for the year | Deficit (expenditure more than income) |
| Capital | Accumulated fund |
| Revenue from customers | Subscriptions from members |

Use the club words in every answer. The examiner reports for June 2022 and March 2025 both list "income statement" written in place of "income and expenditure account" as an error.

## What is the difference between a receipts and payments account and an income and expenditure account?

The **receipts and payments account** is a summary of the club's cash book for the year. It shows every amount received and paid, whatever it was for. The **income and expenditure account** shows only the income earned and the expenses used up in the year.

| Point | Receipts and payments account | Income and expenditure account |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Summary of the cash book | The club's version of an income statement |
| Basis | Cash actually received or paid | Accruals: income earned, expenses incurred |
| Capital items (new equipment, loans) | Included | Left out |
| Items that are not cash (depreciation, written off) | Left out | Included |
| Arrears and advance subscriptions | Ignored | Adjusted for |
| Final figure | Bank or cash balance (an asset) | Surplus or deficit |

Two Paper 1 questions show the difference. In the 7707 November 2023 paper, subscriptions written off had no entry in the receipts and payments account, because no money moved. In the 0452 November 2022 paper, a loan received was a receipt, then a liability in the statement of financial position.

### How do you prepare a receipts and payments account?

Set it out like a cash book with two sides. Receipts go on the left (debit) with the opening balance. Payments go on the right (credit). The closing balance is the difference.

If the opening balance was an overdraft, it starts on the payments side. The worked example below includes a full account.

## What is the accumulated fund and how do you find it?

The **accumulated fund** is the club's total assets less its total liabilities. It is also all the surpluses the club has made since it started, less all its deficits. It does the job that capital does in a business.

Do not define it as just "capital". The June 2023 report called that answer incorrect, and said answers that gave surpluses with no deficits fell short too. The mark scheme accepted either full definition above, as long as it was about a club. Learn one of them.

To find the opening accumulated fund, prepare a **statement of affairs**: a list of the club's assets and liabilities on the first day of the year.

| Usually assets | Usually liabilities |
|---|---|
| Equipment, buildings (at book value) | Subscriptions in advance |
| Café or bar inventory | Café or bar trade payables |
| Subscriptions in arrears | Expenses owing |
| Expenses prepaid | Loans |
| Bank and cash | Bank overdraft |

**Accumulated fund = total assets − total liabilities**

At the year end:

**Closing accumulated fund = opening accumulated fund + surplus (or − deficit)**

So a fall in the accumulated fund means a deficit. The 0452 November 2022 Paper 1 used exactly this idea to work back to the subscriptions figure.

<Tip title="Arrears are assets, advance is a liability">
Subscriptions in arrears are money members owe the club, so they are an asset. Subscriptions in advance are money the club owes back in services, so they are a liability. The March 2025 report listed mixing these up as a common error.
</Tip>

## How do you prepare a subscriptions account?

**Subscriptions** are the fees members pay to belong to the club. The cash received in a year is rarely the income for that year. Some members are behind (**in arrears**) and some have paid next year's fee early (**in advance**). The subscriptions account finds the true income to put in the income and expenditure account.

| Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d: arrears at the start | Balance b/d: advance at the start |
| Income and expenditure account (the missing figure) | Bank: subscriptions received |
| Balance c/d: advance at the end | Subscriptions written off |
| | Balance c/d: arrears at the end |

Under the totals, bring both closing balances down to the first day of the next year. Arrears come down on the debit side and advance on the credit side.

As a check, you can use the same figures as a calculation:

**Income = received + opening advance − closing advance − opening arrears + closing arrears + written off**

### What happens to subscriptions written off?

Sometimes a member leaves without paying the arrears. The club writes the amount off, like an irrecoverable debt. It goes on the credit side of the subscriptions account and also appears as an expense in the income and expenditure account.

In the June 2023 Paper 22, the amount written off had to be calculated first (a number of members times the fee). The report says it was often left out, and many candidates also missed it as an expense.

This is where students get stuck. They can draw a subscriptions account, but they put the opening balances on the wrong sides, or use the bank figure as income. Write the four balances (arrears and advance, start and end) in a small list before you draw anything, and mark each one Dr or Cr.

## How do you account for a club café or bar?

Many clubs run an activity that sells things to members: a café, a bar, a shop, refreshments at matches. The syllabus calls these revenue-generating activities. Each one gets its own small trading account, so the committee can see if it pays its way.

The layout is a trading account from your [income statement format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format):

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Café revenue (takings) | X |
| Opening inventory | X |
| Add purchases | X |
| Less closing inventory | (X) |
| Cost of sales | (X) |
| Gross profit | X |
| Less café wages and other direct café costs | (X) |
| **Profit on café** | **X** |

Two steps catch students out:

- **Purchases are rarely given.** You get cash paid to suppliers from the receipts and payments account. Adjust it for opening and closing café trade payables, as in my [incomplete records guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-incomplete-records).
- **Only café items go in.** Subscriptions, rent of the ground and general club expenses belong in the income and expenditure account, not here.

The **profit on café** then becomes one line of income in the income and expenditure account. Café inventory and café payables go into the statement of financial position.

If the café makes a loss, I teach students to show it under expenditure. That is standard practice, not a Cambridge quote: both activities in the mark schemes I checked made a profit.

For the June 2022 refreshments question the report said that "as only a calculation was required, any format was acceptable". If the question says "calculate", a neat calculation is enough.

Smaller activities, such as a dance or a competition, work the same way in one line: takings less costs gives the profit or loss on that event.

## How do you answer a full clubs and societies question?

Riverside Tennis Club runs a café for its members. Its year ends on 31 December 2026.

**Balances at 1 January 2026**

| Item | $ |
|---|---:|
| Equipment at book value | 18 000 |
| Café inventory | 900 |
| Subscriptions in arrears | 400 |
| Insurance prepaid | 150 |
| Bank | 2 750 |
| Subscriptions in advance | 300 |
| Café trade payables | 600 |
| Electricity owing | 250 |

**Riverside Tennis Club: Receipts and payments account for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| Receipts | $ | Payments | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 2 750 | Café suppliers | 5 200 |
| Subscriptions | 12 600 | Café wages | 2 400 |
| Café takings | 9 800 | Rent of courts | 4 800 |
| Competition entry fees | 1 400 | Insurance | 900 |
| | | Electricity | 1 100 |
| | | Competition prizes | 650 |
| | | New equipment | 3 000 |
| | | Balance c/d | 8 500 |
| | **26 550** | | **26 550** |

**Notes at 31 December 2026**

1. Subscriptions in arrears were $500 and subscriptions in advance were $450.
2. A member who owed $100 of the opening arrears has left. This amount is to be written off.
3. Café inventory was $1 100 and café trade payables were $700.
4. Insurance prepaid was $200 and electricity owing was $300.
5. All equipment was valued at $18 900.

### W2: the subscriptions account

**Subscriptions account**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan 2026 Balance b/d | 400 | 1 Jan 2026 Balance b/d | 300 |
| 31 Dec 2026 Income and expenditure | 12 650 | 31 Dec 2026 Bank | 12 600 |
| 31 Dec 2026 Balance c/d | 450 | 31 Dec 2026 Subscriptions written off | 100 |
| | | 31 Dec 2026 Balance c/d | 500 |
| | **13 500** | | **13 500** |
| 1 Jan 2027 Balance b/d | 500 | 1 Jan 2027 Balance b/d | 450 |

The credit side totals 13 500. The known debits are 850, so income is 13 500 − 850 = **$12 650**. Check: 12 600 + 300 − 450 − 400 + 500 + 100 = 12 650.

### W3: the café trading account

Café purchases: 5 200 paid − 600 opening payables + 700 closing payables = **$5 300**.

| Item | Working | $ |

A note on arrears. I show subscriptions in arrears on their own line. The June 2022 mark scheme added them to the prepaid rent and showed one figure for other receivables. Both put arrears in current assets, which is what matters. If you name them, write the label in full, as the mistakes table below explains.

## How are clubs and societies questions marked?

Paper 2 has five compulsory questions of 20 marks each in both 0452 and 7707. In the three 0452 papers below, clubs filled one whole question.

| Paper | Part | Marks |
|---|---|---:|
| June 2022 Paper 21 | Subscriptions account | 6 |
| | Would the treasurer be satisfied? | 3 |
| | Profit on refreshments | 7 |
| | Statement of financial position extract (current assets) | 4 |
| June 2023 Paper 22 | Subscriptions account (with written off) | 7 |
| | Income and expenditure account | 6 |
| | Should the club raise subscriptions? | 5 |
| | Define a receipts and payments account | 1 |
| | Define the accumulated fund | 1 |
| March 2025 Paper 22 | Opening accumulated fund | 4 |
| | Subscriptions account | 6 |
| | Rent and insurance account | 5 |
| | Coffee shop income statement | 5 |

What this tells you:

- **The subscriptions account is in every one.** It earned 6 or 7 marks each time.
- **Small details carry marks.** One mark was for correct dates, and one for both opening balances on the correct sides. The balances brought down at the start of the next year earned marks too.
- **The income figure is an own figure (OF) mark.** If your subscriptions account is wrong, you still earn the mark for carrying your figure into the income and expenditure account correctly.
- **Decisions need a club view.** The June 2023 report said many answers treated the club like a business with customers. Use the club's own facts (falling membership, rising arrears) and finish with a clear recommendation.

The topic also appears in Paper 1, the multiple choice paper. The 0452 reports for June 2022, November 2022, June 2023, June 2025 and March 2026, and the 7707 report for November 2023, all discuss Paper 1 questions on it.

## What mistakes do examiners report?

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Using subscriptions received from the bank as the income figure (June 2023) | Transfer the figure from the subscriptions account |
| Putting the bank balance in the subscriptions account (June 2022) | Only subscriptions received go in, labelled "Bank" |
| Opening balances on the wrong sides, or the whole account reversed (June 2022, June 2023) | Arrears b/d on the debit side, advance b/d on the credit side |
| Labels such as "owing", "income statement" or "receipts and payments" in the account, or missing dates (June 2022, March 2025) | Write "Balance b/d", "Income and expenditure" and "Bank", with a date on every entry |
| Leaving out subscriptions written off (June 2023) | Credit it in the subscriptions account and show it as an expense |
| Using cash paid to café or refreshment suppliers as purchases (June 2022, March 2025) | Adjust for opening and closing trade payables |
| Putting subscriptions and club expenses into the refreshments or café account (June 2022, March 2025) | Only the activity's own revenue and costs go there |
| Missing subscriptions in arrears from current assets, or writing "subs" (June 2022) | Write "Subscriptions in arrears" in full and total the section |

The June 2023 report also noted that "Club and society accounts appear to be a less popular area" of the syllabus. It asked for subscriptions accounts to be practised more often. The three practice questions below are a start.

<Tip title="Use the club's language in discussion answers">
When asked whether a treasurer should be satisfied, compare subscriptions received with subscriptions due for the year. Say whether arrears and advance went up or down. Then give a clear opinion.
</Tip>

## Can you answer these practice questions?

Try each one on paper before you read the answer.

### Practice question 1

On 1 April 2025 a chess club's members owed $250 and had paid $180 in advance. During the year to 31 March 2026 the club received $8 400 in subscriptions. On 31 March 2026 members owed $320 and had paid $210 in advance. Prepare the subscriptions account.

**Answer.**

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Apr 2025 Balance b/d | 250 | 1 Apr 2025 Balance b/d | 180 |
| 31 Mar 2026 Income and expenditure | 8 440 | 31 Mar 2026 Bank | 8 400 |
| 31 Mar 2026 Balance c/d | 210 | 31 Mar 2026 Balance c/d | 320 |
| | **8 900** | | **8 900** |
| 1 Apr 2026 Balance b/d | 320 | 1 Apr 2026 Balance b/d | 210 |

Income for the year is **$8 440**.

### Practice question 2

On 1 July 2025 a sports club had: clubhouse $40 000, equipment $6 500, bar inventory $1 200, subscriptions in arrears $300, bank $2 400, subscriptions in advance $500, bar trade payables $900 and a loan of $10 000. Calculate the accumulated fund.

**Answer.** Assets = 40 000 + 6 500 + 1 200 + 300 + 2 400 = 50 400. Liabilities = 500 + 900 + 10 000 = 11 400. Accumulated fund = 50 400 − 11 400 = **$39 000**.

### Practice question 3

A cricket club's bar took $14 600. The club paid bar suppliers $8 900. Bar trade payables were $700 at the start and $1 050 at the end. Bar inventory was $1 300 at the start and $1 100 at the end. The barman was paid $2 000. Calculate the profit on the bar and say where it goes.

**Answer.** Purchases = 8 900 − 700 + 1 050 = 9 250. Cost of sales = 1 300 + 9 250 − 1 100 = 9 450. Gross profit = 14 600 − 9 450 = 5 150. Profit on bar = 5 150 − 2 000 = **$3 150**. It is shown as income in the income and expenditure account.

## FAQ

### Is a receipts and payments account the same as an income and expenditure account?

No. A receipts and payments account is a summary of the cash book and ends with a bank balance. An income and expenditure account shows income earned and expenses incurred in the year and ends with a surplus or deficit.

### What is the accumulated fund of a club?

It is the club's total assets less its total liabilities. It also equals all past surpluses less all past deficits. Do not write only "capital".

### Are subscriptions in arrears an asset or a liability?

An asset. Members owe the money to the club, so it is a current asset. Subscriptions in advance are a current liability.

### Is a surplus the same as a profit?

It plays the same role, but clubs say surplus because they do not aim to make a profit. A club activity such as a café or bar still shows a profit or loss.

### Where does the profit on a club café or bar go?

It goes into the income and expenditure account as one line of income. The standard treatment for a loss is to show it under expenditure.
