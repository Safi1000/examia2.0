When a partnership changes, through a new partner, a retirement or a new profit sharing ratio, the old partners must be given their share of everything the business is worth on that date. In 9706 you do this in two steps. A revaluation account shares any gain or loss on assets in the **old** ratio. Goodwill is credited to the old partners in the **old** ratio, then, if no goodwill account is kept, debited to the partners in the **new** ratio.

## What does the 9706 syllabus say about partnership changes?

Partnership changes are topic 3.1.2 in the 9706 syllabus for 2026 to 2028. This is A Level content, so it is tested on Paper 3, Financial Accounting. You must understand:

- goodwill, and the difference between purchased goodwill and inherent goodwill
- partners' capital and current accounts showing goodwill and revaluation entries on a change in the profit sharing ratio, the admission of a new partner, the retirement of a partner and the dissolution of a partnership
- the appropriation account, statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position, including a change part way through the year
- the realisation account and the revaluation account

The AS basics from topic 1.5.3, such as capital and current accounts and the Partnership Act 1890, are assumed knowledge. If you need those first, start with my [partnership accounts basics](/blog/igcse-accounting-partnership-accounts). You can tick off every point on the [9706 syllabus checklist](/syllabus/a-level-accounting-9706).

Paper 3 is 1 hour 30 minutes for 75 marks: three structured questions of 25 marks each, worth 30% of the A Level. In the recent Paper 3 papers I checked, partnership changes appeared inside these 25 mark questions, often next to a sale of the partnership to a limited company.

## What is goodwill, and why is it not kept in the books?

**Goodwill** is the amount a business is worth above the fair value of its separate net assets. It comes from things such as a good reputation, loyal customers, a good location and skilled staff. The June 2025 9706 Paper 32 mark scheme credited reasons of this kind.

| Type | How it arises | In the accounts? |
|---|---|---|
| Inherent goodwill | Built up by the business's own efforts over time | Not recorded |
| Purchased goodwill | Paid for when one business buys another: the price paid above the fair value of the net assets taken over | Recorded as an intangible non-current asset |

That split follows the 2023 specimen Paper 3 mark scheme. A partnership's own goodwill is inherent, so most questions say a goodwill account is **not** to be kept. The June 2025 Paper 32 scheme accepted reasons such as: the goodwill is inherent, not bought; its value is a matter of opinion; prudence says assets should not be overstated; and it is hard to measure in money.

### Why must a new partner pay for goodwill?

The old partners built the goodwill. A new partner who joins now shares in future profits that the goodwill helps to earn. So the new partner pays the old partners for that share, either in cash or through the capital accounts. Either way, the old partners are rewarded for their past work. The June 2025 Paper 32 scheme credited this reward idea.

## How do you record a change in a partnership, step by step?

This is the method I teach for admission, retirement and a change in ratio:

1. **Revalue the assets and liabilities** in a revaluation account. Increases are credits, decreases are debits.
2. **Share the revaluation profit or loss in the old ratio** in the capital accounts.
3. **Credit goodwill to the old partners in the old ratio.** This gives them their share of what they built.
4. **If no goodwill account is kept, debit goodwill to the partners in the new ratio.** This removes it from the books.
5. **Record cash** brought in by a new partner, or paid or owed to a retiring partner.
6. **Close the retiring partner's current account** into the capital account if there is one, and transfer any balance owed to a loan account if it is not paid at once.

The ratios matter most. **Old ratio for gains and goodwill credited. New ratio for goodwill written off.** If the partners have no agreement, the Partnership Act 1890 says profits and losses are shared equally. That is why the June 2025 Paper 32 scheme gave a retiring partner half the goodwill and half the revaluation loss.

## Worked example 1: admitting a new partner

Farah and Gul share profits and losses in the ratio 3 : 2. They keep fluctuating capital accounts. Their statement of financial position at 31 March 2026 is below. All figures are my own.

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Premises | 120 000 |
| Fixtures | 30 000 |
| Inventory | 24 000 |
| Trade receivables | 18 000 |
| Bank | 8 000 |
| Trade payables | (15 000) |
| **Net assets** | **185 000** |
| Capital: Farah | 110 000 |
| Capital: Gul | 75 000 |
| **Total capital** | **185 000** |

On 1 April 2026 Hassan is admitted. The new ratio is Farah 2 : Gul 2 : Hassan 1. It is agreed that:

- premises are revalued at $150 000, fixtures at $24 000 and inventory at $21 000
- an allowance for irrecoverable debts of $1 000 is created
- goodwill is valued at $60 000 and no goodwill account is kept
- Hassan brings in $70 000 cash as capital

### Step 1: the revaluation account

| Debit | $ | Credit | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Fixtures (30 000 − 24 000) | 6 000 | Premises (150 000 − 120 000) | 30 000 |
| Inventory (24 000 − 21 000) | 3 000 | | |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | 1 000 | | |
| Profit on revaluation: Farah (3/5) | 12 000 | | |
| Profit on revaluation: Gul (2/5) | 8 000 | | |
| **Total** | **30 000** | **Total** | **30 000** |

The net gain is $20 000. It is shared in the **old** ratio 3 : 2, because it arose while only Farah and Gul owned the business. Hassan gets none of it.

### Step 2: goodwill

| Partner | Credited in old ratio 3 : 2 | Debited in new ratio 2 : 2 : 1 | Net effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Farah | 36 000 | 24 000 | + 12 000 |
| Gul | 24 000 | 24 000 | 0 |
| Hassan | 0 | 12 000 | − 12 000 |
| **Total** | **60 000** | **60 000** | **0** |

The net column shows what really happened. Hassan has paid $12 000 for his one fifth share of goodwill. All of it went to Farah, because Farah's share fell from 3/5 to 2/5, while Gul's share stayed at 2/5.

### Step 3: the capital accounts

| | Farah $ | Gul $ | Hassan $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Balance b/d | 110 000 | 75 000 | |
| Bank | | | 70 000 |
| Revaluation profit | 12 000 | 8 000 | |
| Goodwill credited | 36 000 | 24 000 | |
| Goodwill written off | (24 000) | (24 000) | (12 000) |
| **Balance c/d** | **134 000** | **83 000** | **58 000** |

In ledger form, the goodwill written off and closing balances sit on the debit side, and the opening balance, cash, revaluation profit and goodwill credited sit on the credit side.

### Step 4: check against the new statement of financial position

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Premises | 150 000 |
| Fixtures | 24 000 |
| Inventory | 21 000 |
| Trade receivables (18 000 − 1 000) | 17 000 |
| Bank (8 000 + 70 000) | 78 000 |
| Trade payables | (15 000) |
| **Net assets** | **275 000** |
| Capital: Farah + Gul + Hassan (134 000 + 83 000 + 58 000) | **275 000** |

Both sides agree, so the entries are complete. I always do this check in the exam. It takes a minute and catches a missing entry.

## Worked example 2: a partner retires

Go back to Farah and Gul's statement of financial position at 31 March 2026. Suppose that, instead of Hassan joining, Gul retires on that date and Farah carries on alone. The same revaluation and goodwill of $60 000 apply. No goodwill account is kept.

**Amount due to Gul**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Capital account balance | 75 000 |
| Share of revaluation profit (2/5 × 20 000) | 8 000 |
| Share of goodwill (2/5 × 60 000) | 24 000 |
| **Amount due to Gul** | **107 000** |

Gul is paid $5 000 in cash. The remaining $102 000 is transferred to a loan account.

**Farah's capital account**

Farah's capital = 110 000 + 12 000 (revaluation) + 36 000 (goodwill credited) − 60 000 (all the goodwill written off, as she now owns 100%) = **$98 000**.

**Check.** Revalued net assets were 150 000 + 24 000 + 21 000 + 17 000 + 8 000 − 15 000 = 205 000. Take away the $5 000 cash paid and the $102 000 loan: 205 000 − 5 000 − 102 000 = **$98 000**. That matches Farah's capital.

Two things are different from admission. The retiring partner's balances are added up into one **amount due**, and that amount is either paid or moved to a **loan account**. If the retiring partner had a current account, its balance would be part of the amount due too. In the June 2025 Paper 32 scheme, the retiring partner's capital, current account and loan to the business were all included before goodwill and the revaluation loss were added or taken away.

## How are other changes handled?

### A change in the profit sharing ratio

No one joins or leaves, but the ratio changes, so the same logic applies. Revalue and share the result in the old ratio. Credit goodwill in the old ratio and write it off in the new ratio. A partner whose share falls gains, and a partner whose share rises pays.

### A change part way through the year

The syllabus asks you to prepare the appropriation account when a change happens part way through the year. Split the year's profit between the two periods, using any basis the question gives, then appropriate each period with the salaries, interest and ratio that applied then. The November 2024 9706 Paper 32 question paper asked for the profit to be divided between two periods, assuming revenue and other expenses built up at a steady rate over the year.

### Two sole traders forming a partnership

The November 2022 9706 Paper 33 question merged two sole traders into a partnership. That paper was the older 3 hour, 150 mark Paper 3. Each owner's goodwill was credited to them, and the total was written off in the new profit sharing ratio. Its scheme gave a mark for each partner's goodwill credit and each goodwill debit.

### Dissolution: the realisation account

When a partnership closes or is sold, a **realisation account** works out the profit or loss on closing. Assets are transferred into it at book value. The proceeds, or the purchase consideration from a buyer, and any asset a partner takes over are credited. The balance is the profit or loss on realisation, shared in the profit sharing ratio. The partners' capital accounts are then settled, in cash or, where a company buys the business, in shares. Several of the Paper 3 questions I read combine this with the buyer's journal entries, which I cover in my [limited company accounts guide](/blog/a-level-accounting-9706-limited-company-accounts).

## How are partnership change questions marked?

The June 2025 9706 Paper 32 mark scheme shows how a typical calculation question is split. The question asked for the amount payable to a retiring partner and the cash a new partner brought in.

| Part of the working | How the scheme gave marks |
|---|---|
| Retiring partner's opening balances | 1 mark for the capital, current account and loan together |
| Share of goodwill | 1 mark |
| Revaluation working | Marks for the revaluation result and for the partner's share |
| Final amount due | 1 mark, own figure allowed |
| New partner's cash | Separate marks for the goodwill payment, each step of the capital working, and the total |
| Bank balance in the new statement | 1 mark for each cash flow and 1 for the balance |

Several of these marks are own figure (OF) marks. A wrong revaluation total costs you its own marks, but if your later method is right, you still earn the later ones. So always show your workings, labelled W1 and W2.

## Where do students lose marks on partnership changes?

These come from 9706 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026. The middle column is my summary.

| Mistake | What to do instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Goodwill entries reversed in the capital accounts, or the revaluation left out | Credit goodwill in the old ratio, debit in the new; post the revaluation | November 2022 Paper 33, Question 3(c) (some) |
| In a statement of profit or loss on realisation, entries in the wrong direction, or goodwill left out of the purchase consideration | The consideration and anything a partner takes over add to the result; the book values of the assets given up reduce it | June 2022 Paper 32, Question 4(a) (a sale to a company) |
| A vehicle taken over by a partner treated wrongly in the realisation account | Credit the realisation account and debit that partner's capital account | June 2023 Paper 32, Question 3 (the most common error) |
| Current account balances not worked out and transferred to the capital accounts on closing | Find each current account balance, then close it into the capital account | June 2023 Paper 32, Question 3 (very few made a realistic attempt, which cost four marks) |
| Goodwill left out or miscalculated | Work out goodwill the way the question says and include it | June 2023 Papers 31 and 33, Question 1 (capital accounts, giving the wrong profit on realisation); November 2023 Paper 31, Question 2(c) (the journal entry) |
| Goodwill entered after the profit on realisation was already posted | Goodwill is part of the consideration, so it goes in before the balance is found | March 2025 Paper 32, Question 2(c) |
| Describing goodwill and a tangible asset separately when asked for differences | Write each point as a comparison of the two | March 2025 Paper 32, Question 2(a) |
| Generic advice not based on the question | Use the figures and facts given | June 2023 Paper 32, Question 3 (some) |

## Can you answer these practice questions?

All three are my own. Try each before reading the answer.

**1.** Asif and Bina share profits 1 : 1. Goodwill is valued at $48 000 and no goodwill account is kept. Chen is admitted, and the new ratio is Asif 3 : Bina 2 : Chen 1. Show the net effect of goodwill on each partner's capital account. [3]

**Answer.** Credited in old ratio: Asif 24 000, Bina 24 000. Debited in new ratio: Asif 24 000, Bina 16 000, Chen 8 000. Net: **Asif 0, Bina + 8 000, Chen − 8 000.** Asif's share stayed at one half, so he neither gains nor pays.

**2.** Dev, Eli and Fay share profits 3 : 2 : 1. Fay retires. Her capital account is $46 000 and her current account has a credit balance of $3 500. There is a revaluation loss of $9 000 and goodwill is $30 000. Calculate the amount due to Fay. [3]

**Answer.** Fay's share is 1/6. Revaluation loss: 1/6 × 9 000 = 1 500. Goodwill: 1/6 × 30 000 = 5 000. Amount due = 46 000 + 3 500 − 1 500 + 5 000 = **$53 000**.

**3.** Explain why a revaluation profit is shared in the old profit sharing ratio, not the new one. [2]

**Answer.** The increase in value happened while the old partners owned the business (1). A new partner should not gain from value created before joining, and a retiring partner should be paid for it (1).

## FAQ

### Is goodwill shared in the old ratio or the new ratio?

Both. It is credited to the old partners in the old ratio, then written off against all the continuing partners in the new ratio when no goodwill account is kept.

### What is the difference between purchased and inherent goodwill?

Inherent goodwill is built up by the business itself and is not recorded. Purchased goodwill is paid for when buying another business and is shown as an intangible non-current asset.

### What happens if partners have no partnership agreement?

The Partnership Act 1890 applies, so profits and losses are shared equally. Revaluation results and goodwill are then shared equally too.

### Where does the amount owed to a retiring partner go if it is not paid?

It is transferred from the capital account to a loan account, shown as a liability in the statement of financial position.

### Is the revaluation account the same as the realisation account?

No. A revaluation account is used when the partnership continues, to update asset values. A realisation account is used when the partnership is dissolved or sold, to find the profit or loss on closing.

### Is partnership change on 9706 Paper 2 or Paper 3?

Paper 3. It is A Level content, topic 3.1.2. Paper 2 covers the AS partnership basics in topic 1.5.3.
