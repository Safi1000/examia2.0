Plenty of students sit Cambridge exams without a school. Some have left school, some are retaking a subject, and some go to a school that does not offer Accounting, Business or Economics at all. The first question is always the same: how do I actually register? Here is the process for 2027 as the British Council publishes it, what is known about dates and fees, and the mistakes that cost private candidates the most.

## The short answer

In Pakistan, private candidates do not register with Cambridge directly. You enter through the **British Council**, which registers you, collects your fees and seats you at one of its exam venues. Registration happens online, in the British Council's School Registration System (SRS).

Two things are not known yet. When I checked for this article, I could not find the British Council's deadline table for **May / June 2027** or the 2027 fees. Do not trust any website, including this one, that gives you exact 2027 numbers before the British Council does. I explain below what last year's dates looked like and where to check.

## Step by step: how registration works

The British Council describes these steps for private candidates:

1. **Create an account in SRS.** If you are under 18, a parent or guardian completes it.
2. **Choose the session, the qualification and your subjects.** Check every syllabus code. O Level Accounting is 7707 and IGCSE Accounting is 0452: they are different exams, with different papers.
3. **Check your details before you submit.** Your name and date of birth must match your ID exactly, because they are printed on your certificate.
4. **Pay the fee.** SRS shows the exact amount for each subject. For retakes, the British Council accepts a bank deposit slip, an ATM receipt or an IBFT confirmation as proof of payment.
5. **Send your proof of payment and your registration summary to your British Council office.** The British Council says registration is only complete once it receives both.
6. **Wait for your Statement of Entry.** It lists your subjects, papers, dates and venue. Check it the day it arrives and report any mistake straight away.

The fourth step is the easiest to get wrong. Filling in the online form is not enough: the fee has to be paid by the deadline for the stage you are entering at.

## Deadlines: what we know for 2027

The British Council publishes a deadline table for each session, with a normal deadline and one or more late stages that cost more. These are the most recent dates I have:

| Session | Normal deadline | Late stage |
|---|---|---|
| May / June 2026 | Second week of February 2026 | 17 March 2026 (late stage 1) |
| October / November 2026 | 6 August 2026 | 3 September 2026 (late stage and retakes) |
| May / June 2027 | Not published when I checked | Not published when I checked |

If May / June 2027 follows the pattern of 2026, the normal deadline will fall in February 2027. That is a pattern, not a promise. Check the British Council's private candidate page before you plan around it.

Two earlier deadlines matter for some students. Modified papers and access arrangements close well before the normal deadline (for October / November 2026, the dates were 29 June and 21 July 2026). If you need extra time or any other arrangement, apply as early as you can.

## Fees

I am not going to quote a fee, because the British Council shows the fee for each subject inside SRS and it changes between sessions. Three things are reliable:

- **Fees are per subject**, so three subjects cost roughly three times one.
- **Late stages cost more.** Entering at the normal deadline is the cheapest option.
- **Always check SRS before paying.** The British Council's own retake page tells candidates to use the SRS dashboard for current pricing.

## Pick the right code for 2027

Two Business syllabuses change code from 2027. If you are a private candidate, nobody at a school will catch a wrong entry for you, so check this table against your SRS entry.

| Subject | O Level | IGCSE | AS and A Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting | 7707 | 0452 | 9706 |
| Economics | 2281 | 0455 | 9708 |
| Business | **7081** (new, replaces 7115) | **0264** (new, replaces 0450) | 9609 |

Both new Business syllabuses say in their own documents that private candidates can enter for them. They also say you cannot take O Level Business 7081 and IGCSE Business 0264 in the same exam series, so choose one. If you are unsure what the new codes mean, I have written a [plain guide to the Business changes](/blog/igcse-business-0264-o-level-7081-changes-2027-parents).

Not every subject is open to private candidates. The British Council says the full list is in SRS, so confirm your subjects there before you start studying.

### O Level or IGCSE?

For Accounting, Business and Economics, both are written papers with no coursework, and both are graded from A\*. The difference is the bottom of the scale: O Level runs from A\* to E, and IGCSE runs from A\* to G. If a university or employer has asked for one specifically, follow that.

## AS and A Level as a private candidate

At A Level you have a choice. You can sit AS first and the rest of the A Level in a later session, with your AS result carried forward, or you can sit all the papers in one session. The carry forward rules and time limits are in the Cambridge Handbook, so check them with the British Council before you enter for the second part.

The route also changes the marks you need. In June 2026, the A\* line for A Level Business 9609 was between 153 and 157 out of 200 for students who took all four papers together, and between 153 and 163 for the staged combinations. I explain this in detail in [how many marks you need for an A\* in 9706, 9708 and 9609](/blog/a-level-a-star-marks-9706-9708-9609).

## May / June or October / November?

May / June is the main session and the one most students sit. October / November is smaller, and many students use it for retakes or to finish A Level after AS. If you are starting a subject now, in November 2026, May / June 2027 is realistic for one or two subjects if you work steadily. Keep October / November 2027 as your backup rather than your plan.

## What is hard about studying alone

Registration is the easy part. The hard part is that nobody marks your written answers. These are the places where students working alone tend to drop marks:

- **Accounting:** formats and layouts. A correct figure in the wrong place in a statement can still lose marks.
- **Business:** application. From 2027, 30% of the marks in O Level and IGCSE Business are for applying your knowledge to the business in the question.
- **Economics:** analysis and evaluation. Examiners want a chain of reasoning and a supported judgement, not a list of points.

A simple routine helps: one topic at a time, then past paper questions on that topic, marked strictly against the mark scheme, then full timed papers from March onwards. My [private candidate guide for Pakistan](/private-candidates/pakistan) has a month by month plan to May / June 2027.

## Before you pay: a checklist

- The session is right (May / June 2027 or October / November 2027).
- Every syllabus code is right, especially Business (7081 or 0264 from 2027).
- Your name and date of birth match your ID exactly.
- You know which stage you are paying at, and the fee for that stage.
- You have kept your proof of payment and your registration summary, and sent both to the British Council.
- You have a date in your calendar to check your Statement of Entry.
