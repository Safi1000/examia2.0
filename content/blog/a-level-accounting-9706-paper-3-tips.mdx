A Level Accounting 9706 Paper 3 is Financial Accounting: 1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, three structured questions of 25 marks each, all compulsory. The source material for each question is on a separate insert, and you answer on the question paper. It is worth 30% of the A Level. A large share of the marks comes from preparing statements, but questions usually finish with a written part: advise, explain or discuss.

## What is the format of 9706 Paper 3?

| Feature | 2026 to 2028 syllabus |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Marks | 75 |
| Questions | Three structured questions, 25 marks each, answer all |
| Material | A separate insert holds the sources for each question |
| Content | A Level financial accounting (section 3), with AS content assumed |
| Weighting | 30% of the A Level |
| Skills | Knowledge and understanding, analysis, evaluation |

The [9706 syllabus](/syllabus/a-level-accounting-9706) adds three rules that cost marks if ignored. Use international terms and formats. Use only the ratio formulae in the syllabus appendix, because those are the only ones accepted. And when asked to recommend or decide, support the answer with a balance of reasons and justify it.

### Why do the 2022 Paper 3 papers look different?

Because they were set on the old syllabus. I opened the covers of every 9706 Paper 3 on disk. The March, June and November 2022 papers were 3 hours and 150 marks. Every paper from March 2023 to June 2026, and the 2023 specimen Paper 3, is 1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks. The March 2023 report called that session the first examination of the revised syllabus.

So practise mainly on 2023 onwards. The 2022 papers still have useful financial accounting questions in Section A, but they are twice as long and also had a Section B on cost and management accounting, which is now Paper 4. Do not use them for timing.

## What topics does Paper 3 test?

Section 3 of the syllabus has five parts. This is what each means in the exam, in my words.

| Syllabus section | What you prepare or explain |
|---|---|
| 3.1 Preparation of financial statements | Partnership changes (goodwill, revaluation, admission, retirement, dissolution, realisation account); clubs (receipts and payments, subscriptions, income and expenditure, accumulated fund, life membership); manufacturing accounts with factory profit and unrealised profit; limited company statements, including the statement of cash flows, statement of changes in equity and schedule of non-current assets |
| 3.2 Regulatory and ethical considerations | IAS 1, 2, 7, 8, 10, 16, 36, 37 and 38; the five ethical principles; auditors and stewardship |
| 3.3 Business acquisition and merger | Journal and ledger entries when businesses merge or a company buys a business; goodwill on acquisition; the new statements |
| 3.4 Computerised accounting systems | Moving manual records to a computer system safely |
| 3.5 Analysis and communication | Working capital cycle, net working assets to revenue, interest cover, gearing, and the investment ratios; interpreting them and advising |

From the examiner reports since 2023, the statements that keep appearing include manufacturing accounts with unrealised profit, club accounts, statements of cash flows, partnership and acquisition accounts, and ratio analysis. Short IAS and audit parts sit inside bigger questions.

My [statement of cash flows guide for 9706](/blog/a-level-accounting-9706-statement-of-cash-flows) covers the IAS 7 layout in full. The partnership side is in [partnership changes for 9706](/blog/a-level-accounting-9706-partnership-changes-goodwill).

## How long should I spend on each question?

Here is my plan, not a Cambridge rule. 75 marks in 90 minutes is 1.2 minutes a mark. I suggest 28 minutes a question instead, which is 1.12 minutes a mark, so you keep 6 minutes at the end.

| Part of the paper | Time |
|---|---|
| Question 1 (25 marks) | 28 minutes |
| Question 2 (25 marks) | 28 minutes |
| Question 3 (25 marks) | 28 minutes |
| Check workings, labels and decimal places | 6 minutes |
| Total | 90 minutes |

My advice: read the insert for each question just before you answer it, not all three at the start. Then read every part of the question before you write anything: later parts often tell you what the earlier figures will be used for.

**Watch the big parts.** On some papers one statement carries far more marks than the rest of its question. In the June 2024 Paper 33, the cost and accumulated depreciation sections of a schedule of non-current assets were worth 20 marks. In November 2025 Paper 32, the statement of cash flows was worth 22. Give that part most of the question's 28 minutes.

## How are Paper 3 questions marked?

### Statements and accounts

Most marks are 1 per correct line or figure. Own figure marks mean a wrong earlier figure can still score later if you use it correctly. Layout and labels matter: the November 2023 Paper 31 report said a few candidates presented the statement of cash flows as a list, and some lost marks for entries in the wrong section.

Follow the instructions in the question exactly. The November 2023 Paper 32 report said candidates told to call the balancing figure "other operating expenses" often ignored this. The same report said a high number did not enter the opening and closing bank balances in the statement of cash flows, although they were given.

### Ratios

Use the syllabus formula and give the number of decimal places asked. The reports for March 2023, June 2023 (Papers 31 and 33) and March 2025 all mention answers not given to two decimal places, and November 2023 Paper 33 mentions one decimal place. Two formulae appear in the reports' lists of errors:

- **Gearing**: non-current liabilities ÷ (issued ordinary share capital + all reserves + non-current liabilities) × 100. The March 2023 report said marks were often lost by including the bank overdraft. The March 2025 report listed leaving the debentures out of the bottom line as a common error.
- **Dividend yield and dividend per share**: the annual ordinary dividend is the interim dividend paid plus the final dividend proposed for the year. The March 2025 report listed using the 2023 final dividend in the dividend yield as a common error, so check which dividends belong to the year in the question.

### Advise and justify

In the two schemes I checked, an advice part was worth 5 marks: up to 2 for points on each side, then 1 for a decision supported by a reason. That is how the November 2023 Paper 32 scheme marked advice on factory profit (5 marks) and the March 2026 Paper 32 scheme marked advice to a club committee (5 marks).

So a one sided answer caps your marks. The June 2023 Papers 31 and 33 reports said a high number of candidates gave two reasons why the directors should be concerned, but very few gave reasons why they should not be.

<Tip title="Both sides, then decide">
For every advise question, write two developed points for the option, two against (or for the other option), then one line starting "I advise ... because ...". Use a figure from the question in at least one point.
</Tip>

## What does a full mark advice answer look like?

Here is my own example, with every figure checked. It combines investment ratios with a recommendation, a common pairing on this paper.

> Farah wants to invest in one of two companies for a steady income. Extracts for the year:
>
> | | P plc | Q plc |
> |---|---|---|
> | Profit for the year | $1,800,000 | $1,350,000 |
> | Issued ordinary shares | 6,000,000 | 3,000,000 |
> | Market price per share | $4.50 | $3.60 |
> | Interim dividend paid | $240,000 | $300,000 |
> | Final dividend proposed | $360,000 | $420,000 |
> | Non-current liabilities | $2,000,000 | $4,500,000 |
> | Share capital and all reserves | $8,000,000 | $5,500,000 |

**Step 1: the ratios, using the syllabus formulae, to two decimal places.**

| Ratio | P plc | Q plc |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings per share | $1,800,000 ÷ 6,000,000 = $0.30 | $1,350,000 ÷ 3,000,000 = $0.45 |
| Price/earnings | $4.50 ÷ $0.30 = 15.00 | $3.60 ÷ $0.45 = 8.00 |
| Dividend per share | $600,000 ÷ 6,000,000 = $0.10 | $720,000 ÷ 3,000,000 = $0.24 |
| Dividend yield | $0.10 ÷ $4.50 × 100 = 2.22% | $0.24 ÷ $3.60 × 100 = 6.67% |
| Dividend cover | $1,800,000 ÷ $600,000 = 3.00 times | $1,350,000 ÷ $720,000 = 1.88 times |
| Gearing | $2,000,000 ÷ $10,000,000 × 100 = 20.00% | $4,500,000 ÷ $10,000,000 × 100 = 45.00% |

The annual dividend is interim plus final: $240,000 + $360,000 = $600,000 for P, and $300,000 + $420,000 = $720,000 for Q.

**Step 2: the advice.**

> For Q plc: its dividend yield of 6.67% is three times P's 2.22%, so Farah would receive far more income for each dollar invested **[1]**. Its earnings per share of $0.45 is also higher than P's $0.30 **[1]**.
>
> Against Q plc: dividend cover is only 1.88 times against P's 3.00, so a small fall in profit could force Q to cut its dividend **[1]**. Gearing of 45.00% is more than double P's 20.00%, so more of Q's profit goes on interest first, which adds risk to future dividends **[1]**.
>
> I advise Farah to buy shares in Q plc, because she wants income now and its yield is much higher, but she should watch its profits closely given the low cover and high gearing **[1]**.

That is 5 marks on the pattern above, as my own judgement, not a Cambridge scheme. Notice that each point uses a figure, compares the two companies, and says why it matters to Farah. A point that only says "Q has a higher yield" without the figures or the reason would be thin.

In my teaching, this is the part of Paper 3 students rush. Most of the time goes on the statement, and the advice gets whatever is left.

## How do I handle unrealised profit in a manufacturing question?

This appears often, and the March 2026 report listed it among the things many candidates could not show they understood. Here is my own short example.

> A company transfers finished goods from its factory at cost plus 20%. Finished goods inventory, at transfer price, was $27,000 at the start of the year and $36,000 at the end.

**Unrealised profit in each inventory**: the profit is 20/120 of the transfer price.
Opening: $27,000 × 20/120 = $4,500. Closing: $36,000 × 20/120 = $6,000.

**Provision for unrealised profit account**: the balance rises from $4,500 to $6,000, so the increase is $1,500.

**Statement of profit or loss**: the $1,500 increase is deducted from the factory profit, because that much of this year's factory profit sits in unsold inventory.

**Statement of financial position**: finished goods = $36,000 minus $6,000 = $30,000, which is their cost.

Check: $30,000 × 1.2 = $36,000.

The March 2023 report said the most common error in the provision account was reversing the entries. The March 2026 report said some did not deduct factory profit from the transfer price to find the production cost of goods completed. The March 2023 and March 2026 reports both mention factory profit left out of the statement of profit or loss.

## What mistakes do examiners report on Paper 3?

All from the 9706 Paper 3 reports since the format changed in 2023.

| Mistake (session and paper) | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Ignoring an instruction to support advice with calculations (March 2023 P32) | Put at least one figure from your workings in the advice |
| Giving facts about one item when asked for differences (March 2025 P32, no credit) | Write "whereas" sentences that compare both |
| Including the bank overdraft in gearing (March 2023 P32), or leaving out debentures (March 2025 P32) | Non-current liabilities only, over total long term capital |
| Not giving ratios to the decimal places asked (March 2023, June 2023 P31 and P33, March 2025, November 2023 P33) | Check the instruction before you round |
| Reversing the entries in the unrealised profit account (March 2023 P32) | An increase in the provision is debited to profit or loss |
| Statement of cash flows as a list, or entries in the wrong section (November 2023 P31) | Use the three IAS 7 headings |
| Advice that covers one side only (June 2023 P31 and P33) or is generic, not applied (June 2023 P32, November 2023 P33) | Two points each side, using the business in the question |
| Calculations given when the question said none were needed (November 2023 P32) | Answer in words when told to |

## How do I practise for Paper 3?

My order, as a teacher's advice:

1. Learn the formats first: one page each for the manufacturing account, club accounts, partnership realisation account, statement of cash flows and statement of changes in equity.
2. Do the statements from 2023 onwards on paper, timed at 28 minutes a question.
3. Mark them against the mark scheme line by line, then read the examiner report for that paper. The reports name the exact errors.
4. Keep a list of the IAS numbers with one sentence each, since short IAS parts appear inside many questions.

## Practice questions

These are my own short questions, with answers. Try each before you read the answer.

**1. Unrealised profit.** A factory transfers goods at cost plus 25%. Finished goods inventory at transfer price was $30,000 at the start of the year and $45,000 at the end. Calculate the change in the provision for unrealised profit and the closing inventory figure for the statement of financial position.

Answer: the profit is 25/125 of the transfer price. Opening provision $30,000 × 25/125 = $6,000. Closing provision $45,000 × 25/125 = $9,000. The provision increases by $3,000, which is deducted from factory profit. Closing inventory at cost: $45,000 minus $9,000 = $36,000. Check: $36,000 × 1.25 = $45,000.

**2. Gearing.** A company has 6% debentures of $900,000, a bank overdraft of $150,000, issued ordinary share capital of $1,500,000 and reserves of $600,000. Calculate the gearing ratio to two decimal places.

Answer: $900,000 ÷ ($1,500,000 + $600,000 + $900,000) × 100 = $900,000 ÷ $3,000,000 × 100 = 30.00%. The overdraft is a current liability, so it stays out.

**3. Dividend ratios.** Profit for the year is $480,000 and there are 2,000,000 ordinary shares, priced at $2.40. An interim dividend of $0.05 a share was paid and a final dividend of $0.07 a share is proposed. Calculate the dividend per share, dividend yield and dividend cover.

Answer: dividend per share $0.05 + $0.07 = $0.12. Dividend yield $0.12 ÷ $2.40 × 100 = 5.00%. Annual dividend $0.12 × 2,000,000 = $240,000, so dividend cover is $480,000 ÷ $240,000 = 2.00 times.

## FAQ

### How long is 9706 Paper 3 and how many marks?

1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks: three questions of 25 marks each, all compulsory. It is worth 30% of the A Level.

### Is cost accounting on Paper 3?

No. Paper 3 is Financial Accounting. Cost and management accounting is on Paper 4, which is 1 hour and 50 marks.

### Why are the 2022 Paper 3 papers 150 marks?

They were set on the older syllabus, at 3 hours and 150 marks, with a cost and management accounting section that is now Paper 4. Papers from March 2023 onwards follow the current 75 mark format.

### Can I use my own ratio formulas?

No. The syllabus says the formulae in its appendix are the only ones accepted in candidate answers.

### What is on the insert?

The source material for each question. The syllabus says more information is also given within each question on the question paper, where you write your answers.

### How do I get full marks on an advise question?

Give developed points on both sides, use figures from the question, and finish with a clear decision and a reason. In the schemes I checked, the decision itself was worth 1 mark.
