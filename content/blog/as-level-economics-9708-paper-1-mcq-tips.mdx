Paper 1 is a third of your AS Economics grade, and it is decided in one hour. Most students who drop marks here know the theory. They lose marks to a diagram read too fast, a calculation done the wrong way round, or an option that is true but does not answer the question. Here is the method I teach for each of those.

## Paper 1 facts (from the 9708 syllabus for 2026 to 2028)

| | AS Level Paper 1 |
|---|---|
| Questions | 30 multiple choice, answer all |
| Time | 1 hour |
| Marks | 30 |
| Weighting | 33% of the AS Level |
| Content | AS Level topics |
| Answers | On a separate answer sheet |

If you go on to the full A Level, Paper 3 is the multiple-choice paper at that stage: 30 questions in 1 hour 15 minutes, on A Level content, and it assumes you know the AS material.

### Your pace

30 questions in 60 minutes is **two minutes per question**. Definition questions take 30 seconds. That time is what pays for the diagram and calculation questions, so do not spend it rereading easy ones.

### Where students actually score

In the June 2024 examiner report for Paper 11, Cambridge says most candidates answered between 14 and 23 questions correctly. That is a wide spread on the same paper, and it is mostly technique, not knowledge.

## A method for each question type

### 1. Calculations: formula first

Write the formula in the margin before you look at the options. Then work it out, and only then match it to A, B, C or D. The wrong options are the answers you get from the usual slips, so if you read the options first, a wrong one will look right.

The usual slips:

- Dividing the wrong way round in elasticity.
- Using the change in units instead of the **percentage** change.
- Forgetting the sign: a negative income elasticity is the whole point of the question.

### 2. Diagrams: draw on the paper

1. Mark the starting equilibrium.
2. Decide which curve shifts and which way. Draw the new curve.
3. Mark the new equilibrium.
4. Now read the options.

The classic trap is an option that uses the new curve with the old price. If you have drawn both equilibria, you will spot it.

### 3. Tax and subsidy questions: who pays?

With a specific tax, the vertical gap between the two supply curves is the tax per unit. The price rise paid by consumers is their share. The rest is paid by producers. If demand is more inelastic than supply, consumers pay the larger share.

### 4. "Most likely" and NOT questions

For **NOT**, mark each option true or false. Three will be true. The odd one out is the answer.

For **most likely**, write the chain of effects as arrows, one step at a time: interest rate rises → borrowing costs more → consumption falls. More than one option may be possible. You want the one that follows most directly.

## Practise on these (my own questions)

I wrote these in the Paper 1 style. Cover the answer and try each one first.

**Question A.** A specific tax of $2 per unit is placed on a good. The market price rises from $10.00 to $11.50. What share of the tax is paid by consumers?

A 25%  
B 50%  
C 75%  
D 100%

**Answer: C.** Consumers pay $1.50 of the $2 tax: 1.50 ÷ 2 × 100 = 75%. Producers pay the other $0.50. Option A is the producers' share.

**Question B.** Incomes rise by 10% and the quantity demanded of a good falls by 5%. What type of good is it, and what is its income elasticity of demand?

A a normal good with YED of +0.5  
B an inferior good with YED of −0.5  
C an inferior good with YED of −2  
D a luxury good with YED of +2

**Answer: B.** YED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in income = −5 ÷ 10 = −0.5. A negative YED means an inferior good. Option C divides the wrong way round.

**Question C.** The price of good X rises by 20% and the quantity demanded of good Y rises by 10%. What is the cross elasticity of demand, and how are the goods related?

A +0.5, substitutes  
B +0.5, complements  
C −0.5, complements  
D +2, substitutes

**Answer: A.** XED = % change in quantity demanded of Y ÷ % change in price of X = 10 ÷ 20 = +0.5. A positive XED means substitutes: X became dearer, so people bought more Y.

**Question D.** With the same resources, Country P can produce either 10 cars or 20 tonnes of wheat. Country Q can produce either 6 cars or 18 tonnes of wheat. Which terms of trade would allow both countries to gain if P exports cars and Q exports wheat?

A 1 car for 1.5 tonnes of wheat  
B 1 car for 2.5 tonnes of wheat  
C 1 car for 3.5 tonnes of wheat  
D 1 car for 4 tonnes of wheat

**Answer: B.** The opportunity cost of 1 car is 2 tonnes of wheat in P (20 ÷ 10) and 3 tonnes in Q (18 ÷ 6). P has the comparative advantage in cars. Both gain only if 1 car trades for between 2 and 3 tonnes of wheat, and only B is in that range.

**Question E.** In a floating exchange rate system, what is most likely to cause a country's currency to appreciate?

A a rise in its imports  
B a fall in foreign demand for its exports  
C a rise in its interest rates compared with other countries  
D a rise in its inflation rate compared with other countries

**Answer: C.** Higher interest rates attract saving from abroad, which raises demand for the currency. A, B and D all reduce demand for the currency or raise its supply, so each would push it down.

## The traps that catch good students

| Trap | What to do |
|---|---|
| Movement vs shift | A change in the good's own price is a movement along the curve, never a shift. |
| Sign of elasticity | PED is negative for normal demand curves. YED and XED signs tell you the type of good. |
| Absolute words | "Always" and "never" are rarely right in economics, because effects depend on conditions. |
| True but irrelevant | An option can be a correct statement and still not answer the question asked. Reread the stem. |
| Units vs percentages | Elasticities use percentage changes, not the change in units. |

## A time plan for the hour

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0 to 40 minutes | First pass. Answer everything you can quickly. Circle the hard ones and move on. |
| 40 to 52 minutes | Second pass on the circled questions, using elimination. |
| 52 to 57 minutes | Make a final choice on anything still open. A blank cannot score. |
| 57 to 60 minutes | Check the answer sheet: one answer per row, in the right row. |

<Tip>
Shade the answer sheet as you go. If you skip a question, check that your next answer goes into the next numbered row, not the one you skipped. One slip early on can move every answer after it.
</Tip>

## How to practise

Do one past Paper 1 under timed conditions every week from January. When you mark it, sort every wrong answer into one of four groups: did not know the content, misread the question, calculation slip, or picked a true but irrelevant option. After three papers, one group will stand out. That is the thing to fix next.

If you keep losing marks to the same group, book a trial lesson and bring your marked papers. We will go through them together.
