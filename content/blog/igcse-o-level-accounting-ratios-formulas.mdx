From 2027, IGCSE Accounting 0452 and O Level Accounting 7707 list ten ratios: four for profitability, two for liquidity and four for efficiency. The syllabus prints a formula for each and says these are the only formulas accepted. Below is every formula, one full set of accounts with all ten ratios worked out, how to comment on them, and practice questions with answers.

Both syllabuses print the same list, word for word, so this page works for either exam. Keep it open while you practise. Everything about the syllabus comes from Cambridge's 0452 and 7707 syllabuses for 2027 to 2029, and the mistakes section comes from examiner reports from 2022 to 2026.

## Which ratios are in the 0452 and 7707 syllabus from 2027?

Topic 6, Analysis and interpretation, asks you to calculate and understand ten ratios. The formulas sit on the accounting ratios page at the end of the subject content. The note under topic 6.1 says you must use those formulas, and that they are "the only formulas accepted in candidate responses".

Here they are, grouped the way the syllabus groups them.

### Profitability ratios

Profitability ratios show how well a business turns sales and capital into profit.

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin (%) | Gross profit ÷ Revenue × 100 | How much gross profit each $100 of sales earns |
| Mark-up (%) | Gross profit ÷ Cost of sales × 100 | How much is added to the cost of goods to set the selling price |
| Profit margin (%) | Profit for the year ÷ Revenue × 100 | How much profit is left from each $100 of sales after all expenses |
| Return on capital employed (ROCE) (%) | Profit for the year before interest ÷ Capital employed × 100 | How much profit the business earns on the long-term money invested in it |

The syllabus defines **capital employed** as issued shares plus reserves plus non-current liabilities. That is the company version. The June 2025 examiner report confirms the same figure can be found as total assets less current liabilities.

### Liquidity ratios

Liquidity is the ability to pay debts that fall due soon.

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Current (working capital) ratio | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities | Whether current assets cover current liabilities |
| Acid test (liquid) ratio | (Current assets less inventory) ÷ Current liabilities | Whether the business can pay current liabilities without selling inventory |

The syllabus says both answers are presented as a ratio. So write 2.47 : 1, not 2.47 and not 247%.

### Efficiency ratios

Efficiency ratios show how quickly inventory sells and how quickly money comes in and goes out.

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Rate of inventory turnover (times) | Cost of sales ÷ Average inventory | How many times a year the business sells its average inventory |
| Inventory turnover (days) | Average inventory ÷ Cost of sales × 365 days | How many days, on average, goods sit before being sold |
| Trade receivables turnover (days) | Trade receivables ÷ Credit sales × 365 days | How many days credit customers take to pay |
| Trade payables turnover (days) | Trade payables ÷ Credit purchases × 365 days | How many days the business takes to pay its credit suppliers |

**Average inventory** is (opening inventory + closing inventory) ÷ 2. Notice that the two inventory ratios use average inventory, but the receivables and payables ratios use the trade receivables and trade payables figures you are given.

### Profitability: the workings

**Gross profit margin** = 120 000 ÷ 300 000 × 100 = **40.00%**

Use revenue after sales returns ($300 000), not $305 000. Examiner reports flag this mistake more than once (more below).

**Mark-up** = 120 000 ÷ 180 000 × 100 = **66.67%**

**Profit margin** = 36 000 ÷ 300 000 × 100 = **12.00%**

**ROCE**

- Profit for the year before interest = 36 000 + 3 000 = $39 000
- Capital employed = 150 000 + 20 000 + 34 000 + 50 000 = $254 000
- Check: total assets less current liabilities = 284 000 less 30 000 = $254 000
- ROCE = 39 000 ÷ 254 000 × 100 = **15.35%**

### Liquidity: the workings

**Current ratio** = 74 000 ÷ 30 000 = **2.47 : 1**

**Acid test ratio** = (74 000 less 32 000) ÷ 30 000 = 42 000 ÷ 30 000 = **1.40 : 1**

### Efficiency: the workings

Average inventory = (26 000 + 32 000) ÷ 2 = $29 000

**Rate of inventory turnover** = 180 000 ÷ 29 000 = **6.21 times**

**Inventory turnover (days)** = 29 000 ÷ 180 000 × 365 = **58.81 days**

**Trade receivables turnover** = 32 000 ÷ 240 000 × 365 = **48.67 days**

**Trade payables turnover** = 24 000 ÷ 160 000 × 365 = **54.75 days**

## How do you show the answer: %, times, days or : 1?

Examiner reports keep saying the same thing: candidates lose easy marks on presentation. Use this checklist.

| Ratio | Show the answer as |
|---|---|
| Gross profit margin, mark-up, profit margin, ROCE | A percentage, e.g. 40.00% |
| Current ratio, acid test ratio | A ratio to 1, e.g. 1.40 : 1 |
| Rate of inventory turnover | A number of times, e.g. 6.21 times |
| Inventory, trade receivables and trade payables turnover | A number of days, e.g. 48.67 days |

Always round to the number of decimal places the question asks for. Questions in the 2023 to 2026 reports often asked for two decimal places.

Calculating is the easier half. Writing about the ratios is where many students get stuck.

## How do you interpret accounting ratios?

A ratio on its own means very little. 12% profit margin is good or bad only when you compare it with something. Topic 6.2 asks you to compare results for different years, and topic 6.3 asks you to compare two businesses.

For every comment, do three things:

1. **Direction.** Has the ratio gone up or down?
2. **Judgement.** Is that an improvement or a worsening? The March 2023 examiner report criticised answers that said a ratio had increased but not whether it had improved.
3. **Reason or effect.** Why might it have changed, and what does it mean for the business?

### Comparing year on year

Here are Nova's ratios for 2026 next to the ratios for the year before (the 2025 figures are given, as they would be in an exam question).

| Ratio | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross profit margin | 36.00% | 40.00% |
| Mark-up | 56.25% | 66.67% |
| Profit margin | 13.50% | 12.00% |
| ROCE | 17.20% | 15.35% |
| Current ratio | 1.80 : 1 | 2.47 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 0.95 : 1 | 1.40 : 1 |
| Rate of inventory turnover | 7.50 times | 6.21 times |
| Inventory turnover | 48.67 days | 58.81 days |
| Trade receivables turnover | 40.00 days | 48.67 days |
| Trade payables turnover | 60.00 days | 54.75 days |

What a good student reads from this:

- **Gross profit margin improved.** Nova earns more gross profit on each $100 of sales. Possible reasons: higher selling prices, cheaper buying (a new supplier or trade discounts), or a change in the mix of goods sold.
- **Profit margin worsened.** So expenses took a bigger share of revenue. The gap between the two margins grew from 22.5 to 28 percentage points. Nova should look at wages and general expenses.
- **ROCE worsened.** Each $100 of capital employed earned less profit. This matters to shareholders, who could compare it with what they would earn elsewhere.
- **Liquidity improved.** The acid test ratio rose above 1 : 1, so Nova can now pay its current liabilities from liquid assets without selling inventory.
- **Efficiency worsened.** Inventory is selling more slowly and credit customers are paying later. Nova is also paying its suppliers sooner. All three put pressure on cash.

Notice how the ratios link. Slower inventory and slower receivables both raise current assets, which is partly why the current ratio rose. A higher current ratio is not always good news. It can mean money is sitting idle in inventory and debts owed by customers.

### What affects gross profit and profit for the year?

Topic 6.2 names the causes you should be ready to explain:

- **Gross profit** can change because of how closing inventory is valued, the quantity of goods sold, and changes in selling prices and purchase prices.
- **Profit for the year** can change because of changes in gross profit, other income and expenses.

An overvalued closing inventory lowers cost of sales, so it raises gross profit and profit. That is why a change in how inventory is valued can make one year look better than another with no real change in trading.

### Why can a profitable business be short of cash?

The syllabus also asks for the reasons cash and profit differ. The June 2023 examiner report noted that many candidates did not realise profit and cash are not the same. Common reasons:

- Buying non-current assets uses cash but only depreciation reaches the statement of profit or loss.
- Credit sales count as revenue before the cash arrives.
- Buying extra inventory uses cash but is not an expense until it is sold.
- Repaying loans, paying dividends or drawings use cash but are not expenses.
- Depreciation is an expense but no cash leaves the business.

### Comparing two businesses

Here is Nova next to a rival in the same trade, Orbit Limited (invented figures).

| Ratio | Nova | Orbit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross profit margin | 40.00% | 40.50% |
| Profit margin | 12.00% | 16.00% |
| Acid test ratio | 1.40 : 1 | 0.85 : 1 |

The gross margins are almost the same, which is what you would expect from two businesses selling similar goods. The difference is in expenses. Orbit keeps more of its gross profit, perhaps because it pays less in wages or runs from cheaper premises. But Orbit's acid test ratio is below 1 : 1, so it may struggle to pay its debts as they fall due.

The June 2023 examiner report made the same point about a question with two similar traders. Most candidates missed that a rival in the same business should have similar margins, so the difference had to come from expenses such as rent.

Topic 6.3 asks for the **factors that may affect the ratios of two businesses** and the **problems of inter-business comparison**. Points worth learning:

- They may use different accounting policies, for example different depreciation methods or inventory valuation, so the figures are not prepared on the same basis.
- One may own its premises and the other may rent them, which changes expenses and capital employed.
- They may be different sizes, in different locations, or have different year ends.
- They may not sell exactly the same goods, so "the same trade" can still hide big differences.
- Published statements give only the year-end position, which may not be typical of the whole year.

The November 2022 examiner report said many candidates named three problems of inter-firm comparison but wrote only a few words on each. If a question says "explain", develop each point into a full sentence with a reason.

## How do you write a 4 to 6 mark analysis or evaluation answer?

Written questions after a ratio calculation often ask you to comment, advise, or evaluate. The syllabus command words define **evaluate** as judging the quality, importance or value of something, and **advise** as suggesting a course of action. That means you need both sides and a decision.

Examiner reports give three repeated warnings:

- Relate every point to the business in the question. The March 2026 report criticised general statements that did not fit the scenario.
- Give a clear recommendation when asked to advise. The June 2022 report expected at least one advantage, one disadvantage and a clear recommendation.
- Read which year the given ratios belong to. The March 2023 report noted many candidates treated last year's ratios as this year's.

### My method in four steps

1. **Pick the right ratios.** If the question is about profitability, do not drift into liquidity.
2. **Make each point with evidence.** Quote both years' figures.
3. **Say what it means.** Improved or worsened, and one reason or effect.
4. **Conclude.** Answer the actual question in one or two sentences.

### Model answer

*Question: The directors of Nova Traders Limited say the business performed better in 2026 than in 2025. Use the ratios above to evaluate this view. (6 marks)*

> **For the view:** The gross profit margin rose from 36.00% to 40.00%. Nova now earns $4 more gross profit on every $100 of sales, possibly because it raised its selling prices or found a cheaper supplier. Liquidity also improved. The acid test ratio rose from 0.95 : 1 to 1.40 : 1, so Nova can now pay its current liabilities without selling inventory.
>
> **Against the view:** The profit margin fell from 13.50% to 12.00%. This shows expenses took a bigger share of revenue, so the better gross profit did not reach the final profit. ROCE fell from 17.20% to 15.35%, so shareholders earned less on the capital invested. Inventory now takes 58.81 days to sell instead of 48.67 days, and customers take 48.67 days to pay instead of 40.00, which ties up cash.
>
> **Conclusion:** Nova did not perform better overall. Trading improved and it is better able to pay its debts, but it made less profit on each dollar of sales and on its capital. The directors should control expenses and collect debts faster.

Why this works: every point has a figure from both years, a judgement, and a reason. It covers both sides and ends with a decision that answers the question.

### Suggestions for improving the ratios

Topic 6.2 also asks for suggestions to improve profitability, liquidity and working capital. Have these ready:

| To improve | Suggestions |
|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | Raise selling prices; find cheaper suppliers; ask for trade discounts |
| Profit margin | Cut expenses; increase other income such as rent or commission received |
| Liquidity | Collect debts faster; reduce inventory levels; bring in long-term finance; sell unused non-current assets |
| Trade receivables days | Offer cash discount for early payment; check customers' credit before selling; chase overdue debts |

Each suggestion has a cost. Cutting selling prices may raise sales but lowers the gross margin. The March 2023 report noted many candidates missed this when advising a trader on a price cut. Mention the downside, then decide.

## What are the limitations of ratio analysis?

Topic 6.5 names three limitations of accounting statements. Ratios are calculated from those statements, so they share the same limits.

- **Historic cost.** Assets are recorded at what they cost. Over time this may be far from their current value, so ROCE and other ratios can mislead.
- **Application of accounting policies.** Businesses choose methods such as how to depreciate and value inventory. Different choices give different profits, which makes comparison harder.
- **Non-financial aspects.** Statements leave out things that matter, such as the skill of the workforce, the location of the business and the economic climate.

The June 2022 examiner report noted that when asked for non-financial factors, some candidates gave financial ones such as demand and selling price. Reputation, staff reliability and government decisions are the kind of points that fit.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from 0452 and 7707 examiner reports from 2022 to 2026. I have grouped them so you can check your own work.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Using gross revenue instead of revenue less sales returns (0452 March 2026; 0452 and 7707 June 2023) | Deduct sales returns first, then divide |
| Getting gross profit or profit for the year wrong before the ratio (0452 June 2022, March 2025) | Rebuild the statement carefully; include other income in profit for the year (March 2023) |
| Swapping opening and closing inventory (0452 June 2022; 7707 November 2023) | Opening inventory is added in cost of sales, closing inventory is deducted |
| Wrong inventory turnover formula (0452 March 2023) | Cost of sales ÷ average inventory |
| Leaving inventory in the acid test, or deducting it from receivables only (0452 March 2023, March 2026) | Total current assets less inventory, then divide by current liabilities |
| Adding non-current assets or reserves to the liquidity ratios (0452 June 2022; June 2023) | Use only current assets and current liabilities |
| ROCE using profit after interest, or leaving out debentures (0452 November 2022, June 2025) | Add interest back; include non-current liabilities in capital employed |
| Wrong credit purchases figure for payables days (0452 June 2025) | Use credit purchases only, not total purchases |
| Missing %, times, days or : 1, or wrong decimal places (many reports) | Check the unit and rounding on every answer |
| No workings (0452 March 2023, June 2023, March 2025) | Show formula and figures every time |
| Saying a ratio rose or fell without saying if it improved (0452 March 2023) | Direction, judgement, reason |

Two more traps are worth knowing. The June 2022 report explained that a mark-up of 25% on cost is the same as 20% of revenue. Many candidates applied the 25% to the selling price. The November 2023 report on 7707 described a multiple choice question where candidates misapplied a 2 : 1 current ratio when working back to a missing figure.

<Tip title="Mark-up and margin are not the same">
Mark-up divides gross profit by cost of sales. Margin divides it by revenue. A 25% mark-up means cost $100, profit $25, selling price $125, so the margin is 25 ÷ 125 = 20%. Write out the three figures when you convert, rather than guessing.
</Tip>

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one before reading the answer. More real questions are in the [0452 past papers on accounting ratios](/past-papers/igcse-accounting-0452/topics/6-1-calculation-and-understanding-of-accounting-ratios).

### Question 1: calculate the ratios

Leila runs a gift shop. For the year ended 31 March 2026: revenue $150 000, cost of sales $105 000, profit for the year $15 000, inventory at 1 April 2025 $12 000, inventory at 31 March 2026 $16 000. There were no sales returns.

Calculate, to two decimal places: (a) gross profit margin, (b) mark-up, (c) profit margin, (d) rate of inventory turnover, (e) inventory turnover in days.

**Answer**

- Gross profit = 150 000 less 105 000 = $45 000
- (a) 45 000 ÷ 150 000 × 100 = **30.00%**
- (b) 45 000 ÷ 105 000 × 100 = **42.86%**
- (c) 15 000 ÷ 150 000 × 100 = **10.00%**
- Average inventory = (12 000 + 16 000) ÷ 2 = $14 000
- (d) 105 000 ÷ 14 000 = **7.50 times**
- (e) 14 000 ÷ 105 000 × 365 = **48.67 days**

### Question 2: missing figures and the effect of a transaction

Tariq's mark-up is 25% and his cost of sales is $80 000. His current assets are $45 000, including inventory of $15 000, and his current liabilities are $18 000.

(a) Calculate his revenue and gross profit margin. (b) Calculate his current ratio and acid test ratio. (c) Tariq pays $6 000 of trade payables from the bank. Calculate both ratios again.

**Answer**

- (a) Gross profit = 25% × 80 000 = $20 000. Revenue = 80 000 + 20 000 = **$100 000**. Gross profit margin = 20 000 ÷ 100 000 × 100 = **20.00%**
- (b) Current ratio = 45 000 ÷ 18 000 = **2.50 : 1**. Acid test = (45 000 less 15 000) ÷ 18 000 = 30 000 ÷ 18 000 = **1.67 : 1**
- (c) Current assets fall to $39 000 and current liabilities fall to $12 000. Current ratio = 39 000 ÷ 12 000 = **3.25 : 1**. Acid test = (39 000 less 15 000) ÷ 12 000 = 24 000 ÷ 12 000 = **2.00 : 1**

Both ratios rise. The same amount came off both figures, and liabilities were the smaller figure, so they fell by a bigger share. This works whenever the ratio starts above 1 : 1. This is a favourite multiple choice idea, so work through it rather than guessing.

### Question 3: comment and suggest

A trader's trade receivables turnover rose from 35 days to 52 days. Her trade payables turnover fell from 40 days to 30 days. Comment on these changes and suggest two ways to improve the position. (5 marks)

**Answer**

> Trade receivables turnover has worsened. Customers now take 52 days to pay instead of 35, so cash comes in 17 days later. This raises the risk of irrecoverable debts. Trade payables turnover has also changed: she now pays suppliers in 30 days instead of 40. This may keep suppliers happy, but it means cash leaves sooner. Together, cash comes in later and goes out sooner, so her liquidity is likely to suffer and she may need an overdraft.
>
> She could offer a cash discount to customers who pay early, though this would reduce her profit. She could also check customers' credit records before selling on credit and chase overdue debts promptly.

## FAQ

### How many ratios are in IGCSE Accounting 0452 from 2027?

Ten: four profitability ratios, two liquidity ratios and four efficiency ratios. O Level 7707 has the same ten.

### Can I use a different formula if it gives the same answer?

The syllabus says its printed formulas are the only ones accepted. Use them exactly, and write the formula in your workings so the examiner can follow you.

### What is capital employed in ROCE?

The syllabus defines it as issued shares plus reserves plus non-current liabilities. The same figure is total assets less current liabilities. The profit used is profit for the year before interest.

### Do I use average inventory or closing inventory?

Use average inventory for both inventory ratios: (opening + closing) ÷ 2. Receivables and payables days use the trade receivables and trade payables figures given.

### What is a good current ratio?

The syllabus does not give a target figure. Judge it against the business's previous year and similar businesses, and remember a very high ratio can mean cash is tied up in inventory and receivables.

### How many decimal places should ratio answers have?

As many as the question asks for. Recent papers have usually asked for two, and examiner reports say marks are lost when this instruction is ignored.
