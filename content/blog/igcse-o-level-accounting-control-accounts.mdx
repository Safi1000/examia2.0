A control account is one summary account that holds the totals of a whole ledger. The sales ledger control account (trade receivables control) adds up everything owed by credit customers. The purchases ledger control account (trade payables control) adds up everything owed to credit suppliers. You build each one from totals in the books of prime entry, then balance it like any ledger account.

## What does the syllabus say about control accounts?

Control accounts are section 3.4 of the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-3.4) and of the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. You need to understand four things:

1. The purposes of purchases ledger and sales ledger control accounts.
2. The books of prime entry as the sources of information for each entry.
3. How to prepare both accounts with credit sales and purchases, receipts and payments, cash discounts, returns, irrecoverable debts, dishonoured cheques, interest on overdue accounts, contra entries, refunds, and opening and closing balances on both sides.
4. How digital recording of transactions affects the preparation and use of control accounts.

The syllabus adds a note: you do not need to reconcile a control account balance with the list of ledger balances. So you will not be asked to find why the two totals differ.

From 2027, Paper 1 is 40 multiple choice questions in 1 hour 30 minutes. Paper 2 is a written paper of 1 hour 45 minutes for 100 marks. The 2022 to 2026 examiner reports show control accounts in both papers.

## What are the sales ledger and purchases ledger?

A business that sells on credit keeps one account for each customer. All the customer accounts together form the **sales ledger**. One account for each credit supplier forms the **purchases ledger**.

With 200 customers, the total owed is spread across 200 accounts. A control account puts that total in one place. It records the same transactions as the individual accounts, but as monthly totals.

| Name you may see | Means |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger control account | Trade receivables control account |
| Purchases ledger control account | Trade payables control account |
| Debtors and creditors | Older names for trade receivables and trade payables |

## Why do businesses keep control accounts?

The March 2023 Paper 2 mark scheme accepted these purposes:

- They help to **locate errors**. If the control account balance does not match the total of the ledger, the error is in that ledger.
- They check the arithmetic of the ledger they control.
- They give the total of trade receivables and trade payables at once.
- They let draft financial statements be prepared quickly.
- They summarise the transactions affecting trade receivables and trade payables.
- They may help to **reduce fraud**. The March 2026 report explained why: the account should be prepared by someone who does not write up the ledger.

Watch two words. Control accounts help to locate errors, not correct them. They reduce fraud, they do not prevent it. The March 2023 and March 2026 reports both listed preventing fraud and correcting errors as common wrong answers.

Say "at once" or "quickly" too. The March 2023 report called answers about totals incomplete when they left out the time factor.

### Is a control account always worth keeping?

No. In March 2026, Paper 2 described a trader with very few credit sales. Strong answers said a control account would not be worthwhile for that trader. General answers such as "time consuming" were listed as common incorrect answers, so use the facts in the question.

### Is a control account part of the double entry?

In these syllabuses, no. The double entry is made in the individual customer and supplier accounts. The control account is a checking device built from totals. The March 2026 mark scheme gave a mark for saying the duality principle does not apply to it.

## Where does each figure come from?

Every entry comes from a book of prime entry, never from the customer or supplier accounts. The November 2023 7707 report said a significant number of candidates did not know that the sales ledger accounts are not a source.

| Item | Book of prime entry |
|---|---|
| Credit sales | Sales journal |
| Sales returns (returns inwards) | Sales returns journal |
| Credit purchases | Purchases journal |
| Purchases returns (returns outwards) | Purchases returns journal |
| Money received or paid, discounts allowed and received, dishonoured cheques, refunds | Cash book |
| Irrecoverable debts, interest on overdue accounts, contra entries | General journal |

Cash discounts sit in the discount columns of the cash book. That is why discount allowed and discount received come from the cash book. The November 2022 Paper 2 mark scheme (variant 22) listed contra entries and interest charged by suppliers under the general journal. In June 2025 Paper 1, a significant number of candidates wrongly put purchases returns in the general journal.

## Which side does each item go in the sales ledger control account?

Think of the sales ledger control account as one giant customer. Anything that increases what customers owe goes on the debit side. Anything that reduces it goes on the credit side.

| Debit side (increases trade receivables) | Credit side (reduces trade receivables) |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (debit balances at the start) | Balance b/d (credit balances at the start) |
| Credit sales | Sales returns |
| Dishonoured cheques | Money received (bank and cash) |
| Interest charged on overdue accounts | Discount allowed |
| Refunds paid to customers | Irrecoverable debts |
| Balance c/d (credit balances at the end) | Contra (set-off) with the purchases ledger |
| | Balance c/d (debit balances at the end) |

Four items need a sentence each.

- **Dishonoured cheque.** A customer's cheque their bank refused to pay. The customer owes the money again, so debit. It is not an irrecoverable debt. The November 2022 report made that point in Paper 1.
- **Interest on overdue accounts.** Interest you charge a customer who paid late. It increases what they owe.
- **Credit balances.** A customer who has overpaid, or returned goods after paying, has a credit balance. You owe them, so it is a liability. In the November 2023 7707 report, a significant proportion of candidates could not recognise this.
- **Refund.** When you repay that customer, the credit balance is cleared. So the refund goes on the debit side.

## Which side does each item go in the purchases ledger control account?

The purchases ledger control account is one giant supplier. Anything that increases what you owe goes on the credit side. Anything that reduces it goes on the debit side.

| Debit side (reduces trade payables) | Credit side (increases trade payables) |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (debit balances at the start) | Balance b/d (credit balances at the start) |
| Purchases returns | Credit purchases |
| Money paid (bank and cash) | Interest charged by suppliers |
| Discount received | Refunds received from suppliers |
| Contra (set-off) with the sales ledger | Balance c/d (debit balances at the end) |
| Balance c/d (credit balances at the end) | |

A debit balance in the purchases ledger usually means you overpaid a supplier. The June 2022 Paper 1 report worked through exactly this. An overpayment was carried down as a debit balance alongside the usual credit balance.

### What is a contra entry?

Sometimes a business buys from and sells to the same firm. Say Bilal owes you $500 as a customer and you owe Bilal $800 as a supplier. You agree to set off $500, so only the net $300 changes hands.

The November 2022 mark scheme describes the entry. Debit Bilal's account in the purchases ledger and credit Bilal's account in the sales ledger. In the control accounts this means:

- Debit the purchases ledger control account.
- Credit the sales ledger control account.

The contra is always the same amount in both accounts. It always reduces both trade receivables and trade payables.

### Which items never go in a control account?

| Item | Why it stays out |
|---|---|
| Cash sales and cash purchases | No credit customer or supplier owes anything |
| Trade discount | It never appears in the ledger |
| Discount received (in the sales ledger control) | It belongs to suppliers |
| Discount allowed and irrecoverable debts (in the purchases ledger control) | They belong to customers |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | It is an estimate. No customer's account changes |

The cash, trade discount and wrong ledger rows match errors named in the examiner reports. The November 2022 report put it plainly: "Cash transactions will not be recorded in the purchase ledger". The last row is my own teaching point. I have not seen it in a report, but the rule is the same: only what changes a customer's account goes in.

<Tip title="Name the account, not the event">
Write "Bank", "Sales returns" or "Discount allowed" in the details column. Never "money received from customers". The March 2023 and November 2022 reports both took marks off for descriptions.
</Tip>

This is where students get stuck. The reports show marks lost on the contra, on extra items and on account names far more than on arithmetic.

## How do you prepare control accounts step by step?

This is the method I teach.

1. **Cross out the distractors.** Cash sales, cash purchases, trade discount and anything for the other ledger.
2. **Enter the opening balances.** The main balance first, then the small balance on the opposite side.
3. **Post each total to its side.** Use the tables above. Use the account name in the details.
4. **Enter the closing small balance** if one is given. It is a balance c/d on the opposite side to where it will sit.
5. **Find the main balance c/d** as the difference between the two sides.
6. **Total both sides and bring both balances down** on the first day of the next period.

## How do you balance both control accounts? A worked example

Imran is a wholesaler who keeps both control accounts. The figures below are for October 2026.

| Item | $ |
|---|---:|
| Sales ledger debit balances, 1 October | 18 400 |
| Sales ledger credit balances, 1 October | 260 |
| Purchases ledger credit balances, 1 October | 12 300 |
| Purchases ledger debit balances, 1 October | 180 |
| Credit sales | 42 750 |
| Cash sales | 6 300 |
| Credit purchases | 27 640 |
| Cash purchases | 2 100 |
| Sales returns | 1 850 |
| Purchases returns | 1 120 |
| Received from credit customers: bank | 36 900 |
| Received from credit customers: cash | 1 200 |
| Paid to credit suppliers by bank | 24 580 |
| Discount allowed | 740 |
| Discount received | 610 |
| Irrecoverable debts written off | 980 |
| Customer's cheque dishonoured | 450 |
| Interest charged to a customer on an overdue account | 75 |
| Interest charged by a supplier on an overdue account | 90 |
| Refund paid by bank to a customer with a credit balance | 160 |
| Refund received by bank from a supplier with a debit balance | 180 |
| Sales ledger balance set off against the purchases ledger | 620 |
| Sales ledger credit balances, 31 October | 140 |
| Purchases ledger debit balances, 31 October | 95 |

Prepare the sales ledger control account and the purchases ledger control account for October 2026.

### Step 1: remove the distractors

Cash sales of $6 300 and cash purchases of $2 100 stay out. No credit customer or supplier is involved.

### Step 2: the sales ledger control account

**Imran: Sales ledger control account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Oct 1 Balance b/d | 18 400 | Oct 1 Balance b/d | 260 |
| Oct 31 Sales | 42 750 | Oct 31 Sales returns | 1 850 |
| Bank (dishonoured cheque) | 450 | Bank | 36 900 |
| Interest charged | 75 | Cash | 1 200 |
| Bank (refund) | 160 | Discount allowed | 740 |
| Balance c/d | 140 | Irrecoverable debts | 980 |
| | | Contra | 620 |
| | | Balance c/d | 19 425 |
| | **61 975** | | **61 975** |
| Nov 1 Balance b/d | 19 425 | Nov 1 Balance b/d | 140 |

Debit side: 18 400 + 42 750 + 450 + 75 + 160 + 140 = 61 975.

Credit side before the balance: 260 + 1 850 + 36 900 + 1 200 + 740 + 980 + 620 = 42 550. So the closing debit balance is 61 975 − 42 550 = $19 425.

The account name for the dishonoured cheque and the refund is "Bank". The words in brackets are my notes so you can tell the lines apart.

Credit customers owe Imran $19 425. Imran owes some customers $140.

### Step 3: the purchases ledger control account

**Imran: Purchases ledger control account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Oct 1 Balance b/d | 180 | Oct 1 Balance b/d | 12 300 |
| Oct 31 Purchases returns | 1 120 | Oct 31 Purchases | 27 640 |
| Bank | 24 580 | Interest charged | 90 |
| Discount received | 610 | Bank (refund) | 180 |
| Contra | 620 | Balance c/d | 95 |
| Balance c/d | 13 195 | | |
| | **40 305** | | **40 305** |
| Nov 1 Balance b/d | 95 | Nov 1 Balance b/d | 13 195 |

Credit side: 12 300 + 27 640 + 90 + 180 + 95 = 40 305.

Debit side before the balance: 180 + 1 120 + 24 580 + 610 + 620 = 27 110. So the closing credit balance is 40 305 − 27 110 = $13 195.

Imran owes credit suppliers $13 195. One supplier owes Imran $95.

### Step 4: read the balances

- The supplier refund of $180 clears the opening debit balance of $180.
- In the statement of financial position, the $19 425 is trade receivables and the $13 195 is trade payables. The small balances on the "wrong" side are shown separately: the $140 as a current liability and the $95 as a current asset.

## How do you find a missing figure with a control account?

In incomplete records questions you often know the opening and closing trade receivables but not the credit sales. Set up the control account and the missing figure is the balancing figure. The June 2025 report said a simple control account would have helped candidates find these figures.

Ayesha's figures for the year ended 30 June 2026:

| Item | $ |
|---|---:|
| Trade receivables, 1 July 2025 | 9 600 |
| Trade receivables, 30 June 2026 | 11 250 |
| Received from credit customers | 48 300 |
| Discount allowed | 900 |
| Irrecoverable debts | 450 |
| Sales returns | 1 200 |

**Ayesha: Sales ledger control account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 9 600 | Sales returns | 1 200 |
| Credit sales (balancing figure) | 52 500 | Bank | 48 300 |
| | | Discount allowed | 900 |
| | | Irrecoverable debts | 450 |
| | | Balance c/d | 11 250 |
| | **62 100** | | **62 100** |

Credit side: 1 200 + 48 300 + 900 + 450 + 11 250 = 62 100. So credit sales are 62 100 − 9 600 = $52 500.

The same method finds credit purchases from a purchases ledger control account. My [incomplete records guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-incomplete-records) shows where that figure goes next.

## How does digital recording change control accounts?

The syllabus lists this point, but none of the examiner reports I checked has covered it yet. So this is my own explanation, not a mark scheme answer.

On accounting software, each transaction is entered once. The program updates the customer's or supplier's account and the totals together. So a control account can be produced at any time, and arithmetic errors in the totals are less likely.

Software does not stop an entry going to the wrong customer, or being left out. Someone still has to review the figures.

## How is a control account question marked?

I checked four Paper 2 mark schemes: 0452 March 2022, November 2022, March 2023 and March 2025. The pattern was the same:

- **Usually one mark for each correct entry**, with the right account name, on the right side. In March 2023, cash and bank shared one mark.
- **One mark for both opening balances together** when there were two, in March 2022 and November 2022.
- **No mark for the balance c/d itself.** The mark is for the balance b/d, as an own figure mark.
- In March 2023, the sales ledger control account was worth 6 marks and the purchases ledger control account 7.

An own figure mark rewards your correct closing balance even if an earlier entry was wrong. So always total both sides and bring the balance down.

The November 2022 report added that every entry needs a date, the name of the other account and the amount. Short written parts also appear: the purposes of control accounts, a contra explained in words, or the book of prime entry for each item.

In Paper 1, the reports describe questions on the source of a figure, the purpose of the account, and the balances left after an overpayment.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the examiner reports. November 2023 is the 7707 report and the rest are 0452.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Including cash sales or cash purchases (March 2022, November 2022, March 2023, March 2026) | Credit transactions only |
| Describing the event in the details column (November 2022, March 2023) | Name the account: "Sales returns", "Bank" |
| Leaving out the contra or putting it on the wrong side (November 2022, March 2023) | Credit in sales ledger control, debit in purchases ledger control |
| Items from the other ledger: discount received in sales, irrecoverable debts in purchases (March 2022, March 2023) | Each discount and each debt belongs to one ledger only |
| Including trade discount or the purchases account total (March 2025) | Trade discount never enters the books. Use credit purchases only |
| Posting each invoice and receipt separately (March 2025, November 2023) | Post monthly totals from the books of prime entry |
| Leaving out the opening balance (March 2025) | Start with balance b/d on both sides if there are two |
| Saying control accounts prevent fraud or correct errors (March 2023, March 2026) | They reduce fraud and help locate errors |

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper first. All figures are my own.

### Practice question 1

For each item, name the control account, the side and the book of prime entry.

1. A customer's cheque for $300 was dishonoured.
2. A supplier charged $45 interest on an overdue account.
3. A debt of $210 was written off as irrecoverable.
4. A $400 balance in the sales ledger was set off against the purchases ledger.

**Answer**

| Item | Account and side | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales ledger control, debit | Cash book |
| 2 | Purchases ledger control, credit | General journal |
| 3 | Sales ledger control, credit | General journal |
| 4 | Sales ledger control credit, purchases ledger control debit | General journal |

### Practice question 2

Sara's purchases ledger had credit balances of $5 400 on 1 May. During May: credit purchases $18 200, cash purchases $1 300, payments to suppliers by bank $16 900, discount received $350, purchases returns $640 and a contra of $300. Calculate the closing balance on the purchases ledger control account.

**Answer**

Leave out the cash purchases of $1 300.

Credit side: 5 400 + 18 200 = 23 600.

Debit side: 16 900 + 350 + 640 + 300 = 18 190.

Closing balance: 23 600 − 18 190 = $5 410 credit, brought down on 1 June.

### Practice question 3

Omar does not record the money received from credit customers. For the year: trade receivables at the start $6 800 and at the end $7 350, credit sales $31 500, sales returns $900, discount allowed $420, irrecoverable debts $380 and a dishonoured cheque of $250. Calculate the money received from credit customers.

**Answer**

**Omar: Sales ledger control account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance b/d | 6 800 | Sales returns | 900 |
| Sales | 31 500 | Bank (balancing figure) | 29 500 |
| Bank (dishonoured cheque) | 250 | Discount allowed | 420 |
| | | Irrecoverable debts | 380 |
| | | Balance c/d | 7 350 |
| | **38 550** | | **38 550** |

Debit side: 6 800 + 31 500 + 250 = 38 550. Other credits: 900 + 420 + 380 + 7 350 = 9 050. Money received: 38 550 − 9 050 = $29 500.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between a control account and a sales ledger?

The sales ledger holds one account for each credit customer. The sales ledger control account holds the totals of all of them in one account.

### Is the sales ledger control account the same as the trade receivables control account?

Yes. They are two names for the same account. The purchases ledger control account is also called the trade payables control account.

### Why is a dishonoured cheque debited to the sales ledger control account?

The customer's payment has been cancelled, so they owe the money again. It is not an irrecoverable debt.

### Which side does a contra entry go?

Credit in the sales ledger control account and debit in the purchases ledger control account, with the same amount in both.

### Do cash sales go in the sales ledger control account?

No. Only credit transactions do, because a cash customer never owes the business anything.

### Do I need to reconcile the control account with the ledger balances?

No. The 2027 to 2029 syllabus says this is not required for 0452 or 7707.
