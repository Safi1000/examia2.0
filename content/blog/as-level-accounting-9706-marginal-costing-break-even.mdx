Marginal costing treats only variable costs as the cost of a product. Fixed costs are charged in full against the period. The key figure is contribution: selling price minus variable cost per unit. Break-even point is total fixed costs divided by contribution per unit, always rounded up to a whole unit. In AS Accounting 9706 this is syllabus section 2.2.3, with cost volume profit analysis in 2.2.4.

## What is contribution, and why does marginal costing use it?

**Variable costs** change in total with output: direct materials, direct labour paid per unit, variable overheads, and commission on sales. **Fixed costs** stay the same in total over a range of output: rent, salaries, depreciation.

**Marginal cost** is the extra cost of making one more unit. The March 2026 Paper 22 mark scheme gave the mark for exactly that idea. The June 2023 Paper 23 report said few candidates defined marginal cost correctly, often leaving out the one additional unit.

**Contribution per unit** is selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit. It is what each unit adds towards fixed costs. Once fixed costs are covered, contribution becomes profit.

That is why marginal costing is used for short term decisions. Fixed costs will be paid whatever you decide this month, so the question is simply: does this choice add contribution?

### What are the formulas?

The 9706 syllabus appendix lists ratio formulas but no costing formulas, so learn these by heart. These are the standard versions, written my way.

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Contribution per unit | Selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit |
| Break-even point (units) | Total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit |
| Contribution to sales ratio | Contribution per unit ÷ selling price per unit (or total contribution ÷ total revenue) |
| Break-even revenue | Total fixed costs ÷ contribution to sales ratio |
| Units for a target profit | (Total fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit |
| Margin of safety (units) | Budgeted or actual sales in units minus break-even units |
| Margin of safety (value) | Margin of safety in units × selling price per unit |

## How do I work through a full marginal costing question?

Here is my own example. Every figure is checked.

> Rehana Lamps makes one model of table lamp. Each lamp sells for $40. Variable costs per lamp: direct materials $12, direct labour $9, variable overheads $3. Fixed costs are $28,800 a month. Budgeted sales are 2,400 lamps a month.

**Step 1: contribution per unit.**
Variable cost = $12 + $9 + $3 = $24. Contribution = $40 minus $24 = $16 per lamp.

**Step 2: break-even point in units.**
$28,800 ÷ $16 = 1,800 lamps.

**Step 3: contribution to sales ratio and break-even revenue.**
$16 ÷ $40 = 0.4, or 40%. Break-even revenue = $28,800 ÷ 0.4 = $72,000. Check: 1,800 × $40 = $72,000.

**Step 4: margin of safety.**
In units: 2,400 minus 1,800 = 600 lamps. In value: 600 × $40 = $24,000. As a percentage of budgeted sales: 600 ÷ 2,400 = 25%.

**Step 5: profit at budgeted sales.**
600 lamps above break-even × $16 = $9,600.

**Step 6: the marginal costing statement.**

| Rehana Lamps: marginal costing statement for one month | $ | $ |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (2,400 × $40) | | 96,000 |
| Direct materials (2,400 × $12) | 28,800 | |
| Direct labour (2,400 × $9) | 21,600 | |
| Variable overheads (2,400 × $3) | 7,200 | 57,600 |
| Contribution | | 38,400 |
| Fixed costs | | 28,800 |
| Profit for the month | | 9,600 |

The statement and the shortcut agree: $9,600 both ways.

**Step 7: target profit.**
The owner wants $12,500 profit a month. Units needed = ($28,800 + $12,500) ÷ $16 = $41,300 ÷ $16 = 2,581.25, so 2,582 lamps.

Round up, because 2,581 lamps give $41,296 of contribution and only $12,496 profit. 2,582 lamps give $12,512, which reaches the target.

### Why must break-even and target units be rounded up?

You cannot sell part of a lamp, and rounding down leaves you just short. Four examiner reports raise this. The November 2022 Paper 22 report said a minority of candidates did not know a break-even point must always be a whole number, rounded up, and that some lost marks for wrong rounding on the target profit units. The March 2023 Paper 22, June 2022 Paper 23 and June 2023 Paper 23 reports made the same point about rounding break-even up.

The mark schemes show it too. In November 2022 Paper 22, fixed costs of $20,700 and a contribution of $2.45 gave 8,449 units, though the division is 8,448.98.

<Tip title="Always round break-even up">
A break-even answer of 1,812.5 units is 1,813 units. Show the division with its decimals, then write the rounded whole number with the word units after it.
</Tip>

## How do I lay out a marginal costing statement?

Two recent reports name layout faults, so the labels carry real marks.

1. Start with **revenue**, not "selling price". The March 2026 Paper 22 report said some candidates wrote selling price instead of revenue, and that missing labels matter in this statement.
2. List each variable cost on its own line, then total them.
3. Label the next total **contribution**. The November 2023 Paper 21 report said several candidates did not label contribution correctly.
4. Deduct fixed costs, then label the last line **profit** (or "profit for the month"). The same report said the final figure should be labelled profit.

Two traps sit inside the figures:

- **Commission on sales is a variable cost.** The November 2022 Paper 22 report said several candidates did not see this, so their contribution was wrong.
- **Use the period the question asks for.** The June 2023 Paper 23 report said some candidates used a full year of fixed costs instead of one month. The June 2022 Paper 23 report said the same about a full year of depreciation.

You can also work per unit and then multiply. The November 2022 Paper 22 mark scheme accepted both a total layout and a per unit layout that ended with total contribution. What does not work is a per unit profit: the March 2026 Paper 22 report said a few candidates started with a per unit calculation and then took away the total fixed costs.

## Why do marginal and absorption costing give different profits?

Absorption costing puts a share of fixed production overheads into the cost of each unit. Marginal costing does not. So when inventory changes, some fixed overhead sits in closing inventory under absorption costing and moves into the next period.

Rehana Lamps again. Suppose this month it makes 2,500 lamps and sells 2,400, with no opening inventory. Of the $28,800 fixed costs, $20,000 are fixed production overheads, absorbed over the budgeted 2,500 lamps at $8 a lamp. The other $8,800 are administration and selling. Actual production and overheads equal the budget, so nothing is under or over absorbed.

| | Marginal costing | Absorption costing |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per lamp for inventory | $24 | $24 + $8 = $32 |
| Closing inventory (100 lamps) | $2,400 | $3,200 |
| Profit for the month | $9,600 | $10,400 |

Absorption profit: revenue $96,000 minus cost of sales $76,800 ($80,000 production minus $3,200 closing inventory) = $19,200, minus $8,800 other fixed costs = $10,400.

The reconciliation:

| Reconciliation for the month | $ |
|---|---|
| Profit using absorption costing | 10,400 |
| Less fixed overheads carried forward in closing inventory (100 × $8) | (800) |
| Profit using marginal costing | 9,600 |

The rule: when inventory rises, absorption costing shows the higher profit. When inventory falls, marginal costing does. The November 2023 Paper 21 mark scheme listed "higher profit when inventory levels increase" as a point for absorption costing.

This statement is short but often missed. The November 2023 Paper 21 report said candidates were clearly not familiar with it, and only well prepared candidates scored. Its 3 marks in that scheme went to the absorption profit, the inventory adjustment and the marginal profit.

## How does marginal costing help with decisions?

The syllabus names five decisions. The rule behind all of them is the same: compare the contribution gained with any extra costs, and ignore fixed costs that will be paid anyway.

| Decision | What to compare |
|---|---|
| Special order at a lower price | Contribution from the order minus any extra fixed costs; check there is spare capacity |
| Make-or-buy | Variable cost of making against the buying price, plus any fixed costs saved |
| Closing a business unit | Contribution lost against fixed costs saved |
| Limiting factor | Contribution per unit of the scarce resource |
| Target profit | Units or price needed to cover fixed costs plus the target |

For Rehana Lamps: a hotel group offers to buy 300 lamps at $30 each, with $300 of extra delivery cost. The factory can make 2,800 lamps a month, so there is room. Contribution per lamp on the order = $30 minus $24 = $6. Extra profit = (300 × $6) minus $300 = $1,500. On the numbers, accept.

Then weigh the factors that are not in the numbers. Regular customers may hear of the $30 price and ask for it. The hotel group may become a repeat buyer. These are the non-financial factors the syllabus asks you to consider.

### How do I rank products with a limiting factor?

When a resource such as materials or labour hours is short, rank products by **contribution per unit of the limiting factor**, not by contribution per unit.

My own example: a business has 9,000 labour hours a month.

| | Product A | Product B |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution per unit | $16 | $21 |
| Labour hours per unit | 2 | 3 |
| Contribution per labour hour | $8 | $7 |
| Monthly demand | 3,000 units (6,000 hours) | 1,500 units (4,500 hours) |

Demand needs 10,500 hours, so labour limits output. A ranks first. Make 3,000 of A (6,000 hours), then use the last 3,000 hours for 1,000 of B. Total contribution = $48,000 + $21,000 = $69,000.

Ranking by contribution per unit would put B first: 1,500 of B ($31,500), then 2,250 of A with the 4,500 hours left ($36,000), a total of $67,500. That is $1,500 less.

The November 2022 Paper 21 report said candidates who used contribution per unit of the limiting factor usually scored high, while some ranked by contribution per unit and ignored the limiting factor. The March 2025 Paper 22 report said many candidates did not understand the relevance of contribution when a material was limited.

In my teaching, this is where students get stuck: the formulas are fine, but a two option question with a limiting factor needs a clear plan of workings before any numbers go down.

## How do I read a break-even chart?

You only interpret a chart in 9706. Output is on the horizontal axis and dollars on the vertical axis.

| What you are asked | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Fixed costs | Where the total cost line meets the vertical axis |
| Break-even point | Where total revenue crosses total cost; read down for units, across for revenue |
| Profit or loss at an output | The vertical gap between revenue and total cost at that output |
| Margin of safety | The horizontal distance from break-even output to budgeted output |

Margin of safety can also be given as a percentage. Then break-even revenue is the rest of sales. For Rehana Lamps, the margin of safety is 25%, so break-even revenue of $72,000 is 75% of sales, and sales are $72,000 ÷ 0.75 = $96,000. The March 2026 Paper 12 report, on a multiple choice question of this kind, said candidates could work out break-even revenue, and that this figure then had to be treated as the remaining percentage of sales revenue.

## What are the advantages and limitations of break-even and CVP analysis?

These are my own summaries of points in the November 2022 Paper 22 and June 2022 Paper 23 mark schemes.

| Advantages | Limitations (the assumptions) |
|---|---|
| Helps short term decisions | Fixed costs stay the same over the relevant range |
| Finds the best mix of price, cost and volume | Variable cost per unit stays the same |
| Helps set a price that covers costs | Selling price per unit stays the same |
| Helps control costs | One product, or a constant mix of products |
| | All costs split neatly into fixed and variable |
| | Inventory levels do not change |

The November 2022 Paper 22 report said answers on both the advantages and the limitations of CVP analysis were generally very poor. In June 2022 Paper 23, the report said some candidates left out "per unit" when writing about selling price and variable cost. "The selling price stays the same" is vague. "The selling price per unit stays the same" is the assumption.

### What are the uses and limitations of marginal costing?

The March 2023 Paper 22 mark scheme credited points such as: it avoids sharing out fixed costs on an arbitrary basis, avoids under or over absorption, helps short term decisions, identifies break-even and margin of safety, and compares products. The report for that paper said few gave correct answers, because many wrote "useful for decision making" without the key word short term.

The November 2023 Paper 21 mark scheme listed points on both sides. For marginal costing they included that not every cost can be divided into fixed and variable, and that the method fits a business making one product best. For absorption costing, one credited point was that IAS 2 accepts it.

## How are marginal costing questions marked?

These patterns come from the Paper 2 mark schemes named. The 2022 papers were 1 hour 30 minutes, not 1 hour 45, for the same 90 marks.

| Question type | Marks seen | How marks are given |
|---|---|---|
| Define a term (contribution, margin of safety, marginal cost) | 1 each | One precise idea (March 2023 P22, March 2026 P22) |
| Break-even point | 2 or 3 | Contribution, method, answer in whole units; own figure marks follow on (March 2023 P22, November 2022 P22) |
| Margin of safety formula | 1 each | Separate marks for units and value in June 2022 P23 |
| Target profit units | 2 | Method, then rounded answer (November 2022 P22) |
| Marginal costing statement | 6 to 10 | Mostly 1 mark per line (6 for two months in November 2023 P21); contribution and profit were own figure marks in November 2022 P22 (10 marks) and March 2023 P22 (7 marks) |
| Reconciliation | 3 | Absorption profit, inventory adjustment, marginal profit (November 2023 P21) |
| Advise or recommend | 5 to 7 | Points on each option, then a mark for the decision (June 2022 P23, November 2022 P22, November 2023 P21) |

**Own figure** (OF) marks matter. If your contribution is wrong, your break-even point can still score if you use your own figure correctly. So never leave a later part blank because an earlier part went wrong.

For the advice question, the syllabus says you must support a recommendation with a balance of reasons and justify it. In the November 2022 Paper 22 scheme, the comments carried up to 6 marks and the decision 1. The June 2023 Paper 23 report said some candidates repeated information from the question. The June 2022 Paper 23 report said some only commented on the option they recommended. Discuss both options, then decide.

## What mistakes do examiners report most?

| Mistake (session and paper) | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Not rounding break-even up (November 2022 P22, March 2023 P22, June 2022 P23, June 2023 P23) | Always round up to the next whole unit |
| Treating sales commission as fixed (November 2022 P22) | Commission varies with sales, so it is variable |
| A year of fixed costs or depreciation in a monthly question (June 2022 P23, June 2023 P23) | Convert to the period asked for |
| Ranking by contribution per unit when a resource is short (November 2022 P21) | Rank by contribution per unit of the limiting factor |
| Missing contribution and profit labels (November 2023 P21, March 2026 P22) | Label revenue, contribution and profit |
| Not working out the current margin of safety, so no change could be shown (June 2023 P23) | Calculate both, then the difference |
| "Useful for decision making" with no "short term" (March 2023 P22) | Name short term decisions |
| A line of workings with no final figure (June 2022 P23) | A net value is needed for the mark |

## Practice questions with answers

All three are my own.

### Practice question 1

> Faraz Bakes sells cake boxes at $15 each. Variable costs are $9.40 a box, including a sales commission of $0.40. Fixed costs are $12,600 a month. Budgeted sales are 2,800 boxes a month.
>
> (a) Calculate the break-even point in units. [2]
>
> (b) Calculate the margin of safety in units and in sales value. [2]

**Answer**

(a) Contribution = $15 minus $9.40 = $5.60. Break-even = $12,600 ÷ $5.60 = 2,250 boxes.

(b) 2,800 minus 2,250 = 550 boxes. Value: 550 × $15 = $8,250.

### Practice question 2

> Same business. The owner wants a profit of $3,500 a month. How many boxes must be sold? [2]

**Answer**

($12,600 + $3,500) ÷ $5.60 = $16,100 ÷ $5.60 = 2,875 boxes. Check: 2,875 × $5.60 = $16,100, minus $12,600 = $3,500.

### Practice question 3

> Malik Ltd makes 4,000 units in a month and sells 3,600. There is no opening inventory. Variable production cost is $10 a unit. Fixed production overheads of $24,000 are absorbed over the 4,000 units. Profit using marginal costing is $18,000.
>
> Calculate the profit using absorption costing. [3]

**Answer**

Fixed overhead per unit = $24,000 ÷ 4,000 = $6. Closing inventory = 400 units. Fixed overhead carried forward = 400 × $6 = $2,400. Inventory rose, so absorption profit is higher: $18,000 + $2,400 = $20,400.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between marginal and absorption costing?

Marginal costing values products at variable cost only and charges all fixed costs to the period. Absorption costing adds a share of fixed production overheads to each unit. The profits differ only when inventory levels change.

### Do I have to draw a break-even chart in 9706?

No. The syllabus says candidates will not be asked to prepare a break-even chart. You must be able to interpret one.

### Should break-even be rounded up or down?

Up, to the next whole unit. Four 9706 Paper 2 reports from 2022 and 2023 name failing to round up as an error.

### What is the contribution to sales ratio?

Contribution divided by revenue, per unit or in total. Divide fixed costs by it to find break-even revenue.

### Why is profit higher under absorption costing when inventory rises?

Some fixed production overhead is carried forward in closing inventory, so less of it is charged this period. Marginal costing charges all of it now.

### Is commission a fixed or variable cost?

Commission on sales is variable, because it rises with sales. The November 2022 Paper 22 report said several candidates missed this.
