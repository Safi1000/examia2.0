"What percentage do I need for an A\*?" is the question I hear most in results week. The honest answer is that there isn't one. In June 2026, an A\* in O Level Accounting 7707 needed about 70% of the weighted marks on one set of papers and about 78% on the other. Same subject, same session, same grade, different line.

The system works the same way for IGCSE, O Level and A Level grade thresholds. Once you understand why, you can plan your revision around marks instead of guessing at a percentage.

## Why there is no fixed percentage

Cambridge does not decide the grade lines before the exam. In its own words: "After all the marking has been done, we set the grades." The reason is that papers vary in difficulty. Cambridge explains that if the questions in a paper "have been more difficult than last time, we have to lower the thresholds", so that nobody gets a lower grade just because they sat a harder paper.

That is why the A\* line moves so much between subjects and sessions. Here are the June 2026 A\* thresholds for variant 1 of each syllabus, taken from Cambridge's published tables:

| Syllabus | Maximum weighted mark | A\* threshold | As a percentage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| IGCSE 0452 / O Level 7707 Accounting | 143 | 100 | 69.9% |
| IGCSE 0450 / O Level 7115 Business | 160 | 95 | 59.4% |
| IGCSE 0455 / O Level 2281 Economics | 150 | 118 | 78.7% |
| A Level 9706 Accounting | 250 | 192 | 76.8% |
| A Level 9609 Business | 200 | 157 | 78.5% |
| A Level 9708 Economics | 180 | 124 | 68.9% |

The lower grades move just as much. A grade C in IGCSE Accounting 0452, variant 1, needed 40 out of 143 in June 2026. That is 28%. Anyone who tells you "a C is 50%" is describing a different exam system.

## How Cambridge sets the thresholds each session

Cambridge describes a process that combines statistics with examiner judgement:

1. **The papers are marked first.** Thresholds are set afterwards, using the real scripts.
2. **Statistical evidence.** Cambridge looks at how comparable groups of candidates performed in the exam series.
3. **Expert judgement.** It also considers the principal examiner's view of how difficult each question paper was. Cambridge is clear that "the decisions are not made automatically by a computer."
4. **Paper by paper, then combined.** A threshold for each grade is set for each paper (Cambridge calls them components). The paper thresholds are then added together, after weighting where the syllabus uses weighting, to give the syllabus thresholds.
5. **A\* is calculated last.** "Grade A\* does not exist as a component grade." Cambridge says the A\* line for each combination of papers is calculated relative to the A and B thresholds.

You can see that last step in the numbers. In every Accounting, Business and Economics table on our [grade thresholds](/grade-thresholds) page, the A\* line sits exactly as far above A as A sits above B. For O Level Economics 2281 in June 2026, variant 1: B was 86 and A was 102, a gap of 16, and A\* was 102 + 16 = 118.

## Why variants have different thresholds

Cambridge writes separate question papers for different parts of the world. Its syllabuses explain that "to keep our exams secure, we produce question papers for different areas of the world, known as administrative zones." These are the variants. The second digit of the paper number tells you which one you sat: papers 11 and 21 are variant 1, papers 12 and 22 are variant 2.

Different papers mean different difficulty, so each variant gets its own thresholds. A higher threshold means candidates found those papers easier to score on, so more marks were needed for the same grade.

**O Level Accounting 7707, June 2026**

| Variant | Papers | A | A\* | A\* as a percentage |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 11 and 21 | 80 | 100 | 69.9% |
| 2 | 12 and 22 | 88 | 111 | 77.6% |

Thresholds also move from one session to the next on the same variant. The A line for 7707 variant 2 was 86 in June 2024, 83 in June 2025, 87 in November 2025 and 88 in June 2026, all out of 143.

## How weighted totals work

In IGCSE and O Level Accounting (0452, 7707) and Economics (0455, 2281), Paper 1 counts for 30% of the grade and Paper 2 for 70%. The papers do not have 30% and 70% of the raw marks, though, so Cambridge scales each paper before adding them up. In Cambridge's words, "for some syllabuses we apply a weighting factor to the component thresholds before we add them together."

For the papers sat up to 2026:

| Syllabus | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Weighted total |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Economics 0455 / 2281 | 30 marks, each worth 1.5 weighted marks (45 in total) | 90 marks, each worth about 1.167 (105 in total) | 150 |
| Accounting 0452 / 7707 | 35 marks, each worth about 1.226 (about 42.9 in total) | 100 marks, each worth about 1.001 (about 100.1 in total) | 143 |

You can check this against Cambridge's own table. In June 2026, 2281 variant 1, the A line was 23 out of 30 on Paper 1 and 58 out of 90 on Paper 2. Weighted, that is 23 × 1.5 = 34.5, plus 58 × 1.167 = 67.7, a total of 102.2. Cambridge's syllabus A threshold for that variant: 102.

Cambridge does not publish its rounding in these tables, so if your weighted total is within a mark of a threshold, treat it as too close to call.

### Worked example 1: O Level Economics 2281, June 2026, variant 1

Bilal scores 24 out of 30 on Paper 1 and 60 out of 90 on Paper 2.

- Paper 1: 24 × 1.5 = 36
- Paper 2: 60 × 1.167 = 70
- Weighted total: **106 out of 150**

The thresholds were A 102 and A\* 118, so Bilal gets an **A**. To reach A\* he needed 12 more weighted marks. Paper 1 could give him at most 9 more (6 marks × 1.5), so the A\* had to come from Paper 2: about 11 more marks there.

### Worked example 2: same raw marks, different totals

Sana and Zain both score 78 raw marks out of 120 on the same 2281 papers.

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Raw total | Weighted total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sana | 28 | 50 | 78 | 42 + 58.3 = 100.3 |
| Zain | 18 | 60 | 78 | 27 + 70 = 97 |

Both get a B, because A was 102. But Sana is less than 2 marks from an A and Zain is 5 away. On these papers, a Paper 1 mark was worth 1.5 weighted marks and a Paper 2 mark about 1.167. Adding raw marks together hides that difference.

This changes for O Level Economics 2281 from 2027, when Paper 1 becomes 40 marks and Paper 2 becomes 80, still at 30% and 70%. Then each Paper 1 mark is worth 0.75% of the grade and each Paper 2 mark 0.875%.

### Worked example 3: O Level Accounting 7707, June 2026

Hira scores 25 out of 35 on Paper 1 and 70 out of 100 on Paper 2. That is 95 out of 135 raw, or 70.4%.

- Paper 1: 25 × 1.226 = 30.6
- Paper 2: 70 × 1.001 = 70.1
- Weighted total: **100.7 out of 143**

On variant 1, where the A\* line was 100, that is an **A\***. On variant 2 the A\* line was 111, so the same total there would have been an A. Remember that the two variants were different papers: the same student would not have scored exactly the same on both. The point is that "70%" can be an A\* in one session and an A in another.

## Percentage uniform marks are not percentages

Some statements of results show a percentage uniform mark (PUM) next to each grade. It looks like a percentage, but Cambridge says it "is not the total mark achieved for the syllabus." It is a point on a common scale that shows whether you were near the top, middle or bottom of your grade.

| Grade | PUM range |
|---|---|
| A\* | 90 to 100 |
| A | 80 to 89 |
| B | 70 to 79 |
| C | 60 to 69 |
| D | 50 to 59 |
| E | 40 to 49 |
| F (IGCSE only) | 30 to 39 |
| G (IGCSE only) | 20 to 29 |

At AS Level, where there is no a\*, grade a runs from 80 to 100.

Cambridge gives two examples. A candidate with the minimum mark for an A\* gets a PUM of 90. A candidate halfway between the D and C thresholds gets 55. So a PUM of 90 means you just reached the A\* line, wherever that line was. It does not mean you scored 90% of the marks, and it cannot be used with the grade threshold tables.

## What this means for your revision

- **Aim above the highest recent line, not an average.** Across the last four sessions on our page, the A\* line for O Level Economics 2281 ranged from 112 to 126 out of 150 (about 75% to 84%). For O Level Accounting 7707 it ranged from 93 to 111 out of 143 (about 65% to 78%). If your mocks clear the top of the range, the session's difficulty stops mattering.
- **Treat every paper separately.** Your weak paper costs you more than a raw percentage suggests when it carries 70% of the grade.
- **Ignore anyone quoting a fixed boundary.** If a number does not come from a Cambridge threshold table for your syllabus, your session and your variant, it is a guess.
