Yes, you can prepare for Accounting, Business and Economics on your own. Split your time into three phases: learn every syllabus topic, practise past paper questions topic by topic, then sit full papers against the clock. In 10 months that means about 4 topics a week across the three subjects. In 4 months it means about 9 or 10. Mark everything strictly with the mark scheme.

## How much is there to cover in each subject?

Start with the real size of the job. These counts come from the 2027 to 2029 syllabuses. A topic here is one numbered heading, such as 2.5 Price changes.

| Subject | Sections | Topics |
|---|---:|---:|
| IGCSE Accounting 0452 (O Level 7707) | 7 | 28 |
| IGCSE Economics 0455 (O Level 2281) | 6 | 36 |
| IGCSE Business 0264 (O Level 7081) | 6 | 29 |
| All three | 19 | 93 |

The O Level syllabuses have the same topic numbers and the same paper structure as the IGCSE ones, so every timetable below works for both. IGCSE Business 0264 replaces Business Studies 0450 from 2027, and O Level Business 7081 replaces 7115.

Economics has the most topics, and section 2 alone has 10. Accounting has the fewest, but its topics are long. Sole traders, partnerships and limited companies each need a full set of accounts, not a page of notes.

You can see every topic with a tick box on the [syllabus checklists](/syllabus). Print the one for each of your subjects. That list is your plan.

## What papers will you sit in 2027?

Each subject has two papers, and every candidate takes both. These are the figures from the 2027 to 2029 syllabuses.

| Subject | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting 0452 / 7707 | Multiple choice: 40 questions, 40 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes, 30% | Structured: five compulsory questions, 100 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes, 70% |
| Economics 0455 / 2281 | Multiple choice: 40 questions, 40 marks, 1 hour, 30% | Structured: Section A (one compulsory question, 20 marks) and Section B (three from four, 20 marks each), 80 marks, 2 hours, 70% |
| Business 0264 / 7081 | Short answer and data response: four compulsory questions, 80 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes, 50% | Case study: four compulsory questions on an insert, 80 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes, 50% |

Two facts from this table shape your plan.

First, there is no choice of topics except in Economics Section B. Accounting and Business questions are all compulsory, and every paper can draw on the whole syllabus. So you cannot skip a topic and hope.

Second, the written papers carry most of the marks. In Accounting and Economics, Paper 2 is 70%. Multiple choice is easier to practise alone because the answer key tells you instantly if you are right. Written answers are where self study students need the most care.

## What does a 10 month timetable look like?

Ten months is about 43 weeks. If your exams are in May and June, that means starting around August. I split it into three phases. The phases and the weekly paces in this article are my own planning advice as a teacher, not Cambridge guidance.

| Weeks | Phase | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 24 | Learn | About 4 topics a week across the three subjects. Finish each topic with 3 to 5 past paper questions on it |
| 25 to 34 | Topic practice | Past paper questions section by section. One full Paper 1 per subject each week |
| 35 to 43 | Timed papers | Full papers under exam timing. Learn again the topics where you lose marks |

### How fast is "about 4 topics a week"?

The 93 topics over 24 weeks is 3.9 a week. Split by subject, that is 1 or 2 topics per subject each week:

| Subject | Topics | Pace over 24 weeks |
|---|---:|---|
| Accounting | 28 | 20 weeks of 1 topic, 4 weeks of 2 |
| Economics | 36 | 12 weeks of 1 topic, 12 weeks of 2 |
| Business | 29 | 19 weeks of 1 topic, 5 weeks of 2 |

Give the double weeks to the short topics. In Accounting, 1.1 The purpose of accounting and 1.2 The accounting equation fit in one week. Limited companies does not. In Economics, the four topics in section 1 are short, so two weeks of 2 cover them.

Study the three subjects side by side, not one after another. If you finish Accounting in month 3 and do not touch it again until month 8, you will have forgotten the layouts. A little of each subject every week keeps all three warm.

### What happens in the topic practice phase?

By week 25 you have seen every topic once. Now you go back section by section and do every past paper question you can find on it. The [past papers sorted by topic](/past-papers/topics) group the questions for 0452, 0455 and the old 0450 Business papers, with mark schemes.

Do the most frequently tested topics first. The [most repeated 0455 topics](/past-papers/igcse-economics-0455/most-repeated-topics) page counts how often each topic appeared, and the same page exists for 0452 and 0450. Use it to choose what to practise first, not what to skip.

### What happens in the last nine weeks?

Sit one full set of papers for each subject every week, under exam timing. A full set is long:

| Subject | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounting | 1 hour 30 minutes | 1 hour 45 minutes | 3 hours 15 minutes |
| Economics | 1 hour | 2 hours | 3 hours |
| Business | 1 hour 30 minutes | 1 hour 30 minutes | 3 hours |

That is 9 hours 15 minutes of writing a week for all three, before you mark anything. Plan it like a timetable: for example, two papers on Saturday and two on Sunday, and the last two in the week. Mark each paper the next day, then spend the rest of the week on the topics that cost you marks.

## What does a 4 month timetable look like?

Four months is about 17 weeks. It is the same three phases, squeezed.

| Weeks | Phase | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 10 | Learn | About 9 or 10 topics a week across the three subjects, with 2 or 3 past paper questions each |
| 11 to 14 | Topic practice | Questions on your weakest sections. One Paper 1 per subject each week |
| 15 to 17 | Timed papers | One full set per subject each week |

Be honest about the arithmetic. The 93 topics over 10 weeks is 9.3 a week. Per subject, that is about 3 Accounting topics, 3 or 4 Economics topics and 3 Business topics every week, with no week off.

That is possible if you are studying full time. If you are also at school or working, 4 months for three subjects from zero is a very heavy load. I would rather you sit fewer subjects well this session than three badly. The [private candidate guide for Pakistan](/private-candidates/pakistan) covers entry dates and retakes, so you can check when the next session is before you decide.

## How do you fit Economics into 10 weeks? A worked example

Here is the learning phase of the 4 month plan for Economics alone, with real topic numbers. There are 36 topics to fit into 10 weeks.

**Step 1: find the weekly pace.** 36 ÷ 10 = 3.6. So some weeks take 4 topics and some take 3. Six weeks of 4 is 24 topics. Four weeks of 3 is 12 topics. 24 + 12 = 36.

**Step 2: keep each section together.** Do not split a section across a break if you can avoid it. Ideas inside one section build on each other.

| Week | Topics | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | 1.1 to 1.4: the basic economic problem, factors of production, opportunity cost, PPC diagrams | 4 |
| 2 | 2.1 to 2.4: markets, demand, supply, price determination | 4 |
| 3 | 2.5 to 2.7: price changes, price elasticity of demand and of supply | 3 |
| 4 | 2.8 to 2.10: market economic system, market failure, mixed economic system | 3 |
| 5 | 3.1 to 3.4: money and banking, households, workers, firms | 4 |
| 6 | 3.5 to 3.7: firms and production, costs and revenue, types of markets | 3 |
| 7 | 4.1 to 4.4: government intervention, fiscal, monetary and supply side policy | 4 |
| 8 | 4.5 to 4.7: economic growth, unemployment, inflation | 3 |
| 9 | 5.1 to 5.4: living standards, poverty, population, development | 4 |
| 10 | 6.1 to 6.4: free trade, globalisation, exchange rates, current account | 4 |
| Total | | 36 |

**Step 3: check the total.** 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 + 4 = 36. Every topic is covered once.

**Step 4: plan the rest.** Weeks 11 to 14 go on topic questions and one Paper 1 a week. Weeks 15 to 17 are three full sets of Paper 1 and Paper 2. That gives you three timed attempts at the 2 hour Paper 2 before the real one.

Notice that section 2 gets three weeks. That is deliberate. Demand, supply and elasticity sit underneath most of the rest of the course, so it is worth going slowly there.

## How do you mark your own answers?

This is the hardest part of studying alone. A teacher in a classroom marks your work. On your own, you have to be the examiner, and you have to be stricter than you want to be.

1. **Write the full answer first.** Never look at the mark scheme before you finish. Reading it first makes every answer look like yours.
2. **Mark the next day.** You read what you meant to write when you mark straight away. A day later you read what you actually wrote.
3. **Give a mark only for a point that is on the page.** If the mark scheme wants a reason and you only gave the point, you do not get the mark.
4. **Use the right marking style.** Most questions are point marked: each correct point earns a mark. In the 2027 specimens, the 8 mark discuss parts in Economics Paper 2 and the part (b) questions in Business Paper 2 are marked in levels. The examiner reads the whole answer and decides which level fits. My guide on [how to answer Economics questions](/blog/how-to-answer-igcse-o-level-economics-questions) shows how the levels work for 8 mark answers.
5. **Keep a mistakes log.** One line per lost mark: the topic, what went wrong, and the fix. Read it before every timed paper.

In Accounting, check your layout as hard as your figures. Abbreviations and missing opening balances lose marks even when the arithmetic is right. My [A\* plan for 0452 and 7707](/blog/how-to-get-a-star-igcse-o-level-accounting) lists the layout slips examiners see most often.

<Tip title="The honest test">
If you gave yourself full marks on an 8 mark Economics answer, ask whether a stranger would. If you are not sure, you probably did not earn Level 3.
</Tip>

Multiple choice is easy to mark alone. Long answers are not. When you study alone, nobody tells you why an answer you thought was complete only earned half the marks.

## How should you use past papers when you study alone?

Use them in three ways, in this order.

1. **After each topic:** a few questions on that topic only. You are checking that you understood it, not testing speed.
2. **In the practice phase:** every question on a section, with the mark scheme beside you after each one.
3. **In the last weeks:** whole papers, timed, marked the next day.

Older papers are still useful, but check them against the 2027 syllabus. The new syllabuses changed some content:

- **Accounting:** 7.2 Ethical considerations and 7.3 Technology and sustainability are new from 2027, so older papers will not test them. Paper 2 has not changed.
- **Economics:** some old content was removed or cut down, such as fixed exchange rates and the old trade unions topic, and new content was added, such as arguments for and against the mixed economic system. Skip old questions on removed content.
- **Business:** 0264 Paper 1 questions now have four parts instead of five, and every Paper 2 part (a) is now an applied question. Old 0450 papers still train the same skills.

Cambridge publishes specimen papers for each new syllabus. Do them in the timed phase, because they are the only papers written for the 2027 syllabuses.

## Where do self study students lose marks?

These come from the June 2025 examiner reports for 0452, 0455 and 0450 (the paper 0264 replaces). They hit every candidate, but they hit hardest when no one is checking your work.

| Mistake | What examiners saw | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Abbreviations in accounts | Short forms used for account names and items in statements | 0452 June 2025 report |
| No recommendation | Discuss answers listed advantages and disadvantages but did not decide | 0452 June 2025 report |
| Ignoring the source material | Section A answers that did not use the information given | 0455 June 2025 report |
| Not reading the question closely | Questions asked for two points in bold, and many gave four | 0455 June 2025 report |
| Poor time on 8 mark parts | Too long spent on descriptive answers, too little left for part (d) | 0455 June 2025 report |
| One sided discuss answers | Part (d) needs both sides of the issue for full credit | 0455 June 2025 report |
| Repeating the same application | One fact from the case used twice is credited once | 0450 June 2025 report |
| No link to the business | Answers to questions about a named business made no reference to it | 0450 June 2025 report |

Every one of these is a habit, not a gap in knowledge. That is why the mistakes log matters. You will not fix a habit by reading more notes.

In Business, the [guide to application marks](/blog/igcse-o-level-business-application-marks) shows how to use a different fact from the case in each point.

## When should you start timed papers?

Start full timed papers only once you have learned every topic. Before that, a timed paper just tells you that you have not finished the syllabus.

There is one exception. Time single questions early. In the practice phase, give yourself the exam rate for each question:

| Paper | Time per mark |
|---|---|
| Accounting Paper 2 | 105 minutes for 100 marks: just over 1 minute a mark |
| Economics Paper 2 | 120 minutes for 80 marks: 1.5 minutes a mark |
| Business Papers 1 and 2 | 90 minutes for 80 marks: just over 1 minute a mark |

So an Economics 8 mark part gets 12 minutes. A 20 mark Accounting question gets about 21 minutes. Practising at these rates early stops the most common problem in the timed phase: running out of time on the last question.

Sit your timed papers at the time of day your real exams will be, if you can. Use the [revision planner](/tools/revision-planner) to lay out the weeks so you can see the timed phase coming.

## What should you do when you are stuck?

You will get stuck. Studying alone, the risk is that one stuck topic stops the whole plan.

- **Give it one day, not one week.** Mark the topic, move to the next one, and come back. The plan keeps moving.
- **Read the mark scheme answer as a lesson.** Take a past paper question on the topic, read the mark scheme slowly, then close it and write the answer yourself.
- **Try a different explanation.** A textbook chapter, a second textbook or a video may explain it in a way that clicks.
- **Write down the exact question you cannot answer.** "I do not understand partnerships" is too big. "I do not know why interest on drawings goes on that side of the appropriation account" can be fixed in ten minutes by someone who knows.
- **Ask for help early.** If a topic is still stuck after two attempts, ask a teacher. A week lost in month 2 is easy to recover. A week lost in month 9 is not.

## Practice questions

**1. You have 14 weeks before your exams and want to sit Business only. You plan 9 weeks of learning. How many topics a week is that?**

Business has 29 topics. 29 ÷ 9 = 3.2, so you need some weeks of 4 and some of 3. Two weeks of 4 is 8 topics and seven weeks of 3 is 21 topics. 8 + 21 = 29. That leaves 5 weeks for practice and timed papers.

**2. How long should you spend on a 6 mark part in Economics Paper 2?**

Paper 2 is 80 marks in 120 minutes, so 1.5 minutes a mark. A 6 mark part gets 6 × 1.5 = 9 minutes. That leaves the right share of time for the 8 mark part (d), which gets 12 minutes.

**3. You only have an older 0452 Paper 1 with 35 questions. How long should you give yourself?**

The 2027 Paper 1 is 40 questions in 90 minutes, which is 2.25 minutes a question. 35 × 2.25 = 78.75, so give yourself about 79 minutes. The older papers allowed 1 hour 15 minutes for 35 questions, so either timing is fair practice.

## FAQ

### Can I get an A\* studying alone?

Yes, it is possible. All candidates take the same two papers, whether they study in school or alone. Nobody can promise a grade, but a full syllabus, steady past paper practice and strict marking give you the best chance.

### Is 4 months enough to study Economics from scratch?

For one subject it can be done, if you keep to 3 or 4 topics a week for 10 weeks and then practise. For three subjects at once, 4 months is very tight. Check the next session dates before you decide.

### Should I make notes or do past papers first?

Do both, in that order for each topic. Learn the topic, write one page of notes, then do past paper questions on it the same week. Notes without questions feel like progress but are not tested.

### Are old 0450 Business papers useful for 0264?

Yes. The skills are the same: knowledge, application, analysis and evaluation. The 0264 Paper 1 questions now have four parts instead of five, so use the specimen papers to practise the new format.

### How many past papers should I do?

Aim to do every question on every topic during the practice phase, then one full set per subject each week in the timed phase. In the 10 month plan that is nine timed sets per subject.

### How do I know if my long answers are good enough?

Mark them against the level descriptions in the mark scheme, a day after writing. If you cannot decide between two levels, give yourself the lower one, and have a teacher check a few.
