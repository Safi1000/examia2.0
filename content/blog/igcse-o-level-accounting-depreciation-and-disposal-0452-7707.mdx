Depreciation spreads the cost of a non-current asset over the years the business uses it. For IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 you calculate it by straight line, reducing balance or revaluation. You record it in a provision for depreciation account. When an asset is sold, a disposal account compares the proceeds with the net book value to find the profit or loss.

## What does the syllabus say about depreciation and disposal?

Depreciation and disposal is section 4.2 of the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-4.2) and of the O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. You need to understand:

1. The meaning of depreciation and why businesses account for it.
2. How to calculate it by the straight line, reducing balance and revaluation methods.
3. Which method suits which type of non-current asset.
4. Journal entries and ledger accounts to record depreciation.
5. Journal entries and ledger accounts for buying and selling a non-current asset: the asset account, the provision for depreciation account and the disposal account.
6. How to calculate the profit or loss on disposal.

Section 5 of both syllabuses then asks you to show accumulated depreciation in the statement of financial position, using the same three methods.

## What is depreciation and why do businesses charge it?

A **non-current asset** is something the business keeps and uses for more than a year: a van, a machine, shop fittings. It wears out, gets used up or goes out of date. **Depreciation** is the part of its cost that is used up in one year.

The November 2022 0452 report described the aim as to "spread the net cost of a non-current asset over the expected life". Net cost means the cost less any residual value, the amount you expect to get when the asset is finally sold.

Businesses charge depreciation for two reasons I teach:

- **Matching.** The van helps earn revenue every year it is used. So part of its cost should be an expense in each of those years.
- **Prudence.** Without depreciation, profit and the value of the assets would both be overstated.

Depreciation does not show what the asset is worth on the market. In the November 2023 7707 report, a significant number of candidates chose that answer in Paper 1. Non-current assets are shown at **net book value**: cost less the depreciation charged so far. No cash leaves the business when depreciation is charged.

## How do you calculate depreciation by each method?

### Straight line method

The same charge every year.

Annual depreciation = (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life in years

A machine costs $26 000, will last 6 years and should sell for $2 000 at the end. Depreciation is (26 000 − 2 000) ÷ 6 = $4 000 a year.

Questions often give a percentage of cost instead, such as "20% a year on cost". Then the charge is simply 20% of cost every year.

### Reducing balance method

A fixed percentage of the net book value. The charge falls every year, because the net book value falls.

A van costs $25 000. It is depreciated at 20% a year by reducing balance.

| Year | Depreciation ($) | Net book value at year end ($) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 25 000 × 20% = 5 000 | 20 000 |
| 2 | 20 000 × 20% = 4 000 | 16 000 |
| 3 | 16 000 × 20% = 3 200 | 12 800 |

Residual value is not used in this calculation. The rate is applied to what is left.

### Revaluation method

Used for many small items that are hard to track one by one, such as loose tools. You value them at the start and end of the year.

Depreciation = opening valuation + purchases in the year − closing valuation

Loose tools were valued at $1 800 at the start of the year. Tools costing $650 were bought. At the year end they were valued at $1 900. Depreciation is 1 800 + 650 − 1 900 = $550.

You can check straight line and reducing balance figures year by year with my [depreciation calculator](/tools/depreciation-calculator).

### Which method suits which asset?

A November 2024 Paper 2 question, set for both 0452 and 7707, asked candidates to match three assets to a method. The mark scheme gave straight line for fixtures and fittings, revaluation for loose tools and no depreciation for land. Here is how I teach the choice:

| Asset | Method I would choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Motor vehicles | Reducing balance | They lose most value in the early years |
| Machinery used evenly, fixtures and fittings | Straight line | The benefit is roughly the same each year |
| Loose tools | Revaluation | Many small items, hard to record one by one |
| Land | No depreciation | It does not normally wear out |

The motor vehicles row and the reasons in the last column are my own explanation. Use the facts in the question if they point another way.

## What if the asset was bought or sold during the year?

Every question states a policy. Read it before calculating anything. The common ones are:

| Policy in the question | What you do |
|---|---|
| A full year's depreciation in the year of purchase and none in the year of sale | Charge only on assets held at the year end |
| Depreciation calculated monthly | Multiply the yearly charge by months owned ÷ 12 |

A machine bought on 1 April for $12 000, depreciated at 10% a year on cost by the month, with a 31 December year end: 12 000 × 10% × 9/12 = $900.

The March 2025 0452 report showed a calculation like this: one van was charged for 9 months out of 12. In the same question, some candidates wrongly included depreciation on a van that had been sold.

## Which accounts record depreciation?

Three ledger accounts work together. Keep them apart in your head.

| Account | What goes in it | Usual balance |
|---|---|---|
| Asset account (for example Motor vehicles) | Cost of assets bought, cost of assets sold | Debit: cost of assets still owned |
| Provision for depreciation account | Charge for the year, depreciation on assets sold | Credit: total depreciation on assets still owned |
| Disposal account | Cost, depreciation and proceeds of the asset sold | None: it closes to the income statement |

The asset account stays **at cost**. The June 2023 report on Paper 21 said many candidates found the asset account challenging. It added that the provision for depreciation does not belong in it. The 7707 report covers the same question.

### What are the journal entries for depreciation?

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Buy an asset by bank | Asset account | Bank |
| Buy an asset on credit | Asset account | Supplier's account |
| Charge depreciation for the year | Income statement | Provision for depreciation |

The June 2022 report (0452 and 7707) described a purchase on credit where the asset account, not purchases, should have been debited. A narrative was also required. The March 2026 report confirmed the depreciation entry: debit the income statement, credit the provision.

## How do you record the disposal of a non-current asset?

When an asset is sold, four entries move everything about it into the disposal account.

| Step | Debit | Credit | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Transfer the cost | Disposal | Asset account | Original cost |
| 2. Transfer the depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal | Depreciation on that asset to date |
| 3. Record the proceeds | Bank (or cash) | Disposal | Selling price |
| 4. Close the account | Income statement or Disposal | Disposal or Income statement | The difference |

After steps 1 to 3, look at the disposal account:

- **Debit side bigger:** the asset sold for less than its net book value. That is a **loss on disposal**. Credit the disposal account, debit the income statement.
- **Credit side bigger:** it sold for more. That is a **profit on disposal**. Debit the disposal account, credit the income statement.

A profit goes on the debit side of the disposal account. The November 2023 7707 report said a significant proportion of candidates thought it would be a credit entry.

### How do you calculate profit or loss on disposal?

Profit or loss = proceeds − net book value at the date of sale

The June 2023 report noted that candidates who gave a wrong answer with no workings lost every mark for the calculation. Always show cost, depreciation to date, net book value and proceeds.

The November 2022 0452 report also covered a part exchange. The supplier's allowance for the old machine counts as the proceeds. The loss was the book value less that allowance.

<Tip title="Name the account, every time">
Write "Motor vehicles", "Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles", "Bank" and "Income statement". The June 2025 report called labels like "MV at Cost", "Depreciation", "Proceeds" and "Loss on Sale" incorrect wording.
</Tip>

This is where students get stuck. The arithmetic is short, but the three accounts must agree with each other, and one wrong figure in the provision account breaks all three.

## How do the three accounts work together? A worked example

Zara runs a delivery business. Her year ends on 31 December. Motor vehicles are depreciated at 20% a year on cost by the straight line method. A full year's depreciation is charged in the year of purchase and none in the year of sale.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Van A | Bought 1 January 2023 for $18 000 |
| Van B | Bought 1 January 2024 for $20 000 |
| Van C | Bought 1 January 2025 for $22 000 |
| 1 April 2026 | Van A sold for $6 500, received by bank |
| 1 July 2026 | Van D bought for $25 000, paid by bank |

Prepare the motor vehicles account, the provision for depreciation of motor vehicles account and the disposal of motor vehicles account for 2026.

### Step 1: find the opening balances

Cost at 1 January 2026: 18 000 + 20 000 + 22 000 = $60 000.

| Van | Years charged before 2026 | Depreciation ($) |
|---|---|---:|
| A | 2023, 2024, 2025 | 3 × 3 600 = 10 800 |
| B | 2024, 2025 | 2 × 4 000 = 8 000 |
| C | 2025 | 1 × 4 400 = 4 400 |
| **Total** | | **23 200** |

### Step 2: work out the disposal figures for van A

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Cost | 18 000 |
| Less depreciation to date | 10 800 |
| Net book value | 7 200 |
| Proceeds | 6 500 |
| **Loss on disposal** | **700** |

Van A gets no depreciation in 2026, because none is charged in the year of sale.

### Step 3: the charge for 2026

Vans held at 31 December: B, C and D. Cost: 20 000 + 22 000 + 25 000 = $67 000. Charge: 67 000 × 20% = $13 400. Van D gets a full year even though it was bought in July.

### Step 4: the ledger accounts

**Zara: Motor vehicles account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 60 000 | 2026 Apr 1 Disposal | 18 000 |
| Jul 1 Bank | 25 000 | Dec 31 Balance c/d | 67 000 |
| | **85 000** | | **85 000** |
| 2027 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 67 000 | | |

**Zara: Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Apr 1 Disposal | 10 800 | 2026 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 23 200 |
| Dec 31 Balance c/d | 25 800 | Dec 31 Income statement | 13 400 |
| | **36 600** | | **36 600** |
| | | 2027 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 25 800 |

Check: van B has 3 years (12 000), van C has 2 years (8 800) and van D has 1 year (5 000). 12 000 + 8 800 + 5 000 = $25 800.

**Zara: Disposal of motor vehicles account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Apr 1 Motor vehicles | 18 000 | 2026 Apr 1 Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles | 10 800 |
| | | Apr 1 Bank | 6 500 |
| | | Dec 31 Income statement | 700 |
| | **18 000** | | **18 000** |

### Step 5: the journal entries

| Date | Account | Debit ($) | Credit ($) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Apr 1 | Disposal of motor vehicles | 18 000 | |
| | Motor vehicles | | 18 000 |
| Apr 1 | Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles | 10 800 | |
| | Disposal of motor vehicles | | 10 800 |
| Apr 1 | Bank | 6 500 | |
| | Disposal of motor vehicles | | 6 500 |
| Jul 1 | Motor vehicles | 25 000 | |
| | Bank | | 25 000 |
| Dec 31 | Income statement | 13 400 | |
| | Provision for depreciation of motor vehicles | | 13 400 |
| Dec 31 | Income statement | 700 | |
| | Disposal of motor vehicles | | 700 |

In the exam, add a short narrative under each entry when the question asks for one, for example "Sale of van A".

### Step 6: where the figures go in the financial statements

The [income statement](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format) shows two expenses: depreciation of motor vehicles $13 400 and loss on disposal $700. A profit on disposal would be added as other income below gross profit.

The [statement of financial position](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-statement-of-financial-position-format) shows:

| Non-current assets | Cost ($) | Provision for depreciation ($) | Net book value ($) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Motor vehicles | 67 000 | 25 800 | 41 200 |

The June 2023 report listed leaving out these three column headings as a common error.

## How is a depreciation question marked?

I checked three 0452 Paper 2 mark schemes where most of one question was built on this topic. They are June 2023 (variant 21), June 2025 (variant 21) and March 2026 (variant 22).

| Part | Marks in those papers |
|---|---|
| Asset account | 2 |
| Provision for depreciation account | 5 |
| Disposal account | 3 or 4 |
| Profit or loss on disposal, or total depreciation to date, as a calculation | 3 or 4 |

The pattern inside each account was the same:

- Most entries earned one mark each, often marked as an own figure. An own figure mark rewards a correct method even when an earlier figure was wrong.
- In the provision account, the charge for the year was worth 2 marks in June 2023 and March 2026, so show the working beside it.
- The March 2026 scheme gave a separate mark for dates in the provision account.
- In June 2023 and March 2026, the closing balance earned its mark as the balance brought down. The balance carried down had no mark beside it.

The same questions also carried short parts on capital and revenue expenditure, accounting principles and advice to the owner. In those mark schemes, delivery and installation were capital. Fuel and insurance were revenue.

Depreciation also hides inside bigger questions. In November 2022, capital spending had been included in repairs, so it had to be added to the asset before depreciation was calculated. In the November 2023 7707 report, it was quite common for candidates to take a percentage of the provision for depreciation. The base should be cost or net book value.

## What else do examiners ask about depreciation?

- **Changing method.** The June 2022 report (0452 and 7707) described a question on switching from reducing balance to straight line. Strong answers discussed the effect on the yearly charge and on book value, and explained consistency, not just how each method works.
- **Buying a new asset.** In March 2026, many candidates missed that new shop fittings would increase the depreciation charge.
- **Selling a fully depreciated asset.** In March 2023, candidates advised a trader whether to sell office equipment with no book value left. A balanced answer with a recommendation was expected.

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the examiner reports. Reports marked 7707 are O Level; the rest are 0452.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Putting provision for depreciation or proceeds in the asset account (June 2023, March 2026) | The asset account holds cost only |
| Putting the cost or net book value in the provision account (June 2023, March 2026) | The provision account holds depreciation only |
| Leaving out the depreciation on the asset sold (June 2023, March 2026) | Debit it to the provision account and credit it to disposal |
| Counting the wrong number of years to the sale (June 2023, June 2025) | List each year charged and check the policy on the year of sale |
| Ignoring residual value in straight line (November 2022, June 2025) | Deduct residual value from cost before dividing |
| Using the opening provision when the closing one was asked for (June 2025) | Add this year's charge |
| Taking a percentage of the provision (November 2023, 7707) | Straight line uses cost; reducing balance uses net book value |
| Short labels in place of account names (June 2025) | Use the exact name of the other account |

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper first. All figures are my own.

### Practice question 1

Equipment was bought on 1 January 2024 for $16 000. It is depreciated at 25% a year by reducing balance. The year end is 31 December. Calculate the depreciation for 2026 and the net book value on 31 December 2026.

**Answer**

| Year | Depreciation ($) | Net book value ($) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2024 | 16 000 × 25% = 4 000 | 12 000 |
| 2025 | 12 000 × 25% = 3 000 | 9 000 |
| 2026 | 9 000 × 25% = 2 250 | 6 750 |

Depreciation for 2026 is $2 250. Net book value is $6 750.

### Practice question 2

A machine was bought on 1 January 2022 for $9 000. It is depreciated at 15% a year on cost by straight line, with a full year in the year of purchase and none in the year of sale. It was sold on 30 June 2026 for $3 200 by bank. Prepare the disposal account.

**Answer**

Years charged: 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025. Depreciation: 4 × 1 350 = $5 400. Net book value: 9 000 − 5 400 = $3 600. Loss: 3 600 − 3 200 = $400.

**Disposal of machinery account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Jun 30 Machinery | 9 000 | 2026 Jun 30 Provision for depreciation of machinery | 5 400 |
| | | Jun 30 Bank | 3 200 |
| | | Dec 31 Income statement | 400 |
| | **9 000** | | **9 000** |

### Practice question 3

Loose tools were valued at $2 400 on 1 January. During the year tools costing $900 were bought. On 31 December they were valued at $2 600. Calculate the depreciation and say where it appears.

**Answer**

2 400 + 900 − 2 600 = $700. It is an expense in the income statement. The tools appear at their closing valuation of $2 600 in the statement of financial position.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between depreciation and provision for depreciation?

Depreciation is the charge for one year, shown as an expense. The provision for depreciation is the total charged so far on the assets still owned, and it builds up year after year.

### Does reducing balance use residual value?

No. You apply the percentage to the net book value each year. Residual value is used in the straight line method.

### Is a profit on disposal a debit or a credit in the disposal account?

A debit. The matching credit goes to the income statement, where it is added as other income.

### Is depreciation charged in the year an asset is sold?

Only if the policy says so. Read the question: it will say either a full year in the year of purchase and none in the year of sale, or a monthly charge.

### Why is depreciation not a cash expense?

No money is paid out when it is charged. It only spreads a cost that was paid when the asset was bought.

### Where does the provision for depreciation appear in the statement of financial position?

It is deducted from cost in the non-current assets section to give net book value. It is not a liability.
