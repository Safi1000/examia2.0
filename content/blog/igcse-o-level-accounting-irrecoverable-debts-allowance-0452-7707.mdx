An irrecoverable debt is money a credit customer will never pay. For IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 you write it off: debit irrecoverable debts, credit the customer. The allowance for irrecoverable debts is an estimate of debts that may not be paid. Only the change in the allowance goes to the income statement, and the full allowance is deducted from trade receivables.

## What does the syllabus say about irrecoverable debts?

This is section 4.4 of the [IGCSE Accounting 0452 syllabus](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452) and of the [O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus](/syllabus/o-level-accounting-7707) for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. You need to understand:

1. The meaning of irrecoverable debts and irrecoverable debts recovered.
2. Journal entries and ledger accounts to record irrecoverable debts.
3. Journal entries and ledger accounts to record irrecoverable debts recovered.
4. Why a business keeps an allowance for irrecoverable debts.
5. Journal entries and ledger accounts to create the allowance and to adjust it.

Section 5.1 then asks you to make these adjustments when you prepare the financial statements of a sole trader. The same adjustments appear in partnership and limited company questions.

### Why do past papers say "provision for doubtful debts"?

The 2027 to 2029 syllabuses use two newer names. The 0452 and 7707 past papers, mark schemes and examiner reports I used, from 2022 to 2026, use the older ones. The 2027 specimen papers already use the new names.

| Older name (papers up to 2026) | Name in the 2027 to 2029 syllabus |
|---|---|
| Provision for doubtful debts | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Income statement | Statement of profit or loss |

Irrecoverable debts has the same name in both. The double entry is identical. I use the new names below, and "income statement" because that is what most past papers you will practise on say.

## What is an irrecoverable debt?

A **trade receivable** is a credit customer who owes the business money. Usually they pay. Sometimes they cannot: the customer has closed down, disappeared or simply refuses. When the business decides the money will never come, the debt is **irrecoverable**.

Keeping that amount in trade receivables would overstate the asset. So the debt is **written off**: removed from the customer's account and treated as an expense of the year.

Two things are **not** irrecoverable debts:

- **A dishonoured cheque.** The customer's bank refused to pay it, so the customer owes the money again. The November 2022 0452 report made this point in a multiple choice question.
- **Discount allowed.** That is a reward for paying early, not a loss from not paying.

## How do you write off an irrecoverable debt?

Writing off is recorded in the **general journal**, because no goods or cash change hands. The November 2022 0452 report gave irrecoverable debts as an example of a general journal entry.

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Write off a customer's debt | Irrecoverable debts | The customer, by name |
| Year end: transfer the total | Income statement | Irrecoverable debts |

Credit the **named customer**, for example "Kamal", not "Trade receivables". The November 2022 Paper 23 report noted entries made to the trade receivables account instead of the named business. That same report said a narrative "should indicate why the journal entry was made". So write "Debt written off as irrecoverable", not "Irrecoverable debts debited, Kamal credited".

At the year end the irrecoverable debts account is closed. Every debt written off in the year goes to the income statement as **one** expense. In that November 2022 question, a debt written off in part (a) and other debts written off were added into one figure.

## What if a debt written off is paid later?

Sometimes a customer whose debt was written off pays after all. That is an **irrecoverable debt recovered**.

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Money received from a debt written off earlier | Bank (or cash) | Irrecoverable debts recovered |
| Year end: transfer the total | Irrecoverable debts recovered | Income statement |

The credit does **not** go back to the irrecoverable debts account. Both the November 2022 and June 2023 reports (0452, and 7707 in June 2023) described candidates choosing that wrong credit in multiple choice questions. The correct credit was the debts recovered account.

In the income statement, irrecoverable debts recovered is **other income**, added to gross profit.

## What is the allowance for irrecoverable debts and why keep one?

Some customers who owe money at the year end will not pay, but you do not yet know which ones. The **allowance for irrecoverable debts** is an estimate of that amount, usually a percentage of trade receivables.

The June 2023 Paper 21 mark scheme gave these reasons for keeping one:

- Some customers will fail to pay, so the business expects that loss in advance.
- **Prudence**: profit and trade receivables are not overstated.
- **Matching**: the expected loss is charged in the same year as the sales that caused it.

The 2027 specimen Paper 2 links a failure to update the allowance to the matching principle.

The June 2023 report said most candidates gave prudence and then kept expanding on it, when a second, different reason was needed. Give prudence and matching as two separate points.

### How is the percentage chosen?

Questions usually give you the percentage. But the November 2022 Paper 22 asked for two factors a trader would consider when setting it, and the report called it challenging. The mark scheme accepted points like these:

| Factor | What it means |
|---|---|
| Past experience | How many customers paid late or not at all in earlier years |
| Each customer's credit record | Whether particular customers have a history of slow payment |
| Age of the debts | Debts outstanding a long time are less likely to be paid |
| The local economy | Customers struggle to pay when the economy is weak |

## How do you calculate the allowance?

Follow the same three steps every time.

1. **Find the correct trade receivables figure.** If a debt still has to be written off at the year end, deduct it first.
2. **Apply the percentage** to that figure. This is the new allowance, the closing balance.
3. **Compare with the opening allowance.** The difference is the increase or decrease. Only this goes to the income statement.

Step 1 needs the most care. Read the question closely, because the figure can come either way:

- In a June 2025 multiple choice question, a debt of $200 still had to be written off. Some candidates did not deduct it before taking the percentage.
- In a November 2022 Paper 23 question, the closing trade receivables figure was already after the write offs. Many candidates deducted the write offs again.

My rule: if the question says a debt "is to be written off" at the year end, it is still inside the trade receivables figure. Deduct it. If the debts "were written off during the year" and you are given the closing balance, they are already out.

## Which entries record the allowance?

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Create the allowance, or increase it | Income statement | Allowance for irrecoverable debts |
| Decrease the allowance | Allowance for irrecoverable debts | Income statement |

In the income statement:

| Change | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| Increase in allowance | Expense |
| Decrease in allowance | Other income, added to gross profit |

The allowance account normally has a **credit balance**. The balance is carried down on the debit side and brought down on the credit side. The March 2026 report confirmed both points, and that the increase is debited to the income statement.

In the statement of financial position, the full closing allowance is **deducted from trade receivables** under current assets.

<Tip title="Only the change, never the balance">
The opening or closing allowance is never an expense. The June 2022 reports (0452 and 7707) called putting either balance in the income statement a very common error. Work out the difference, then write that one figure.
</Tip>

This is where students get stuck. Four accounts, two years and a percentage all meet in one question, and one wrong figure in step 1 spoils the allowance account, the income statement and the statement of financial position together.

## How do the accounts work together? A worked example

Imran is a sole trader. His year ends on 31 December. He keeps an allowance for irrecoverable debts at 3% of trade receivables at the year end. All figures are my own.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1 January 2026 | Allowance for irrecoverable debts $600 |
| 14 March 2026 | Kamal's debt of $350 written off as irrecoverable |
| 20 August 2026 | $180 received by bank from Rashid, whose debt was written off in 2025 |
| 31 December 2026 | Trade receivables $24 400, before any year end adjustment |
| 31 December 2026 | Bina's debt of $400 is to be written off as irrecoverable |

Prepare the journal entries, the irrecoverable debts account, the irrecoverable debts recovered account and the allowance account. Then show the extracts from the income statement and the statement of financial position.

### Step 1: the year end write off and the allowance

Bina's debt is still inside the $24 400, so deduct it first.

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Trade receivables before adjustment | 24 400 |
| Less Bina's debt written off | 400 |
| Trade receivables at 31 December 2026 | 24 000 |
| Allowance needed: 24 000 × 3% | 720 |
| Less allowance at 1 January 2026 | 600 |
| **Increase in allowance** | **120** |

Kamal's $350 was written off in March. It is already out of the $24 400, so it is not deducted again.

### Step 2: the journal entries

| Date | Account | Debit ($) | Credit ($) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Mar 14 | Irrecoverable debts | 350 | |
| | Kamal | | 350 |
| Aug 20 | Bank | 180 | |
| | Irrecoverable debts recovered | | 180 |
| Dec 31 | Irrecoverable debts | 400 | |
| | Bina | | 400 |
| Dec 31 | Income statement | 120 | |
| | Allowance for irrecoverable debts | | 120 |

Narratives, in order: "Debt written off as irrecoverable", "Debt previously written off now recovered", "Debt written off as irrecoverable", "Increase in allowance for irrecoverable debts to 3% of trade receivables".

### Step 3: the ledger accounts

**Imran: Irrecoverable debts account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Mar 14 Kamal | 350 | 2026 Dec 31 Income statement | 750 |
| Dec 31 Bina | 400 | | |
| | **750** | | **750** |

**Imran: Irrecoverable debts recovered account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Dec 31 Income statement | 180 | 2026 Aug 20 Bank | 180 |

**Imran: Allowance for irrecoverable debts account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Dec 31 Balance c/d | 720 | 2026 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 600 |
| | | Dec 31 Income statement | 120 |
| | **720** | | **720** |
| | | 2027 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 720 |

Notice what is **not** in the allowance account: trade receivables and irrecoverable debts. Those belong in other accounts.

### Step 4: the financial statements

**Imran: Extract from the income statement for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| | $ |
|---|---:|
| Other income: irrecoverable debts recovered | 180 |
| Expenses: irrecoverable debts | 750 |
| Expenses: increase in allowance for irrecoverable debts | 120 |

Together these reduce profit by 750 + 120 − 180 = $690.

**Imran: Extract from the statement of financial position at 31 December 2026**

| Current assets | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trade receivables | 24 000 | |
| Less allowance for irrecoverable debts | 720 | 23 280 |

The $23 280 is the amount Imran realistically expects to collect.

### What happens when the allowance falls?

In 2027, trade receivables at the year end are $20 000. The allowance stays at 3%, so it should be 20 000 × 3% = $600. It was $720, so it falls by $120.

**Imran: Allowance for irrecoverable debts account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2027 Dec 31 Income statement | 120 | 2027 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 720 |
| Dec 31 Balance c/d | 600 | | |
| | **720** | | **720** |
| | | 2028 Jan 1 Balance b/d | 600 |

The $120 decrease is other income in the 2027 income statement. The June 2025 Paper 22 question had a decrease like this. In a June 2025 multiple choice question, some candidates thought the change was a debit to the allowance account when it was a credit, because that allowance had risen. Decide first whether the allowance went up or down, then place the entry.

What the marks were for, inside the accounts:

- Each correct entry usually earned one mark. The transfer to the income statement was often marked as an own figure, so a correct method still scored after an earlier slip.

## What else do examiners ask about irrecoverable debts?

- **Ways to reduce irrecoverable debts.** Mark schemes accepted credit references, credit limits, sending invoices and statements promptly, chasing overdue accounts, refusing further supplies and, as a last resort, legal action. The November 2022 scheme also listed selling for cash only. But the June 2025 scheme did not, and that report was surprised how many answers gave it, calling it unrealistic where credit sales are the normal way to trade. My advice is to give one of the other ways.
- **Prudence explained.** The June 2023 Paper 22 report gave a second mark for linking prudence to irrecoverable debts specifically: writing them off stops profit and assets being overstated.
- **Advice questions.** In November 2022 a trader chose between charging interest on overdue accounts and refusing supplies to late payers. Answers needed both options discussed and a recommendation. A March 2022 question on employing a credit controller rewarded answers that weighed fewer irrecoverable debts against the salary cost.
- **Credit terms.** The November 2023 7707 report said answers on longer credit focused too much on irrecoverable debts themselves. The better point was the **risk** of irrecoverable debts, and the effect on liquidity.
- **Inside other questions.** Irrecoverable debts are credited in the sales ledger control account. The November 2023 7707 report noted them being omitted there, and the March 2023 report found them wrongly placed in the purchases ledger control account. My [control accounts guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-control-accounts) shows where each item goes. In clubs, unpaid subscriptions written off reduce subscription income, which the June 2023 report said was frequently missed.

The full income statement with every adjustment is in my [income statement format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format). The layout of current assets is in the [statement of financial position guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-statement-of-financial-position-format).

## What do examiners say candidates get wrong?

These come from the examiner reports. Reports marked 7707 are O Level; the rest are 0452.

| Mistake | What to do instead |
|---|---|
| Putting the opening or closing allowance in the income statement (June 2022, 0452 and 7707) | Only the increase or decrease goes there |
| Transferring the whole closing allowance to the income statement (June 2023, 0452 and 7707) | Transfer just the change |
| Trade receivables or irrecoverable debts inside the allowance account (June 2023, November 2023 7707) | The allowance account holds the balances and the change only |
| Not deducting a year end write off before taking the percentage (June 2025) | Deduct any debt still to be written off first |
| Deducting write offs from a figure that was already net (November 2022) | Check whether the debts were already written off during the year |
| Crediting irrecoverable debts when a written off debt is paid (November 2022, June 2023) | Credit irrecoverable debts recovered |
| Adding the allowance to current assets, or deducting it from their total (March 2026) | Deduct it from trade receivables only |
| Showing the allowance as a current liability (June 2025) | It is a deduction from an asset, not a liability |

## Practice questions with worked answers

Try each one on paper first. All figures are my own.

### Practice question 1

On 1 July 2025 the allowance for irrecoverable debts was $1 320. On 30 June 2026 trade receivables were $38 300. A debt of $800 is to be written off. The allowance is kept at 4% of trade receivables. Calculate the change in the allowance and prepare the statement of financial position extract.

**Answer**

Trade receivables: 38 300 − 800 = $37 500. Allowance: 37 500 × 4% = $1 500. Increase: 1 500 − 1 320 = $180, an expense.

| Current assets | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Trade receivables | 37 500 | |
| Less allowance for irrecoverable debts | 1 500 | 36 000 |

### Practice question 2

Prepare journal entries, with narratives, for these two events.

1. 9 May: Tariq owes $260 and has left the country. His debt is written off.
2. 2 October: $90 cash is received from Huma, whose debt was written off two years ago.

**Answer**

| Date | Account | Debit ($) | Credit ($) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| May 9 | Irrecoverable debts | 260 | |
| | Tariq | | 260 |
| Oct 2 | Cash | 90 | |
| | Irrecoverable debts recovered | | 90 |

Narratives: "Debt written off as irrecoverable, customer left the country" and "Debt previously written off now recovered".

### Practice question 3

On 1 April 2025 the allowance for irrecoverable debts was $1 250. On 31 March 2026 trade receivables, after all write offs, were $21 000. The allowance is 5%. Profit for the year before this adjustment was $15 600. Prepare the allowance account and calculate the revised profit.

**Answer**

New allowance: 21 000 × 5% = $1 050. Decrease: 1 250 − 1 050 = $200.

**Allowance for irrecoverable debts account**

| Details | $ | Details | $ |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026 Mar 31 Income statement | 200 | 2025 Apr 1 Balance b/d | 1 250 |
| Mar 31 Balance c/d | 1 050 | | |
| | **1 250** | | **1 250** |
| | | 2026 Apr 1 Balance b/d | 1 050 |

A decrease is other income, so revised profit is 15 600 + 200 = $15 800.

## FAQ

### Is an irrecoverable debt an expense?

Yes. Every debt written off in the year is added together and shown as one expense in the income statement.

### Is the allowance for irrecoverable debts a liability?

No. It is deducted from trade receivables under current assets in the statement of financial position. It reduces an asset to the amount you expect to collect.

### Where does a decrease in the allowance go?

It is other income in the income statement, added to gross profit. The double entry is debit the allowance account, credit the income statement.

### What is the double entry for irrecoverable debts recovered?

Debit bank or cash and credit irrecoverable debts recovered. At the year end that account is closed to the income statement as other income.

### Do I deduct irrecoverable debts before calculating the allowance?

Only debts that still have to be written off at the year end. Debts already written off during the year are not in the closing balance, so do not deduct them again.

### Is provision for doubtful debts the same as allowance for irrecoverable debts?

Yes. Provision for doubtful debts is the older name used in past papers up to 2026. The 2027 to 2029 syllabus uses allowance for irrecoverable debts, and the entries are the same.
