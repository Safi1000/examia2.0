Limited company accounts for IGCSE 0452 and O Level 7707 are three linked statements. The statement of profit or loss works out profit for the year. The statement of changes in equity adds that profit to retained earnings and takes off dividends and transfers to the general reserve. The closing equity figures then go into the statement of financial position, which must balance.

## What does the 0452 and 7707 syllabus ask for on limited companies?

Limited companies are topic 5.3 in both the 0452 and the 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The wording is the same in both. You need to understand:

- that a limited company can be a trading, service or manufacturing business, or a mix of these
- the advantages and disadvantages of operating as a limited company
- the terms limited liability and equity
- the capital structure: preference share capital, ordinary share capital, general reserve and retained earnings
- the features of ordinary share capital and loan capital (debentures)
- issued, called up and paid up share capital
- how to prepare the statement of profit or loss, the statement of changes in equity and the statement of financial position
- the same year end adjustments as for a sole trader (topic 5.1)

Notice what is not on the list: share premium, bonus issues, rights issues and tax. Those belong to AS Level, and I cover them in my [9706 limited company accounts guide](/blog/a-level-accounting-9706-limited-company-accounts). For 0452 and 7707, keep to the four parts of equity above.

This topic comes up a lot. On my past paper topic pages, 117 questions from 2022 to 2026 papers are filed under section 5.3. That is 73 for 0452 and 44 for 7707. You can tick off each point on the [0452 syllabus checklist](/syllabus/igcse-accounting-0452#s-5.3).

## What is a limited company, and what does limited liability mean?

A limited company is a business owned by **shareholders**, who buy shares in it. It is a separate legal entity: the company itself owns the assets, owes the debts and can be taken to court. Directors run it day to day.

**Limited liability** means a shareholder can lose only the amount they invested in shares. If the company cannot pay its debts, the shareholders' own houses and savings are safe. The March 2023 0452 paper tested exactly this: the shareholders, not the directors, employees or lenders, have limited liability.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Limited liability for shareholders | Legal requirements to follow when forming and running the company |
| Separate legal entity from its owners | Costs of forming the company or converting to one |
| Continuity: the company carries on if a shareholder leaves or dies | Selling new shares can dilute the control of existing shareholders |
| Easier to raise more capital and obtain loans | Shareholders expect dividends |

A **public** limited company may offer its shares to the public and must publish its financial statements. A **private** limited company cannot offer shares to the public. The November 2024 0452 mark scheme accepted either difference.

When a question asks whether partners should convert to a limited company, read the scenario first. The June 2023 0452 and 7707 reports both describe a question where nobody except the two partners would hold shares. Answers about selling shares to the public or losing control did not apply.

## What is equity, and what is it made of?

**Equity** is the owners' stake in the company. The June 2025 0452 mark scheme accepted "the total funds provided by the owners of a business". It also accepted the other view: assets less liabilities. The report for that paper said only a minority of candidates could define equity, so learn both versions.

For 0452 and 7707, a company's equity has up to four parts:

| Part of equity | What it is |
|---|---|
| Ordinary share capital | Money raised from ordinary shareholders, the owners of the company |
| Preference share capital | Money raised from preference shareholders, who usually receive a fixed rate of dividend before ordinary shareholders are paid |
| General reserve | Past profits the directors have set aside and chosen not to pay out as dividends |
| Retained earnings | Profits kept in the company after dividends and transfers to the general reserve |

Retained earnings are the company's version of a sole trader's profit added to capital. A **dividend** is the share of profit paid to shareholders. It is the company's version of drawings: it reduces retained earnings and is never an expense.

### Issued, called up and paid up share capital

These three terms often appear as short definition questions.

- **Issued share capital**: the value of shares the company has actually issued to shareholders.
- **Called up share capital**: the part of the issued capital the company has asked shareholders to pay so far.
- **Paid up share capital**: the part of the called up capital the shareholders have actually paid.

Here is an example with my own figures. A company issues 100 000 ordinary shares of $1 each. It asks shareholders to pay $0.60 per share now. They have paid $58 000 so far.

| Term | Working | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Issued | 100 000 × $1 | 100 000 |
| Called up | 100 000 × $0.60 | 60 000 |
| Paid up | Amount received | 58 000 |

So $40 000 has not yet been called up, and $2 000 that was called up is still owed by shareholders. In most exam questions the shares are fully paid, so all three figures are the same.

## How are ordinary shares different from debentures?

A debenture is a long term loan to the company. Debenture holders are lenders, not owners. This difference decides where every figure goes.

| | Ordinary shares | Debentures |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds them | Owners (members) of the company | Lenders to the company |
| Return | Dividend, only if the directors decide to pay one | Interest at a fixed rate, paid even if there is a loss |
| Voting | Ordinary shareholders can vote | No vote |
| Repayment | A permanent source of finance, not repaid | Must be repaid, and may be secured on the company's assets |
| Where it goes | Equity | Non-current liabilities (current if due within a year) |
| Its return goes to | Dividends paid: statement of changes in equity | Debenture interest: statement of profit or loss |

The June 2022 7707 report made the point that debenture holders receive interest whatever the profit, are not members and cannot vote. The June 2023 7707 report added that a company with poor results may pay its ordinary shareholders nothing. A question on dividend policy (November 2023 7707) expected retained earnings, not share capital, as the figure it affects.

## What is the layout of a limited company statement of profit or loss?

It is the same as a sole trader's statement, with one extra line: debenture interest. Cambridge papers also call it the income statement. My [income statement format guide](/blog/igcse-o-level-accounting-income-statement-format) covers the revenue, cost of sales and expense lines in full.

| Line | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Sales less sales returns |
| Less cost of sales | Opening inventory + purchases less closing inventory |
| **Gross profit** | |
| Add other income | Rent received, discounts received and similar |
| Less expenses | Wages, rent, depreciation, irrecoverable debts and so on, adjusted for accruals and prepayments |
| **Profit from operations** | Profit before interest |
| Less debenture interest | The full year's interest, whether paid or not |
| **Profit for the year** | Goes to retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity |

Cambridge set out loan interest this way in a June 2024 mark scheme for 0452 and 7707: profit from operations, then the interest, then profit for the year. The November 2022 0452 report calls profit before debenture interest the operating profit.

Three things never appear here: dividends, transfers to the general reserve and share capital. The November 2023 7707 report lists dividends, retained earnings and share capital among the extra items candidates wrongly put into an income statement.

Profit from operations matters later. Return on capital employed (ROCE) uses profit for the year before interest, so you add the debenture interest back. The November 2022 0452 report says many candidates used profit after interest. The June 2025 report says many knew the formula but chose the wrong figures.

## What is a statement of changes in equity?

The statement of changes in equity shows how each part of equity moved during the year. There is a column for each part of equity, plus a total column. Each row is one event. This is the layout Cambridge mark schemes for 0452 and 7707 use:

**[Company name] Limited: Statement of changes in equity for the year ended [date]**

| Details | Share capital $ | General reserve $ | Retained earnings $ | Total $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| On [opening date] | X | X | X | X |
| Issue of shares | X | | | X |
| Profit for the year | | | X | X |
| Dividends paid | | | (X) | (X) |
| Transfer to general reserve | | X | (X) | |
| On [closing date] | X | X | X | X |

Five rules sit behind it:

- **Use full row labels.** "Profit for the year", "Dividends paid", "Transfer to general reserve", "Issue of shares". The March 2026 0452 report names "dividend" and "shares" as incomplete labels.
- **Date the first and last rows only.** The opening row has the first day of the year and the closing row the last day.
- **Brackets mean a deduction.** Dividends paid and the transfer out of retained earnings go in brackets.
- **A transfer leaves the total unchanged.** It moves profit from retained earnings into the general reserve, so nothing is added to the total column.
- **Check down and across.** Add each column down. Then add the closing row across. Both must give the same total.

The June 2022 0452 report described this statement as "a straightforward exercise, but it does take practice". Some candidates put the opening balances at the year end or gave a row the wrong sign. The same report recommends the check down and across. If a question gives a preference share capital column, it works the same way.

<Tip title="Retained earnings in three moves">
Closing retained earnings = opening retained earnings + profit for the year less dividends paid less the transfer to general reserve. Write this as a working beside your statement. If the profit figure is your own from an earlier part, you still earn the follow through marks.
</Tip>

This is where most students get stuck. The statement is short, but each figure has to come from the right place and go into the right column. The worked example below shows every step.

## How do you prepare limited company accounts from a trial balance?

Falcon Limited is my own company with my own figures. Try it on paper first.

**Falcon Limited: Trial balance at 31 December 2026**

| Account | Dr $ | Cr $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Premises at cost | 150 000 | |
| Equipment at cost | 60 000 | |
| Accumulated depreciation of equipment | | 18 000 |
| Inventory at 1 January 2026 | 18 000 | |
| Revenue | | 260 000 |
| Purchases | 150 000 | |
| Wages and salaries | 38 000 | |
| Rent and rates | 9 600 | |
| General expenses | 7 400 | |
| Debenture interest | 1 600 | |
| Trade receivables | 20 000 | |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | | 500 |
| Trade payables | | 14 600 |
| Bank | 52 800 | |
| 8% debentures (repayable 2032) | | 40 000 |
| Ordinary shares of $1 each, fully paid | | 150 000 |
| General reserve | | 15 000 |
| Retained earnings at 1 January 2026 | | 21 300 |
| Dividends paid | 12 000 | |
| **Totals** | **519 400** | **519 400** |

### The notes at 31 December 2026

1. Inventory was valued at $21 000.
2. Rent of $800 was paid in advance. General expenses of $600 were owing.
3. The debentures were in issue all year. Only half the year's interest has been paid.
4. Equipment is depreciated at 20% a year on cost. Premises are not depreciated.
5. The allowance for irrecoverable debts is to be 4% of trade receivables.
6. On 1 July 2026 the company issued 50 000 ordinary shares of $1 each at par. They are included in the trial balance.
7. The dividend paid was $0.08 per share, paid after the share issue.
8. The directors transfer $10 000 to the general reserve.

### Workings

| Item | Working | $ |
|---|---|---:|
| Cost of sales | 18 000 + 150 000 less 21 000 | 147 000 |
| Rent and rates | 9 600 less 800 prepaid | 8 800 |
| General expenses | 7 400 + 600 owing | 8 000 |
| Depreciation of equipment | 60 000 × 20% | 12 000 |
| Allowance | 20 000 × 4% = 800, less opening 500 | 300 increase |
| Debenture interest | 40 000 × 8% = 3 200, paid 1 600 | 1 600 owing |
| Opening share capital | 150 000 less 50 000 issued in the year | 100 000 |
| Dividend check | 150 000 shares × $0.08 | 12 000 |

The share issue is already in the trial balance, so the opening share capital is $100 000. The dividend check agrees with the trial balance, so nothing more is owed to shareholders.

### Statement of profit or loss

**Falcon Limited: Statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| | $ | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | | 260 000 |
| Less cost of sales | | (147 000) |
| **Gross profit** | | 113 000 |
| Less expenses | | |
| Wages and salaries | 38 000 | |
| Rent and rates | 8 800 | |
| General expenses | 8 000 | |
| Depreciation of equipment | 12 000 | |

| Less debenture interest | | (3 200) |
| **Profit for the year** | | **42 700** |

The debenture interest is the full $3 200, not the $1 600 paid. The dividend is nowhere in this statement.

### Statement of changes in equity

**Falcon Limited: Statement of changes in equity for the year ended 31 December 2026**

| Details | Share capital $ | General reserve $ | Retained earnings $ | Total $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| On 1 January 2026 | 100 000 | 15 000 | 21 300 | 136 300 |
| Issue of shares | 50 000 | | | 50 000 |

| Transfer to general reserve | | 10 000 | (10 000) | |
| **On 31 December 2026** | **150 000** | **25 000** | **42 000** | **217 000** |

Check down the total column: 136 300 + 50 000 + 42 700 less 12 000 = 217 000. Check across the closing row: 150 000 + 25 000 + 42 000 = 217 000. Both agree.

### A ratio from the same figures

Questions often go on to ask for ROCE. The syllabus defines capital employed as issued shares + reserves + non-current liabilities.

- Capital employed: 150 000 + 25 000 + 42 000 + 40 000 = $257 000
- ROCE: 45 900 ÷ 257 000 × 100 = 17.86%

The top line is profit before interest ($45 900), not profit for the year. Recent mark schemes asked for two decimal places and the % sign. You can check ratio answers with my [ratio calculator](/tools/ratio-calculator).

## How are limited company questions marked?

Paper 2 for both 0452 and 7707 is 1 hour 45 minutes. You answer five compulsory questions for 100 marks, and the paper is 70% of the qualification. A company question often has a statement of changes in equity, then a statement of financial position, then a ratio and an advice question.

| Part | Marks in recent 0452 mark schemes |
|---|---|
| Statement of changes in equity | 4 (June 2025, March 2026); 7 (November 2024, with two dividend rows) |
| Statement of financial position | 7 (June 2025, March 2026); 8 (June 2026) |
| ROCE | 3 (June 2025, June 2026) |
| Shares or debentures advice | 5 (June 2025, June 2026) |

In the statement of changes in equity, each row usually earns one mark, and the closing row is an "own figure" mark. That means the examiner follows your figures: if one row is wrong but your closing row adds up, you still earn it.

In the statement of financial position, the equity section often earns one mark for the three figures together. They must match your statement of changes in equity. Figures you had to adjust and section totals earn the other marks.

### How do you answer the shares or debentures question?

The advice question gives a choice: issue shares, issue debentures or take a bank loan. The June 2025 mark scheme gave up to 4 marks for points for and against, and 1 for a clear recommendation. The 0452 reports for November 2022 and June 2023, and the 7707 report for November 2023, all say weaker answers discussed only one option.

- **Shares, for**: a permanent source of finance, no interest, dividends do not have to be paid.
- **Shares, against**: control may be diluted, shareholders will expect dividends.
- **Debentures, for**: no dilution of control, debenture holders do not vote.
- **Debentures, against**: interest must be paid even in a loss year, and the loan must be repaid.

Finish with one sentence that chooses, and give a reason linked to the company in the question.

## Where do candidates lose marks?

These come from Cambridge examiner reports for 0452 and 7707 between 2022 and 2026.

| Error | Reported in |
|---|---|
| Incomplete row labels such as "Profit", "Dividend" or "General reserve" | 0452 November 2022, June 2025, March 2026; 7707 November 2023 |
| Dates missing from the opening and closing rows | 0452 November 2022 |
| Extra items in the statement of changes in equity, such as debentures, bank or non-current assets | 0452 November 2022; 7707 November 2023 |
| Wrong signs or wrong columns, such as the dividend in the share capital column | 0452 and 7707 June 2022; 7707 November 2023 |
| Equity section left out, shown as one figure, or retained earnings missing | 0452 and 7707 June 2023; 0452 June 2025, March 2026 |
| Debenture interest, dividends paid or profit for the year put in the statement of financial position | 0452 and 7707 June 2023; 0452 June 2025, March 2026 |
| Debentures shown as a current liability, or the overdraft as non-current | 0452 June 2025 |
| ROCE worked from the wrong figures, such as profit after interest or capital employed without the debentures | 0452 November 2022, June 2025 |

The same reports carry good news. In June 2023 many candidates on both syllabuses earned high marks on the company statement of financial position. The March 2026 report says the stronger answers itemised each part of equity. They took the figures from their statement of changes in equity.

<Tip title="One source for every equity figure">
Never type an equity figure straight from the trial balance into the statement of financial position. Prepare the statement of changes in equity first, then copy its closing row. The trial balance shows retained earnings at the start of the year, not the end.
</Tip>

## Practice questions with answers

Try each one before reading the answer. For real exam questions with mark schemes, use the [0452 limited companies questions](/past-papers/igcse-accounting-0452/topics/5-3-limited-companies) or the [7707 set](/past-papers/o-level-accounting-7707/topics/5-3-limited-companies).

### Question 1: share capital terms

Saba Limited issued 80 000 ordinary shares of $1 each. It has asked for $0.80 per share so far. Shareholders have paid $60 000. State the issued, called up and paid up share capital.

**Answer**

| Term | $ |
|---|---:|
| Issued (80 000 × $1) | 80 000 |
| Called up (80 000 × $0.80) | 64 000 |
| Paid up | 60 000 |

Shareholders still owe $4 000 of the amount called up.

### Question 2: statement of changes in equity

Rehan Limited's balances on 1 April 2026: ordinary share capital $80 000, general reserve $10 000, retained earnings $14 500. During the year to 31 March 2027 the company issued 20 000 ordinary shares of $1 each at par. Profit for the year was $26 000. Dividends paid were $9 000, and $6 000 was transferred to the general reserve. Prepare the statement of changes in equity.

**Answer**

| Details | Share capital $ | General reserve $ | Retained earnings $ | Total $ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| On 1 April 2026 | 80 000 | 10 000 | 14 500 | 104 500 |
| Issue of shares | 20 000 | | | 20 000 |
| Profit for the year | | | 26 000 | 26 000 |
| Dividends paid | | | (9 000) | (9 000) |
| Transfer to general reserve | | 6 000 | (6 000) | |
| On 31 March 2027 | 100 000 | 16 000 | 25 500 | 141 500 |

Down: 104 500 + 20 000 + 26 000 less 9 000 = 141 500. Across: 100 000 + 16 000 + 25 500 = 141 500.

### Question 3: find the retained earnings

At 31 December 2026 Kiran Limited had total assets of $190 000 and current liabilities of $18 000. It had 7% debentures of $30 000, ordinary share capital of $100 000, 5% preference share capital of $20 000 and a general reserve of $12 000. Calculate the retained earnings, prepare the equity section, and calculate capital employed.

**Answer**

Equity = assets less liabilities: 190 000 less 18 000 less 30 000 = $142 000. Retained earnings = 142 000 less 100 000 less 20 000 less 12 000 = **$10 000**.

| Equity | $ |
|---|---:|
| Ordinary share capital | 100 000 |
| Preference share capital | 20 000 |
| General reserve | 12 000 |
| Retained earnings | 10 000 |
| | 142 000 |

Capital employed = 142 000 + 30 000 = $172 000. Check: total assets less current liabilities, 190 000 less 18 000, also gives $172 000.

## FAQ

### What is a statement of changes in equity in O Level Accounting?

It shows how each part of a company's equity changed during the year: share capital, general reserve and retained earnings. Profit for the year is added, and dividends paid and transfers are deducted, to reach the closing balances.

### Do dividends go in the income statement?

No. A dividend is a payment to the owners, not an expense. It goes in the statement of changes in equity as a deduction from retained earnings.

### Are debentures part of equity?

No. Debentures are loans, so they are a non-current liability. They become a current liability only when they are due for repayment within a year. Their interest is an expense in the statement of profit or loss.

### What is the difference between the general reserve and retained earnings?

Both are profits kept in the company. The general reserve holds profits the directors have chosen to set aside. Retained earnings hold what is left after dividends and that transfer.

### Do I need share premium or bonus issues for 0452 or 7707?

No. Topic 5.3 in both 2027 to 2029 syllabuses does not list them. They are AS Level 9706 content.

### Where is this topic in the syllabus?

Limited companies are topic 5.3 in both 0452 and 7707. The adjustments come from topic 5.1 and topic 4. Ratios such as ROCE are topic 6.1.
